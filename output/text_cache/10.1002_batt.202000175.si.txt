increases with the scan rate (Fig. S3a). This is due to the increase of the oxidation 
peak intensity of S4*-/S42- reaction current with the scan rate (Fig. S3b). Since the 
exchange current density of S4*-/S42- reaction is very high (𝐼0(𝑆4

Figure S3. (a) Simulated CVs of 6 mM S8 in 1 M LiTFSI in TEGDME:DOL with different 
scan rates produced without the S3*- and S32- reactions and (b) Simulated individual

Unlike the experimental CVs of 50 mM Li2S8 and Li2S6 in 1 M LiTFSI in TEGDME:DOL 
(Figs. 9a and 10a), the OXI2 peaks are visible in the corresponding simulated CVs 
produced without the S3*- and S32- reactions (Fig S4). 
In summary, we could fit the simulated CV produced using a simpler reaction 
mechanism with the experimental one corresponding to 6 mM S8 in 1 M LiTFSI in 
TEGDME:DOL at 5 mV.s-1 by adjusting certain parameters. However, it is not possible 
to capture experimental trends such as the OXI2 peak intensity variation with the scan 
rate (Fig. S3a) and the impact of the initial polysulfides speciation on the CV 
characteristics (Fig. S4) without considering S3*- and S32- reactions. As reported in our 
main paper, these peak intensity variations and features could be simulated correctly 
only by considering S3*- and S32- reactions in our cyclic voltammetry model.

S.2. Evolutions of polysulfides concentrations during the oxidation potential 
scans 
Since the S32- concentration starts to decrease significantly beyond -1.2 V (Figs. S5a 
and b), the oxidation of S32- to S3*- is the dominant reaction during the OXI2 peak. Since 
the concentrations of all the polysulfides ions and radicals decrease significantly 
beyond -0.6 V, they undergo simultaneous oxidations during OXI1 peak. Finally, S8 is 
the dominant species in the electrolyte near the electrode surface at the end of the 
oxidation scans of both S8 and Li2S8 solutions (Figs. S5a and b). 
 
Figure S5. Simulated species concentrations at the electrode surface ((𝑐𝑖)𝑥=0) during 
oxidation potential scans of (a) 6 mM S8 solution at 5 mV.s-1 and (b) 50 mM Li2S8 
solution at 100 mV.s-1 produced using our comprehensive reaction mechanism.

S.3. Evolutions of sulfur based solid precipitates 
 
The simulated Li+ concentration and S8(s) and Li2S(s) volume fractions at the electrode 
surface do not significantly evolve during the oxidation and reduction scans of 6 mM 
S8 solution at 5 mV.s-1 (Figs. S6a and b).
