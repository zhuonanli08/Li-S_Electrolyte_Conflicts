S-2
Preparation of porous carbon–sulfur composites
Three commercial porous carbons with different pore size distributions (Cmicro, Cmicro-meso, and Cmeso) were 
used. Cmicro, Cmicro-meso, and Cmeso were mixed with sulfur (Fujifilm Wako Pure Chemical Co., > 99%) at weight 
ratios of 76:24 (Cmicro:S), 46:54 (Cmicro-meso:S), and 40:60 (Cmeso:S). The mixtures were sealed in a stainless-
steel container and heated at 155°C for 5 h under air. Thus, Cmicro-S, Cmicro-meso-S, and Cmeso-S were obtained. 
The sulfur distribution in porous carbons was determined using the following formula:
theoretical sulfur content ratio [%] =
𝑉total × 𝑑Li2S × (𝑀S/𝑀𝐿𝑖2𝑆)
[𝑉𝑡𝑜𝑡𝑎𝑙× 𝑑Li2S × (𝑀S/𝑀Li2S)] + 1 × 100
where Vtotal represents the total pore volume of porous carbons, dLi2S the density of Li2S (1.66 g cm3), Ms the 
molar mass of sulfur (32.065 g mol−1), and MLi2S the molar mass of Li2S (46.947 g mol−1). The reason for 
adding 1 to the denominator is that it is assumed that sulfur is composited with 1 g of carbon. The sulfur 
confinement to the porous carbons using the ratio calculated in this formula matches the volume of Li2S 
produced during discharge with the total pore volume of the porous carbons. Thus, this sulfur distribution is 
the theoretical sulfur content ratio that accounts for sulfur expansion during the charge–discharge process. In 
the case of Cmicro-meso, the theoretical sulfur content ratio was calculated using more accurate total pore volumes 
provided by the supplier.

S-3
Preparation of porous carbon–sulfur composite cathodes
The Cmicro-S cathode was prepared by mixing Cmicro-S, acetylene black (AB, HS-100, Denka Co., Ltd.), 
carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC, F-AG, DKS Co., Ltd.), and styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR, TRD 2001, JSR 
Co.) in a ratio of 89:5:3:3 by weight. An Al foil was used as the current collector. The Cmicro-S cathode was 
punched into circular disks of 12 mm diameter. The cathode’s surface area was 1.13 cm2. The areal sulfur 
loading of the Cmicro-S cathode was 1.0 mg cm–2. The Cmicro-meso-S and Cmeso-S cathodes were prepared using 
the same method. The areal sulfur loading of the Cmicro-meso-S and Cmeso-S cathodes were 1.2 and 1.4 mg cm–
2, respectively.

S-4
Preparation of 0.2 M Li2S8/DME solution
Li2S (Strem Chemicals, Inc., 98%) was mixed with sulfur (Fujifilm Wako Pure Chemical Co., > 99%) in 
stoichiometric amounts (Li2S:S = 8:7 by mol). This mixture was added to DME (Kishida Chemical Co., Ltd.) 
and stirred overnight at 80°C. Thus, 0.2 M Li2S8/DME solution was obtained. All operations were performed 
in an Ar-filled glovebox.

S-9
Figure S5. XRD angle-dependent intensity profiles of porous carbon-sulfur composites.
XRD measurements 
XRD measurements were performed to investigate whether sulfur was confined to the pores of the porous 
carbons (Fig. S5). The XRD patterns of all the porous carbon–sulfur composites showed a halo pattern. It 
has been reported that the sulfur impregnated into micropores loses its thermodynamically stable 
orthorhombic S8 (α-S) phase and transforms into an amorphous phase (S2, S3). Hence, all the prepared 
porous carbon–sulfur composites contained sulfur in their pores, and sulfur did not exist on the porous 
carbon surface; this phenomenon has been reported previously (S4).

Electrochemical performances of high-sulfur-loading Cmicro-meso-S cathode/Li anode pouch cell
To evaluate the electrochemical performance of our Li-S batteries under practical conditions, a Cmicro-meso-S 
cathode with high sulfur loading per area was prepared. Cmicro-meso-S, AB, CMC, and SBR were mixed in a 
ratio of 89:5:3:3 by weight. Three-dimensional Al porous metal (Celmet, Sumitomo Electric Industries, 
Ltd.) was used as the current collector. The cathode’s surface area was 2.5 cm × 2.3 cm. The areal sulfur 
loading of the Cmicro-meso-S cathode was 16.8 mg cm–2. The lithium-metal foil was cut to form two 3 cm × 3 
cm sheets used as the anodes. Two 3 cm × 2.7 cm sheets of microporous polypropylene membrane were 
used as the separator. The electrolyte [μL]/sulfur [mg] (E/S) was fixed at 7.2. The high-sulfur-loading cell 
was constructed by placing the cathode in the center and the anodes on either side of it and sealing them in 
an aluminum pouch.
The charge–discharge curves and cycle plots of the Li-S cells using the Cmicro-meso-S cathode with high sulfur 
loading are shown in Fig. S10. The Cmicro-meso-S cathode/Li cell with 3 M LiFSI/DME exhibited large 
overpotential and did not reach the charge cut-off voltage at the 1st cycle; thus, the cell could not cycle (Fig. 
S10a). In contrast, the Cmicro-meso-S cathode/Li cell with 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE showed stable cycle 
performance (Fig. S10b, c). The discharge capacities at the 2nd and 50th cycles of the Cmicro-meso-S 
cathode/Li cell with 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE were 1203.7 and 787.3 mAh g–1, respectively. Moreover, the 
average coulombic efficiency was 98.6%. These enhanced performances of the Li-S cell even with the
