a b s t r a c t
Comprehensive analyses on thermal runaway mechanisms are critically vital to achieve the safe lithium
esulfur (LieS) batteries. The reactions between dissolved higher-order polysulﬁdes and Li metal were
found to be the origins for the thermal runaway of 1.0 Ah cycled LieS pouch cells. 16-cycle pouch cell
indicates high safety, heating from 30 to 300 C without thermal runaway, while 16-cycle pouch cell with
additional electrolyte undergoes severe thermal runaway at 147.9 C, demonstrating the key roles of the
electrolyte on the thermal safety of batteries. On the contrary, thermal runaway does not occur for 45-
cycle pouch cell despite the addition of the electrolyte. It is found that the higher-order polysulﬁdes
(Li2Sx  6) are discovered in 16-cycle electrolyte while the sulfur species in 45-cycle electrolyte are
Li2Sx  4. In addition, strong exothermic reactions are discovered between cycled Li and dissolved higher-
order polysulﬁde (Li2S6 and Li2S8) at 153.0 C, driving the thermal runaway of cycled LieS pouch cells.
This work uncovers the potential safety risks of LieS batteries and negative roles of the polysulﬁde
shuttle for LieS batteries from the safety view.

1. Introduction
The advance of electric vehicles and high-end portable devices
requires batteries with high energy density exceeding 400 Wh kg1
(Liu et al., 2021a, 2022; Piao et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2021; Yuan
et al., 2020). Lithium (Li) metal batteries (LMBs), especially the
lithiumesulfur (LieS) batteries have attracted signiﬁcant attentions
owning to their high theoretical speciﬁc energy density (2600 Wh
kg1), cost-effective, and environmentally friendly features (Cai
et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2018).
However, the practical applications of LieS batteries face many
obstacles, such as the “shuttle effect” of the intermediate lithium
polysulﬁdes (LiPSs) between anodes and cathodes (Bilal & Eroglu,
2021; Ji et al., 2021; Kaiser et al., 2019), the intrinsic insulation
property of S (Hou et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2021),
and dendrite issues of Li anode during cycles (Kong et al., 2021;
Wang et al., 2019). More importantly, the safety issue induced by
the highly active Li metal is an essential bottleneck for the practical
applications of LieS batteries (Chen et al., 2020; Cheng et al., 2021;
Huang et al., 2021, 2022).
Li metal is highly reductive and becomes dendritic after cycles,
leading to its violent parasitic reactions with oxidative agents such
as S electrode with a low melting point at about 100 C (Xu, Jiang,
et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022). In addition, the S/C cathode is highly
combustible which is thought to be a heat resource of LieS batteries
during thermal runaway. The thermal risks of LieS pouch cells with
short cycles (cycle number2) have been systematically investi-
gated. The interactions between electrolyte and sulfur species, the
redox reactions between Li anode and S/C cathode were demon-
strated to trigger the self-heating and the thermal runaway of

deeply cycled LieS batteries are employed for electrochemical
performance evaluation, while the safety features of them are less
involved though they are with much more practical value. Thermal
runaway behaviors of LMBs have been widely investigated and they
are closely correlated to the cycle numbers(Huang et al., 2020; Lai
et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2020, 2021b; Wang, Yuan, et al., 2021). The
speciﬁc surface area of Li anode grows with the increase of cycle
numbers, resulting in the aggravation of side reactions related to
the pulverization of Li metal and a sharp decline in thermal safety
of LMBs(Jiang et al., 2022). Therefore, it is of great importance to
investigate the thermal runaway behaviors of deeply cycled LieS
batteries.
This contribution clearly clariﬁes the inducements of thermal
runaway for long-term LieS pouch cells (16 cycles and 45 cycles).
The thermal runaway features between 16-cycle and 45-cycle LieS
pouch cells with and without additional electrolyte were system-
atically evaluated by accelerating rate calorimetry (ARC). The
thermal runaway occurs for the 16-cycle LieS pouch cell at 147.9 C
after adding electrolyte, while the other pouch cells (16-cycle cell
without the addition of electrolyte, 45-cycle with and without the
addition of electrolyte) are with thermal stability until 300 C. The
huge differences in battery thermal behaviors are attributed to the
high viscosity of electrolyte and the different LiPS species contained
in cycled electrolyte. The violent side reactions between battery
components are inhibited by the high viscosity electrolyte, while
they can be intensiﬁed by the injection of fresh electrolyte. The
higher-order polysulﬁdes (Li2Sx  6) and lower-order polysulﬁdes
(Li2Sx  4) are discovered in 16-cycle and 45-cycle electrolyte
respectively, which have different reactivities with Li metal. The
liquid electrolyte with higher-order polysulﬁde (Li2Sx  6) reacts
violently with cycled Li (147.9 C), which is the most important
trigger of the thermal runaway of cycled LieS pouch cells. These
novel understandings can provide a new insight into achieving a
safe LieS pouch cell.
2. Results and discussion
2.1. Electrochemical characterization of LieS pouch cells
The high sulfur content in the cathode side (5.25 mg cm2) and
low mass ratio of electrolyte to sulfur (E/S ratio ¼ 3 gelectrolyte gS1)
were strictly controlled in LieS pouch cells for the pursuit of
practical applications(Hou et al., 2022). In addition, it is generally
considered that the long cycle-number is detrimental to the ther-
mal safety of LMBs. Therefore, the 1.0 Ah LieS pouch cells with long
cycle numbers (16 cycles and 45 cycles) rather than short activated
cycle numbers (<2) were carried out (see Fig. 1(a) and Fig. S2.
Figs. S1eS6 are in the Supplementary Data). The pouch cells deliver
an initial capacity of about 5.0 mAh cm2. After long-term cycling,
the discharge capacity of the LieS pouch cells decreases drastically,
exhibiting only 2.97 and 2.03 mAh cm2 at the 16th and 45th cycle,
respectively, which is associated with the serious loss of active S
and Li metal. Furthermore, the severe polarization of the LieS
pouch cells occurs, leading to the collapse of the ﬁrst discharge
platform as well (Yan et al., 2020). The morphologies of battery
components including S/C cathode, Li anode, and separator for LieS
pouch cells with various cycle numbers were further investigated
(Fig. 1(bed)). There is no obvious morphological change for S/C
cathode after long cycles. In comparison, the shine Li anode be-
comes dark with rough appearance, which may aggravate the un-
desirable exothermic side reactions involving the cycled Li during
thermal runaway. What’ more, the separator changes from white to
yellow (16 cycles) and pale yellow (45 cycles), implying the pres-
ence of LiPSs in cycled electrolyte and the different polysulﬁde
species contained in electrolytes with various cycle numbers (Zhao
et al., 2016).
2.2. Cell-level thermal safety
With the aim to explore the thermal safety of LieS pouch cells,
the 16-cycle and 45-cycle pouch cells with 100% state of charge
were evaluated in ARC (Figs. S3(aec)). Surprisingly, the 16-cycle
and 45-cycle pouch cells indicate high thermal stability without
the sharp increase in temperature rate though they are internal-
short-circuited (ISC) at 156.8 and 153.5 C, respectively, corre-
lating to the collapse of PP-PE-PP separator (Fig. 2(aed)). In fact, the
thermal resistance of the cells is very high due to the lack of elec-
trolyte containing DOL and DME solvents with low boiling points.
Therefore, internal short circuit cannot result in the thermal
runaway of the cycled LieS pouch cells (Feng et al., 2019).
Polysulﬁde saturation is a common phenomenon of LieS pouch
cells in lean electrolyte systems, resulting in high viscosity and low
ionic conductivity of electrolytes (Lee et al., 2017; Liu, Elias, et al.,
2021). Notably, no electrolyte can be poured from the cycled
pouch cells though they have been hanged for 8 h (Fig. 3(a)). The
relationship between thermal runaway and the viscosity of elec-
trolyte has been systematically investigated in our previous
research (Jiang et al., 2023). The high viscosity, meaning the bad
diffusion behaviors of the electrolyte and undesirable activity of
charge transfer, leads to the poor contact between battery com-
ponents. As a result, the detrimental exothermic reactions between
Li metal and electrolyte, Li anode and S cathode are mitigated
efﬁciently. Furthermore, the highest temperatures of the pouch
cells with lean electrolyte are 310.0 (16 cycles) and 309.8 C (45
cycles) and the battery remains intact after the harsh ARC test
(Fig. 2(eeg)).
However, the high E/S ratio (>15 mL mg1) rather than lean
electrolyte is widely adopted to ensure fast redox kinetics in LieS
pouch cell (He et al., 2021). Therefore, the undesirable exothermic
reactions may be activated because of the low concentration of
polysulﬁde-containing electrolyte (Huang et al., 2021, 2022). The
cycled pouch cells were injected with 2.3 g fresh electrolyte to
simulate the LieS pouch cell with E/S ratio of 5 mL mg1, then fol-
lowed with the ARC tests (Fig. 3(b)). The exothermic reactions
contained in 45-cycle pouch cell with additional electrolyte (45-
cycle cell þ electrolyte) are too mild to trigger its thermal
runaway. As a result, its highest temperature rate is only 0.07 C s1
and the highest temperature is 304.0 C (Fig. 3(d), (f) and Fig. S3(e)).
In contrast, the 16-cycle pouch cell with additional electrolyte (16-
cycle cell þ electrolyte) undergoes severe thermal runaway with
the thermal-runaway temperature (T2) of 147.9 C and the highest
temperature (T3) of 436.0 C (see Fig. 3(c), (e) and Table S4.
Tables S1eS4 are in the Supplementary Data). The pouch bag
obviously bulges after the ARC test, attributing to the generation of
gas (C2H4, CH4, H2S, H2 and CO2 et al.), which have serious negative
effects on the safety of batteries (Fig. S3(d)) (Huang et al., 2022).
Therefore, it is vital to ﬁgure out the key exothermic reactions
which result in the thermal runaway of LieS pouch cells.
2.3. Materials-level thermal behaviors
Comparing the different thermal behaviors of 16-cycle pouch
cells with and without additional electrolyte, liquid electrolyte is
regarded as one of the most essential factors resulting in the
thermal runaway of cycled LieS pouch cells. Furthermore, 45-cycle
pouch cell with additional electrolyte indicates high thermal safety
from room temperature to 300 C. Therefore, it can be deduced that
LiPSs dissolved in ether solvents are essential for the thermal
runaway of cycled LieS pouch cell. As a result, the thermal stability

The species of LiPS in electrolyte absorbed in Li anode, S/C
cathode, and separator have been investigated by inductively

presented in Li anode because of the aggravated polysulﬁde shuttle

cycle pouch cells are Li2Sx  4. These results work with DSC and
ARC data to conﬁrm the key role of the reactions between cycled Li
and higher-order polysulﬁde in the thermal runaway of batteries.
Furthermore, the residual active Li metal in cycled pouch cells was
tested by Li || Li symmetrical coin cells (Fig. S6). There is enough Li
residual on a piece of the cycled anode for 16-cycle (28.52 mg) and
45-cycle (5.37 mg) LieS pouch cells. In addition, the two sides of
anodes for cycled LieS pouch cells is intact. Therefore, the coexis-
tence of higher-order polysulﬁde (Li2Sx  6) and Li metal are
indispensable to trigger the thermal runaway of cycled LieS pouch
cells. In addition to avoiding the direct contact between Li anode
and S cathode due to the failure of separators and the sublimation
of sulfur, it can be deduced that the strategies which inhibit the
polysulﬁde shuttle are beneﬁcial to improve the thermal safety of
cycled LieS battery (Wang, Ke, et al., 2021). There are many stra-
tegies proposed in the previous studies to inhibit the polysulﬁde
shuttle of LieS batteries including the use of single-atom metal
catalysts embedded cathode and suitable solid-state electrolytes,
rational engineering of ionic function layer on the separator, and
the introduction of self-supporting cofunction host, which are po-
tential to greatly enhance the thermal safety of LieS batteries (Chen
et al., 2022; Han et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2020; Zhang
et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2022).

3. Conclusion
The key exothermic reactions inducing the thermal runaway of
cycled LieS pouch cells have been systematically investigated.
Electrolyte viscosity and the species of polysulﬁde in the electrolyte
have signiﬁcant inﬂuences on the thermal safety of cycled LieS
pouch cells. The 16-cycle LieS pouch cells indicate high thermal
stability, while the thermal runaway is activated with the addition
of fresh electrolyte to decrease the viscosity of cycled electrolyte
(T2 ¼ 147.9 C). In contrast, the thermal behaviors for 45-cycle
pouch cells with additional electrolyte keep consistent with that
for cells without addition electrolyte. The difference of thermal
risks are attributed to the various LiPS species between 16-cycle
(Li2Sx  6) and 45-cycle pouch cells (Li2Sx  4) in cycled electro-
lyte. The violent exothermic reactions between high-order poly-
sulﬁde (Li2Sx  6) and cycled Li locate at 153 C while the lower-
order polysulﬁde (Li2Sx  4) is thermally stable against cycled Li.
Therefore, it can be concluded that the exothermic reactions be-
tween cycled Li and high-order polysulﬁde (Li2Sx  6) push the
cycled LieS pouch cells into thermal runaway. This work system-
atically reveals the thermal runaway mechanisms of cycled LieS
pouch cells and provides novel research signiﬁcance to avoid the
polysulﬁde shuttle from the view of the thermal safety for LieS
pouch cells.
