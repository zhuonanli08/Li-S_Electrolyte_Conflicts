Material characterization
Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) was measured by a BRUKER D8 ADVANCE (Germany) diffractometer using a Cu Kɑ source. X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) were collected on a Kratos AXIS Ultra DLD surface analyzer using a monochromatic Al K  source at 15 kV (1486.71 eV). Binding energies were corrected by referencing the C 1s peak of adventitious carbon to 284.5 eV. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were taken by a JEOL JSM-6700F scanning electron microscope. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) were based on a JEOL 2100F transmission electron microscope operating at 200 kV accelerate voltage. UV-vis spectroscopy was performed on Shimadzu UV-2450. Elemental analysis was based on ICP-OES using a Thermo Scientific iCAP 6200 Duo Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectrometer.

Figure S13. Comparison of shuttle currents of Li–S cells in a G4 electrolyte with an unprotected Li anode; and in m-G4 electrolytes with an unprotected Li anode or a Li3PS4-protected Li anode. The sulfur loading of the cells was 1.5 mg cm-2. The cells were charged and discharged at 0.1C for three cycles, and then discharged to the capacity of 800 mAh g-1 (~50% depth of discharge). After resting for 10 minutes, the current of the open-circuited cells was recorded until it reached a steady value. The open circuit voltage of the cells would decrease steadily due to the polysulfide shuttle if there was no externally applied electric current. The current recorded under the open-circuit condition is a shuttle effect manifestation, according to a previous study (J. Electrochem. Soc. 2015, 162, A1-A7).








Figure S14. Electrochemical impedance spectra of catalyzed Li-S cells in G4 and m-G4 electrolytes, recorded at (a) the open-circuit voltage and (b) 1.7 V in discharge. The cells for EIS characterizations were measured at 0.2 C with a sulfur loading of ~1.5 mg cm-2. Their equivalent circuits consist of an electrolyte resistance (Re), an interphase contact resistance (Rint, which includes the Li2S/Li2S2 film formation resistance), the charge transfer resistance (Rct), and a Warburg impedance. In comparison with the G4 electrolyte, the m-G4 electrolyte yielded significantly lower resistances in both Rint and Rct, indicating the effective catalyst surface rejuvenation effect in the m-G4 electrolyte.
