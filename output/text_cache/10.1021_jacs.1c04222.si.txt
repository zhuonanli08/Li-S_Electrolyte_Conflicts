Experimental Section  
Materials. 1,2-Benzenedithiol (1,2-BDT), 1,3-benzenedithiol (1,3-BDT), 1,4-benzenedithiol 
(1,4-BDT) were purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (TCI). The composition of 
commercial Li-S electrolyte is 1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonimide) (LiTFSI) and 
1wt% lithium nitrate (LiNO3) in 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) and 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) 
(DME/DOL=1:1 v/v) which was purchased from DoDoChem. The 3-Ah pouch cell was 
provided by Zhongke Paisi Energy Storage Technology Co., Ltd. 
Preparation of cathode materials and electrolytes. Firstly, the bucky paper with a diameter 
of 12 mm was dried at 100 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 h. Secondly, a certain amount of sulfur 
was dissolved in CS2 solution. 20 μL of solution containing 1.2 mg sulfur was taken and 
dropped on the carbon paper. Finally, the electrode was dried at 60 °C for 12 h in an oven. 1,2-
BDT, 1,3-BDT, and 1,4-BDT were dissolved in commercial Li-S electrolyte to form solutions 
with different concentrations. 
Fabrication of Li-S and Li/Li symmetric cells. CR2032 coin cells were assembled in an Ar-
filled glove box (H2O<0.1 ppm, O2<0.1ppm). 20 μL electrolyte with or without BDT was 
dropped on the sulfur cathode, then a Celgard 2400 separator was placed on the top of sulfur 
cathode followed by adding 20 μL electrolyte with or without BDT on top of the separator. 
Finally, a lithium metal anode was placed on separator. Then the Li-S cell was encapsulated 
and taken out of the glove box for testing. For the fabrication of high loading Li-S cells, two 
layers of the sulfur carbon electrodes with sulfur mass of 2.5-4 mg were used, and the following

procedure was the same as above. For the fabrication of the Li/1,4-BDT cell, 20 μL of 1,4-
BDT electrolyte was added directly in the bucky paper without sulfur. 20 μL of 1,4-BDT 
electrolyte was added on top of the separator. For the fabrication of Li/Li symmetric cells: 15 
μL electrolyte with or without BDT was dropped on the lithium metal electrode, then a Celgard 
2400 separator was placed on the top of lithium anode followed by adding 15 μL electrolyte 
with or without BDT on the top of separator. Finally, a lithium metal electrode was placed on 
separator. Then the Li/Li cell was encapsulated and taken out of the glove box for testing. A 3 
Ah Li-S pouch cell was prepared by stacking the sulfur cathodes and lithium metal anodes. The 
total mass of sulfur is 2.8 g and the total mass of lithium metal is 2.225 g in the pouch cell. The 
cathode was double-coated with sulfur/carbon composite, and the thickness of lithium metal 
anode is 100 μm. The bag is about twice the size of the electrodes in order to accommodate the 
gases generated during the assembly. The electrolyte was added in the pouch cell in a dry room 
and the cell was sealed. The electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio in the pouch cell was 3.5 μL mg-1. 
Gases were generated immediately. After 15 min resting, the pouch cell was sealed again along 
the edge of electrodes and the gases were released. The pouch cell was evaluated at 25 °C on 
a LAND CT2001A battery cycler and it was pressed by a metal plate. The current for cycling 
the pouch cell was 300 mA. 
Characterizations. Ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography quadrupole time-of-flight 
mass spectrometry (UPLC-QTof-MS, Waters Acquity UPLC coupled with a Xevo G2-XS 
QTof mass spectrometer) was used to analyze the charged and discharged products for the cells. 
The cycled cathodes were immersed in a mixed solution of 20% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) 
and 80% methanol for 30 min in the Ar-filled glove box, 1 mL of the solution was taken with 
a pipette and added into an UPLC-MS vial with 1 mL chromatographic methanol. The vial was 
sealed and taken out of the glove box for testing. The solvent phase for UPLC was 100% 
chromatographic methanol and 0.5 μL of the solution was tested in electrospray ionization

(ESI) positive mode and the first-order mass spectrometry model was applied. The solvent 
phase velocity was 0.2 mL min-1 and the same test condition was used for all the samples in 
the UPLC-MS measurements. Raman spectroscopy was performed on the Lab RAM HR 
Evolution Laser Raman spectrometer. For the in situ test, a sealed cell was used and assembled 
in the Ar-filled glove box. Therefore, the sample was under anhydrous and oxygen-free 
conditions during the test. Ultraviolet and visible spectrophotometry (UV-vis) was performed 
by Agilent 8453 spectrometer with the range of 200-800 cm-1 to characterize the polysulfide 
species in the cathode. The cells were disassembled in the Ar-filled glove box. The cathodes 
were immersed in a mixture solvent (DME/DOL=1:1 v/v) for 2 h, then it was filtered. The 
obtained solution was used for UV-vis test. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was 
conducted with a Carl Zeiss 500 field emission. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Bruker, 
Dimension Icon) measurements were carried out in the Ar-filled glove box. Force-
displacement (F-d) curve was collected in the quantitative nanomechanics mode (QNM) using 
RTESPA-150 tip (Bruker Corporation). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) 
measurements were conducted with PHI 5000 VersaProbe II. 
Electrochemical measurements. The cycling performances of the cells were evaluated on a 
LANHE battery cycler at different C rates (1 C = 1675 mA g-1 based on the mass of sulfur). 
Cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.8 V and 3.0 V. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) 
measurements were performed on a Bio-Logic VMP-3 potentiostat with a scan rate of 0.02 mV 
s-1 between 1.8-3.0 V. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were 
collected with a Bio-Logic VMP-3 impedance analyzer in the frequency range of 200 kHz to 
0.1 Hz. For the visible cell tests, an electrode with a sulfur mass of 1.3 mg was used. 8 mL 
blank electrolyte and the 1,4-BDT electrolyte were used for the control cell and Li-S cell with 
1,4-BDT, respectively. They were discharged at a current of 0.1 mA from 3.0 V to 1.8 V on a 
LAND battery cycler.
