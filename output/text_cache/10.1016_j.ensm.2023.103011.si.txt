Dual-functional chloropyrazine additives for enhanced performance of lithium-sulfur batteries

Electrolyte preparation 
The blank electrolyte was prepared using a basic electrolyte for lithium-sulfur batteries (LSBs), which consists of a solution of 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO3 in a mixture of DOL (dioxolane) and DME (dimethoxyethane) in a volume ratio of 1:1. To prepare electrolytes with pyrazine (PZ), 2-chloropyrazine (2-CP), and 2,5-dichloropyrazine (2,5-DCP), these compounds were dissolved in the above-mentioned electrolyte at concentrations of 30, 40 and 50 mM.

Assembly and performance evaluation of LSBs: 
Typically, XC-72 (commercial carbon black) and sulfur powder were mixed in a mass ratio of 3:7 and ground. The mixture was then heated at 155 °C under an argon atmosphere for 12 h to deposit sulfur, resulting in a sample with a sulfur content of 70 wt.%. The sample was mixed with SuperP conductive carbon black (20 wt.%) and PVDF (10 wt.%) and dissolved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The mixture was coated onto aluminum foil current collectors and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h to remove the solvent. The dried electrode was then cut into disks with a diameter of 12 mm. The sulfur content in the positive electrode was 49%. Unless otherwise specified, the typical sulfur loading in the electrode was approximately 1.2‒1.4 mg cm-2. Celgard 2400 was used as the separator, and lithium metal was used as the anode. The aforementioned electrolyte, either without additives or containing PZ, 2-CP, or 2,5-DCP, was added. Unless otherwise specified, the electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio (E/S) was fixed at 30 μL mg-1. Button cells were assembled using stainless steel cell cases (2032 type) in an argon-filled glove box. During electrochemical performance testing, the current rate and specific capacity calculations were based on the sulfur mass in the cathode (1 C = 1675 mA g-1). The batteries were tested on a Neware battery testing system with a voltage range of 1.7‒2.8 V. Cycle voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed using a CHI 650D electrochemical analyzer. The CV measurements were conducted with a scan rate of 0.1 mV s-1 and a voltage range of 1.7‒2.8 V, while the EIS measurements were performed over a frequency range of 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz. The ion conductivity of the electrolyte was measured using a conductivity meter equipped with platinum electrodes. To ensure the accuracy and repeatability of the data, multiple tests were conducted for each sample while continuously monitoring their stabilities during testing. The values reported in the manuscript are the averages derived from these multiple tests, underscoring the reliability of the ionic conductivity measurements.

For the symmetrical cell, electrode disks were prepared using 80 wt.% XC-72, 10 wt.% Super P conductive carbon black, and 10 wt.% PVDF following the same procedure as before. Subsequently, 30 μL of 0.2 M Li2S6 + 20 μL of blank/pyrazine-based electrolyte were added on both sides of the separator. Lithium foil was replaced with electrode disks, while keeping other components unchanged for the assembly of the symmetrical cell.

Experiments of Li2S nucleation 
CR2032 coin cells were assembled using XC-72 electrode for the symmetrical cell, lithium metal anode, and Celgard 2400 separator. 0.50 mol L−1 [S] of Li2S8 dissolved in tetraglyme with 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI was prepared as basic electrolyte. Additional 40 mM pyrazine additives were added to the basic solution to obtain a polysulfide catholyte. 20 μL catholyte was dropped onto XC-72 electrode, while 20 μL blank electrolyte that was otherwise identical but without polysulfides was added to the anode compartment [1]. To study the nucleation kinetics of Li2S, the assembled cell was first discharged at a constant current from 0.112 mA to 2.06 V, followed by constant potential discharge until the current dropped below 0.01 mA, while maintaining a voltage of 2.05 V to ensure complete Li2S nucleation.

Physical characterization
The materials and solvents used in the study were purchased from commercial sources (such as Aldrich, Canrd, Dodochem) without further purification. Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) was performed using a German Car Zeiss Ultra Plus microscope. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was carried out using a British VG Escalab 210 instrument with a Mg 300-W X-ray source.

Theoretical calculations
In this work, the density functional theory (DFT) method was employed using the Gaussian 16 program [2]. Calculations were performed using the B3LYP functional and the 6-31G(d) basis set. All structures were optimized and verified, and no imaginary frequencies were obtained. The energy levels of their lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) were calculated at the same level of theory. Multiwfn was used for rendering and visualization, and the visual molecular dynamics (VMD) software was employed for visualization purposes.

Figure S1. The cycling performance of LSBs at 0.2 C rate with three different concentrations of the additives. As the concentration increases from 30 mM to 40 mM, a significant improvement in discharge specific capacity is observed in three types of batteries. However, with a further increase in concentration from 40 mM to 50 mM, the capacity begins to decline. The results indicate that incorporating the three additives at 40 mM enables the batteries to maintain high specific capacity and stable cycling performance.
