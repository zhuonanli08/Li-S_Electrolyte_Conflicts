Figure S6. Raman and FTIR analysis of HFE solvent and the supernatant in the 1.0 M LiFSI 
in HFE electrolyte. We further characterized the Raman and Fourier-transform infrared 
spectroscopy (FTIR) of the HFE solvent and the supernatant in the 1.0 M LiFSI in HFE electrolyte, 
aiming to avoid the misjudgment caused by the overlapped characteristic peak of C-O-C between 
HFE and PC. It was found that there is no change in the characteristic peaks for HFE, further 
demonstrating the incapability of HFE solvent to dissolve LiFSI. Thus, HFE does not participate 
in the solvation process with Li+.

Figure S9. XPS characterization of the separator after five cycles in the SPAN | LiC6 full-cell. 
For XPS characterizations of the separator, the SPAN | LiC6 full-cell was (dis-)charged for five 
cycles, and the separator was taken out for the XPS analysis whether there was any lithium 
polysulfide. Only LiFSI (i.e., 171.6 eV and 170.2 eV in S2p) was detected on the separator, where 
there was no evidence for lithium polysulfide (i.e., Li2Sx), due to the absence of any significant 
peaks in S2p (i.e., 162-168 eV range for Li2Sx). These results further corroborate that the SPAN 
cathode follows a “solid-solid” mechanism without the dissolution of lithium polysulfide.
