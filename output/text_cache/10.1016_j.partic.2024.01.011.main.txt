a b s t r a c t
Lithiumesulfur (LieS) batteries stand out the energy storage systems because of extremely high energy
density (2600 W h Kg1) and low-cost sulfur cathode. Unfortunately, the sluggish deposition from liquid
Li polysulﬁdes (LiPSs) to solid Li2S leads to mild power density and short cycle life. Understanding and
regulating Li2S2/Li2S deposition are conceived to be importance to deliver second-plateau capacity in
acceptable kinetics, which has the potential to operation LieS batteries under electrolyte-lean conditions.
This perspective aims to summarize the proposed models that can describe the nucleation and propa-
gation of three-dimensional Li2S2/Li2S, as well as affords critical views how electrolyte dictates LiPS
conversion from liquid to solid. It hopes to encourage necessary scaffold strategies and electrolyte for-
mulations to further improve energy density and life span of LieS batteries.

1. Introduction
Energy storage systems are currently being widely developed to
power an increasingly diverse applications, from smart devices,
electric vehicles, to power grids (Cheng et al., 2017; Jie et al., 2023;
Seh et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2023). Particularly, the
capacity of lithium-ion batteries powering electric vehicles is more
than 500 GWh in the past year (Zhang et al., 2023). Long driving
range and better safety operation have motivated intensive efforts
to explore conversion battery chemistries that deviated from
intercalation battery chemistries (e.g. Li-ion batteries) (Bi et al.,
2023; Chen, Cheng, et al., 2023). Lithiumesulfur (LieS) batteries
attract wide attentions because of extremely high energy density
(2600 W h Kg1) proﬁted from multi-electron reaction between a
lithium anode and a sulfur cathode (Duan et al., 2023; Fang et al.,
2016; Kong et al., 2018; Nanda et al., 2020; Peng et al., 2017a).
Meanwhile, reserves and resources of sulfur is far massive than
existing cathode elements like nickel (Ni), cobalt (Co) and manga-
nese (Mn), which prompts sulfur to be the next-generation and
ideal cathode materials.
Despite of the facilitating energy density, multi-electron and
conversion redox reaction of sulfur also brings grand challenges in
commercialization,
including
polysulﬁde
migration,
lithium
dendrite growth, sluggish redox kinetics and electrolyte con-
sumption (Chen, Zhang, et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023; Jiang et al.,
2023; Kwok et al., 2023; Li, Sami, et al., 2023). Among them, the
sluggish kinetics derived from the reduction of Li2S4 to Li2S cause
massive electrolyte dosage and mild cell-level energy density,
which encourage tremendous efforts to model Li2S precipitation for
the purpose of enhancing discharge capacity and reducing elec-
trolyte volume (Feng et al., 2022; Hua et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2022;
Yan et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024).
In this perspective, the mechanism of Li2S precipitation in
macroscopic
and
microscopic
scale
is
discussed,
especially
emphasizing on the polysulﬁde solubility and solvation in regu-
lating the morphology of Li2S precipitation, as well as corre-
sponding modeling in the describing Li2S precipitation. Finally,
some general directions of developing long-life and high-rate LieS
batteries are proposed.
2. Electrochemical deposition of Li2S2/Li2S
There are several approaches that have been demonstrated to
promote the liquid (LiPSs)esolid (Li2S2/Li2S) conversion and pro-
long the second plateau capacity in recent years, while fewer at-
tentions are centered on the entire Li2S2/Li2S deposition from
electrolyte chemistry and mechanistic modeling (Gu et al., 2021;
Jiang et al., 2023; Wen et al., 2015). The reduction of sulfur cathode
encounters a series of intermediates such as Li2S8, Li2S6, Li2S4, Li2S2,

external circuit and dissolves into electrolyte. The combination of
negatively charged S82 with positively charged Liþ evolves into
liquid LiPSs (Li2S8, Li2S6) and solid LiPSs (Li2S4, Li2S2, Li2S) (Bhargav
et al., 2020; Manthiram et al., 2014). The slope of bule curve (ﬁrst
plateau) in Fig. 1(a) represents the dissolution of S8, and evolution
of LiPSs from soluble to semi-soluble species, until the occurrence
of oversaturated point. The oversaturated point signiﬁes the
beginning of decomposition of Li2S2/Li2S nucleus. The preformed
Li2S2/Li2S nucleus beneﬁts the subsequent growth of Li2S2/Li2S,
leading to the slight climb of voltage in the second plateau. The
redox reactions of soluble LiPSs are kinetically much easier than the
liquidesolid LiPS conversion, which are usually manifested as the
rapid reduction of second plateau capacity while the ﬁrst plateau
capacity remains mildly affected (Fig. 1(b)). Unveiling the nucle-
ation and propagation of the Li2S2/Li2S is supposed to promote
effective sulfur scaffolds and electrolyte formulation to tackle the
mild electron and ion transfer during the second discharge process
(Zhao et al., 2020).
Li2S2/Li2S deposition shares similar features of classical elec-
trochemical deposition (Guo et al., 2011). The growth of individual
island in microscopical dimension occurs layer by layer growth in
case the operational voltage [Un(Mþ/S)] is deviated from thermo-
dynamic voltage [Ueq(Mþ/M)]. If the [Un(Mþ/S)] slightly deviates
from the [Ueq(Mþ/M)], the deposited atoms on nucleus have sufﬁ-
cient time to migrate toward favorable planes of crystals, and they
ﬁnally develop into crystals with speciﬁc originations. If the
[Un(Mþ/S)] markedly deviates from the [Ueq(Mþ/M)], the strong
overpotential quickly drive the ions desolvation for subsequent
deposition on nucleus with diffusion length being largely limited,
which usually causes the isotropic morphology, like spheres
(Fig. 1(c)). The LiPSs conversion and deposition is much more
complex and involve multiple electrochemical and chemical
reduction and disproportionation both in electrolyte media and
cathode scaffolds. Taking the regular electrolyte formulation that
dissolves 1 M Li bis((triﬂuoromethyl)sulfonyl)azanide (LiTFSI) in
1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) for example,
the highly soluble LiPSs are considered as a speciﬁc Li salts and are
solvated by electrolyte solvents, most likely the DME, forming a
solvation structure of contact ion pair (CIP), which gradually con-
gregates into clusters as the discharge proceeds. Shortening LiPS
chains and intensifying LieS bonds upon lithiation of LiPSs lead to
the transformation from soluble LiPSs (Li2S8 and Li2S6) to semi-
soluble Li2S4 and further to insoluble Li2S2/Li2S, which migrates
onto the surface of electron-conductive substrates for subsequent
deposition (Fig. 1(d)) (Fan et al., 2018; Ji et al., 2009; Xu et al., 2016).
Once the conductive surface is buried by nonconductive Li2S2/Li2S,
the electrochemical process is arrested if without additional charge
transfer pathways, which is the root cause for the low discharge
capacity in the second plateau.
The solvation ability of a solvent (Hou, Li, et al., 2022; Kong et al.,
2021; Li, Hou, et al., 2023; Song et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2022), the
chemical afﬁnity of the substrate (Wang et al., 2020; Yang et al.,
2014) and operational conditions (e.g. test protocol) (Fan et al.,
2015; Li et al., 2019) impact Li2S deposition modes in diverse
manners (Fig.1(e)). The ﬁrst commonly used approach is the design
of conductive scaffold, in which the porous and high-speciﬁc-
surface-area carbon skeletons can be modiﬁed by polar mediators
to anchor the Li2S2/Li2S. It increases the contact area between LiPSs
and substrates, and hence enlarge the discharge capacity (Cheng
et al., 2014; Tang et al., 2020). Another important strategy is
based on working conditions. The external applied current density
signiﬁcantly changes the nucleation quantities, with high current
density being small particles while low current density being large
particles (Kong et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2022). The recent research
enthusiasm to regulate Li2S2/Li2S turns to electrolyte design (Geng
et al., 2023; Tsao et al., 2019) a simple and effective method of
straightforwardly tailoring the LiPSs solubility and suppressing the
Li2S2/Li2S passivation (Fan et al., 2022; Hou, Zhang, et al., 2022;
Zhang et al., 2021), which falls into the core scope of this
perspective.

deposition of sulfur species (Sun et al., 2023). The electrolyte serves
as a medium to transfer the cations and anions, as well as to

The solvent plays a crucial role in the LiPS redox and electro-
chemical deposition. The electrochemical redox kinetics in LieS
batteries is controlled by transport and solvation properties of Liþ
and LiPSs in electrolyte, which essentially determine the Li2S2/Li2S
deposition modes such as surface diffusion of adatoms on the
substrate (two-dimensional deposition) or bulk diffusion of pre-
cursors (three-dimensional deposition) (Bewick et al., 1962; Hyde
& Compton, 2003), the latter of which promotes the adequate
utilization of active sulfur materials. The coordination strength of
solvents are notable factors to mediate Li2S2/Li2S deposition.
Donor number (DN) and dielectric constant (ε) are generally
used to evaluate the ability of solvation (Cuisinier et al., 2015; Gupta
et al., 2019). Solvent permittivity shields the electrostatic force
between cations and anions. Solvents with high permittivity like
dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) and sulfolane (SL) weakens coulombic
interactions of salt cations and anions, resulting in solvated Liþ,
while low permittivity solvents promote the CIP species. The donor
number is another crucial descriptor to access the ability of electron
donating for Liþ, which is relevant to the standard molar enthalpy
of the reaction of solvents with Lewis acid antimony pentachloride
among 1, 2-dichloroethane (Baek et al., 2020). The description of
LiPS solvation by DN and ε can be referred to our previous work
(Zhang et al., 2024). The DN and ε of commonly used solvents and
anions are summarized in Fig. 2(a and b). Generally, solvents with
high DN and ε have strong coordination strength when steric hin-
drance is leaved out, as the spatial conﬁguration is hard to unitedly
deﬁned.
Highly coordinating solvents, like DMSO and SL, promote three-
dimensional (3D) growth on account of its speciﬁc solvated struc-
ture of Liþ, LiPSs and the stable existence of S3 intermediates.
Among them, the strong Lewis acid Liþ changes to a soft Lewis acid
when it is solvated by highly coordinating solvents because of the
electrostatic shielding effect. Based on the hard-soft-acid-base
theory, the solvated Liþ stabilizes the long chain LiPSs (soft base),
which
augments
their
solubility.
Accordingly,
the
high-
concentration long chain LiPSs promote the disproportionation
reactions with short chain LiPSs and alter the Li2S2/Li2S deposition
pathways from surface mode to solution-mediated growth mode,
allowing the 3D deposition. In other words, the soluble LiPS in
electrolyte disproportionate the solid insoluble LiPSs to dissolve
into electrolyte and redeposit on the surface of cathode, which
signiﬁcantly increases the utilization of sulfur. Besides, the high
coordination strength of solvents facilitates the formation of S3
free radical with low electronic cloud density (Cuisinier et al., 2015;
Fan & Chiang, 2017; Gupta et al., 2019; He et al., 2018), which
provides an additional conversion pathway between sulfur and
long chain LiPS to enhance LiPS solubility.
The highly coordinating solvents beneﬁt the disproportionated
reactions and enhance LiPS solubility, leading to the deposition
manners from 2D to 3D. In other words, the facilitating solution-
mediated deposition contributes to the deep discharge through
alleviating the covering of conductive substrates.
The coordination strength of anions equally has the crucial ef-
fects on the Li2S2/Li2S deposition model. Similar with highly coor-
dinating solvents, the highly coordinating anions such as NO3 and
Br have higher electron density than LiPS anions, leading to
squeezing out the polysulﬁde anions in the ﬁrst solvation sheath.
Therefore, the Liþ bounden by highly coordinating anions analo-

The redox mediators (RMs) similar to the homogenous catalysts
can dissolve in the electrolyte for accelerating the sluggish kinetics
and altering the deposition mode (Kwok et al., 2023; Liu et al.,
2023; Peng et al., 2017a, Peng et al., 2017b; Zhao et al., 2020).
Different from the disproportionation reaction, RMs exhibit
reversible redox couples that are electrochemically oxidized at the
surface of the conductive skeleton and chemically reduced in the
electrolyte. The combination of RMs and LiPSs decreases the
overpotential and increases the diffusion coefﬁcient of LiPSs on the
surface of insulated Li2S, which facilitates the 3D growth of Li2S2/
Li2S nucleus. The RM is a kind of thermodynamically feasible and
kinetically beneﬁcial external species to signiﬁcantly improve the
utilization of sulfur. The selection and design of RMs are as: (i)
thermodynamically appropriate redox potential, (ii) high diffu-
sivity for better dynamics, and (iii) low-molecular weight to
reduce mass load.

sition are straightforward and signiﬁcant for the enhancement of
redox kinetics (Liu et al., 2021). High coordination strength sol-
vents and anions are beneﬁcial for achieving the high sulfur uti-
lization and low electrolyte dosage. Solvents like sulfone, multi-
denticity ether, pyridine, and pyrrole, as well as salt anions such

Designing and selecting appropriate solvents and anions for the
3D Li2S2/Li2S deposition need to consider the above issues for
better battery performances, which also require the sufﬁcient
understanding of the characterization of complicated Li2S2/Li2S
deposition.
4. Characterization of Li2S2/Li2S deposition models
The fundamental understanding of Li2S2/Li2S deposition facili-
tates the design of high-performance LieS batteries. However, the
complicated, dynamic, and meta-stable LiPSs intermediates give
the difﬁculties in characterization of Li2S2/Li2S deposition (Rehman
et al., 2022; Yao et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2021). Chiang et al. (Fan &
Chiang, 2017) proposed the chronoamperograms to probe and
quantify Li2S2/Li2S (Fig. 3(a)), which vividly reﬂects the details of
deposition features. According to this model, the transition from
liquid LiPSs to solid Li2S2/Li2S is divided into three processes:
nucleation, island growth and cease caused by insulation.
The adequate energy drive and extensive energy ﬂuctuation are
indispensable for the nucleation. Therefore, the initial current drop
signiﬁes a gestation period of nucleation (the blue area), which
implies that the preformed Li2S2/Li2S nucleus signiﬁcantly reduces
nucleation overpotential and facilitates the subsequent propaga-
tion. The up-to-date view for the nucleation process identiﬁed by in
situ liquid cell coupling with electrochemical transmission electron
microscopy considers that the meta-stable Li2S4 presented as a
dense liquid phase, which induces a suddenly instantaneous crys-
tallization rather than a classical step-by-step transformation (Zhou
et al., 2023). Based on minimum energy principle, the active centers
of nucleation are mainly deposited at the defects of conductive
substrate, such as lattice imperfection and grain boundary. There-
fore, the external voltage, current density and substrates signiﬁ-
cantly impact the quantity and rate of nucleation, which alters the
deposition mode and subsequent growth.
Li2S2/Li2S growth takes place immediately at the surface of
initial nucleus driven by the electron tunneling effect, which
transfers electron through the insulating Li2S2/Li2S, and is gradually
suppressed by the increment of the several atomic layer thickness.
Meanwhile, an active center of triple phase boundary among initial
Li2S2/Li2S, substrate and electrolyte provide facilitating sites for the
continued growth, where the required energy is less than initial
nucleation energy. The combination of LiPSs diffusing through
surface islands and electronic transferring via electrode leads to the
formation of faceted islands, resulting the 2D island growth. The
covered area of scaffold and responded current density are
continuously augmenting and reach the maximum when the in-
dependent islands contact with each other. This process is
commonly named island impingement (Avrami, 2004). The elec-
tron tunneling effect, the quantity of insulating of Li2S2/Li2S, as well
as the solubility and the mobility of LiPS collectively the shape the
Li2S2/Li2S deposition model.
With the current density approaching to 0, the discharging is
practically ceased by the loss of electronic contact of cathode with
LiPS due to the completely covered insulation Li2S2/Li2S (green
area). Two reactions contributing to the remained current density,
reduction and disproportionation of LiPSs. The letter is dominantly
impacted by the solubility of long chain LiPSs.

5. Outlook
Prolonging Li2S2/Li2S electrochemical deposition and suppress-
ing surface passivation are pendent and complicated issues to
realize the commercial and long-life lithiumsulfur batteries.
Uncovering the Li2S2/Li2S deposition mechanism and character-
izing Li2S2/Li2S deposition modes are indispensable to tailor the
discharge behaviors and improve rate performance. In general, the
Li2S2/Li2S deposition undergo several stages: (i) solvated LiPSs
diffuse in the bulk electrolyte, (ii) liquid LiPSs are attacked by Liþ
and acquire electron either from cathode substrates or from solu-
tion, resulting in chain scission, (iii) LiPSs remove solvents and
further combine with Liþ to form the solid Li2S nucleus on the
scaffold, and (iv) nucleus absorbs additional Li2S2/Li2S precipitation
to propagate in 2D or 3D structure. Besides probing the Li2S2/Li2S
deposition mechanism with the aid of different coordinating sol-
vents and Li salts, accurate characterization of precipitation models
is emphasized here to unveil the coupling between species evolu-
tion and experimental characterization. In addition, polysulﬁde
solubility and solvation in regulating the deposition models are
underscored. Electrolyte with high coordination strength, including
both lithium salts and solvents, possesses the higher solubility and
stabilize the S3, which facilitate the 3D deposition of Li2S2/Li2S
through providing the sufﬁcient dissolution and disproportionation
of LiPSs. This perspective summarized the mechanism of Li2S2/Li2S
deposition and reveal the characterization of Li2S2/Li2S evolution,
hoping to stimulate new strategies for the energy-dense and
power-intensive LieS batteries.
