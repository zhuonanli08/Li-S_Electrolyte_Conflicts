I.
Calculations with graphite as a model surface
The adsorption energy, Eads, values for the “parallel” and “perpendicular” geometries for the Li2Sx (x =
1, 2, 4, 6) on a single graphene layer reported in Table 1 of the main text were recalculated with additional
graphene layers, in order to mimic more precisely the electronic structure of graphite. A total of 4 graphene
sheets in A-B layering were considered. To the optimized adsorbate structure on a single graphene layer, 3
additional graphene sheets were added and single point calculations were performed. The recalculated Eads
values are summarized in Table S1, along with the charge transfer |ΔQ| that takes place upon adsorption as stems
from the Bader analysis.

II.
Effect of the implicit solvent on the adsorption energies
Although in this work we have selected only two values of the dielectric constant corresponding to two
prototypical solvents commonly used in lithium batteries (ie. DME ε/ε0 = 7.2 and DMF ε/ε0 = 36.7) as
representative examples in order to assess the effect of the solvent on the adsorption and surface decomposition
of lithium polysulfides on graphene, in our previous work [1], we have performed an extensive ε/ε0 value sweep
in the range 1 - 64 for the adsorption energy of Li2S on graphene with the following result:
Fig. S3 Binding energy of a Li2S molecule on graphene immersed in a dielectric as a function of the dielectric
constant (in logarithmic scale). The red dotted vertical lines indicate the values corresponding to the solventes
selected in this work, DME (ε/ε0 = 7.2) and DMF (ε/ε0 = 36.7). Data taken from Ref. [1].
The results show a good agreement with Born equation, which states that solvation energy given by a solvent
treated as a continuous dielectric of permittivity given by εr = ε/ε0 is proportional to (1 - 1/εr), thus explaining the
greater impact on the adsorption energy caused by increasing the dielectric constant from vacuum (ε/ε0 = 1) to
DME (a ca. 7 fold increment in ε/ε0, resulting in a ca. 2 fold decrease in Eads for Li2S and Li2S2 and a ca. 1.3 fold
decrease for Li2S4 and Li2S6, see Table 2 in the main text), compared to the relatively mild effect produced by a
further ca. 5 fold increment of ε/ε0, that is when changing from DME to DMF, with a negligible (ie. within
calculation errors) effect on Li2S, and a reduction of only 10 - 20 % for the other polysulfides.

Comparing the differential charge analysis in the different environments, it becomes clear that as the
dielectric constant increases, the charge transfer between Li2S and graphene is diminished. This picture is also
supported by the Bader charge analysis shown in Table 2 in the main text. Projected density of states gives a hint
on the underlying reasons behind this hampered charge transfer. As the dielectric constant of the environment
increases, the states of the molecule are shifted towards lower energies, while those corresponding to graphene
are shifted towards higher energies, thus resembling the pristine surface. Therefore, the frontier orbitals are
further apart and the interaction between them decreases.
