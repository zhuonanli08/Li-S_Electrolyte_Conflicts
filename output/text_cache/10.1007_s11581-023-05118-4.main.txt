4‑Aminobenzoic acid as an electrolyte additive for enhancing 
the electrochemical properties of the sulfurized polyacrylonitrile 
cathode in ether electrolyte
Xiaojia Zheng1 · Fangfang Zou1 · Hao Yang1 · Xu Su1 · Xuebu Hu1 · Xuecheng Liu2
Received: 20 February 2023 / Revised: 5 June 2023 / Accepted: 6 July 2023 
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2023
Abstract
Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) is a polysulfide with high specific capacity due to its stable bonding structure. Although 
it is able to exhibit good electrochemical performance in ester electrolytes, its capacity in ether electrolyte decays rapidly, 
accompanied by shuttle effects. Herein, 4-aminobenzoic acid (4-ABA) was selected as an additive for ether electrolytes to 
inhibit shuttling of polysulfides. The CV curves of Li-Al cells and reconstituted cells together verified that the addition of 
4-ABA had an effect on the SPAN cathode but not on the lithium anode. Further characterizations revealed that the SPAN 
cathode surface was protected by cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) formed in electrolyte containing 4-ABA. Compared 
with the blank electrolyte, the cell with electrolyte containing 4-ABA exhibited better cycling performance, with a revers-
ible capacity of 1178.73 mAh ­g−1 after 100 cycles at 0.5 C, with a capacity retention of 88.81%. These results show that the 
proper design of electrolyte additives is an effective way to enhance the performance of SPAN.
Keywords  Electrolyte additives · CEI film · Shuttling effect · Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile
Introduction
Due to the depletion of fossil energy sources and deterio-
rating environmental conditions, energy storage materials 
which are clean and non-polluting have received attention 
widely [1]. Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) are one of the 
effective ways to solve the above problems [2–4]. Never-
theless, it also faces some problems such as low energy 
density and limited resources, so it is crucial to develop 
low-cost and high-energy–density materials. As we all 
know, lithium-sulfur (Li–S) batteries have high energy 
density and abundant raw material reserves, so it has a 
promising prospect [5, 6]. However, Li–S batteries are still 
impeded by poor electrical conductivity of sulfur and dis-
charge products ­(Li2S2 and ­Li2S) and the shuttling effect of 
lithium polysulfides [7–9]. The performance of Li–S bat-
teries has been greatly improved by various host materials, 
separator modification, binder improvement, electrolyte 
optimization, and lithium metal protection [10].
Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) is a special kind of 
organosulfur composite that has drawn attention in recent 
years because it can be stably cycled in traditional carbon-
ate-based electrolyte. Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile differs 
from sulfur cathode in that sulfur and polyacrylonitrile are 
bonded to produce sulfurized polyacrylonitrile in which sul-
fur does not exist in the free state or is adsorbed on the sur-
face of the host material, and this chemical bonding force is 
stronger [11–13]. Moreover, SPAN has higher conductivity 
than pure sulfur cathode [14–16]. But the sulfur loading of 
SPAN (30–44 wt%) is lower than that of pure sulfur cathode 
(40–60 wt%) [10, 17]. The SPAN cathode has good cycling 
and rate performance in ester electrolytes, but the growth 
of lithium dendrites in such electrolytes usually leads to the 
short circuits of cells [18]. Conversely, ether electrolytes 
have good compatibility with lithium anode and could avoid 
the formation of lithium dendrites. In addition, easier ion 
transport in ether electrolytes improves the utilization of 
sulfur. However, lithium polysulfides will dissolve in ether 
electrolytes, which will cause the decay of cell’s capacity.

Lithium polysulfide shuttles to the lithium anode surface and 
corrodes the lithium anode which will result in less sulfur 
being available for reversible reactions [19, 20]. In view of 
the above advantages and disadvantages, it is particularly 
important to improve the utilization of the active sulfur.
Many efforts have also been made to improve the 
cycling performance of the SPAN cathode in ether elec-
trolytes. On the one hand, the dissolution of lithium poly-
sulfide was reduced by controlling the concentration of 
electrolyte salt [21, 22] and changing the type of solvent 
[23]. High-concentration electrolytes were used to inhibit 
the shuttling of lithium polysulfide and achieve lithium 
deposition without dendrite [21, 22]. A mixed diisopro-
pyl ether-based (mixed-DIPE) electrolyte was proposed 
to effectively protect lithium anode in Li–S batteries with 
sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) cathodes [23]. On 
the other hand, cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) film 
formed by electrolyte additives hindered the loss of active 
sulfur and accelerated the kinetic conversion of lithium 
polysulfide [24–26]. ­LiNO3 was used as an electrolyte 
additive, and it participated in the formation of SEI and 
CEI films effectively inhibiting the shuttling of lithium 
polysulfide and protecting the lithium anode [24, 25]. 
Zhang et al. selected soluble organoselenide compound 
phenyl diselenide (PDSe) as a soluble electrolyte additive 
to enhance the reaction kinetics of SPAN [26]. However, 
high-concentration salt electrolytes are costly, viscous, 
and harmful to the transportation of various ions dur-
ing electrochemical reactions. Meanwhile, the synthesis 
of new ether solvents and solutes is time-consuming and 
costly. It could be reasonable to choose a small amount of 
additive to construct CEI film for eliminating the shuttling 
of polysulfides.
In this paper, 4-ABA was selected as an electrolyte addi-
tive to improve the cycling performance of SPAN. The 
additive decomposed and formed a protective layer which 
has good electrical conductivity and low impedance on the 
SPAN cathode surface. Li/SPAN cells with electrolyte con-
taining 4-ABA had a more stable discharge voltage. As a 
result, the discharge capacity of the Li/SPAN cell with elec-
trolyte containing 4-ABA reached 1178.73 mAh ­g−1 after 
100 cycles at a current density of 318.25 mAh ­g−1.
Experiment
Synthesis of SPAN and pPAN
The sulfur powder was mixed with polyacrylonitrile (PAN) 
in the ratio of 8:1 and ball-milled at 400 rpm for 5 h using 
ethanol as the dispersant. The lightly yellow powder was 
heated in ­N2 atmosphere at 350 °C for 4 h, obtaining the 
black power (SPAN). Pyrolyzed PAN (pPAN) was prepared 
at the same calcination condition.
Material characterization
The purity of the samples was measured by the X-ray dif-
fraction (XRD, Cu-Ka, λ = 0.15405) with a voltage of 40 kV 
and a current of 40 mA. The structure of the samples was 
characterized using the Fourier transform infrared spectros-
copy. The content of sulfur in the samples was determined 
by elemental analysis (CHNS, Unicube, Elementar). X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were 
measured on the multifunctional imaging electron spectrom-
eter (Thermo ESCALAB250XI).

Electrode preparation
The CR2032 cells were assembled by using lithium foil as 
the anode and SPAN electrode as the cathode in an Ar-filled 
glove box containing less than 1 ppm water and oxygen. 
A Celgard 2400 membrane was used as the separator. The 
SPAN cathode was prepared by mixing the active material, 
acetylene black, and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) with a 
weight ratio of 8:1:1. The slurry was coated with aluminum 
foil and dried and then punched into 12-mm diameter discs. 
The loading of SPAN is ~ 1.5 mg ­cm−2 and the capacity is 
calculated based on the mass of sulfur. The blank electrolyte 
was 1 M LiTFSI dissolved in a mixture of 1,2-dimethox-
yethane (DME) and 1,3-dioxyethane (DOL) with a volume 
ratio of 1:1. Three additive-containing electrolytes were pre-
pared by adding 0.25%, 0.5%, and 1.0% 4-ABA (in weight) 
in blank electrolyte, respectively. The electrolyte/SPAN ratio 
is 20 µL ­mg−1.
Electrochemical measurements
Cyclic voltammetry (CV) profiles were carried on the electro-
chemical workstation (Jiangsu Donghua Analytical Instrument 
Co., Ltd.) at a scan rate of 0.1 mV ­s−1 between 1 and 3 V. The 
galvanostatic charge and discharge tests were conducted on 
LAND test system (CT 2001A). Rate performance of the cells 
was evaluated at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 and 0.1 C. The electrochemi-
cal impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of the cells was measured 
on the electrochemical station (Autolab PGSTAT128N) in the 
frequency range from 0.1 MHz to 0.1 Hz.

To study the interfacial impedance between the SPAN cath-
ode and electrolyte, EIS is conducted on the Li/SPAN cells with 
blank and 0.5% 4-ABA electrolytes. The EIS results are fitted 
with the equivalent circuit (Fig. 4a and b), and the fitted results 
of the impedance spectra are summarized in Fig. 4c. As shown in 
the figures, the EIS spectra of the cells contain a high-frequency 
semicircle reflecting the surface-film resistance (Rct) and a low 
frequency straight line corresponding to the Warburg imped-
ance [32, 33]. The Rct of the cell with electrolyte containing 
0.5% 4-ABA is lower than that in blank electrolyte after 50 and 
100 cycles. Specifically, the Rct of the cell with blank electro-
lyte increases to 69.14 Ω after 100 cycles, while that using 0.5% 
4-ABA electrolyte slightly increases to 45.68 Ω (Fig. 4c), mani-
festing that the use of 4-ABA effectively inhibits the growth of 
interfacial resistance. The substantial increase of interfacial resist-
ance for the cell with blank electrolyte can be attributed to the 
continuous decomposition of electrolyte on the SPAN cathode 
surface. The slight increase of interfacial resistance for the cell 
with 0.5% 4-ABA electrolyte can be attributed to the preferential 
reduction of 4-ABA, which effectively inhibits the decomposition 
of electrolyte and reduces the interfacial resistance upon cycling.

Figure 6 shows XPS results of the SPAN cathode using 
electrolyte with/without 4-ABA. XPS is conducted on SPAN 
cathode after 40 cycles at a charged (delithiated) state to 
examine the compositions of the CEI formed in different 
electrolytes. The peak intensity of the C-O in 0.5% 4-ABA 
electrolyte is lower than that in blank electrolyte, indicat-
ing that the electrolyte decomposition in electrolyte con-
taining 4-ABA is weaker than in blank electrolyte (Fig. 6a 
and d) [34]. Both C-F and LiF peaks are observed in two 
cases (Fig. 6b and e). The C-F is attributed to the PVDF 
binder, while the LiF derived from the LiTFSI salt is the 
component of the CEI film [35]. The SPAN cathode from 
electrolyte containing 0.5% 4-ABA has a higher concentra-
tion of LiF in the component of CEI layer. A LiF-rich CEI 
on the SPAN electrode surface is believed to facilitate the 
transportation of lithium ions [36]. Figure 6 c and f show 
the S 2p spectra of the cycled SPAN cathode. Doublets at 
167.91 eV and 161.32 eV are assigned to ­Li2SOx and ­Li2S/
Li2S2. The intensity of the ­Li2S/Li2S2 from the SPAN cath-
ode cycled in blank electrolyte is higher than that cycled 
in electrolyte containing 4-ABA. The result indicates that 
dissolution of polysulfides takes place in blank electrolytes 
due to the lack of a robust CEI as the protective layer [25]. 
At the same time, the intensity of C-S/S–S signals from the 
SPAN cathode cycled in blank electrolyte is lower than in 
electrolyte containing 4-ABA [37]. It could be explained that 
­Li2S/Li2S2 accumulated on the surface of the sulfur cathode 
prevents the detection of the underlying structure of SPAN 
[24]. The additive can effectively inhibit the decomposition 
of the electrolyte and protect the SPAN cathode.
Figure 7 shows SEM images of fresh and the cycled 
SPAN cathodes. As shown in the figure, the surface of the 
SPAN cathode cycled in blank electrolyte appears obvious 
cracks, implying that the structure of the SPAN cathode sur-
face is destroyed after cycling. And the surface of the SPAN 
cathode cycled in 0.5% 4-ABA electrolyte is similar to the 
fresh SPAN cathode, showing a better structural stability 
of the former. These results show that 4-ABA decomposes 
on the surface of the sulfur cathode, and the decomposition 
products protect the SPAN cathode and avoid the loss of the 
active sulfur. Therefore, the addition of 4-ABA is beneficial 
to the structural integrity of the SPAN cathode, which results 
in the improved capacity retention of the SPAN cathode.

In order to more fully demonstrate that 4-ABA can effectively 
protect the sulfur cathode and hinder polysulfide shuttling to the 
lithium anode, the cycled lithium anodes were characterized. 
Figure 8 shows the XPS results of the lithium anodes cycled in 
blank and 0.5% 4-ABA electrolytes. The peaks at 162.50 and 
161.45 eV correspond to ­Li2S2, and the peak at 160.54 eV is 
assigned to ­Li2S [38–40]. The peak intensities of ­Li2S2 and ­Li2S 
on the lithium anode cycled in 0.5% 4-ABA electrolyte are lower 
than those in blank electrolyte, indicating the contents of ­Li2S2 
and ­Li2S deposited on the lithium anode are lower. The results 
show that the shuttle effect is weaker in the cell containing 0.5% 
4-ABA electrolyte than the cell with blank electrolyte. It is dem-
onstrated that 4-ABA is effective on protecting the sulfur cathode.
Conclusion
In conclusion, using 4-ABA as an electrolyte additive 
can improve the electrochemical performance of SPAN. 
In ether electrolyte containing 0.5% 4-ABA, 4-ABA can 
participate in the formation of CEI film before the solvent 
component of the electrolyte decomposes, and the CEI can 
hinder the dissolution of discharge intermediate products. 
The reconstituted battery test and XPS analysis can further 
prove that 4-ABA is involved in the formation of CEI film 
on the surface of SPAN cathode. The CEI film can well 
protect the SPAN cathode, inhibiting the dissolution of 
lithium polysulfide and reducing the loss of active material. 
As a result, the performance of the cell containing 0.5% 
4-ABA electrolyte with a capacity retention of 88.81% was 
better than that of the cell containing blank electrolyte with 
a capacity retention of 65.56% for 100 cycles. This work 
could provide a reasonable idea for improving the cycling 
performance of SPAN in terms of electrolyte additive.
