1. Experimental Section 
1.1. Raw materials 
Lithium (Li) metal foils were purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, DoDoChem), 1,3-
dioxolane (DOL, DoDoChem), 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropylether (HFE, TCI), LiNO3 (Aladdin), lithium bis-
(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, DoDoChem), Li2S (Alfa), elemental sulfur (S8, Alfa), aluminum (Al) foils, polypropylene (PP) 
membranes (Celgard 2400), carbon nanotubes (CNTs, Cnano), poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, HSV-300), and N-methylpyrrolidone 
(NMP, Aladdin) were used as purchased without further purification. 
 
1.2. Preparation of electrolytes 
A mixture of DOL/DME (1:1, by vol.) or mixed with 10%, 20% or 30% HFE by volume was first prepared. 1.0 M LiTFSI was then 
dissolved in the above four mixed solvents with 2 wt.% LiNO3 to obtain electrolytes with different solvating power. To prepare the 
electrolyte with Li2S8 or Li2S6, Li2S and S8 were mixed with a molar ratio of 8:7 or 8:5, respectively, in the above electrolytes with a 
concentration of 1.0 M[S]. All the electrolytes were prepared in an argon-filled glove box with O2 and H2O contents below 0.1 ppm. 
 
1.3. Preparation of sulfur cathodes 
Sulfur/CNT (S/CNT) composite was prepared through ball-milling. A mixture of CNT and S8 (3:7, by wt.) was ball-milled with 
PVDF binders at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a homogeneous slurry. The slurry was then cast onto an Al foil and dried at 
60°C for 12 h to obtain S cathodes. The S cathodes were punched into disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm and the areal S loading was 
1.3 or 4.3 mg cm−2 for electrochemical measurements. 
 
1.4. Evaluation on anode kinetics 
For the measurements of anode kinetics, lithium–sulfur (Li–S) cells were assembled with 600 µm Li anodes (16.0 mm in 
diameter), 1.3 mg cm−2 S cathodes, PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 25 µL electrolyte without LiNO3. The Li–S cells were 
tested on a Neware battery cycler (CT-4008T, Shenzhen, China) at room temperature. For the shuttle current tests, the Li–S cells 
were firstly galvanostatically charged to 2.90 V and then discharged to 2.40, 2.39, 2.38, 2.37, 2.36, 2.35, and 2.34 V step by step. At 
each voltage step, the cells were kept potentiostatically for 1.0 h. The shuttle current was determined by the steady-state current at 
different potentiostatic voltages. Furthermore, the Li–S cells were cycled at 0.1 C (1 C = 1672 mA g−1) with a voltage range of 1.70–
2.60 V for Coulombic efficiency (CE) evaluation and further analyses. 
 
1.5. Evaluation on cathode kinetics 
For the overall cathode kinetic evaluation, Li | Li2S8 cells were assembled with 600 µm Li anodes (16.0 mm in diameter), carbon 
paper (CP) cathodes (13.0 mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 25 µL electrolyte with 1.0 M[S] Li2S8. Cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) was performed with a scan rate of 0.5 mV s−1 and a voltage window of 1.70–2.80 V. 
For the liquid–liquid cathode kinetic evaluation, Li2S6 symmetric cells were assembled with CP electrodes (both 13.0 mm in 
diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 40 µL electrolyte with 1.0 M[S] Li2S6. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy 
(EIS) was performed at the open circuit voltage with a sinusoidal voltage amplitude of 10 mV and a frequency range from 100 kHz to 
0.1 Hz. 
For the liquid–solid cathode kinetic evaluation, chronoamperometric nucleation tests were conducted. The Li | Li2S8 cells were 
discharged galvanostatically at 0.117 mA to 2.06 V and then potentiostically discharged at 2.05 V. All the above tests were conducted 
on a Solartron 1470E electrochemical workstation. 
For the decoupling of cathode kinetics, galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) was conducted. Li–S cells were 
assembled with 50 µm Li anodes (15.0 mm in diameter), 4.3 mg cm−2 S cathodes, PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 35 µL 
electrolyte without lithium polysulfides. The as-assembled cells were discharged at 0.1 C with a ten-minute discharge and a two-hour 
rest at each step between 1.70 and 2.60 V on the Neware battery cycler. To obtain the ohmic resistance, EIS was performed at the 
open circuit voltage before the GITT test with a sinusoidal voltage amplitude of 10 mV and a frequency range from 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz 
on the Solartron 1470E electrochemical workstation. 
 
1.6. Material characterization 
Li metal anodes after 5 cycles were disassembled from Li–S cells, washed for 3 times with DME to remove residual electrolyte 
and dried in the glove box thoroughly. The morphology was characterized by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM 7401F, 
JEOL Ltd., Japan) operated at 3.0 kV. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) experiments were carried out on a scanning X-ray 
microprobe (Quantera SXM, ULVAC-PHI. INC) operated at 250 kV with monochromated Al Kα radiation as the X-ray source. 7Li 
nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra (JNM-ECA600) were recorded at 233.2 MHz at room temperature. 
 
1.7. Li–S full cell assembly and battery performance evaluation 
The Li–S full cell performances were evaluated using standard 2032 coin-type cells. The Li–S coin cells were assembled with 
ultrathin Li anodes of 50 μm (15.0 mm in diameter, 43% excess in theory), high-S-loading cathodes of 4.3 mg cm−2, PP separators 
(19.0 mm in diameter), and 35 µL electrolyte (electrolyte to S (E/S) ratio of 6.1 μL mg−1). The cells were galvanostatically cycled with 
a voltage range of 1.70–2.60 V at 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5 C for rate performance evaluation and at 0.1 C for cycling performance 
evaluation on the Neware battery cycler.

For Li–S pouch cell evaluation, the S/CNT slurry prepared in section 1.3. was double-side coated on Al foils and dried at 60°C for 
12 h. Then, S cathodes with a single-side areal S loading of 7.4 mg cm−2 were cut into a size of 4 × 7 cm2. The thickness of Li metal 
anodes was 75 µm on a single side and 4 × 7 cm2 in size (25% excess in theory). The pouch cells were assembled with stacks of PP 
separators, double-side Li metal anodes, and double-side S cathodes in a dry room with a dew point below −40°C. A low E/S ratio of 
3.5 g gS−1 was employed. The pouch cells were tested at 0.05 C (1 C = 1000 mA g−1) with a voltage range of 1.70–2.55 V on the 
Neware battery cycler. The energy density was calculated based on the total mass of the pouch cell and the details are listed in Table 
S3. 
 
1.8. Density functional theory (DFT) calculation 
The DFT calculations were carried out in Gaussian (G16) program[1] with Becke’s three-parameter hybrid method using the Lee–
Yang–Parr correlation functional (B3LYP)[2] at 6-311+G(d,p) level. The solvation effect was considered with the universal solvation 
model of SMD[3] employing parameters of DME. 
The binding energy (Eb) between a lithium polysulfide (LiPS, Li2S8 or Li2S4) and solvents (DME and/or HFE) was defined as 
follows: 
Eb = Ecluster – (ELiPS + Esolvents) 
where Ecluster and ELiPS represent the total energy of the cluster and LiPS, respectively. Esolvents is the total energy of two DME solvents, 
one DME solvent and one HFE solvent, or two HFE solvents that coordinate with LiPS. 
 
1.9. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation 
MD simulations were conducted by the Large Scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS) code.[4] Four 
models were constructed to simulate electrolytes without or with 10%, 20%, or 30% HFE by volume for Li2S8 or Li2S4 systems, 
respectively, and the detailed compositions of these models are listed in Tables S6. The force field parameters for solvents were 
generated by the LigParGen web server[5] except that restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) atomic partial charges were assigned 
to each atom, which was obtained based on electrostatic potential (ESP) charges using the Multiwfn program.[6] The parameters for 
Li+, TFSI−, S82−, and S42− were obtained from Jensen et al.,[7] Koddermann et al.,[8] and Rajput et al.,[9] respectively. 
The initial atomic coordinates were generated with Packing Optimization for Molecular Dynamics Simulations (Packmol) 
program.[10] Each system was first equilibrated in the NPT ensemble using the Parrinello–Rahman barostat for 2 ns to maintain a 
temperature of 298 K and a pressure of 1 atm with time constants of 0.1 and 1 ps, respectively.[11] After that, the electrolytes were 
heated from 298 K to 420 K within 2 ns, and maintained at 420 K for 4 ns, followed by annealing from 420 K to 298 K within 2 ns. 
Subsequently, all systems were equilibrated at 298 K in the NPT ensemble for another 4 ns. A 12 ns production run was finally 
conducted in the NVT ensemble under Nose–Hoover thermostat.[12] Only the final 5 ns trajectory was sampled for the analysis of 
solvation structures. The solvation structures were visualized by VESTA.[13]
