Experimental section 
Synthesis of homogeneous catalyst of Cu-ETL 
1,10-phenanthroline and copper (II) chloride dihydrate were mixed at a mole ratio of 
1:1 in the mixed solvent of deionized water and ethanol (Vwater:VEtOH=1:10). The mixed 
reactants were heated at 120 ℃ in a sealed Teflon-lined autoclave (Anhui CHEMN Co. 
Ltd.) and kept over 24 h. After cooled down to room temperature, green crystal was 
obtained after filtration and washing with icy solvent. Then the green crystal (denoted as 
Cu2(phen)2Cl2) was dissolved in ultra-pure water and anion-replaced by KPF6 to obtain 
blue precipitants. The Cu-2-PF6 powder was dissolved in DME/DOL electrolyte containing 
1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3 to form Cu-ETL. The concentration of Cu-EIT is 
0.25 wt.%. 
Synthesis of heterogeneous catalyst of Cu-INT 
20 mg Cu2(phen)2Cl2 was dispersed in 10 mL ethanol solvent and then dropwise 
added into ethanol dispersions containing 180 mg super P. The mixture was stirred for 24 
h and then filtrated. After dried at 60 ℃, the black powder was mixed with polyvinylidene 
fluoride (PVDF) at a mass ratio of 9:1 with NMP as the solvent to form slurry. The slurry 
was uniformly coated on Celgard 2500 PP film and experienced the vacuum drying (60 oC) 
procedure to form the Cu-INT. The newly produced separator was punched into disks with 
a diameter of 19 mm prior to use.  
Preparation of S/C cathodes 
The super P and sulfur powder were first mixed at a mass ratio of 1:2 and then sealed 
into a glass bottle to heat at 155 oC over 6 h. After being thoroughly ground, the S/C 
composites were mixed with LA133 binder at a mass ratio of 9:1 to form slurry. The as-
prepared cathode slurry was coated on a piece of Al foil and then dried at 60 oC under 
vacuum condition. The attained cathode film was further punched into small disks with a 
diameter of 13 mm.  
Material characterizations 
The FTIR spectra were collected on Nicolet iS50 Fourier Transform Infrared 
Spectroscope. UV–vis spectra were collected on a UV–vis spectrometer (Shimadzu, 
UV1900). XRD patterns of samples were achieved on a Nalytical X’Pert Pro 
Diffractometer. Morphologies of samples were inspected by Zeiss sigma 500 Scanning 

<<PAGE 4>>
Electron Microscope. AFM images were gathered on a Alysum Research Cypher ES 
Atomic Force Microscope. 
Synchrotron radiation X-ray 3D nano-CT tests 
After Li2S nucleation test, the cell was disassembled to take out the cathode. The 
cathode powder was uniformly dispersed in ethanol solution by virtue of ultrasonic 
treatment. Subsequently, 10.0 μL of the obtained mixer was dropped onto a piece of 
carbon-free formvar film with 100 mesh, and dried at room temperature. The entire sample 
preparation process was carried out under pure argon atmosphere. The loaded formvar film 
was then placed into the vacuum chamber for imaging tests. The imaging tests were 
conducted on the beamline BL07W at the National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory 
(NSRL), Hefei, China, (flux: 2 × 1010 Phs s−1, spatial resolution: 30 nm). 
Li2S nucleation and dissociation tests 
Commercial CP was punched into small disks with a diameter of 13 mm. The 
electrocatalyst powder was dispersed into ethanol and then loaded on CP to formed Cu-
INT/CP. Cells were fabricated with Cu-INT/CP as the cathode, lithium foil as the anode, 
20 μL Li2S8 (0.2 mol L–1) solution as the catholyte and 20 μL LiTFSI (1.0 mol L–1) solution 
without Li2S8 as the anolyte. As for the sample of Cu-ETL/CP and Cu-ETL-INT/CP, the 
Li2S8 and Cu-ETL were used as the electrolyte. Then the cells were first galvanostatically 
discharged to 2.06 V at a constant current of 0.112 mA and then discharged 
potentiostatically at 2.05 V until the current was below 10–5 A. As for the dissociation tests, 
the cells were first galvanostatically discharged at the current of 0.112 mA till the voltage 
was 1.7 V. Subsequently, the cells were charged at the voltage of 2.35 V until the current 
was 10–5 A. 
Assembly of symmetric cells and CV tests 
Symmetric cells were assembled by applying Cu-INT/CP as the working and counter 
electrodes, and the electrolyte contain 20 μL Li2S6 (0.2 mol L–1). CV tests were conducted 
between –1 to 1 V at a scan rate of 50 mV s–1. As for the Cu-ETL/CP and Cu-ETL-INT/CP, 
Li2S6 electrolyte containing Cu-ETL catalyst was used as the electrolyte. 
Li plating/stripping tests 
Cu||Li cells were assembled by using Li foil as the anode, Cu foil as the cathode. 
commercial electrolyte with/ without Cu-2-T act as the electrolyte for the Cu-ETL-INT and 

<<PAGE 5>>
Cu-INT, respectively. The Li plating/stripping tests were performed at a current density of 
0.5 mA cm–2 with a plating/stripping capacity of 0.5 mA h cm–2. And the Li||Li cells were 
fabricated with two pieces of Li foils as the working electrodes. Celgard 2500 PP film was 
used as separator for the Cu-ETL related cell and Cu-INT electrocatalyst coated on Celgard 
2500 were used as separators for the Cu-ETL-INT and Cu-INT corresponding cells. 
Electrochemical evaluation 
As for Cu-INT sample, the batteries were assembled with Cu-INT coated Celgard 
2500 PP film as the separator, lithium foil as the anode and a mixture of DME/DOL 
solution containing 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3 as the electrolyte (purchased 
from Canrd Technology Co. Ltd.). While the batteries with Cu-ETL were fabricated with 
Celgard 2500 as the separator, Cu-ETL as electrolyte. And the battery with Cu-ETL-INT 
was fabricated with Cu-INT PP film as separator and Cu-ETL as electrolyte. The 
electrolyte/sulfur ratios were 12.0 and 4.0 μL mg–1 for routine and high-load batteries, 
respectively. GCD curves, rate and cycling performance measurements were carried out 
on NEWARE Battery Test System (CT-4008T-5V 20 mA-164 and 4008T-5V 100 mA-
124, Shenzhen, China) in the voltage window of 2.8–1.7 V. CV, LSV and EIS profiles of 
batteries were recorded on a Metrohm Autolab G204 Electrochemical Workstation.
