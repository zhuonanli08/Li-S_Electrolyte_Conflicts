Supporting Information Section
Within this study the conducting salt concentration was kept constant at 1 m in order to be comparable to our previous research.[1] All experimental work was conducted with the use of our previous sulfur cathode system which is highly scalable, can be produced via time efficient methods in water-based, environment friendly solvents, only contains sulfur (60 %), a basic carbon-conducting additive (C65, 30 %), a standard binder (CMC, 10 %) and has a moderate mass loading of 3 mgS/cm². This standard system was produced to create a more difficult higher scaled system on a coin cell level that is very prone to polysulfide shuttling and Li dendrite formation as described in our previous research.[1]

S4. SEM-supported postmortem analysis
These particular cells showed for each system a low initial capacity plateau after the first 5 cycles (Figure S5 a). An explanation to this event was not found but the continuation did show a comparable cycling behavior thus REF_FSI and DEGMEE_FSI reached a similar discharge capacity to previous tests. Strangely, the CE for this REF_FSI coin cell was remarkably better than for previous tests. A comparison of the postmortem analysis of REF_FSI from Figure S2 and Figure S6 reveals no severe “dead Li” on the separator and Li anode, while in an additional SEM-supported analysis (see Figure S7) which was only carried out for REF_FSI and DEGMEE_FSI, the cycling performance and CE matched with previous cycling (compare Figure 2 & S4) as well as the postmortem analysis (compare Figure S2). It is thus assumed that due to quick evaporation of DOL/DME[2,3] during cell building such a larger variation in cycling performance (compare Figure 4 & S5) did occur. Moreover, DEGDEE_FSI obtained a significantly lower capacity output which might stem from the poor reproducibility. As shown in Figure S5 b in the case for a repeated DEGDEE_FSI cell a similar cycling behavior for the first 75 cycles with low capacity output of ~160 mAh gS-1 was obtained, which after 75 cycles did increase back to the expected specific discharge capacity. Up to that point only an assumption could be made and it was believed that due to inconsistencies in the S cathode such a large deviation of specific discharge capacity occurred. Postmortem digital pictures of the cells with DEGDEE_FSI from the preliminary cycling experiment (see Figure S2) as well as these for the SEM-supported postmortem analysis in Figure S6 c show a high amount of reddish colored electrolyte and leftovers on the separator and Li anode. These findings support the earlier estimation of an incomplete reaction to only Li2S4 which could be a possible reason for the low capacity outcome. In the case of DEGMEE_FSI a more brownish coloration occurred (see Figure S6 b) which is typical for common PS shuttling.[4] However, the separator for DEGMEE_FSI (Figure S6 b) exposes a deep violet coloration[5,6] implying a complete reaction to Li2S. The reference REF_FSI had almost completely dried out compared to the other electrolytes. It did not show a black surface like in the postmortem sample investigation for the preliminary cycling experiment analysis (compare Figure S2). As previously explained, this black precipitate is believed to be “dead Li” formed by Li plating.
