Revealing the Coordination and Mediation Mechanism of
Arylboronic Acids Toward Energy-Dense Li-S Batteries
Runhua Gao, Bosi Huang, Mengtian Zhang, Xinru Wu, Yanze Song, Xiao Xiao,
Zhihong Piao, Zhoujie Lao, Zhiyuan Han, and Guangmin Zhou*
Lithium-sulfur (Li─S) batteries oﬀer a promising avenue for the next
generation of energy-dense batteries. However, it is quite challenging to
realize practical Li─S batteries under limited electrolytes and high sulfur
loading, which may exacerbate problems of interface deterioration and low
sulfur utilization. Herein, the coordination and mediation chemistry of
arylboronic acids that enable energy-dense and long-term-cycling Li─S
batteries is proposed. The coordination chemistry between NO3
−and
arylboronic acids breaks the resonance conﬁguration of NO3
−and
thermodynamically promotes its reduction on the anode, contributing to a
mechanically robust interface. The mediation chemistry between lithium
arylborate and polysulﬁdes distorts S─S/Li─S bonds, alters the
rate-determining step from Li2S4→Li2S2 to Li2S6→Li2S4, and homogeneously
accelerates the sulfur redox kinetics. Li─S batteries using
3,5-bis(triﬂuoromethyl)phenylboronic acid (BPBA) show excellent cycling
stability (1000 cycles with a low capacity decay rate of 0.033% per cycle) and a
high energy density of 422 Wh kg−1 under aggressive chemical environments
(high sulfur loading of 17.4 mg cm−2 and lean electrolyte operation of 3.6 mL
gS
−1). The basic mechanism of coordination and mediation chemistry can be
extended to other arylboronic acids with diﬀerent conﬁgurations and
compositions, thus broadening the application prospect of arylboronic acids
in the electrolyte engineering of Li─S batteries.
1. Introduction
Owing to the multi-electron transfer process during the sulfur
reduction reaction (SRR), lithium-sulfur (Li─S) batteries feature
high theoretical capacity and gravimetric energy density,[1] mak-
ing them highly promising for applications in weight-sensitive
ﬁelds like urban air mobility. However, the stable operation of
Li─S batteries still faces many challenges, chief among which

sulfur.[4] Therefore, regulating SEI prop-

Electrolyte engineering oﬀers a promis-
ing pathway to regulate the electrochem-

ous works illustrated that lithium nitrate
(LiNO3) could participate in the forma-
tion of an inorganic nitrogen-containing
interface on the Li anode, enhancing the ionic conductivity and
mechanical strength of the SEI.[6,7] Additionally, as an oxidiz-
ing agent, LiNO3 can chemically oxidize LiPSs intermediates
into oxysulfates (such as sulﬁte, sulfonate, and sulfate), which
passivates the SEI and blocks the continuous parasitic reaction
of LiPSs with Li metal.[7] For the sulfur cathode, introducing
homogeneous catalysts into the electrolyte is eﬀective in tun-
ing the SRR kinetics.[8] Although lots of heterogeneous catalysts
like transition metal compounds,[9] single-atom,[10] or dual-atom
catalysts[11] were applied to accelerate the LiPS conversion, the ac-
tive site of such catalysts may be covered by insulating discharge
products during cycling, resulting in failure of the catalysts.[12]
In contrast, homogeneous catalysts dissolved in electrolytes pro-
vide the maximum exposure of active sites, which remain uncov-
ered by solid discharge products. These catalysts anchor LiPSs
through metal-sulfur or nonmetal-lithium interactions, form-
ing well-dispersed molecular complexes with higher reaction ac-
tivity, thus lowering the energy barrier of SRR and mediating
the full conversion of sulfur species.[12] To realize the practical

2. Results
2.1. Coordination Chemistry Between Arylboronic Acid and NO3
−
To investigate the coordination eﬀect between arylboronic acid
and NO3
−, phenylboronic acid (PBA) and BPBA were introduced
into the commercialized Li─S electrolyte with 2 wt.% LiNO3.
Herein, PBA was studied as a fundamental arylboronic acid,
while BPBA grafted ─CF3 onto PBA was to preliminarily ex-
plore the eﬀect of side chain modiﬁcation on the properties of
arylboronic acids. As shown in Figure S1a (Supporting Infor-
mation), the conventional Li─S electrolyte (noted as Base) was
colorless and transparent, while the PBA and BPBA electrolytes
turned light yellow, indicating that certain chemical reactions
might have occurred in the electrolyte. For comparison, we also
dissolved PBA and BPBA into the electrolyte without LiNO3
(Figure S1b, Supporting Information). It was found that PBA re-
mained soluble and the corresponding solution stayed colorless,
while the BPBA solution became gel-like after stirring. These
phenomena not only conﬁrm that the color change is highly re-
lated to NO3
−, but also suggest that BPBA may promote the ring-
opening polymerization of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) in the absence
of LiNO3. UV–vis spectroscopy was used to test the absorbance
of various electrolytes and the corresponding results are shown
in Figure S2 (Supporting Information). The triplet near 270 nm
corresponds to the vibrational transitions of the benzene ring,
while a broad peak near 290 nm is ascribed to the ─N ═O vibra-
tions in NO3
−. Particularly, for PBA and BPBA electrolytes, new
peaks appear in the range of 350–400 nm, which are assigned
to B─O charge transfer transitions, suggesting the presence of
a coordination eﬀect between arylboronic acid and NO3
−. To il-
lustrate the gradual change in ion pairing as the titration of aryl-
boronic acid in the electrolyte, we conducted 7Li nuclear mag-
netic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy tests on electrolytes with
BPBA concentrations ranging from 0 to 0.2 m (Figure S3, Sup-
porting Information). Upon adding BPBA, the 7Li NMR sig-
nal exhibits a noticeable downﬁeld shift due to the coordina-
tion between NO3
−and B atoms that draws the electron cloud
toward the electron-deﬁcient center, thereby reducing the elec-
tron density around the Li nucleus and leading to a deshield-
ing eﬀect. As the BPBA concentration increases, the downﬁeld
shift of the 7Li signals becomes more pronounced, indicating
that higher concentrations of arylboronic acids produce more
ion pairs and enhance the deshielding eﬀect around the Li nu-
cleus. Furthermore, 11B NMR spectroscopy was applied to PBA
and BPBA solutions with and without LiNO3 (note that the BPBA
solution used 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) as the solvent). The
typical chemical environment of a B nucleus includes triple-
coordination and tetra-coordination. Due to the existence of an
empty orbital, the electron distribution near a triple-coordinated
B nucleus is unbalanced, leading to a relatively strong deshield-
ing eﬀect and a downﬁeld shift of 11B signals. When a B nucleus
is in a tetra-coordinated environment, the bonding electrons are
symmetrically distributed, resulting in enhanced shielding and
an upﬁeld shift of 11B signals. As displayed in Figure 1c,d, PBA
and BPBA solutions in the absence of LiNO3 show chemical
shifts of 28.5 and 27.4 ppm, respectively. Upon adding LiNO3,
the 11B signals signiﬁcantly upshift to 1.1 ppm (PBA + LiNO3)
and 3.1 ppm (BPBA + LiNO3), conﬁrming the occurrence of
tetra-coordination between electron-donating NO3
−and electron-
deﬁcient B atoms. Besides, mass spectrometry (MS) analysis
was also conducted for PBA/BPBA electrolytes (Figure 1e). Char-
acteristic peaks at m/z = 184 and m/z = 320 correspond to
(PBA-NO3)−and (BPBA-NO3)−, respectively, directly validating
the presence of stable complex anions in PBA/BPBA solutions.
To explore the inﬂuence of coordination eﬀect on the charge
distribution of diﬀerent anions, electrostatic potential calcula-
tions were applied to NO3
−, (PBA-NO3)−and (BPBA-NO3)−. As
shown in Figure 1f, the planar triangular structure makes the
electrostatic potential evenly distributed across the three O atoms
in NO3
−. When coordinating with PBA or BPBA to form complex
anions, the balanced distribution of electrostatic potential for the
O atoms is disrupted. Bader charge analysis was applied to cal-
culate the speciﬁc charge values in the N and O atoms of NO3
−
(Figure 1g; Table S1, Supporting Information). After coordina-
tion, a signiﬁcant decrease of the Bader charge for O1/O2 occurs
while O3 becomes more electronegative, indicating that the co-
ordination eﬀect pushes the electron cloud of NO3
−toward the
electron-deﬁcient B atom. Besides, N becomes more electropos-
itive, leading to the most unbalanced charge distribution in the
N─O3 bond which is more unstable compared to other chemi-
cal bonds within the complex anions. A single-electron-reduction
was applied to the three anions based on ab initio molecular
dynamics (AIMD) to study the change of N─O3 bond length
(Figure 1h,i). It is found that NO3
−exhibits only partial deforma-
tion after acquiring one electron and the bond length of N─O3
just slightly elongates, indicating that the reduction of NO3
−
is relatively diﬃcult. In contrast, both (PBA-NO3)−and (BPBA-
NO3)−undergo unstable N─O3 bond cleavage upon gaining an
electron, which demonstrates that the coordination eﬀect of aryl-
boronic acid facilitates the reduction of NO3
−. It is noteworthy
that the strong electron-withdrawing property of ─CF3 makes the
B atom in BPBA more electron-deﬁcient, thus strengthening the
coordination of B-O3 (Figure 1j). Consequently, the N─O3 bond
in BPBA is easier to break than that in PBA. Frontier molecular
orbital theory also indicates that the lowest unoccupied molecular
orbital (LUMO) levels of NO3
−, (PBA-NO3)−, and (BPBA-NO3)−
decrease one by one (Figure S4, Supporting Information), sug-
gesting that (BPBA-NO3)−is more thermodynamically favorable
to be reduced on the Li anode, which may induce more Li3N/Li2O
components and B─O oligomers in the SEI. Except for NO3
−,
the binding energies between arylboronic acids and other poten-
tial oxygen-containing anions in the electrolyte were also calcu-
lated to show possible competitive coordination (Figures S5, S6,
Supporting Information). Among the major anions, TFSI−does
not coordinate with PBA or BPBA, likely due to large steric hin-
drance that prevents binding between the O and B atoms. Be-
sides, during electrochemical cycling, LiPSs may be oxidized by
nitrates to form oxysulfates, which can coordinate with boron but
exhibit weaker binding energies compared to NO3
−, making it

2.2. Mediation Chemistry Between LiABO and LiPSs
To investigate the reaction mechanism between arylboronic acid
and LiPSs, we added a certain amount of LiPSs (including Li2S8,
Li2S6, and Li2S4) into PBA/BPBA solutions. As shown in Figures
S7, S8 (Supporting Information), white needle-like precipitates
formed after the addition of long-chain LiPSs (Li2S8/Li2S6),
which was conﬁrmed as crystalline sulfur by X-ray diﬀraction
(XRD) test. Moreover, the color of wet lead acetate test papers
turned black when they were placed in the vial containing the
mixture of PBA/BPBA-Li2S8/Li2S6, indicating the generation of
H2S during the reaction between PBA/BPBA and long-chain
LiPSs.[14] After adding short-chain polysulﬁdes (such as Li2S4) to
the PBA/BPBA solution, although no white precipitate formed
immediately, an obvious color change was observed, and the wet
lead acetate test papers also turned black (Figure S9, Supporting
Information). These observations suggest that arylboronic acid
may promote the disproportionation reaction of LiPSs. It can
also be inferred that PBA/BPBA might donate protons and ac-
cept Li atoms to form LiABO. Considering the similar proper-
ties of polysulﬁdes with diﬀerent chain lengths, Li2S6 was cho-
sen as a representative polysulﬁde for the subsequent NMR anal-
ysis. First, 7Li NMR spectroscopy was used to verify the forma-
tion of LiABO (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The 7Li
signal exhibits a downﬁeld shift following the reaction between
Li2S6 and PBA/BPBA, which is due to the deshielding eﬀect of
Li─O binding that lowers the electron density around Li nu-
cleus. Furthermore, 11B NMR spectroscopy of the PBA/BPBA
electrolyte reacting with Li2S6 was conducted to characterize the
coordination state of boron (Figure 2a,b). Both PBA-Li2S6 and
BPBA-Li2S6 show a clear triple-coordination peak at low-ﬁeld po-
sitions, implying the coordination tendency of NO3
−with B be-
comes weaker. The MS of the mixed solution of PBA/BPBA-
Li2S6 displays two characteristic peaks at m/z = 127.0682 and
m/z = 270.0015 (Figure 2c,d), corresponding to lithiated PBA and
BPBA (denoted as LPBA and LBPBA, respectively). Meanwhile,
MS signals of LPBA/LBPBA were also observed in the mixture of
PBA/BPBA-Li2S8/Li2S4 (Figure S11, Supporting Information).
To understand the transition from tetra-coordination to triple-
coordination after lithiation, we calculated the electrostatic
potential distribution and Bader charges of PBA/BPBA and
LPBA/LBPBA (Figure 2e,f; Table S2, Supporting Information).
Since the electronegativity of Li is lower than that of H, the elec-
tron cloud is closer to O in LPBA/LBPBA, leading to a signiﬁcant
concentration of negative charge around the B─O region and re-
pulsion of NO3
−. Meanwhile, the positive charge on B decreases
after lithiation, making it have a weaker tendency to coordinate
with NO3
−. AIMD simulations (Figure S12, Supporting Informa-
tion) also clearly indicate that NO3
−tends to move away from B
when arylboronic acid converts to LiABO. Second, the enhanced
reactivity of coordinated NO3
−in the complex anions facilitates
the oxidation of LiPSs to oxysulfate, therefore, NO3
−originally
coordinated with arylboronic acid is more easily consumed by
LiPSs, leading to decoordination. To prove this hypothesis, MS
was used to analyze the NO3
−content in Base, PBA, and BPBA
electrolytes before and after adding Li2S6, with the peak intensity
normalized according to the intensity of TFSI anions (Figure S13,
Supporting Information). The peak intensity of NO3
−in (Base +
Li2S6) only slightly decreases compared to that in Base, indicat-
ing a limited reaction between NO3
−and LiPSs. However, the
peak intensity of NO3
−drops signiﬁcantly when Li2S6 is added
to PBA and BPBA electrolytes, conﬁrming the enhanced reactiv-
ity of the coordinated NO3
−. Based on the normalized intensity,
the residual NO3
−contents after the reaction of Base, PBA, and
BPBA electrolytes with LiPSs are calculated to be 89.5%, 35.2%,
and 14.0%, respectively (Figure 2g). According to these results, it
is the change of charge distribution in LiABO and the enhanced
reactivity of coordinated NO3
−that lead to the variation of coor-
dination number.
The O and Li atoms in LiABO exhibit a strong aﬃnity for Li
and S atoms in LiPSs, respectively, forming a LiABO-LiPS com-
plex through chemical adsorption. The charge density diﬀerence
of LPBA-LiPSs and LBPBA-LiPSs (Figure S14a,c, Supporting In-
formation) reveals signiﬁcant charge distribution on the S─Li
and Li─O bonds, conﬁrming the stable adsorption. AIMD sim-
ulations of the interaction between LiABO and Li2S4 (a repre-
sentative LiPS) also clearly show the bonding process of Li─O
and S─Li (Figure S14b,d, Movies S1 and S2, Supporting Infor-
mation). Furthermore, the changes in Gibbs free energy dur-
ing the SRR of Li2Sx, LPBA-Li2Sx, and LBPBA-Li2Sx were cal-
culated based on density functional theory (DFT) and the re-
sults are shown in Figure 2h and Figure S15 (Supporting Infor-
mation). For Li2Sx, the energy barrier for the rate-determining
step of liquid Li2S4 to solid Li2S2 is 1.19 eV. Interestingly, for
LPBA-Li2Sx and LBPBA-Li2Sx, the rate-determining step shifts
to Li2S6→Li2S4, with an energy barrier of 0.66 eV for both com-
plexes, while the energy barrier of Li2S4→Li2S2 drops to 0.44 eV
(LPBA-Li2Sx) and 0.17 eV (LBPBA-Li2Sx). Overall, the media-
tion of LiABO (particularly LBPBA) reduces the energy bar-
rier for the entire SRR, which raises the utilization of sulfur
species.
To understand the intrinsic mechanism behind the transition
of the rate-determining step for the LiABO-LiPS complex, we cal-
culated the LUMO distribution of LiABO-long-chain LiPS (Li2S8
as the representative) and LiABO-middle-chain LiPS (Li2S4 as the
representative). As the SRR basically involves the process of elec-
tron gaining and S─S/Li─S bond breaking, it is helpful to use
LUMO to analyze the tendency of electron gain and loss at spe-
ciﬁc bonds, thus accurately identifying the preferred bond break-
ing. Figure 2i and Figure S16 (Supporting Information) show the
LUMO distributions of LBPBA-LiPSs, LPBA-LiPSs, and LiPSs.
For long-chain LiPSs, the LUMO primarily distributes around
the S─S bond, while for middle-chain and short-chain LiPSs,
more LUMO is distributed around the Li─S bond. This indicates
that S─S bonds are more likely to gain electrons and break dur-
ing the reduction of long-chain LiPSs, and as the sulfur chains
shorten, the breaking of Li─S bonds increasingly dominates the
reduction process and ﬁnally becomes inevitable. After under-
standing the LUMO distribution in diﬀerent LiPSs, the S─S and
Li─S bond length values were extracted from the stable conﬁgu-
rations of Li2Sx, LPBA-Li2Sx, and LBPBA-Li2Sx (Figure 2j; Table
S3, Supporting Information). Generally, the combination with
LiABO elongates the Li─S bond and shortens the S─S bond.

After understanding the basic mechanism of arylboronic acid
coordination/mediation chemistry, we can infer the overall reac-
tion scheme according to the spatial distribution of LiPSs in the
batteries (Figure S20, Supporting Information). Initially, the coor-
dinated NO3
−is homogeneously distributed throughout the elec-
trolyte. During discharge, LiPSs dissolve from the cathode side,
creating a concentration gradient across the cell. On the cathode
region with a high LiPS concentration, (arylboronic acid-NO3)−
complexes chemically react with LiPSs to generate oxysulfates
and LiABO, which begin to mediate the conversion of LiPSs. On
the anode side with a low LiPS concentration (only shuttle from
the cathode), LiPSs are oxidized by the complex ions into sul-
ﬁte or sulfate species, which become part of the SEI and prevent
LiPS corrosion. Due to the relatively low concentration of LiPSs,
there still contains a considerable amount of unconsumed com-
plex anions in the anode region, which continue to be preferen-

2.3. Robust SEI Constructed by Arylboronic Acid Additives
To experimentally verify the improving eﬀect of the coordina-
tion chemistry between arylboronic acid and NO3
−on Li de-
position, the Aurbach method was applied to test the Coulom-
bic eﬃciency (CE) of Li||Cu half-cells with diﬀerent electrolytes
(Figure 3a).[15] First, under various BPBA concentrations, the
electrolyte with the most ion pairs (0.2 m BPBA) exhibits the
highest CE (Figure S22, Supporting Information). Meanwhile,
the CE of Li||Cu half-cells with 0.2 m BPBA and PBA electrolytes
are 99.64% and 99.49%, respectively, much higher than that of
the Base electrolyte (98.64%), indicating that the addition of aryl-
boronic acid improves the reversibility of Li plating/stripping.
Li||Li symmetric cells were assembled to evaluate the interface
stability of the Li anode. As shown in Figure 3b, the polar-
ization voltage of the cell with PBA/BPBA is lower than that
with Base under various current densities. After 300 h of cy-
cling, the overpotential of the cell with Base signiﬁcantly in-
creases, while the cell with PBA electrolyte can delay this in-
crease until after 500 h. Particularly, the cell with BPBA elec-
trolyte stably operates for over 1200 h with a small overpotential
of ≈50 mV, illustrating the stabilization eﬀect of BPBA on the
Li anode.

The Li anode interface stability is governed by the composition
of the SEI.[16,17] To better understand how BPBA additives regu-
late the SEI components, we disassembled cycled Li─S cells with
Base and BPBA electrolytes and conducted X-ray photoelectron
spectroscopy (XPS) on the Li anodes with diﬀerent Ar+ sputter-
ing times (Figure 3c,d; Figures S23, S24, Supporting Informa-

anode. Studies have shown that the Li+ conductivity of all-Li2O
SEI grown on Li foil is twice that of all-LiF SEI.[18] Therefore, in-
creasing the Li2O content is beneﬁcial for promoting uniform ion
ﬂuxes and achieving a higher CE. In the N 1s spectra, the peak in-
tensities of C─N and Li3N in the BPBA anode are stronger, which
is attributed to the enhanced reactivity of coordinated NO3

are more actively involved in the formation of the SEI. The B 1s
spectra of BPBA anode show clear B─O signals, conﬁrming the
capture of arylboronic acid fragments by the SEI. B oligomers
are considered elastic SEI components that can alleviate inter-
nal stress during Li plating/stripping, thus enhancing interface
stability.[19,20] Additionally, the content of Li2S in the SEI sug-
gests the corrosion degree of Li anode by the shuttle eﬀect.[21]
In the S 2p spectra (Figure S23, Supporting Information), there
are stronger signals of polythionates and sulﬁtes in the outer SEI
and weaker intensities of Li2S in the inner SEI for the BPBA an-
ode. This demonstrates that the shuttling LiPSs are more eﬀec-
tively oxidized by (BPBA-NO3)−to oxysulfates, which passivate
the interface and reduce the corrosion of Li anode by the shut-
tle eﬀect.[7] Furthermore, time-of-ﬂight secondary ion mass spec-
trometry (TOF-SIMS) was applied to the cycled BPBA and Base
anodes in Li─S cells to visualize the component distribution in
the SEI (Figure 3e–g; Figure S25, Supporting Information). For
BPBA anode, relatively abundant BO2
−, C2H−, and SO2
−ions
jointly compose an elastic and LiPS-resistant outer SEI, while the
F−and CNO−components form a mechanically robust inner SEI
with high ionic conductivity. By comparison, the relevant compo-
nents in the Base SEI are either absent or unevenly distributed,
which cannot adequately address the challenges of LiPS corro-
sion and dendrite growth in Li─S chemistry.
To characterize the inﬂuence of interface regulation on SEI
properties and Li plating/stripping behavior, atomic force mi-
croscopy (AFM) was used to test the mechanical strength and
surface morphology of the BPBA anode and Base anode in the
cycled Li─S cells. As displayed in Figure 3h,i, the average mod-
ulus based on the Derjaguin–Muller–Toporov (DMT) model[22]
for the BPBA anode is 2089 MPa, much higher than the DMT
modulus of 741 MPa for the Base anode. This demonstrates that
the organic-inorganic hybrid SEI, originating from the coordina-
tion chemistry of BPBA and NO3
−, is beneﬁcial for enhancing
mechanical strength. Additionally, the surface topography shows
that the average roughness (Ra) of the BPBA and Base anodes
are 65.9 and 227.0 nm, respectively (Figure 3j,k). The smaller
morphological ﬂuctuations indicate that Li plating/stripping is
more uniform and reversible, reducing the risk of local den-
drite growth. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was con-
ducted to examine the Li deposition morphology on a larger scale
(Figure S26, Supporting Information). Distinct cracks and den-
drites are observed in the Base anode, which exhibits a loose and
porous Li deposition morphology that may continuously react
with the solvent and LiPSs. In contrast, the BPBA anode favors
dense and smooth Li deposition, verifying that the SEI regulation
eﬀectively induces homogeneous ion ﬂux and is beneﬁcial for ex-
tending the cycling lifespan. Furthermore, the element mapping
(Figure S27, Supporting Information) shows that there are fewer
S signals on the surface of the BPBA anode compared to those on
the surface of the Base anode, again illustrating that the addition
of BPBA alleviates the shuttle eﬀect and reduces anode corrosion.
2.4. Improved Sulfur Redox Kinetics Enabled by Arylboronic Acid
Additives
A series of experimental characterizations for sulfur redox ki-
netics were applied to Li─S cells with diﬀerent electrolytes to
observe the acceleration eﬀect enabled by LiABO-LiPSs medi-
ation chemistry. The cycling performance of Li─S cells based
on electrolytes with diﬀerent BPBA concentrations is shown in
Figure S28 (Supporting Information). The Li─S cell with 0.2 m
BPBA shows better cycling stability and capacity delivery, hence
0.2 m was chosen as the best concentration in the full cell test-
ing. Ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography-quadrupole
(UPLC-QTof-MS) was carried out to verify the existence of Li-
ABO after prolonged cycling. As shown in Figure S29 (Support-
ing Information), an intense peak assigned to LBPBA appears in
the MS spectrum, providing evidence that LBPBA remains active
in the electrolyte and can sustain its role throughout the cycles.
Figure 4a shows the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves of diﬀerent
cells under a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1. PR1 and PR2 correspond
to the reduction of S8 to liquid LiPSs and liquid LiPSs to solid
Li2S, respectively, while PO1 and PO2 correspond to the reverse
processes. Compared with the Base cell, PBA/BPBA cells exhibit
enhanced current responses for all the peaks. Particularly, the
current response at PR2 shows an increasing trend from Base to
PBA to BPBA, conﬁrming the role of BPBA in reducing the en-
ergy barrier for the key step of liquid-solid conversion. Further-
more, CVs at various scan rates were performed to study the Li+
diﬀusion behavior (Figure 4b,c; Figures S30, S31, Supporting In-

the BPBA cell, which show increased current response compared
to those of PBA and Base cells (Figure S31, Supporting Informa-
tion). The Randles–Sevcik equation was used to linearly ﬁt the
peak currents and the square root of scan rates at PR2 and PO2 in
the CVs, and the square of the calculated slope is proportional to
the Li+ diﬀusion coeﬃcient (DLi+).[13] As displayed in Figure S32
(Supporting Information), PBA/BPBA cells show larger slopes
than the Base cell at PR2 and PO2. According to the calculation
results, the DLi+ of PBA/BPBA electrodes at PR2 is 2.79 and 2.21
times that of the Base electrode, while the DLi+ of PO2 is 1.22 and
4.14 times that of the Base electrode (Figure 4d), demonstrating
that the arylboronic acid additives improve Li+ diﬀusion during
the liquid–solid conversion.

2.5. Electrochemical Performance of Li─S Batteries
To elucidate the detailed physicochemical processes regulated by
the arylboronic acid coordination/mediation chemistry, we con-
ducted ﬁnite element method (FEM) simulations for comprehen-
sive visualization of Li deposition and sulfur utilization in Li─S
batteries with and without BPBA. First, two diﬀerent SEI models
are constructed (Figure 5a) according to the component charac-
terization, in which the BPBA anode features a homogenous SEI
with high ionic conductivity (𝜎SEI), while the Base anode has a
more fragile SEI with unevenly distributed components and low
𝜎SEI. The FEM simulation provides the local current density dis-
tribution on the anodes with diﬀerent SEI models. As shown in
Figure 5a, apparent local current density variations at the Base
anode can be observed, while the local current density at vari-
ous locations of the BPBA anode is more uniform. A concept of
current deviation (Idev) is deﬁned to quantitatively describe the
uniformity of current density distribution on the anode:

the local current density and Japply is the applied current den-

shown in Figure S38 (Supporting Information), the Idev for the
BPBA anode is almost zero throughout the entire charging pe-

highly relevant to the local current density distribution. For the
Base anode (Figure 5b), Li+ preferentially deposits on regions
with higher local current density during charging, resulting in
a rough and uneven morphology. By comparison, the BPBA an-

dendrite problems, while the BPBA anode features smoother de-
position and ensures the reversibility of Li plating/stripping. The
simulation results are also consistent with the conclusions drawn
from the AFM and SEM tests.

3. Conclusion
In this work, we propose the coordination and mediation chem-
istry of arylboronic acid to simultaneously stabilize the Li an-
ode and accelerate SRR kinetics. Combined with various spectro-
scopic techniques and theoretical calculations, we conﬁrm that
the coordination eﬀect between arylboronic acid and NO3
−forms
complex anions with higher reactivity, which are preferentially
reduced on the Li anode to construct a robust and elastic SEI.
Moreover, LiABO, one of the products generated from the re-
action between arylboronic acid and LiPSs, is veriﬁed to have
a signiﬁcant mediation eﬀect on SRR, thereby accelerating the
key step of liquid-solid conversion and enhancing the utiliza-
tion of active sulfur. Li─S batteries based on BPBA, a repre-
sentative arylboronic acid, are experimentally demonstrated to
have smooth Li deposition behavior and improved SRR kinet-
ics. Speciﬁcally, the Li─S cell with BPBA can steadily operate for
1000 cycles with a capacity decay rate of only 0.033% per cycle.
An Ah-level pouch cell with BPBA delivers a high gravimetric
energy density of 422 Wh kg−1, proving the eﬀectiveness of aryl-
boronic acid coordination/mediation chemistry in practical ap-
plications. Meanwhile, as arylboronic acids have rich and diverse
molecular structures (Figure S48, Supporting Information), we
believe that the well-demonstrated fundamental principle of co-
ordination and mediation chemistry can provide insightful ref-
erences for designing more high-performance arylboronic acid
additives with diﬀerent molecular conﬁgurations and composi-
tions (Figure S49, Supporting Information), thus contributing to
the electrolyte engineering of Li-S batteries.
