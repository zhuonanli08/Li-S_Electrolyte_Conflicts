Experimental section
Preparation of homogeneous catalyst (Fe-Hom) 
FePcCl powder was added to a Li–S electrolyte (1 mol L–1 LiTFSI in DME/DOL solution with a volume ratio of 1:1 containing 2.0 wt.% LiNO3, denoted as S03, Canrd), reaching a FePcCl content of 0.1 wt.% in the electrolyte. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 24 h and then allowed to stand for another 24 h. The upper dark green supernatant was collected as the Fe-Hom homogeneous catalyst. 
Preparation of heterogeneous catalyst (Fe-Het)
The undissolved portion was filtered, washed repeatedly with DME, and then dried. Subsequently, 10 mg of the dried powder was uniformly dispersed in 20 mL of ethanol and then dropwise added into 300 mL of ethanol containing 380 mg of Super P under stirring. After continuous stirring for 24 h, the mixture was filtered, washed, and freeze-dried to obtain the heterogeneous catalyst of Fe-Het. Fe-Het was then mixed with PVDF powder in NMP solvent to form a viscous slurry, which was coated onto a polypropylene (PP) separator and punched into 19-mm diameter disks.
Preparation of the sulfur/carbon cathodes
Super P and nano-sulfur powder were uniformly mixed at a mass ratio of 1:3 and placed in a sealed glass container, and subjected to melt-diffusion at 155  ℃ for 6 h to obtain the sulfur@carbon composite. The composite was then mixed with Super P and LA133 binder in a mass ratio of 8:1:1, thoroughly dispersed to form a viscous slurry. The slurry was coated onto an aluminum foil current collector, and further dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h. The coated aluminum foil was punched into 13-mm diameter discs for coin-type battery assembly, or cut into rectangular sheets of specified dimensions for pouch cell fabrication.
Material characterizations
XRD patterns were collected on a Smartlab 3kW Diffractometer. The Raman spectra of the samples were recorded on a Thermo Fisher-DXR2xi system. UV–Vis spectra of electrolytes were obtained using an AOE-A560 spectrometer. The surface chemical composition of materials was measured using a Thermo Scientific K-Alpha XPS. XPS depth profiling of the cycled Li anode was performed on a Thermo SCIENTIFIC Nexsa G2 with monochromatic Al Kα. SEM images and maps were obtained by a TESCAN MIRA LMS scanning electron microscope. TEM images and maps of Fe-Het were employed by the Talos F200S transmission electron microscope. AFM images of cycled Li anodes were conducted on a Bruker Dimension Icon.
Ionic conductivity tests
Ionic conductivities were achieved by collecting EIS data from symmetric stainless-steel cells. The ionic conductivity (σ) was calculated according to the equation below:
σ=L/(R×A)     (1)
where L is the separator thickness, R is the bulk resistance, and A is the electrode area.
Li2S nucleation and dissociation tests
The 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution was prepared by dissolving sublimed sulfur and Li2S powder at a molar ratio of 1:7 in tetraglyme solvent. For the cell with CP‑Fe‑Hom, 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution and Fe‑Hom served as the catholyte, and S03 as the anolyte. Fe-Het powder was dispersed in ethanol and loaded on carbon paper, and dried to obtain CP-Fe-Het. For the cell containing CP‑Fe‑Het, 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution was used as the catholyte, and S03 as the anolyte. For the cell with CP‑Fe-Syg assembled with CP-Fe-Het as the cathode, 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution with Fe-Hom served as the catholyte, and S03 acted as the anolyte. In nucleation testing, cells were firstly galvanostatically discharged at 0.112 mA to 2.06 V, followed by potentiostatic discharge at 2.05 V until the current drops below 0.01 mA. As for decomposition testing, it involved galvanostatic discharge at 0.112 mA to 1.7 V, cells firstly galvanostatic charging to 2.35 V, and finally potentiostatic charging at 2.35 V until the current dropped below 0.01 mA.

Li plating/stripping tests 
For the Li||Li symmetric cells, both the cathode and anode were lithium foils. For the Cu||Li cells, the cathode was a copper foil, and the anode was a lithium foil. An electrolyte dosage of 40 µL was applied per electrode in all cells: the S03 electrolyte was used for Fe-Het-based cells, while the Fe-Hom electrolyte was employed for both Fe-Syg and Fe-Hom systems. The cells related to Fe-Hom directly used a PP separator, and the PP separator coated with Fe-Het was used for cells involving the Fe-Syg and Fe-Het.
Electrochemical measurements
All coin-type Li–S batteries were assembled in an argon-filled glove box (O2 < 0.01 ppm, H2O<0.01 ppm). Lithium foil was used as the anode for all batteries. For Fe-Hom-based batteries, a PP separator and the Fe-Hom electrolyte were employed. For batteries involving Fe Het, a PP separator coated with Fe Het and the S03 electrolyte were used. In the Fe Syg system, the same coated PP separator was paired with the Fe-Hom electrolyte. The sulfur areal loading of the cathode was controlled to 1.4–1.5 mg cm–2 with a sulfur content of 60% for routine batteries. Cycling and rate performances were evaluated on a Neware battery testing system under galvanostatic charge/discharge in the voltage range of 1.7–2.8 V at room temperature. CV, EIS, and other electrochemical measurements were conducted using a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation.
Neutron imaging tests
Neutron imaging tests were conducted at the China Spallation Neutron Source (CSNS) with the following parameters: a 20 mm pinhole, exposure time of 100 s, detector field of view of 7.45 cm, pixel size of 36.5 μm, and spatial resolution of approximately 50–100 μm. For the CC sample, after nucleation testing with CC as the cathode, the cell was disassembled, and the electrode was rinsed with DME and dried for subsequent imaging. For the Li foil samples, Li||Li symmetric cells after different cycle numbers were disassembled, and the Li electrodes were similarly washed with DME and dried. All samples were prepared in an argon-filled glove box (O2 < 0.01 ppm, H2O < 0.01 ppm).
SR SAXS tests
SR SAXS measurements were performed on cycled lithium anodes from Li||Li symmetric cells at the BL16B1 beamline of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). A monochromatic X-ray source with a wavelength of 0.124 nm was used. 2D scattering patterns were collected with a Mar165 CCD detector (2048 × 2048 pixels, 80 μm pixel size), using an exposure time of 0.1 s and a sample-to-detector distance of 5272.8 mm. The acquired 2D scattering images were converted into 1D scattering curves I(q)-q using the Fit2D software, with background scattering corrected using polyimide (PI) tape. The Guinier-Porod model was applied to analyze the SR SAXS data for obtaining the Rg.
Theoretical calculations
All calculations were performed by using density functional theory (DFT) as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP).[1] The projector-augmented wave (PAW) potential was used to describe the electron-ion interactions, [2] and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) function of generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was used to describe the electron exchange and correlation energy. [3] The unit structures of Phthalocyanine and FePcCl were placed into a 20 × 20 ×20 Å3 periodic box for calculations. The cutoff energy for the plane-wave basis set was set as 500 eV, and the total energy convergence was set to be lower than 1 × 10−5 eV with the force convergence set at 0.02 eV/Å. The DFT-D3 empirical correction method was employed to describe van der Waals interactions. [4] The adsorption energy (Eads) was defined as: Eads = Esub+ps–Esub–Eps, where Esub are the energy of phthalocyanine and FeCl-Pc, respectively. Eps is the total energy of sulfur species, and Esub+ps is the total energy of the corresponding substrate with sulfur species.
