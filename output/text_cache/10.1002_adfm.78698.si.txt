Experimental Section
Preparation of electrolyte: Battery-grade LiFSI, DME and PY13FSI were purchased from Dodochem. LiFSI was added into DME solvent to prepare 2 M electrolyte (named DE electrolyte), and PY13FSI solvents to prepare 2 M electrolyte (named IL electrolyte). For hybrid electrolytes, 2 M LiFSI was dissolved into the mixed solvent of DME-IL with different solvent ratios. All the electrolytes were prepared inside an argon-filled glove box.
Preparation of electrodes: The sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) powder was purchase from Canrd. The SPAN cathode was prepared using the following method: SPAN, Super P and Polyacrylic acid (PAA) were mixed at a mass ratio of 8:1:1 in deionized water. The homogeneous slurry was uniformly coated on a carbon-coated aluminum foil and dried at 80℃ under vacuum overnight. By controlling the coating thickness, the SPAN electrode with different loading can be obtained. The SPAN loadings were controlled to be ≈1.2 and 5.4 mg cm−2 for, respectively, low and high loading assessments.
Electrochemical measurement: All the coin cells were assembled and disassembled in an argon-filled glove box with H2O and O2 less than 0.01ppm. For Li||SPAN cells, the pre-weighted SPAN electrode, 16 mm-diameter Celgard 2325 separator and lithium foil ware sequentially placed into a 2032-type coin cell. The Li||SPAN cells were rested for 8 h before electrochemical test. The cut-off voltage was from 1.0 V to 3.0 V. Capacity values were calculated based on the sulfur content in the SPAN powder. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was conducted with Li||stainless steel cells with the CHI660E electrochemical workstation. The sweep voltage range was between 2.5-6.0 V, with a ramp rate of 5 mV s-1. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was also with the CHI660E electrochemical workstation at a scan rate of 0.1-0.5 mV s-1. Electrochemical impendence spectroscopy (EIS) was tested on CHI660E electrochemical workstation in a frequency range from 0.1 HZ to 1 MHZ.
Material characterization: Raman spectra (Horiba LabRAM HR Evolution) and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR, Bruker 400MHZ) spectroscopy were carried out to characterize the solvation structure of various electrolytes. The electrode-electrolyte interphases were analyzed by cold field emission transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEOL JEM F200). The micromorphology of cycled lithium metal anodes was characterized by using field emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7800F). The surface compositions of cycled electrodes were analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha). The sulfur content of SPAN was detected using organic element analyzer (EA, Elementar Unicube). UV-vis absorption spectra (SHIMADZU UV-3600i Plus, Japan) were conducted to investigate the polysulfide dissolution and migration behavior of different electrolytes. The cycled separators were immersed in DME solvent, and the extracted solutions were subjected to UV-vis analysis. 
Computational methods: The molecular structures of DME, FSI, and PY13 were optimized using the MM2 method in ChemBio3D software. Subsequently, geometry optimizations were performed at the B3LYP/6-31g(d) level using Gaussian09, followed by the calculation of 1.2 × CM5 charges with the Multwfn program [1]. Corresponding topology files for molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were generated using LigParGen [2]. MD simulations were carried out with Gromacs2022.4 [3] employing the GAFF2 force field. A cubic simulation box with a side length of 6 nm × 6 nm × 6 nm was constructed. System 1 (2 M LiFSI in DME) contained 260 LiFSI and 938 DME molecules; System 2 (2 M LiFSI in DME-PY13FSI (7:3 vol%)) contained 260 LiFSI, 678 DME, and 170 PY13FSI molecules; System 3 (2 M LiFSI in DME-PY13FSI (5:5 vol%)) contained 260 LiFSI, 542 DME, and 283 PY13FSI molecules; System 4 (2 M LiFSI in DME-PY13FSI (3:7 vol%)) contained 260 LiFSI, 270 DME, and 352 PY13FSI molecules. Energy minimization was performed using the steepest descent algorithm to eliminate unfavorable contacts and resolve steric clashes. After energy minimization, each system was subjected to 500 ps of NPT equilibration, followed by a 50 ns production NPT simulation. Temperature was controlled using the V-rescale algorithm [4], and pressure was maintained at 1 bar using the Parrinello–Rahman barostat [5] with a simulation time step of 1 fs. The temperature was set to 298.15 K. Electrostatic interactions were treated with the particle mesh Ewald (PME) method [6], and both Coulombic and van der Waals interactions were truncated at 10 Å. Hydrogen bonds were constrained using the LINCS algorithm. Analysis of MD trajectories was primarily conducted using built-in tools in Gromacs, while visualization and intermolecular interaction analyses were performed with VMD 1.9.3 [7].
