Promoted rate and cycling capability of Li-S batteries enabled by targeted selection of co-solvent for the electrolyte


Experimental Section
DFT calculations: The adsorption energy between different solvents and Lithium polysulfides were investigated via the first-principles total energy program Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package (CASTEP) [1, 2] within the framework of density functional theory. The plane-wave ultra-soft pseudopotential method was used to describe the interactions between ionic core and valence electrons[3]. Electron exchange and correlation were treated within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerh (PBE) function [4]. In the present calculations, the plane wave cut-off energy was chosen to be 440 eV. Brillouin zones were sampled by a Gamme point. The Pulay scheme of density mixing was applied for the evaluation of energy [5]. The Brodyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) minimization scheme was employed for geometry optimization to complete the relaxation of atoms [5]. The calculation of total energy and electronic structure were followed by cell optimization with self-consistent field (SCF) tolerance of 1.0×10-5 eV atom-1. The maximum ionic displacement was set at 5.0×10-4 Å. A convergence criterion of 0.001 eV Å-1 was used for the maximum ionic Hellmann-Feynman force. For the adsorption, the van der Waals interaction was taken into account using the semi-empirical DFT-D2 approach [6]. 
Materials: Sulfur powder (purity ≥ 99.98%), lithium sulfide powder (Li2S, purity ≥ 99.9% ), Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, purity ≥ 99.9%), Dimethylacetamide (DMAC, purity ≥ 99.8%) and sulfolane (SL, purity ≥ 99.9%) were ordered from Aldrich and used as received. N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), dimethoxyethane (DME), dimethyl carbonate (DMC), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and lithium nitrate (LiNO3) (all in battery-grade purity) were purchased from Suzhou Fosai New Material Co., Ltd. 
Preparation of Sulfur Cathode: Sulfur/carbon black composite (S/C composite, 70% sulfur, 30 wt.% Ketjenblack) was prepared by a melt-diffusion approach at 150℃ for 6 hours. Subsequently, 85 wt.% S/C composite, 5 wt.% Super P carbon and 10 wt.% poly (vinylidene fluoride) were ball-milled for 4 hours. Butanone was used as solvent and N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP) was used as dispersant to obtain homogeneous slurry. The slurry was cast onto aluminum foil and dried at 55℃ under vacuum for 12 hours. As prepared sulfur cathode was cut into disks with a diameter of 12 mm.
Preparation of Electrolytes: 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.2 M LiNO3 were added to the DOL/DME (1:1, in volume ratio) mixture and stirred in the argon-filled glove box to obtain the conventional electrolyte. Modified electrolytes were prepared in the same condition, by partly replacing the solvent mixture with 6% vol. and of 10% vol. sulfolane, respectively. 
Measurements of Electrolytes: Viscosity of electrolytes was conducted with Ubbelohde viscosity meter under strictly controlled temperature, 29.5±0.02℃. The ionic conductivity of the electrolytes was measured using a conductivity meter (Lei-ci, DDS-307) at 25℃.
Measurement of Li2S Solubility: Li2S reacts with AgNO3 and forms AgS2 precipitation as the following equation: 
Li2S+2AgNO3 = 2LiNO3+Ag2S↓

Solvents, DME, DOL, DMC, DMSO and SL, were chosen to measure the solubility of Li2S. Excessive Li2S powder was firstly dissolved in a centrifuge tube and stirred for 24 h. The tube was sealed and centrifuged for 30 minutes with a speed of 500 rpm to separate residual solid Li2S (916 Ti-Touch, Metrohm). The supernatant liquid was drawn for precipitation potentiometric titration using an Ag electrode and a reference electrode. Titrant was prepared by dissolving silver nitrate in high purity DMSO. Concentration of silver nitrate in the titrant was calibrated by titration with sodium chloride standard solution (0.1 M). All operations were performed in an argon-filled glove box.
Electrochemical Measurements: The electrochemical properties of Li-S batteries were evaluated in coin cells. The cell was assembled with as-prepared sulfur cathode, Celgard 2400 and Li foil in an argon-filled glove box (H2O < 1 ppm, O2 < 1 ppm). The volume of electrolyte in each cell was limited to 20 μL. The cells were cycled on a LAND CT2001A battery testing system with a voltage range between 1.7 V and 2.8 V. The symmetric cells with different electrolytes were assembled in glove box and operated at current density of 1.0 mA cm-2 and 2.0 mA cm-2. Autolab Electrochemical Workstation (AUT71864) was operated to obtain cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves with different scan rates and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The EIS measurement was performed over a frequency range of 0.1 Hz to 1 MHz.
Characterizations: The cycled cells were disassembled in argon-filled glove box to retrieve the S cathodes and cycled Li anodes for scanning electron microscopy (SEM, HITACHI S4800) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, ThermoFisher VG Scientific) measurements. The cathodes were carefully washed with DME solvent, then dried for 12 h to remove residues. The cycled Li anodes were washed with DME to remove the impurity. Subsequently, the S cathodes and Li anode were sealed in an argon-filled container and transferred from the glove box to the chamber of SEM or XPS. SEM images were obtained at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV. XPS spectra were collected with an Al-Ka radiation (72W, 12 kV) at a pressure of 10-9 Torr. The diameter of the analyzed area was 500 μm.
