Experimental Section 
The electrolytes studied included 1.0 M LiTFSI in the following solutions: 1:1 v/v DOL:TTE, 1:1 v/v 
DOL:TFETFE, 1:1 v/v DOL:TFEPE, 1:1 v/v DOL:TFEiBE, and 1:1 v/v DOL:BTFE. The electrolyte 
solvents DOL, BTFE (Sigma-Aldrich, 98%), TTE, TFETFE, TFEPE and TFEiBE (Fluoryx Labs, 99%) 
were refluxed over calcium hydride and purified by fractional distillation before use. Karl-Fischer titration 
indicated that the water content of all solvents was under 20 ppm. The LiTFSI salt (Sigma-Aldrich, 99.95 
%) was dried in a vacuum oven (80 oC) overnight in an Ar-filled glovebox before use. Toluene and benzene-
d6 (Sigma-Aldrich, anhydrous) were used as-received. All samples were prepared in an argon atmosphere 
glovebox (<1 ppm of O2 and H2O) by mixing the selected molar ratio of each solvent, or by adding an 
appropriate molar ratio of each solvent to the LiTFSI salt in a vial with stirring until a homogeneous solution 
was obtained. 
The sulfur cathode was prepared by mixing sulfur (99.8%, analytical grade, Sigma-Aldrich), carbon 
black, and PVDF binder (5 wt% in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone) with weight ratios of 50%, 40%, and 10%, 
respectively, and the slurry was then coated onto an aluminum foil current collector. N-Methyl-2-
pyrrolidone (NMP) was used to dissolve the PVDF binder and adjust the slurry viscosity. The coated 
electrodes were dried at 60 oC under vacuum for 12 h and punched into discs with an area of 1.54 cm2 and 
a sulfur loading of about 2 mg cm-2. 
2032 coin cells were assembled with the above sulfur electrode as the cathode, a lithium metal chip as 
the anode, and Celgard 2325 as the separator. The electrolyte volume was 50 μL. The cells were assembled 
in an argon atmosphere glovebox (<1 ppm of O2 and H2O) and cycled at a rate of C/10 on a Maccor cycler 
over a voltage range of 1.5 – 2.8 V.

Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) analyses were carried out on a 300 MHz NMR spectrometer. 
Deuterated benzene was placed in an external coaxial insert and then in the NMR tube with the samples. 
The 1H chemical shifts were referenced to benzene-d6 at 7.16 ppm. All NMR experiments were performed 
on a 300 MHz spectrometer equipped with a z-axis gradient amplifier and an ATMA BBO probe with a z-
axis gradient coil. The maximum gradient strength was 0.214 T/m. Both 1H-DOSY and 7Li-DOSY were 
performed by using the standard dstebpgp3s program, employing a double stimulated echo sequence, 
bipolar gradient pulses for diffusion, and three spoil gradients. The diffusion time was 200-400 ms, and the 
rectangular gradient pulse duration was 500-3000 μs. Gradient recovery delays were 200 μs. Individual 
rows of the quasi-2-D diffusion databases were phased and baseline corrected. Actual diffusion coefficients 
used for the D-α analysis were obtained with commercial software using the T1/T2 analysis module. As 
representative examples, the 1H-DOSY attenuation curves for toluene, DOL, and BTFE in 4:1 DOL:BTFE 
and 1:4:1 LiTFSI:DOL:BTFE electrolyte, as well as the 7Li-DOSY attenuation curve for lithium in 1:4:1 
LiTFSI:DOL:BTFE electrolyte, are shown in Figures S4-S6. 
 
 
Theoretical Deductions of Linear Free-Energy Relationship 
The shuttle factor f is defined in eq. S1, where ks is the reaction constant related to LiPS shuttling 
reaction, QH is the specific capacity of the high plateau for the capacity curve during charge or discharge, 
[Stotal] is the total sulfur concentration, and I is the applied current.S1,S2 By assuming that sulfur is fully 
converted and each sulfur atom can accept or donate 0.5 and 1 electron, respectively, for the high and low 
plateau,S1 empirical eqs. S2 and S3 can be deduced.S3 QCH represents the charge capacity for the high 
plateau, and QCL indicates the charge capacity for the low plateau. Eq. S4 can be deduced from eq. S2 and 
S3. By comparing the shuttle factors of various S/Li cells at a fixed charge capacity with the same charging 
current, [2f-ln(1-f)] is proportional to the reaction constant ks, and thus, log[2f-ln(1-f)] is related to the 
activation energy of the LiPS shuttling reaction.

capacity (QDL) are correlated with the shuttle factor as in eqs. S5 and S6. Therefore, [2f + ln(1 + f)] is

discharge capacity with the same discharge current (eq. S7). As a result, log[2f + ln(1 + f)] is related to the 
activation energy of the LiPS shuttling reaction during the discharge process.
