Nonflammable Cocktail Electrolyte for Stable Li||SPAN Batteries
Yan Li, Han Zhou, Kai Huang, Xiaoyu Jin, Chaofan Liang, Ji Wu, Zhou Fang, Xiaoqun Qi, Ruining Jiang, and Long Qie*
State Key Laboratory of Material Processing and Die & Mould Technology, School of Materials Science and Engineering, Huazhong University of Science and Technology Wuhan, Hubei, 430074, China
E-mail: qie@hust.edu.cn

Experimental section
Materials: Sulfolane (SL, 99%), 1,1,2,2-tetra-fluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (TTE, 98%, RG) were purchased from Adamas. Triethyl phosphate (TEP, 99%, RG), fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC, 99%, RG) were purchased from Macklin. Lithium bis(trifluoromethane sulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, 99.9%, water content <50 ppm) and lithium nitrate (LiNO3, 99.9%) were obtained from DodoChem. Lithium foils were purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. The sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) was synthesized following previously reported procedures with a little modification.[1, 2] In details, commercial sulfur (99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich) and polyacrylonitrile (Sigma-Aldrich, Mw = 150,000) were mixed at a 3 : 1 weight ratio, pre-annealed at 80  °C for 1 hour, and subsequently pyrolyzed at 350  °C for 3.5 hours under argon flow with a ramping rate of 2 °C min−1. 

Preparation of electrodes: The SPAN cathodes were prepared by mixing SPAN powder, ketjen black (KB, RG grade, Adamas), carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC, 4%, Adamas), and styrene butadiene rubber (SBR, Zeon Corporation) at a weight ratio of 8 : 1 : 0.5 : 0.5 in deionized water. The slurry was bladed onto carbon-coated aluminum foil, followed by drying for 12 h at 60 ℃ in the vacuum oven. Then the electrodes were cut into 8 mm discs for coin cells, and 40 mm × 30 mm rectangles for pouch cells. If not specifically marked, the mass loadings of SPAN electrodes for coin cells and pouch cells are 1−1.5 mg cm−2.

Preparation of electrolyte: SL was first liquefied at 35  °C. D-STFE was prepared by dissolving 0.25 M LiTFSI and 0.2 M LiNO3 in solvent mixture (STFE) of SL : TEP : FEC : TTE = 1.00 : 0.45 : 0.26 : 1.26 by molar. Control electrolytes included T-STFE (0.45 M LiTFSI in STFE) and N-STFE (0.45 M LiNO3 in STFE). All electrolytes were prepared and handled in an argon-filled glovebox. The EC/DEC reference electrolyte (1 M lithium hexafluorophosphate in vinyl carbonate/diethyl carbonate = 1 : 1 v/v) was obtained from DodoChem. An adequate amount of molecular sieves (4 Å) was added to each electrolyte to reduce the water content.

Assembly of the cells: For coin cells, a 450-μm Li chip was utilized as the anode and Celgard 2730 was used as the separator. The 2025-type coin cells were assembled with 30 μL electrolyte was added to each cell. The pouch cells were fabricated with 50-μm Li foil anodes (40 mm × 30 mm). Noting that the Li anodes for pouch cells were pre-immersed in FEC solution for 24 h. Both coin cells and pouch cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box. 

Characterizations: 
Contact angle measurement: Contact angles between the electrolytes and the separator were measured using an FTA-200 analyzer. First, the separator was placed on the platform, followed by dropping 2−5 μL of electrolyte using a micro syringe. After droplet stabilization, the contact angle was measured by analyzing the droplet contour through software fitting.
Raman spectroscopy: The electrolytes were sealed in a capillary tube with hot melt adhesive and then subjected to Raman test. Sample preparation was carried out in a glove box with Ar atmosphere. The Raman spectra were acquired using a LabRAM HR800 spectrometer (Horiba Jobin Yvon) with a 532 nm laser (5 mW power). The spectral resolution was optimized to ≤1 cm⁻¹ using an 1800 g mm⁻¹ grating and a 50 μm slit width to ensure accurate peak discrimination for chemical species identification. 
Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy: 7Li-NMR spectra were acquired with a Bruker Avance III 400 MHz spectrometer, using the deuterated solvent of Chloroform-d (CDCl₃, 99.8%, Sigma-Aldrich). The coaxial tube (Wilmad 528-PP-7 outer tube filled with Chloroform-d for locking, and an inner tube containing the electrolyte) was used to ensure the uniformity of the magnetic field. The sample rotates at a frequency of 10 Hz to optimize the uniformity of the magnetic field.
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS): The cycled electrodes were collected and sealed with an Ar atmosphere for test. The XPS spectra were performed on a Thermo Scientific ESCALAB Xi+, with the chamber pressure of 10⁻⁸ ~ 10⁻⁹ torr.
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM): SEM (Hitachi SU8000) was performed to observe the morphologies of deposited Li on Cu in Li||Cu cells with different electrolytes. The Li||Cu cells firstly cycled 10 times with a current density of 0.5 mAh cm−2 and a capacity of 0.5 mA cm−2, and then depositing 3 mAh cm−2 Li on Cu foil for SEM. 
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM): The cycled electrodes were collected and dissolved in triethyl phosphate (TEP) under an Ar atmosphere. The mixture was then sealed and ultrasonicated for 5 minutes to obtain a homogeneous suspension suitable for TEM analysis. TEM was performed using a Tecnai G2 F30 microscope operating at 200 kV. Samples were drop-cast onto carbon-coated copper grids and allowed to dry prior to imaging. 
Ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) spectroscopy: UV-vis spectroscopy was performed to evaluate the formation of elemental sulfur during cycling. Firstly, the Li||SPAN cell was cycled at 0.2 A g⁻1 for 30 cycles. After cycling, the SPAN electrodes (10 mg) were immersed in 600 μL of carbon disulfide (CS₂) solvent for 1 h to extract elemental sulfur. The resulting solutions were then analyzed using a UV-vis spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-260) with a quartz cuvette (1 cm path length). The wavelength range was set from 200 to 360 nm, and the baseline was corrected using pure CS₂ as a reference.
Water content test: The water content of the sample was determined by the Karl Fischer titration (Japan-KEM-MKC-610) with the coulometric method. The system employed a pyridine-free Karl Fischer electrolyte (Hydranal Coulomat AG, containing sulfur dioxide (SO₂), imidazole as the base, and iodine (I₂) in methanol/aqueous buffer solution). The D-STFE was selected as the representative to test the water content because it contains all the reagents used for preparing the T-STFE and N-STFE electrolytes. First, Karl Fischer reagent was added to the electrolytic cell. Then, the sample was injected. The moisture content (ppm) was displayed in the instrument automatically. 

Electrochemical measurements: CR2025 coin cells were assembled in an argon glovebox with 30 μL of electrolyte per cell. Galvanostatic charge–discharge tests were conducted using a NEWARE CT-4008T battery testing system. Li||Cu cells were cycled at 0.5 mA cm⁻² with a deposition capacity of 0.5 mAh cm⁻² per cycle. An electrochemical workstation (Biologic) was used to conduct electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The stainless steel (SS)||electrolyte-soaked separator||SS symmetric cells were assembled for EIS test. The test frequency of EIS was 200 kHz−100 mHz.

The theoretical calculation: Molecular dynamics simulations were carried out using the GROMACS 2021.4 simulation package with the general amber force field (GAFF). RESP2(0.5) partial charges are used for the species involved in this study, which are derived with the help of the Multiwfn program by first optimizing the molecular geometry at the B3LYP/6-31G* level of theory by Gaussian 16, and then fitting to electrostatic potential calculated at the B3LYP/6-311G** level of theory. For the electrolyte, molecules were mixed into a 10 × 10 × 10 nm3 box with three-dimensional periodic boundary conditions. Van der Waals interactions are described by the Lennard Jones (LJ) potential with a cutoff of 1.2 nm, while electrostatic interactions are calculated with the particle-mesh Ewald method with a grid spacing of 1.2 nm. To obtain the reasonable initial configuration, energy minimization is first carried out using integrated protocol with the steepest descent method. The system is subsequently annealed from 0 to 500 K and from 500 K to 298.15 K throughout 0.5 ns, followed by running for another 1.0 ns to reach equilibrium. A last 10 ns NPT run is performed to obtain stable productions. VMD software is used for visualizing the snapshots and analyzing the results.


Figure S1. The cycling performance of Li||SPAN cells with the electrolytes of 0.25 M LiTFSI in (a) SL and (b) SE.

The initial electrolyte formulation was 0.25 M LiTFSI in SL. However, due to the high viscosity of SL, the battery failed to cycle (Figure S1a). To address this, we introduced TTE as diluent to reduce the electrolyte concentration, resulting in a modified formulation: 0.25 M LiTFSI in SL : TTE (1.00 : 0.63 by molar). The mixed solvent of SL and TTE with a molar ratio of 1.00 : 0.63 is denoted as SE. Despite this adjustment, the battery capacity continued to decay, retaining only 65% of its initial capacity after 150 cycles (Figure S1b).


Figure S2. The optical photos of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in (a) SE and (b) STE solvents.

0.2 M LiNO3 was added to the electrolyte to stabilize the Li-metal anode, resulting in a modified formulation: 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in SE. However, the electrolyte turned turbid due to incomplete dissolution of LiNO3 (Figure S2a). To address this, we introduced TEP—a solvent with high LiNO3 solubility—yielding a clear electrolyte with the composition of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in SL : TEP : TTE of a molar ratio of 1.00 : 0.56 : 0.63. The solvent of SL : TEP : TTE = 1.00 : 0.56 : 0.63 by molar is denoted as STE. Figure S2b shows that the solution of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in STE appeared optically transparent after this adjustment.


Figure S3. The digital photo of 0.45 M LiNO3 in STFE.

Figure S3 shows the STFE solution containing 0.45 M LiNO3 remains transparent.


Figure S4. The cycling performance of Li||SPAN cell with the electrolyte of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in STE.

The Li||SPAN cell employing the electrolyte of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in STE exhibits a capacity retention of 78% after 150 cycles (Figure S4). However, the cycling performance remains unsatisfactory.


Figure S5. The cycling performance of Li||SPAN cells with the electrolytes of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in STFE with different content of TEP and FEC. 

Building upon the electrolyte formulation of 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in STE, we introduced a film-forming additive FEC to enhance the cycle stability of the Li||SPAN cells, resulting in a modified solvent system of SL + TEP + FEC + TTE (that is STFE). As shown in Figure S5, substituting part of TEP with FEC effectively improved the cycling stability of the cells. However, considering that increased FEC proportion compromises the overall nonflammability of the electrolyte, we ultimately optimized the formulation to 0.25 M LiTFSI + 0.2 M LiNO3 in SL : TEP : FEC : TTE (1 : 0.45 : 0.26 : 1.26 by molar).

Figure S11. The cross-section morphologies of the plating Li on Cu electrode of Li||Cu cells in (a) D-STFE and (b) T-STFE electrolyte with a plating capacity of 8 mAh cm⁻2. 

Cross-sectional analysis reveals a denser Li deposition on the Cu electrode in D-STFE electrolyte versus in T-STFE electrolyte (Figure S11). With the same plating capacity of 8 mAh cm⁻2, the thickness of plating Li on the Cu electrode of Li||Cu cell in D-STFE electrolyte is 40.5 μm, which is thinner than 44.3 μm of the plating Li with T-STFE electrolyte. Additionally, the plating Li exhibits a uniformly smooth and dense cross-section morphology in D-STFE electrolyte while plating Li shows an uneven and loose sedimentary in T-STFE electrolyte, indicating the SEI formed in D-STFE electrolyte effectively contributes to uniform Li plating. 


Figure S12. Optical photo of the transparent battery device.

To further investigate the distinct lithium deposition behaviors in the D-STFE and T-STFE electrolytes, symmetric Li||Li transparent cells were constructed (Figure S12). This was achieved by attaching lithium metal foil to copper current collectors to form two electrodes, placing these electrodes on opposite sides of a cuvette, and sealing the assembly.
