Supplementary Note 2: Preparation and Structural Characterization 
First, Ni(OH)2 nanoparticles were synthesized using a conventional hydrothermal method. 7.11 
g NiCl2ꞏ6H2O and 10 g urea were dissolved in 300 mL deionized water. The solution was 
vigorously stirred and heated at 90 ℃ for 3 h. After washing and filtration, the Ni(OH)2 product 
was obtained. Then, after heat treatment at 300 ℃ for 3 h in Ar gas in the tube furnace, the Ni(OH)2 
was converted into NiO. Finally, the NiO was converted into Ni-anions with various non-metal 
anions (NiS2, and NiSe2) via gas-solid reactions in a tube furnace with sulfur, and selenium as the 
conversion sources.  
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was employed to study the structural phase of the NiO, NiS2, and 
NiSe2, displayed in Supplementary Fig. 15. The diffraction peaks can be well assigned to NiO, 
NiS2, and NiSe2, respectively, indicating the successful preparation of Ni-based compounds with 
various anions. As illustrated in field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) images 
(Supplementary Fig. 16a), the original Ni(OH)2 had a three-dimensional (3D) structure with 
secondary nanosheets. After heat treatment, NiO remains irregular and smooth two-dimensional 
(2D) nanosheets (Supplementary Fig. 16b). NiS2 and NiSe2 show particle morphology with a 
diameter of 100-200 nm (Supplementary Fig. 16c, d). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) 
analysis indicates NiSe2 has a particle size of 200 nm (Supplementary Fig. 17a). High-resolution 
TEM images (Supplementary Fig. 17b) of NiSe2 indicate a 0.262 nm lattice spacing associated 
with the (111) face of NiSe2. An inverse fast-Fourier-transform image (Supplementary Fig. 17c) 
also indicates a defined crystal structure for NiSe2 rather than the disordered structure. The well-
defined diffraction rings of the selected area electron diffraction pattern (Supplementary Fig. 17d) 
of NiSe2 indicated poly-crystallinity. Furthermore, mapping images and energy-dispersive spectra 
in Supplementary Fig. 17e confirmed uniform distributed elements throughout the structure (see 
other materials in Supplementary Figs. 18 and 19). N2 adsorption-desorption measurements were 
conducted to measure the specific surface area, showing the catalytic materials present a similarly 
low specific area (Supplementary Fig. 20). 
Chemical composition results from an inductively coupled plasma indicate a 1:2.02 ratio (Ni: 
Se) for NiSe2 (Supplementary Table 4), similar to the expected stoichiometry (1:2), which 
indicates saturated coordination of Se to the Ni center without Se vacancies. Hence, the 
corresponding compound is NiSe2 rather than NiSe2-x. High-resolution Ni 2p X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS) of NiSe2 is deconvoluted into three couples of doublet peaks (Supplementary

strong peaks at about 162.6 and 164.1 eV are attributed as the S 2p3/2 and S 2p1/2. The peak at about 
169.0 eV could be recognized as a S-O bond due to surface oxidization.
