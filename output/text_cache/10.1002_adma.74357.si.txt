Section 1. Methods
Note S1. Materials preparation 
A mixture of pyrrolidine (2.84 g) and potassium carbonate (6.08 g) in acetonitrile (40 mL) was stirred for 10 minutes. Bromoethane (10.9 g) was then added, and the reaction was stirred vigorously at 55 °C under reflux for 12 hours. Upon completion, the mixture was filtered, and the filtrate was concentrated under reduced pressure. The resulting residue was washed with hexane (5 ×) to yield a white solid, which was dried at 80 °C for 12 hours. This white solid and an equimolar amount of LiTFSI were simultaneously added to a mixed solvent of acetonitrile/water (1:4 v/v, 20 mL) and stirred at 30 °C for 6 hours. Water was added in excess, and the product was extracted with dichloromethane. The organic layer was collected, concentrated under reduced pressure, and dried at 80 °C for 12 hours to afford the final product Pn-S. The electrolyte E-Pn-S was prepared by dissolving 1 M LiTFSI in a blend of Pn-S, DME, and DOL (volume ratio of 1:2:2, containing 1 wt% LiNO₃). Electrolytes E-Pn-A (Pn-A: 1-Butyl-1-methylpyrrolidinium Bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide, E-Pn-B (Pn-B: trimethylpropylammoniumbis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide) and E-Pn-C (Pn-C: 1-Butyl-1-methylpiperidinium Bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide) were synthesized analogously from their respective precursors (Pn-A, Pn-B, Pn-C). A conventional electrolyte of 1 M LiTFSI in DOL:DME (1:1 v/v) with 1 wt% LiNO₃ served as the blank control.

Note S2. Electrochemical measurement
Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder, carbon-sulfur composite material, and graphene were mixed in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP) solvent in a mass ratio of 8:1:1, and ground uniformly to obtain the cathode slurry. The sulfur loading of the button cell is 1 mg cm-2 to 1.5 mg cm-2. The electrolytes used were E-Pn-S, E-Pn-A, E-Pn-B, and blank electrolyte E; the separator used was Celgard 2400; and the anode used was lithium foil. Two ratios of electrolyte to sulfur were adopted, approximately 2 mL g-1 and 5 mL g-1, corresponding to which the sulfur loading ranges from 2.5 mg cm-2 to 4.5 mg cm-2. Cyclic Voltammetry (CV) and Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) tests were performed using a Bio-Logic VMP-3 multi-channel potentiostat. The CV test voltage window was set at 1.5-3 V, and the EIS test frequency range was 10 mHz to 100 kHz. The procedure of the in-situ EIS test was as follows: The cells were discharged to 2.5 V, 2.3 V, 2.2 V, 2.1 V, 2.0 V, 1.9 V, 1.8 V, and 1.7 V respectively. After each discharge to the corresponding voltage, the EIS test was performed. Upon completion, the discharge was continued to the next voltage point and the test was repeated. Based on the CV curves at different scan rates, the lithium ion diffusion coefficient was calculated using the Randles-Sevcik equation (I =26900×n1.5×A×D0.5×C×v0.5). Cycling performance tests were performed using a LAND CT2001A battery test system, with a voltage range of 1.7-2.8 V and under galvanostatic charge-discharge conditions. Sulfur and lithium sulfide (Li₂S) were mixed in a molar ratio of 7:1 and added to E-Pn-S, E-Pn-A, E-Pn-B, and blank electrolyte E respectively, followed by stirring at 80 °C for 36 hours. The procedure of the Li₂S deposition test was as follows: The battery was first discharged galvanostatically at 0.112 mA to 2.09 V, then discharged potentiostatically at 2.08 V until the current gradually decreased to 0.01 mA. The ionic conductivity of the electrolyte was obtained by performing EIS tests on stainless steel symmetric cells. For the Li||Li cells (type CR2032), the reference electrode was lithium metal, and the working electrodes were copper foil and lithium foil respectively. Each coin cell was filled with 40 microliters of electrolyte. The activation energy was obtained by performing EIS tests at temperatures of 30 °C, 35 °C, 40 °C, 45 °C, and 50 °C. The cells were cycled to 100 cycles, 200 cycles, 300 cycles, 400 cycles, 500 cycles, and 600 cycles respectively at 1 C, and the EIS at different cycle numbers was recorded. The batteries and test clips were placed in a -40°C refrigerator to test the low-temperature cycling data of the batteries.


Note S3. Fabrication of pouch cell 
The overall electrochemical performance of E-Pn-S-based lithium-sulfur batteries was evaluated with Ah-grade pouch cells as the test system. In the glove box, the double-sided coated positive plates (with carbon-coated aluminium foil as the collector), double-sided lithium-copper composite negative plates (with copper as the collector), and Celgard polypropylene diaphragms were stacked layer-by-layer to assemble the cell; nickel lugs were used as the negative lugs and aluminium lugs as the positive lugs. The side with the electrode lugs was first heat-sealed at 200 °C, followed by sequential heat-sealing of the other two sides, with the last side left unsealed; electrolyte injection and vacuum pumping were carried out on the unsealed side, after which the final heat-sealing was performed. The dosage of the electrolyte was determined based on the mass of sulfur, with the mass ratio of electrolyte to sulfur (E/S) set to 1.8~2.0. The resulting pouch cells were subjected to electrochemical performance tests after undergoing a standing treatment.

Note S4. Materials characterization
The Raman spectra of the electrolyte were tested and recorded using a Renishaw inVia confocal micro-Raman spectrometer. Laser wavelength: 532 nm. In situ electrochemical Raman spectroscopy measurements were performed using a HORIBA HR Evolution Raman spectrometer, with an excitation laser wavelength of 1064 nm, a laser power of 50 mW, a grating groove density of 600 gr/mm, a pinhole size (Hole) of 400 μm, an acquisition time of 60 s and a spatial resolution of 1 μm. NMR experiments were acquired on a Bruker AVANCE Ascend 600 MHz liquid nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectrometer. To avoid interfering with the solution structure, no deuterated reagents were added to the solution samples. After all the raw materials were mixed and dissolved in the prescribed ratio, the resulting solution was transferred into an NMR tube. A coaxial NMR tube containing deuterated DMSO was pre-inserted into it, and the whole assembly was then incubated at 70 °C until gelation was achieved. All nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) experiments were performed at a constant temperature of 25 °C. 2D Heteronuclear Overhauser Effect Spectroscopy (2D HOESY) can detect heteronuclear spatial Nuclear Overhauser Effect (NOE) correlations between non-bonded nuclei. The experiments were performed using the standard phase-sensitive pulse sequence from the spectrometer library (sequence ID: hoesyetgp.2), with the relaxation delay set to 1 s. A total of 8 transients were accumulated; the spectral width was 20 ppm for 1H nuclei and 30 ppm for 7Li nuclei. In addition, 128 data points were collected in the F1 dimension (indirect dimension), and 2048 data points in the F2 dimension (direct dimension). H-DOSY: ledbpgp2s pulse sequence; Scanning: 8 scans, gain: 18.0; Spectral width: ¹H dimension 11904.8 Hz (~19.8 ppm); Relaxation delay: 3.0 s; Acquisition time: 0.6881 s. Li-DOSY (for Li⁺ diffusion coefficient measurement): Pulse sequence – ledbpgp2s (standard DOSY sequence); Scanning: 8 scans, gain: 101; Observation frequency: 233.25 MHz; Pulse width: 12.0 µs; Relaxation delay: 3.0 s; Spectral width: 1315.8 Hz; Acquisition time: 2.5 s. The copper mesh was placed on the positive electrode, after which the battery was assembled and subjected to cycling tests. After the cycling test, the copper mesh was retrieved, and the structural and compositional analysis of Li2S was conducted via a JEM-F200 (CF-CR) cryogenic transmission electron microscope (cryo-TEM). The copper mesh was placed on the negative electrode, after which Li-Li symmetric batteries were assembled and subjected to cycling tests. After cycling, the copper mesh was retrieved, and the structural and compositional analysis of the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) was conducted via a JEM-F200 (CF-CR) cryogenic transmission electron microscope (cryo-TEM).

Note S5. Computational methods
Representative solvation structures were extracted from extensive atomistic simulations, and these solvation structures were adopted as starting configurations for additional DFT calculations. Geometry optimizations were first carried out using the Gaussian 16 software package1, employing the B3LYP hybrid functional with the 6-311+G(d,p) basis set and Grimme’s D3 dispersion correction with Becke-Johnson damping (GD3BJ). The optimized solvation structures were then subjected to single-point energy calculations using the M06-2X functional in combination with the def2-TZVP basis set. During these calculations, implicit solvation effects were described using the SMD (Solvation Model based on Density) continuum model, with solvent parameters corresponding to the corresponing solvent molecules used for the corresponding solvation structures. The desolvation energies of these soluvation structures were calculated from DFT calculations.

Note S6. Statistical analysis
The data were analyzed in their raw form, with no transformation or normalization applied, and outliers had been removed. Electrochemical measurements were taken from at least three independent replicates, and the curves presented in the paper are representative ones. No further tests of statistical significance were performed in this study; all conclusions are based on the stable and reproducible trends consistently observed across the repeated experiments. All data processing and analysis were carried out using Origin software.

Figure S12. The distances of each molecule from Li+ in the Li+ solvation structure obtained via molecular dynamics (MD) simulation.

Supplemental Experimental Procedures.
The changes in the solvation structure around Li+ in the E and E-Pn-S electrolytes before and after the addition of high-concentration polysulfides were derived from molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and the distances from each molecule to Li+ are labeled in the figure. It can be observed that at least two polysulfide anions are present in E-LS, which are also closer to Li+ than those in E-Pn-S-LS. This indicates that severe anion aggregation occurs in the E electrolyte after the addition of a high concentration of polysulfides, while E-Pn-S-LS effectively suppresses this phenomenon.

Supplemental Experimental Procedures.
The signal intensity in the 2D HOESY NMR spectrum corresponds to the distance between the hydrogen atoms (H) and lithium ions (Li+) at the corresponding position, where the higher the intensity, the shorter the distance. Fig. S10a shows the distances of the hydrogen atoms (H) corresponding to DOL and DME in the Li+ solvation structure of the E-Pn-B electrolyte from Li+, with the corresponding Fig. S10b presenting that for the E-Pn-C electrolyte. Fig. S10c shows the distances of the hydrogen atoms (H) corresponding to DOL and DME in the Li+ solvation structure of the E-Pn-B electrolyte from Li+ after the addition of a high concentration of polysulfides, with the corresponding Fig. S10d presenting the respective changes for the E-Pn-C electrolyte under the same condition. It can be observed that the distances from DOL and DME to Li+ in both types of electrolytes have increased significantly after the addition of a high concentration of polysulfides, which is caused by the inward extrusion of solvent molecules via the steric size effect induced by the massive aggregation of anions.
