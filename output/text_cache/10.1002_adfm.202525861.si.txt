Dynamic Reconstruction of Polysulfides to Elemental Sulfur Enabling High-Performance Ah-level Lithium-Sulfur Pouch Cells

Zulong Liu, Sheng Liu, Jun Chen, Xuemei Ren, Chi Zhang, Yu Jiao*, Shuchen Liu, Xiaodong Zhu*, Yichao Yan, Tianyu Lei, Dongjiang Chen, Yin Hu, and Wei Chen*
Experimental Section
Materials
The basic electrolyte was 0.4 M lithium bis(trifluoromethane sulfonimide) (LiTFSI) and 0.2 M lithium nitrate (LiNO3) in a solvent mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and 1, 2 dimethoxyethane (DME) (1:1 v/v) was purchased from DoDoChem Co., Ltd. 5-Fluoroisatoic anhydride (FIAn) was purchased from Aladdin (Shanghai) Chemistry Co., Ltd. Lithium metal for lithium sulfur pouch cells were purchased from Sichuan Chengwei Lithium Co., Ltd. or China energy lithium Co., Ltd. SBR (Styrene-Butadiene Rubber) and CMC (Sodium carboxymethyl cellulose) were purchased from Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd.
Cathode preparation
The preparation steps of carbon and sulfur cathodes are as follows: First, ECP600 and sublimated sulfur were homogeneously mixed in a mass ratio of 7:3. Next, the mixed C-S powder was poured into a stainless-steel reactor and vacuum sintered at 200 r/min for 12 hours at a temperature of 200°C, yielding a mixed C/S powder. The CMC, which accounts for 3% of the mass basis of ECP600, was added to the appropriate amount of water and stirred until it was a colorless, transparent, paste-like gel without particulate objects and prepared for use. After adding C-S powder and mixing for 30 mins, add SBR (40% solids), which accounts for 4% of the mass base of ECP600, and appropriate amount of water to adjust the viscosity of the slurry, and mix for 120 mins. It was then transferred to a carbon-coated aluminum foil after coating and transferred to a vacuum oven at 70°C for drying for 12 hours. After drying, slurry was applied to the other side of the carbon-coated aluminum foil and vacuum dried under the above conditions to obtain a double-sided electrode. The single side loading of the double-sided electrode was 2.2 mg cm−2.
Polysulfide preparation
Lithium sulfide and sublimated sulfur were mixed in a molar ratio of 1:7, 1:5, 1:3 respectively and added to the basic electrolyte, which was magnetically stirred for 24 hours at room temperature to obtain a Li2S8, Li2S6, Li2S4 solution. The whole procedure was done in a glove box.
Electrochemical Measurement
The N/P ratio adopted is 5.6, and the corresponding thickness of lithium metal anode is 100 μm for the lithium sulfur pouch cells. We used Celgard 2400 as a separator to assemble pouch cells for testing. The size of the electrode sheet was 40 mm × 50 mm (excluding the tab). The Ah-level pouch cells dimensions were 50 mm × 100 mm (excluding the tab), featuring double-sided coated electrodes with 8 layers and a total sulfur content of 2 g. Using the NEWRAE Battery Test System, performed constant current charge and discharge tests over a voltage range of 1.8-2.6 V. The EIS was tested on a CHI 660E electrochemical workstation in the frequency range of 0.1 Hz to 100k Hz.
Characterization
The UV-vis absorption spectra of polysulfides with FIAn were tested using a UV spectrophotometer (Shimadzu UV-2600i) in the wavelength range of 200-800 nm to determine the changes in polysulfides before and after reaction with FIAn. The homemade cuvette cell was assembled with a carbon cathode, lithium anode, and the supernatant obtained after the reaction between LiPS solution and FAIn as the electrolyte. The electrochemical testing protocol consisted of discharging from the open circuit voltage to 1.5 V, followed charging to 3.0 V, and finally discharging back to 1.5 V. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the precipitated products of the mixed solution of Li2S8 and FIAn were obtained using a Rigaku Smart Lab SE by scanning in the range of 10°~ 80° at the rate of 5° min−1. The recycled lithium-sulfur battery was disassembled to obtain the sulfur cathode and lithium anode, and then washed with DME solution to remove the residual lithium salt on the surface. A Hitachi S-4800 scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was utilized to test the electrode surface morphology. A Thermo Scientific K-Alpha X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to analyze the differences in chemical composition of the electrode surface, and the C-C peak at 284.8 eV in the C 1s spectrum was used for charge calibration. Raman spectroscopy (WITec alpha300R) was used to study the coordination of ions in the simulated electrolyte.
Theoretical Calculation
All our calculations were executed utilizing DFT theoretical calculations, specifically the B3LYP method, in conjunction with the 6-311g (d) basis set within the Gaussian16 software, ensured the utmost accuracy and precision in our findings.
