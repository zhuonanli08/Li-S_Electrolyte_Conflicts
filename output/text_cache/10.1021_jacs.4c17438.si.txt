Methods 
Material characterization. Sublimed sulfur (S), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), rosin(C19H29COOH) 
and carbon disulfide (CS2) were purchased from Aladdin (Shanghai, China); Lithium bromide 
(LiBr), Lithium sulfide (Li2S) were purchased from Macklin (Shanghai, China); 1,2-dimethyl 
ether (DME), 1,3-diamethoxyethane (DOL) and bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)imide lithium 
salt (LiTFSI) were purchased from Dodochem(Suzhou, China). All reagents and starting 
materials were used without further processing. 
Preparation of S loading cathode. The pyrrole was added to the 0.5 M ferric chloride (FeCl3) 
solution and stirred for 12 h to obtain polypyrrole. After drying, a certain mass of potassium 
hydroxide (KOH) (the mass ratio of polypyrrole to KOH was 1:4) was weighed and dissolved 
with distilled water, then added to the polypyrrole, and stirred for more than 30 min to make 
it to the slurry form. After drying in an oven, the above mixture was heated at 800 °C for 1 
hours with a nitrogen atmosphere to obtain porous carbon (PC). Then, the PC and S8 (S) were 
mixed and grinded at a mass ratio of 3:7, and then heated at 155 °C for 12 hours under vacuum 
to obtain the sulfur porous carbon composite (S-PC). Carbon fiber (10 wt%), S-PC powder (80 
wt%) and poly(vinylidene fluoride) (10 wt%) as a binder were dispersed in Nmethyl-2-
pyrrolidone (NMP) to form a slurry, and then coated onto an aluminum (Al) foil and dried 
using a coating machine with different coating thicknesses and dride at 60 °C for 24 h under 
vacuum. Finally, the cathodes were formed. 
Electrolyte and polysulfide preparation. 1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)imide 
(LiTFSI) and 0.3 M lithium nitrate (LiNO3) dissolved in 1,3-dioxetane (DOL)/ 1,2-
diamethoxyethane (DME) (v/v=1:1) mixed solvents was adopted as the blank electrolyte. 0.8, 
0.5 and 0.2M LiTFSI or 0.8 M LiTFSI/0.2 M LiBr and 0.3 M lithium nitrate (LiNO3) dissolved in 
1,3-dioxetane (DOL)/1,2-diamethoxyethane (DME) (v/v=1:1) mixed solvents were prepared. 
The mixtures of lithium sulfides (Li2S) and sulfur (S) with a molar ratio of 1:5 and 1:7 were 
added into DOL/DME mixed solvents. Then the powder was heated under an argon (Ar) 
atmosphere at 60 °C to prepare Li2S6 and Li2S8. 
Material characterizations. The ionic conductivity at different lithium salt fractions was 
tested by an ionic conductivity tester (SevenCompact conductivity meter S230). The dielectric 
constant test was carried out by a high and low frequency dielectric constant tester (GCSTD-

analysis was performed with a field emission scanning electron microanalyzer (Nova Nano 
SEM 450). Elemental mapping was conducted using EDX detector attached on JEOL 200F. 
Raman spectra were recorded on a MicroRaman system (LabRAM HR spectrometer, Horiba) 
with an Olympus BX microscope and an argon ion laser (532 nm). UV-visible absorption 
spectra were measured by using UV-visible absorption spectrophotometry (PE Lambda 950). 
Cell assembly and electrochemical measurements. In a glove box filled with argon (<0.1 
ppm of O2, <0.1 ppm of H2O), the Li-S battery (CR2032) was assembled using SC/S electrodes 
with different loading as the cathode, lithium foil as the counter electrode, and PP membrane 
(Celgard 2400) as separator, respectively.1.0 M LiTFSI、0.8 M LiTFSI and 0.8 M LiTFSI 0.2 M 
LiBr dissolved DOL/DME (1:1 v/v, 0.3 M LiNO3) electrolytes. The cathode diameter is 12 mm, 
Li metal diameter is 16 mm, lithium metal thickness is 450 μm. The E/S ratios were about 15, 
10, 5, and 3 µL mg-1 for the electrodes with sulfur loadings of 2.84, 4.5, 4.25, 6.21mg cm-2, 
respectively. The galvanostatic discharge/charge measurements were conducted using a CHI 
760E (LAND Electronic Co., Ltd) battery test system. The cathode specific capacities were 
normalized only by the mass of sulfur, as per common practice. The charge-discharge voltage 
range was 1.7-2.8 V (vs. Li/Li+) at room temperature (1 C=1675 mA g-1). Fabrication of Li | 
Carbon paper (CP) cells. Specifically, carbon paper was punched into circular disks with 
diameters of 12 mm as the cathode, and 10 µL of blank electrolyte was added into the anode 
side and 20 µL blank electrolyte containing 100 mmol L-1 of 1.0 M LiTFSI +Li2S8, or 0.8 M 
LiTFSI+Li2S8 as catholyte. Lithium foil as the reference/counter electrodes, PP membrane 
(Celgard 2400) as separator, in an Ar-filled glove box (<0.1 ppm of O2, <0.1 ppm of H2O), 
respectively. 
In-situ Raman spectroscopy. A Li-S cells with a perforated stainless steel positive case and a 
quartz window was used for in situ Raman spectroscopy monitor under a 532 nm laser. Each 
spectrum was acquired for 20 s with the frequency of 10%. The cells were tested at a current 
rate of 1.0 C (sulfur loading: 1.0 mg cm-2, 200 μL electrolyte).

The cutoff distance of vdW and electronic interactions was set to 1.0 nm. 
For each simulation, an energy minimization was firstly employed to relax the simulation 
box. Then, an isothermal-isobaric (NPT) ensemble with a 1 fs time step was employed to 
optimized the simulation box, where the temperature was set to 300 K and the pressure was 
set to 1.0 atm. The temperature and pressure were kept via the Nose-Hoover thermostat and 
Nose-Hoover barstat, respectively. The NPT optimization time was set to 0.4 ns, which was 
enough long to obtain a stable box size. Following the NPT simulation, a canonical (NVT) 
ensemble with 0.6 ns was performed to furtherly optimize the simulation box, the time step 
is set to 1 fs. In all the MD simulation, the motion of atoms was described by classical Newton’s 
equation, which was solved using the velocity-Verlet algorithm. All simulations were 
performed using the LAMMPS 11.  
The density functional theory (DFT) implementation in CP2K/Quickstep1 is used to study 
the system energy, adsorption energy and electrostatic potential. The geometries were 
optimized by Broyden-FletcherGoldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) minimizer. The gamma point is used 
for Brillouin zone sampling. Binding energies of the LiBr and Li2S4 were defined as the 
interaction between different molecule fragments. The Eb were calculated using the following 
Equation:  
Eb = Etotal − E1 − E2

The convergence criterion of self-consistent field loop based on orbital transformation (OT)

describe the exchange correlation effects, and the dispersion correction was applied in all 
calculations with the Grimme D3 method3.

spectra, the linear equation was obtained according to the corresponding molar 
quantity of rosin molecule (1657 cm-1) and the integrated peak area. The rosin 
solubility in water and ethanol is ~0 and ~1.5 M, respectively (Figure S4c). Optical 
photographs (Figure S4b) show that color of rosin/ethanol solution appears 
yellow. When the ratio of ethanol and water is higher than 7:3, it is always yellow. 
When the ratio changes to 5:5, the solution appears light yellow, indicating a sharp 
decrease of solubility. Raman spectra analysis indicates that the rosin solubility 
(characteristic peak 1657 cm-1) is 1.20, 1.02, 0.30, 0.13 and 0.12 M at the molar 
ratios of 8:2, 7:3, 5:5, 3:7 and 2:8, respectively (Figure 1e). The nonlinear solubility 
phenomenon exists between the ratio of 7:3 and 5:5 (Figure 1f). The dielectric 
constants of ethanol/water solution show a nearly linear change trend with ratio 
of solvents, also indicating the non relation of nonlinear solubility phenomena and 
dielectric constant (Figure 1f). It confirms the broad existance of nonlinear 
solubility phenomena.

Supplementary Note 3 The detail test methode of the sulfur solubility in different 
molar ratio of DOL/CS2 mixed solvents. 
Because CS2 is a toxic gas and extremely volatile, the sulfur solubility in different 
molar ratio of DOL/CS2 mixed solvents cannot be tested by Raman spectroscopy. 
Therefore, we added an excess of sulfur to CS2 and different molar ratio of 
DOL/CS2 mixed solvents and dissolved it fully, and then put it into a vacuum drying 
oven to evaporate the solvent completely and weighed the mass of sulfur so as to 
derive the sulfur solubility in different molar ratio of DOL/CS2 mixed solvents.
