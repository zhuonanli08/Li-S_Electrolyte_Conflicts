Methods Section 
 
Materials 
1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), dimethyl carbonate (DMC), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), ethylene 
carbonate (EC), ethyl methyl carbonate (EMC), fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC), lithium nitrate 
(LiNO3), trimethylsilyl azide (TMS-N3TMS-N3), lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF6), elemental 
sulfur, and polyacrylonitrile (PAN, MW=150 kDa) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. 1,1,2,2-
tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropylether (TTE) and lithium bis(ﬂuorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) 
were purchase from TCI America. All solvents for electrolyte preparation were dried over 
molecular sieve in the glove box for 24 h before use. 
 
Electrolyte Preparation and conductivity measurement 
The six electrolytes were prepared by dissolving a pre-determined amount of lithium salts into the 
mixed solvents. All solvents were dried over molecular sieve before use. Lithium salts were used 
as received without further purification. The formulation of different electrolytes is shown in Table 
S1. Detailed preparation for the electrolytes are shown below: 
(1) DMC/TTE 
DMC, EC, and TTE with molar ratio of 2:0.2:3 were stirred in a plastic bottle inside glove box for 
1 hr to form a homogenous solution. Afterwards, LiFSI (1.4 M) was added to the above solution 
and stirred overnight. The obtained electrolyte was stored in glove box with H2O content <1 ppm 
and O2 content <1 ppm before use. 
(2) DMC/TTE/TMS-N3 
DMC, EC, and TTE with molar ratio of 2:0.2:3 were stirred in a plastic bottle inside glove box for 
1 hr to form a homogenous solution. Afterwards, LiFSI (1.4 M) was added to the above solution 
and stirred overnight. Finally, TMS-N3 (2 wt% with respect to the mass of electrolyte) was added 
and the obtained solution was stirred for another 2 hours. The obtained electrolyte was stored in 
glove box with H2O content <1 ppm and O2 content <1 ppm before use. 
(3) EC/EMC/FEC 
Inside an Argon filled glove box, EC and EMC with mass ratio of 3:7 were premixed in a plastic 
bottle and stirred for 1hr to obtain a homogenous solution. Afterwards, LiPF6 (1 M) was added to 
the above solution and stirred for 12 hrs. Finally, FEC (8 wt% with respect to the mass of 
electrolyte) was added and the obtained solution was stirred for another 2 hours. The obtained 
electrolyte was stored in glove box with H2O content <1 ppm and O2 content <1 ppm before use. 
(4) DME/TTE 
LiFSI, DME, and TTE with molar ratio of 1:1.2:3 were stirred in a plastic bottle inside glove box 
for 12 hrs to form a homogenous solution. The obtained electrolyte was stored in glove box with 
H2O content <1 ppm and O2 content <1 ppm before use. 
(5) DME/TTE/TMS-N3 
LiFSI, DME, and TTE with molar ratio of 1:1.2:3 were stirred in a plastic bottle inside glove box 
for 12 hrs to form a homogenous solution. Afterward, TMS-N3 (2 wt% with respect to the mass

Conductivity measurement 
The conductivities of prepared electrolytes were measured by electrochemical impedance 
spectroscopy (EIS) under the cell configuration of SS//SS (SS refers to stainless steel). A 25um-
thick Celgard® separator was employed. As shown in Table S1, the conventional carbonate-based 
electrolyte 
(EC/EMC/FEC) 
and 
ether-based 
electrolyte 
(DOL/DME/LiNO3) 
showed 
conductivities of 0.81 mS/cm and 1.08 mS/cm, respectively. The LHCEs showed lower 
conductivity due to the introduction of TTE diluent, i.e. 0.64 mS/cm for DMC/TTE and 0.46 
mS/cm for DME/TTE electrolyte. After introducing TMS-N3 additive, the conductivity remains 
similar because the TMS-N3 cannot solvate Li+ and the addition amount is low (2 wt %). 
 
Optimization of the TMS-N3 additive ratio 
To find out the optimized ratio of TMS-N3 additive in the electrolyte, we first investigated the 
solubility of TMS-N3 in LHCE according to the mixing behaviors of pristine LHCE and TMS-N3 
additive. As shown in Figure S1a, the liquid state TMS-N3 and LHCE were well miscible, and a 
clear solution could be obtained when the TMS-N3 content increase from 0 to 10 wt.%. However, 
when further increasing the TMS-N3 content to 12.5 wt%, the solution became turbid and white 
precipitation of lithium salt could be observed at the bottom of the container. The solubility of 
TMS-N3 in LHCE was thus determined to be around 10 wt.%. 
Note that the solubility of LiFSI in TMS-N3 is lower than 1 wt.% (Figure S1b). In addition, 
TMS-N3 is miscible with both the solvating solvent (DMC) and diluent (TTE) in LHCE. Also 
considering its low content (2 wt.%), we consider TMS-N3 will not participate in the solvating 
process of LiFSI and will not affect the solvating structure of Li+. 
Thereafter, we further measured the Coulombic efficiency of Li//Cu cells in a series of LHCE 
electrolytes with varied TMS-N3 content (0 wt.%, 0.5 wt.%, 2 wt.%, 5 wt.%, and 10 wt.%), aiming 
to figure out the optimized TMS-N3 ratio. When using the PNNL protocol to evaluate the CE, the 
DMC-TTE electrolyte with 2 wt.% TMS-N3 delivered the CE of 96.6% and 99.4% in the 
activation cycle (Fig. S2a) and the initial test cycle (Fig. S2b), respectively, which is obviously 
superior to DMC-TTE electrolytes with other TMS-N3 contents (from 0 wt.%, 0.5 wt.%, 5 wt.%, 
to 10 wt.%). Aside from the highest CE, the Li//Cu cell using the DMC-TTE electrolyte with 2 
wt.% TMS-N3 also exhibited the lowest Li plating/stripping overpotential (Fig. S2c) among all 
tested cells. The possible reason is that a lower TMS-N3 content is unable to form the SEI layer 
with sufficient Li3N component that contributes to facilitating the ionic migration, while a higher 
TMS-N3 content will lead to thick SEI layer formation that increases the interfacial resistance and 
is unfavorable for the reversible Li metal plating/stripping. When adopting the 100% DOD Li//Cu

Sulfurized Polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) Synthesis and SPAN Cathode Preparation 
PAN (1 g) and elemental sulfur (4 g) were ball-milled under an air atmosphere for 4 hr to obtain 
a homogenous mixture. The above powder was then heated at 450 oC for 6 hours in a tube furnace 
at a temperature ramp rate of 2 oC/min and under Ar flow. After cooling to room temperature, the 
obtained SPAN powder was collected and stored in an Argon filled glove box before use. The S 
content in SPAN was determined to be 37.4 % by elemental analysis (Table S2). 
For electrode preparation, the SPAN powder was mixed with Super P, PVDF binder at a mass 
ratio of 6 : 2 : 2 followed by adding a proper amount of NMP solvent to form a slurry. The resulting 
slurry was then cast onto an Al foil and dried overnight at 60 oC under vacuum. The areal mass 
loading of active material is ~1 mg cm−2. The reported specific capacity was calculated based on 
the delivered capacity and the mass of active material. The theoretical capacity to calculate the C-
rate is 500 mAh g−1. The obtained SPAN cathode was cut into 12 mm-diameter discs and stored 
inside glove box before use. 
 
Battery Assembling and Electrochemical Tests 
2032-type coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box with O2 level <1 ppm and H2O 
level <1 ppm. 25 uL electrolyte was firstly added to the 450-um-thick lithium metal (Li0) anode. 
After placing the 25-um-thick Celgard PP separator onto Li0 anode, another 25 uL electrolyte was 
added onto the separator. The pre-weighted cathode was then placed onto the separator. The 
assembled Li0-SPAN batteries were rested for 10 hr before the electrochemical test. The cut-off 
voltages were 1 V for discharging and 3 V for charging. Two activation cycles at 0.1C was 
performed, and the charge-discharge curves are shown in Figure S33. 
Coulombic efficiencies (CE) were tested in a Li//Cu cell configuration under the PNNL 
protocol1. Firstly, 5 mAh cm−2 Li0 was deposited on Cu foil at 0.5 mA cm−2 and then fully stripped 
to ensure the complete passivation of the Cu surface (step 1). Afterward, another 5 mAh cm−2 Li0 
was deposited on Cu foil (step 2), and the battery was then cycled at 0.5 mA cm−2 with a capacity 
of 1 mAh cm−2 for 10 cycles (step 3), followed by stripping till 1 V at 0.5 mA cm−2 (step 4). The 
CE was calculated by the ratio between stripped Li0 and deposited Li0 according to the following 
equation: 
CE=(1 mAh cm−2×10+Q4)/(5 mAh cm−2+1 mAh cm−2×10) 
where Q4 is the amount of stripped Li0 in step 4 under the unit of mAh cm−2.
