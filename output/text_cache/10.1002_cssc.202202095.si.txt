S4 
 
Figure S4 represents the synthesis of the activated carbon (MPAC) and the related sulfur composites. 
The carbonization of the mango pits precursor and the subsequent activation through H3PO4 lead to 
a carbon material benefitting from disordered structure and a complex system of micropores that well 
accommodate sulfur. Indeed, simple mixing at 120 °C between carbon and sulfur (see Experimental 
Section in the Manuscript) allows synthesis of three composites where the active material is tuned to 
either 70% (S@MPAC-73), 80% (S@MPAC-82) or 90% (S@MPAC-91) in weight ratio with respect 
to the MPAC. 
  
 
 
Figure S4. Graphical representation of the MPAC synthesis and preparation of the S@MPAC-73, 
S@MPAC-82, and S@MPAC-91 composites. See Experimental Section for acronyms and synthesis 
details.

Figure S6 reports the EDS mapping performed on SEM images of S@MPAC-73 (Figure S6a, d), 
S@MPAC-82 (Figure S6b, e), and S@MPAC-91 (Figure S6c, f), while insets display the single C 
and S distribution. The figure evidences a very homogeneous distribution of the sulfur into the carbon 
matrix, with a magnitude increasing by increasing the sulfur content in the S@MPAC composite from 
70 wt. % (Figure S6d) to 80 wt. % (Figure S6e) and to 90 wt. % (Figure S6f).  
 
Figure S6. (a-c) SEM images and (d-f) EDS elemental maps showing C and S distribution related to 
the (a, d) S@MPAC-73, (b, e) S@MPAC-82 and (c, f) S@MPAC-91 composites. Right-hand 
corresponding insets display the single C and S EDS elemental maps. See Experimental Section of 
the Manuscript for acronyms.

(Figure S7b), and S@MPAC-91 (Figure S7c) of the curves reporting the peak current intensity (Ipeak) 
and the related square root of the scan rate (ν1/2) values obtained from the corresponding CV profiles 
in the Manuscript (Figure 6a-c). These parameters are used to determine DCV according to Randles-
Sevcik equation[1] (equation 1 in the Manuscript) in the reduction peaks labeled with 1 and 2, and in 
the oxidation ones labeled with 3 and 4. See the discussion of Figure 6 in the Manuscript for further 
details. 
 
Figure S7. Linear fits of peak current intensity (Ipeak) vs. the square root of the scan rate (ν1/2) for

S@MPAC-82, and (c) S@MPAC-91 obtained from corresponding CV (see Figure 6a-c, and Tab. 3 
in the Manuscript for the potential values of the selected CV peaks labeled with numbers herein). The 
slope of the linear fits is used in Randles-Sevcik equation[1] (Equation 1 in the Manuscript) to calculate 
the Li+ diffusion coefficients (DCV, see also Figure 7a in the Manuscript). See Experimental Section 
of the Manuscript for acronyms.

of a lithium cell using the S@MPAC-91 electrode with a sulfur loading increased to 11.0 mg cm-2 
and E/S ratio of 5 μL mg−1 tested at C/20 in order to achieve Li-S cell operation into practical 
condition. 
 
Figure S10. (a) Voltage profiles (top x-axis displays areal capacity) and (b) corresponding cycling 
trend (right-hand y-axes show coulombic efficiency and areal discharge capacity) related to a 
Li|DOL:DME, 1 mol kg-1 LiTFSI, 1 mol kg-1 LiNO3|S@MPAC-91 cell cycled a the constant C-rate 
of C/20 (1C = 1675 mA gS-1). Voltage range: 1.7 – 2.8 V. Cathode sulfur loading: 11.0 mg cm-2 (area: 
1.54 cm-2). E/S ratio: 5 μL mg−1.
