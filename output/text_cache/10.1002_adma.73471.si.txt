Experimental
Preparation of Pc catalysts 
Pc powder was added to Li–S electrolyte (1 mol L–1 LiTFSI into a DME/DOL solution with a volume ratio of 1:1 containing 2.0 wt% LiNO3, Canrd) to prepare a Pc catalyst at concentrations of 0.01, 0.025, and 0.05 wt%. All the procedure was conducted at room temperature inside an Ar-filled glove box.
Preparation of Pc@Li2S8
A defined amount of Pc was added into a 0.1 mol L–1 Li2S8/DME solution. The mixture was stirred at 25 ℃ for 24 h, then underwent sedimentation for 24 h without shaking. The upper-layer dark green solution was subsequently collected to obtain the Pc@Li2S8.
Preparation of the sulfur/carbon cathodes
Super P and nano sulfur (S8) were uniformly mixed at a mass ratio of 1:3 in a glass bottle, followed by melt-diffusion at 155 °C for 6 h. Then, the S/Super P composite, Super P, and LA133 binder were mixed at a mass ratio of 8:1:1 using a mixer for 60 min to form a slurry. The dispersed slurry was coated on an aluminum foil current collector and further dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h. The cathode was punched into 13 mm circular disks for coin-type battery fabrication.
Material characterizations
7Li NMR spectra of Li2S8 and Pc@Li2S8 were collected by a Quantum-I Plus 600-2 system at room temperature. The 1.0 mol L–1 sample of LiCl in D2O was quoted to the internal standard (δ = 0.00). 1H and 17O NMR of DME and Pc @DME spectra were recorded on a Bruker Advance 3HD 600 MHz spectrometer. By using an argon-filled glove box for sample preparation, the composition of the Li surface and powders was measured by X-ray spectrophotometer (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha). Morphologies of samples were inspected by Thermo Scientific Apreo 2C scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with an energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, OXFORD ULTIM Max65). Ultraviolet-visible (UV–Vis, SHIMADZU-UV-3600i Plus) spectra of adsorption tests were acquired. The ATR-FTIR and FTIR spectra of the electrolyte and powder were obtained using a Bruker spectrometer. AFM images of cycled Li anodes were obtained on Bruker Dimension Icon. The Raman spectra of electrolytes were inspected by Thermo Fisher-DXR2xi. Viscosity measurements of the electrolytes were performed on an Anton Paar MCR302 rotational rheometer. Ionic conductivity was measured using a METTLER conductivity meter. TGA and DSC were performed on an TA SDT Q600 instrument from 25 to 400 °C at a heating rate of 5 °C min–1 under N2 atmosphere. XRD patterns were collected on a Rigaku Smartlab 3kW Diffractometer.
Li2S nucleation and dissociation tests
The 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution was prepared by dissolving sulfur and Li2S powder at a molar ratio of 1:7 in tetraglyme solvent. Batteries were assembled with CP as the cathode and Li foil as the anode, 20 μL of Pc-containing electrolyte as the catholyte, and 20 μL of blank electrolyte as the anolyte. For blank sample controls, 20 μL of blank electrolyte was used as the catholyte. During nucleation testing, cells were galvanostatically discharged to 2.06 V, followed by potentiostatic discharge at 2.05 V until the current dropped below 0.01 mA. For decomposition testing, cells were galvanostatically charged to 2.35 V, then held potentiostatically at 2.35 V until current fell below 0.01 mA. 
Li2S6 symmetrical cell tests
Sulfur and Li2S were mixed at a molar ratio of 1:6 and dissolved in Li–S electrolyte to prepare the 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S6 solution. The Li2S6 symmetric cells were assembled with CP as the counter electrode, with 10 μL of Pc-containing electrolyte and 10 μL of 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S6 solution as the electrolyte. CV tests were conducted between –1.0 to 1.0 V at 10 mV s–1, respectively.
Shuttle current tests
Li–S cells were assembled using a LiNO3-free electrolyte. The cells were first charged galvanostatically to 2.8 V at 0.5 C, followed by maintaining this potential for 1200 s. Subsequently, they were galvanostatically discharged to 2.38 V, and finally, chronoamperometry was performed at 2.38 V to monitor current-time evolution.
Neutron imaging tests
Neutron imaging tests were conducted at the China Spallation Neutron Source (CSNS) with a detector field of view of 7.45 cm, a pixel size of 36.5 μm, and a resolution of 50–100 μm. For carbon cloth (CC) cathodes detection, cycled CC cathodes were disassembled after Li2S nucleation testing. CC was utilized for neutron imaging because of its flexible structure, which ensures sample integrity during vertical mounting and allows stacking of multiple layers to enhance neutron absorption signals. For Li metal anode detection, cycled Li anodes were disassembled from Li//Li symmetric cells at various cycle numbers. 
ToF-SIMS tests
Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) was performed on an IONTOF GmbH M6 instrument. A 30 keV Bi3+  primary ion beam was used in depth profiling mode with a scan area of 50 μm × 50 μm, a beam current of 0.30 pA, and a mass range of 0–1000 u, and a 2 keV Cs+ sputter gun was used for etching over an area of 250 μm × 250 μm under positive ion mode.
Li plating/stripping tests 
Li//Li cells were assembled by employing Li foil as both working electrodes. The Cu||Li cells were fabricated with Li foil for the anode and copper foil for the cathode. The usage of electrolyte used by each electrode is 40 μl. The S03 electrolytes with and without Pc act as the electrolytes for the Pc and blank cells, respectively.
Electrochemical measurements
Both coin and pouch Li–S cells were assembled in an argon-filled glovebox (O2 < 0.01 ppm, H2O<0.01 ppm). The S/Super P composite served as the cathode, Li foil as the anode, and coin cells employed carbon-coated separators. The Pc-containing solution is the electrolyte. The cathode sulfur content was around 60% with an area loading of 1.2–1.3 mg cm–2, and high sulfur loading exceeding 5.7 mg cm–2. Galvanostatic charge and discharge tests were conducted on a Neware battery testing system between 1.7–2.8 V at 30 °C. Cyclic voltammetry (CV), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) at different voltages and temperatures, and other electrochemical tests were conducted on a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation.
SR SAXS tests
Synchrotron radiation SAXS measurements of cycled Li anodes in Li||Li symmetric cells were carried out at the BL16B1 beamline of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). A monochromatic X-ray source with a wavelength of 0.124 nm was employed. Two-dimensional scattering patterns were collected using a Mar165 CCD detector (2048×2048 pixels, 80 μm pixel size) with an exposure time of 0.1 s, at a sample-to-detector distance of 5272.8 mm. The raw 2D images were converted into one-dimensional scattering profiles [I(q) vs. q] using Fit 2D software, and the scattering background was corrected by subtracting the air signal. Theoretical calculations
All density functional theory (DFT) calculations in this study were carried out using the Gaussian 16 software package. The model's geometry was fully optimized, followed by single-point energy evaluations at the B3LYP/6-311G (d, p) level of theory. To improve accuracy, Grimme’s DFT-D3 method with Becke-Johnson damping (D3-BJ) was applied to account for dispersion effects throughout all computations. Subsequent analysis of the obtained wave functions was performed with Multiwfn version 3.8. Adsorption energies (Eads) were computed using the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP). Electron exchange and correlation effects were described within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), specifically using the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional. Core electrons were treated via the projector augmented-wave (PAW) approach, while electronic states were expanded in a plane-wave basis set with a cutoff energy of 400 eV. Partial occupancies were handled using Gaussian smearing with a broadening width of 0.05 eV. Electronic self-consistency was considered achieved when the total energy difference between successive iterations dropped below 10⁻⁵ eV. Structural relaxation was terminated once the maximum force on any atom fell beneath 0.02 eV Å⁻¹. Dispersion corrections were included through Grimme’s DFT-D3 formalism. For Brillouin zone integration during geometry optimization, a 1×1×1 Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh was employed. The surface slab model incorporated a vacuum spacing of 15 Å along the z-axis to eliminate inter-slab interactions, with the lower half of the atomic layers constrained during relaxation and the upper region allowed to fully relax.
