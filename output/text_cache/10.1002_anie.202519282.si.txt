Experimental
Synthesis of homogeneous catalyst of Fe-ETL
1,10-phenanthroline and iron (Ⅲ) chloride were mixed at a mole ratio of 1:2 in the mixed solvent of deionized water and ethanol (Vwater:VEtOH=1:10). The mixed reactants were heated at 120 ℃ in a sealed Teflon-lined autoclave and kept over 24 h. After cooled down to room temperature, red crystal was obtained after filtration and washing with icy solvent. To obtain the structure of catalyst, the red crystal was detected on the X-ray single crystal diffractometer equipped with Bruker SMART APEX-Ⅱ CCD detector system at the temperature of 150 K with Mo Kα radiation (λ = 0.71073 Å). The structures were determined by intrinsic phasing methods using SHELXT and refined by full-matrix least-squares on F2 using SHELXL. Then the red crystal was dried and ground to powder before dissolved in DME/DOL electrolyte containing 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3 to form Co-ETL. The concentration of Co-EIT is 0.25 wt.%.
Synthesis of homogeneous catalyst of Co-ETL
1,10-phenanthroline and cobalt (Ⅱ) chloride hexahydrate were mixed at a mole ratio of 1:2 in the mixed solvent of deionized water and ethanol (Vwater:VEtOH=1:10). The mixed reactants were heated at 120 ℃ in a sealed Teflon-lined autoclave and kept over 24 h. After cooling down to room temperature, red crystal was obtained after filtration and washing with icy solvent. Then the red crystal was dried and ground to powder before dissolved in in DME/DOL electrolyte containing 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3 to form Co-ETL. The concentration of Co-ETL is 0.25 wt.%.The red crystal was detected on the X-ray single crystal diffractometer equipped with Bruker SMART APEX-Ⅱ CCD detector system at the temperature of 293 K with Mo Kα radiation (λ = 0.71073 Å).
Synthesis of heterogeneous catalyst of Cu-NCS
Aqueous ammonia (5 mL), deionized water (30 mL) and ethanol (100 mL) were mixed and stirred for 20 minutes before 5 mL tetraethoxysilane was added. The obtained mixture was continuously stirred for 1 h and white suspension was obtained. Then 500 mg dopamine hydrochloride was dissloved and added into the obtained suspension. The mixture was reacted for 12 h before centrifugation at 9000 rpm for 5 minutes. After dried in air-circulating oven for 6 h, the ochre powder was pyrolysis at nitrogen atmosphere first at 400 ℃ for 2 h and then 800 ℃ for 3 h to obtained black powder. The obtained black powder (180 mg) was dispered in ethanol (200 mL) and 20 mg Cu2(phen)2Cl2 was dispersed in 10 mL ethanol solvent and then dropwise added into the aforementioned ethanol dispersions. The mixture was stirred for 24 h and then filtrated. After dried at 60 ℃, the black powder contained copper complex was obtained and noted as Cu-NCS. The Cu-NCS powder was mixed with polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) at a mass ratio of 9:1 with NMP as the solvent to form slurry. The slurry was uniformly coated on Celgard 2500 PP film and experienced the vacuum drying (60 oC) procedure to form the Cu-NCS interlayer. The modified separator is coated by the mixtures of Super P and Cu-NCS at the mass ratio of 1:1. The coating layer thickness is around 3.5 μm, and the loading mass is 0.6 mg cm–2. 
Preparation of S/C cathodes
The super P and sulfur powder at a mass ratio of 1:2 were first mixed thoroughly before sealed into a glass vessel and heated at 155 oC over 6 h. After cooled down to room temperature, the S/C composites were mixed with LA133 binder at a mass ratio of 9:1 to form viscous slurry. The as-prepared cathode slurry was coated on a piece of Al foil and dried at 60 oC under vacuum condition before being punched into the desired size to use. 
Material characterizations
The morphologies of materials were collected on JEOL-JEM 2100 F transmission electron microscopy (TEM). High-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) images and elemental maps of materials were recorded on a JEOL JEM 200F TEM/STEM equipped with a spherical aberration corrector. Zeiss sigma 500 scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was appplied to detect the morphologies of electrodes. The FTIR spectra were collected on Nicolet iS50 Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscope. UV–vis spectra were collected on a UV–vis spectrometer (Shimadzu, UV1900). The single crystal XRD data were recorded on a Bruker SMART APEX-II CCD detector system. AFM images were gathered on a Alysum Research Cypher ES Atomic Force Microscope. 
LC-MS tests of electrolyte
LC-MS tests were performed on LC-MS (8040, Shimadzu). The electrolyte for LC-MS analysis was attained from working batteries at 0.2 C with a sulfur loading of 1.5 mg cm–2 and an electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio of 12.0 µL mg–1. The electrolyte was collected at two different working state: after discharge to 1.7 V and after subsequent charge to 2.8 V.  During disassembly, the cathode, separator, and Li anode were immersed in 5 mL of a 1:1 (v/v) mixture of DME and DOL for 30 min to extract electrochemical species in the electrolyte. The resulting solution was then transferred to a sample vial for LC-MS analysis. All experimental procedures were conducted in an argon-filled glovebox.
Synchrotron radiation X-ray 3D nano-CT tests
After Li2S nucleation test, the cell was disassembled to take out the cathode. The cathode powder was uniformly dispersed in ethanol solution by virtue of ultrasonic treatment. Subsequently, 10.0 μL of the obtained mixer was dropped onto a piece of carbon-free formvar film with 100 mesh, and dried at room temperature. The entire sample preparation process was carried out under pure argon atmosphere. The loaded formvar film was then placed into the vacuum chamber for imaging tests. The imaging tests were conducted on the beamline BL07W at the National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory (NSRL), Hefei, China, (flux: 2 × 1010 Phs s−1, spatial resolution: 30 nm).
Li2S nucleation and dissociation tests
Commercial carbon paper (CP) with a diameter of 13 mm and lithium foil was used as cathode and anode, respectively. The cell was assembled with 20 μL Li2S8 (0.2 mol L–1) solution containing Fe-ETL or Co-ETL as catholyte and 20 μL LiTFSI (1.0 mol L–1) in DOL/DME as anode electrolyte. Then the cell was first galvanostatically discharged to the voltage of 2.06 V at a current of 0.112 mA and then discharged potentiostatically at 2.05 V until the current was down to 10–5 A. As for the dissociation tests, the cell was first galvanostatically discharged at the current of 0.112 mA until the voltage was 1.7 V. Subsequently, the cell was charged at the voltage of 2.35 V until the current was down to 10–5 A. 
Operando XRD measurements
A special cell with a beryllium window was assembled using Fe-ETL or Co-ETL for X-ray penetration. In situ XRD measurement was performed on a Bruker D8 spectrometer with a scan rate of 5° per minute during the discharge and charge between 1.7 and 2.8 V at 0.02 C.
Assembly of symmetric cells and CV tests
Symmetric cells were fabricated with 20 μL Li2S6 (0.2 mol L–1) solution containing Fe-ETL or Co-ETL as electrolyte, CP as the working and counter electrodes, Celgard 2500 PP film as separator. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were conducted at the voltage windows between –1 to 1 V at a scan rate of 3 or 50 mV s–1. 
Li plating/stripping tests
Cu||Li cells were assembled by using Li foil as the anode, Cu foil as the cathode, Fe-ETL or Co-ETL as electrolyte and Celgard 2500 PP film as separator. The Li plating/stripping tests were conducted at a current density of 1.0 mA cm–2 with a plating/stripping capacity of 1.0 mA h cm–2. And the Li||Li cells were fabricated with two pieces of Li foils as the working electrodes. 
Electrochemical evaluation
The coin-type batteries were assembled by employing Cu-NCS as separator, Li foil as the anode and Fe-ETL or Co-ETL as catholyte. And a mixture of DME/DOL solution containing 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 was used as anode electrolyte. The electrolyte/sulfur ratios were respectively 12.0 and 4.0 μL mg–1 for the routine and high-load batteries. Galvanostatic discharge/charge, rate and cycling performance measurements were detected on a Neware Battery Measurement System (CT-4008T-5V 20 mA-164 and 4008T-5V 100 mA-124, Shenzhen, China) at the voltage window of 2.8–1.7 V. The CV, linear scanning voltammetry (LSV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were obtained on a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation.
DFT calculation method
The DFT calculation was performed by the Materials Studio 2020, and the exchange-correlation energy was approximately described by the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional based on the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). In all calculations, a cut-off energy with a value of 480 eV was used for the plane wave basis, and the convergence criteria for the ionic relaxation and 4 electronic self-consistent calculation were set to 0.02 eV Å–1 and 10–4 eV.
The Gibbs free energy of LiPSs conversion was respectively evaluated based on DFT. All DFT calculations were conducted using a Cambridge sequential total energy package (CASTEP). The exchange function was treated with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) function. DFT-D method was employed to calculate the van der Waals (vdW) interaction. Energy and force will not reach convergence until lower than 2×10–5 eV and 0.04 eV/Å, respectively. The Brillouin zones of the supercells were sampled by 2 × 2 × 1 uniform k point mesh, and a plane-wave basis set with an energy cut-off of 480 eV was employed. A vacuum space of more than 15 Å was applied to all calculations. The convergence criterion for the electronic self-consistent field (SCF) loop was set to 1×10-5 eV per atom. The atomic structures were optimized until the residual forces were below 0.02 eV Å-1.
