Failure mechanism of sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) cathode induced 
by boron-contained lithium salt

Hongzhou Zhang , Dawei Song , Lianqi Zhang *

A B S T R A C T
Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) is deemed as the most promising lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries cathode 
owing to high sulfur utilization degree and stable cycling performance. However, abnormal high initial capacity 
of 2683.2 mA h g-1 and severe degradation (100.5 mA h g-1, 100 cycles) induced by LiDFOB salt are observed in 
our work. To conduct in-depth research on related mechanism, LiPF6 and LiTFSI based batteries are tested as fair 
comparisons and relatively cycling performances are exhibited. The electrochemical performance of electrolyte 
and the interfacial properties of cycled Li anode are compared, then the impact of Li ion transfer and parasitic 
interface reactions are excluded. Synchrotron-based pair distribution function (PDF) and Raman spectroscopy 
tests indicate that new B-S bonds are generated on SPAN during the first discharge process in LiDFOB based 
battery, while the insertion of Li ions on S sites are greatly suppressed. Density functional theory method suggests 
that active S sites after S-S bond cleavage will be attacked and bonded by B from DFOB-, which is hard to break 
and continuously inhibit effective reactions between Li ions and S, leading to serious irreversible battery 
degradation. The failure mechanism of SPAN cathode induced by boron-contained lithium salt are further 
verified by LiBOB.
1. Introduction
Rechargeable lithium-sulfur batteries (Li-S batteries) has been 
considered as one of the most promising next-generation energy storage 
devices owing to high theoretical specific capacity (1675 mA h g-1) and 
high energy density (2600 W h kg-1) [1–3]. However, the related 
commercialization is hindered due to the insulation properties of S/Li2S, 
the volume variation, the dissolution of intermediate polysulfides (Li2Sn, 
4 ≤n ≤8) and the resulting “shuttle effect” during cycling [4–7]. Sul­
furized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) is developed to circumvent above­
mentioned obstacles by Wang and his colleagues in 2002 [8]. 
Sulfur-based

and N-S bonds structure allows it to be adapted even under thin elec­
trolyte conditions [13–18].
The actual structure and working mechanism of SPAN have been 
continuously proposed and the factors that affect electrochemical per­
formance have also been revealed (Fig. 1a). Wang et al. [19] pioneered 
that the initial value for the dehydrogenation and cyclization of PAN is 
280–300 ◦C. Fanous et al. [20] suggested that the pyrolysis of PAN will 
be intensified with temperatures and notable capacity fading is observed 
over 500 ◦C. High pressure leads to the decrease of hydrogen content 
and degree of graphitization, which is conducive to the generation of 
more complete conductive molecular structure [21]. PAN fiber di­
ameters also exert impact on electrochemical performance. The lower 
value is beneficial for forming stable electronic conduction structure, 
shortening transmission distance of Li ions, reducing charge transfer 
impedance and improving redox kinetics [22]. Transition metal sulfide 
electrocatalysts are also introduced to accelerate the redox kinetics of 
lithium storage process for SPAN [23,24], the performance of SPAN is 
also strongly affected by electrolyte and local high concentrated elec­
trolytes are suggested to be the most effective candidate to improve 
electrochemical properties [25–33]. Different from the reported con­
cerns, abnormal electrochemical behaviors are observed in LiDFOB 
based battery, to clarify the related mechanism is of great importance.

In this work, LiDFOB, LiPF6 and LiTFSI are selected to investigate the 
influence of Li salt on SPAN. LiDFOB based battery exhibits super high 
initial capacity of 2683.2 mA h g-1 at 0.1 C, which far exceeding the 
theoretical specific capacity. However, a surprisingly decay is presented 
in the second cycle (1188.3 mA h g-1). By stark contrast, LiPF6 and 
LiTFSI based batteries present capacities of 1813.2 /1722.0 mA h g-1 in 
initial cycle and 1327.3/1341.5 mA h g-1 in the second cycle. An obvious 
platform at 1.7 V is presented in LiDFOB based battery, which is negli­
gible in counterparts. Then the electrochemical properties of Li salts and 
the interfacial performance of Li anode are compared, the related im­
pacts can be eliminated. PDF, X-ray diffraction (XRD), XPS, Raman and 
theoretical calculation are used to intensively investigate the cathode 
variation during cycling. In the initial discharge of LiDFOB based bat­
tery, the broken S-S bonds in SPAN is passivated by oxalate borate which 
generated from the broken of B-F bonds in DFOB- under the action of 
electric field, then the newly generated B-S bond block the reaction of 
broken S-S bonds with Li ions, impairing the subsequent electrochemical 
performance. The similar result can be found in LiBOB based battery.
2. Results and discussion
2.1. Electrochemical performance of SPAN/Li batteries
The electrochemical performance of SPAN batteries with different Li 
salts are tested. Fig. 1b shows the cycling performance after 100 cycles at 
0.1 C. The capacities of LiTFSI and LiPF6 based batteries are maintained 
at 1212.7 mA h g-1 and 933.2 mA h g-1, respectively, with capacity 
retention (CR) of 90.4 % and 70.3 %. By stark contrast, LiDFOB based 
battery, with an ultra-high initial discharge capacity of 2863.2 mA h g-1, 
showing almost no capacity after 30 cycles. In Fig. 1c-e (charge/ 
discharge curves of three batteries), a new discharge plateau at 1.7 V is 
appeared in the first cycle of LiDFOB based battery. Compared to the 
counterparts, the sharp capacity decay is manifested in the second cycle. 
In order to comprehensively evaluate the electrochemical performance 
of three batteries, CV tests are conducted and the results are shown in 
Fig. 1f-h. LiTFSI and LiPF6 based batteries present higher peak currents 
in the second cycle, and better reaction kinetics are exhibited. However, 
two reduction peaks are formed in the first cycle of LiDFOB based bat­
tery, implying side reaction happened and affecting electrochemical 
cycling performance. Moreover, to eliminate the influence of current

2.2. Characterization of electrolytes and Li anode
In order to exclude the effect of electrolyte on electrochemical 
properties, the corresponding ion conductivities are tested and shown in 
Fig. S4, 3.37 mS cm-1, 1.43 mS cm-1 and 3.63 mS cm-1 are exhibited for 
LiDFOB, LiPF6 and LiTFSI based electrolytes, respectively. Furthermore, 
the variations in conductivity with temperature are presented in Fig. 2a. 
the activation energies derived from fitting the data using Arrhenius 
equation are 0.046 eV, 0.046 eV and 0.045 eV [34,35]. Li+ transference 
number (tLi
+) is also a key factor in evaluating the migration capability of 
Li+. The tLi
+ of LiDFOB and LiTFSI based electrolytes are 0.24 and 0.22, 
respectively, slightly higher than that of LiPF6 sample (0.18) (Fig. S5) 
[36]. The Li+ transport efficiency can be further characterized by ex­
change current density (i0) in Tafel plot (Fig. 2b). Compared to LiDFOB 
based electrolyte (i0= 0.43 mA cm-2) and LiTFSI based electrolyte (i0=
0.56 mA cm-2), the i0 value of LiPF6 based electrolyte is 0.26 mA cm-2 
[37,38]. The Coulombic efficiency test of Li/Cu batteries is of great 
significance for understanding the nucleation and plating/stripping 
behavior of Li+. The peak in Fig. 2c represents the nucleation of Li+on 
Cu. The nucleation overpotential in LiTFSI based electrolytes is 26 mV, 
which is lower than those of LiDFOB (30 mV) and LiPF6 (34 mV). The 
uniform insertion/extraction behavior of Li+ can also be fully demon­
strated by long-term cycling results of Li/Cu batteries (Fig. 2d). LiDFOB 
based electrolyte exhibits the best stability with an average Coulombic 
efficiency (CE) of 90.2 % after 50 cycles. LiTFSI and LiPF6 based elec­
trolytes exhibit significant fluctuations, with efficiencies of 86.9 % and 
77.6 %, respectively. It shows that more uniform Li+ plating/stripping 
can be achieved in LiDFOB based electrolyte as compared to LiTFSI and 
LiPF6 counterparts [39]. Base on the above results, the electrochemical 
performance basically follows the trend of LiDFOB ≈LiTFSI > LiPF6.

and S6–8). The composition of solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) is 
further analyzed by XPS [40,41]. After cycling, C = O (531.1 eV) and 
C–O (532.5 eV) can be observed in O1s spectra (Fig. 2i) [42,43]. The

2.3. Characterization of SPAN cathode
After excluding the influences of electrolyte inherent performance 
and Li anode stability, ex-situ XRD patterns are conducted on the cath­
ode of LiDFOB, LiPF6 and LiTFSI based batteries after cycling (first 
discharge, first charge, second discharge and second charge), only some 
small peaks belonging to S on SPAN can be observed and no significant 
difference is exhibited (Fig. S10–13) [44,45]. PDF method based on 
synchrotron radiation is further adopted to understand the overall 
structural changes of SPAN, which is not affected by crystallinity and the 
evolution of chemical bonds after electrochemical cycling can be 
monitored sensitively. The PDF results of SPAN cathode in LiDFOB 
based battery during cycling are shown in Fig. 3a. In the original SPAN 
material, five peaks within the range of 1–3 Å are displayed, the peaks 1, 
4, and 5 corresponding to C–N bonds in pyridine ring (Fig. 3b), while 
the peaks at 1.7 Å and 2.1 Å belong to C-S and S-S bonds, respectively. 
After the first discharge, the peaks of C-S/S-S bonds are disappeared, and 
a new peak is found at 1.9 Å, which may be assigned to the generation of 
B-S bond, implying that when S-S bonds is broken during the discharge 
process, oxalate borate will attack active S site prior to Li ion, then S sites 
on SPAN backbone will be passivated and fail to react with Li ions, 
restricting the orderly Li insertion/extraction behaviors [46]. This 
viewpoint is further validated by Raman (Fig. 3c). The peaks at 306 cm-1 
and 370 cm-1 in original SPAN belong to C-S bonds, while the peaks at 
around 475 cm-1 and 936 cm-1 are attributable to S-S bonds [47,48]. 
After the first discharge, the peaks of S-S bond are disappeared, sug­
gesting the broken of S-S bond. The disappearance of C-S bond after 
discharge can be attributed to two factors: partially due to the formation 
of C-S Li+ bond, whose peak shifts to 580 cm-¹, and partially due to the 
formation of B-S bond (525 cm-¹) after the sulfur sites are attacked [49]. 
In subsequent charge states, the strength of S-S bonds gradually 
decreased, while the strength of B-S bond continues to increase, 
demonstrating the irreversibly of S-S bond and implying the passivation 
mechanism of DFOB-. The surface morphology and element distribution 
of SPAN positive electrode are tested using SEM and energy dispersive 
X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) after the first discharge (Fig. S14–17), B 
element is uniformly distributed on SPAN of LiDFOB based battery. 
Furthermore, XPS tests is used to detect the surface composition of SPAN 
cathode after the first discharge. As shown in Fig. 3d, the peaks of 284.8, 
285.8, 286.7, 288.8 and 290.3 eV in C1s spectra are attributed to 
C–C/C–H, C-S, C–O, C = O and O–C = O, respectively. As for O1s 
spectrum, the peak of B-O (533.8 eV) can be found in LiDFOB based

2.4. Reaction mechanism of LiDFOB based batteries
The fracture and bonding energies of chemical bonds during the 
cycling process in LiDFOB based battery are further calculated by DFT. 
As shown in Fig. 4a, the cleavage energies for single bond cleavage and 
double bond cleavage of B-F are 11.3 and 28.6 kcal mol-1, respectively, 
the cleavage energy of S-S bond during discharge is 52.3 kcal mol-1, 
indicating that DFOB- is highly prone to fracture and showing the po­
tential to passivating active S site generated from the broken S-S bonds. 
Then the bonding energy between oxalate borate and S/N sites are 
calculated and B-N/B-S bonds display higher value of 57.7 kcal mol-1/ 
66.2 kcal mol-1 than S-Li bond (12.4 kcal mol-1), suggesting that the 
active sites generated from the broken S-S bond and pyridine ring are 
more easily passivated by oxalate borate. Moreover, the cleavage energy 
of B-S bond after charging is 62.1 kcal mol-1, which is nearly 6 times that 
of S-Li bond (11.3 kcal mol-1), indicating the difficulty in fracture and 
inhibiting the normal Li+ insertion/extraction behavior in following 
electrochemical processes. Therefore, according to aforementioned an­
alyses, the passivation effect mechanism of LiDFOB towards SPAN based 
batteries is proposed and shown in Fig. 4b. During the first discharge, B- 
F bonds suffer from breakage under the action of electric field and

resulting in the formation of B-S/B-N bonds, providing extra platform 
and ultra-high irreversible capacity during the first discharge. However,

and the available capacity is decreased. During the cycling, the observed

2.5. Validation of LiBOB salt
To further confirm the passivation mechanism of oxalate borate on 
active S sites, another B-contained Li salt, LiBOB, is selected. The cycling 
performance of LiBOB based battery at 0.1 C is shown in Fig. 5a. After 
100 cycles, the discharge capacity dropps sharply from 1958.6 mA h g-1 
in the first cycle to 42.9 mA h g-1. In the charge/ discharge curves 
(Fig. 5b), the first discharge plateau is obvious at the voltage of 1.7 V, 
corresponding to the generation of B-S bonds. The rate performance is 
demonstrated in Fig. 5c, there is a sharp decline in capacity at 0.1 C, and 
no capacity is presented at current density above 0.3 C. Two reduction 
peaks in the first cycle can be seen from CV curves (Fig. 5d). The results 
are similar to those of LiDFOB based battery, and the influence of B on 
SPAN batteries is strongly confirmed. In Fig. 5e, the ionic conductivity of 
LiBOB based electrolyte is 1.85 mS cm-1, and the activation energy fitted 
calculated from Arrhenius equation is 0.048 eV. Then Li+ transference 
number is tested and a value of 0.24 is obtained using the steady-state 
current method (Fig. 5f), no significant difference compare to conven­
tional LEs. As shown in Fig. 5 g-h, the Li/Cu battery is assembled and the 
nucleation overpotential of Li+ is determined as 32 mV and average CE 
is calculated as 85.6 %. Moreover, the morphology of Li anode is tested 
and slight corrosion pores can be observed (Fig. S22), indicating that the 
main factor affecting electrochemical performance of LiBOB based bat­
tery is the reaction between Li salts and SPAN cathode. The chemical 
composition of cathode after the first discharge is also analyzed using 
XPS and show in Fig. 5i-l (C1s, O1s, B1s and S2p). The C1s spectrum 
consist of common species such as C–C/C–H (284.8 eV), C-S (285.8 
eV), C–O (286.7 eV), C = O (288.8 eV) and O–C = O (291.3 eV). The 
peaks at 531.1, 532.5 and 533.8 eV in O1s spectrum are belong to C = O, 
C–O and B-O, respectively. Two peaks at 191.3 and 192.3 eV can be 
observed in B1s spectrum, which correspond to B-S and B-O bonds, 
indicating that BOB- also facilitate to the generation of B-S bonds, which 
presents a passivation mechanism on the active S site and blocks the 
normal lithiation/delithiation behavior of Li+ during the reaction pro­
cess, causing serious capacity decay of Li-SPAN batteries. In S2p spectra, 
the peaks of Li2S (160.2 eV), C-S Li+ (161.8 eV), B-S (162.8 eV) and 
thiosulfute (167.1 eV, 168.8 eV) are displayed.
3. Conclusions
In summary, LiDFOB, LiPF6 and LiTFSI are selected to investigate the 
influence of Li salt on electrochemical performance of SPAN based 
battery. Abnormal high capacity of 2683.2 mA h g-1 and ultra-low ca­
pacity retention (8.5 %) are delivered in LiDFOB based battery. By stark 
contrast, capacity retention of 70.3 % and 90.4 % are maintained for 
LiPF6 and LiTFSI counterparts. PDF, Raman, XPS and XRD are conducted 
after the elimination of the influence of electrolyte and Li anode, new B- 
S bonds can be found on SPAN cathode in LiDFOB based battery. DFT 
calculation is further performed to investigate the cleavage energy of S-S 
in SPAN and B-F in DFOB-, while the bonding energy between B and Li, N 
and S in SPAN are also compared. Then the reaction mechanism can be 
depicted as follows: in SPAN system under external electric field, B-F is 
prone to break and attack the active S site generated from the cleavage
