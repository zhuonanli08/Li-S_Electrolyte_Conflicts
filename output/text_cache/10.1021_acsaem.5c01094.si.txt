EXPERIMENTAL SECTION 
All chemicals were purchased without further purification. Elemental sulfur (S8, 
Alfa), lithium foils (200 μm in thickness and 15 mm in diameter, Canrd), carbon paper 
(CP, TORAY, Japan), LiNO3 (99.99%, Aladdin), LiI (99.99%, Aladdin), Li2S (Alfa), 
1,3-dioxolane (DOL, Sigma Aldrich), dimethyl ether (DME, Sigma Aldrich), lithium 
bis(trifluoromethane) sulfonamide (LiTFSI, DodoChem), polyvinylidene difluoride 
(PVDF, Sigma Aldrich) and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, Sigma Aldrich) were used 
as received. PP (polypropylene, Celgard 2500, DodoChem) membranes with a diameter 
of 19 mm were used as separator.
The baseline electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (vol 1:1). 
The electrolyte with LiI was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI with 2 wt.% LiI in DOL/DME 
(vol 1:1). The electrolyte with LiNO3 was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI with 2 wt.% 
LiNO3 in DOL/DME (vol 1:1). The Li2S6 electrolyte was prepared by adding Li2S and 
S powders with a molar ratio of 1:5 into the solvent with DME and DOL (v:v=1:1), 
followed by vigorous magnetic stirring. The polysulfide electrolytes also contained 1.0 
M LiTFSI as the lithium salt. The concentration of the Li2S6 in the polysulfide 
electrolytes was 0.2 M. The polysulfide electrolyte with LiI was prepared by adding 2 
wt% of LiI in the Li2S6 electrolyte. The polysulfide electrolyte with LiNO3 was 
prepared by adding 2 wt% of LiNO3 in the Li2S6 electrolyte. The above electrolytes 
were prepared in an Ar-filled glove box with H2O and O2 contents below 0.01 ppm.
A 2032 coin-type cell was fabricated by using a piece of lithium foil as the anode 
and a carbon paper with a diameter of 12.0 mm as the cathode. 40 μL electrolyte was 
used in the cells. The symmetrical cells were fabricated by using lithium foil as the 
working electrode and counter electrode. Electrochemical characterization was carried 
out using an electrochemical workstation (Solartron Analytical 1400 or Autolab M204) 
and Landian multichannel battery tester. The EIS analysis was conducted by using a 
Solartron 1470E Frequency Response Analyzer (Solartron Analytical) in a frequency 
range from 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz. Galvanostatic discharge-charge tests were performed at a

Figure S7. XRD patterns of the sulfur cathodes in the lithium-sulfur batteries with 
different electrolytes (a and b) after discharging to 1.7 V and (c and d) after recharging 
to 2.6 V. 
Figure S7a and b showed the XRD patterns of the sulfur cathodes after discharging to 
1.7 V. The absence of the peaks for the sulfur and the presence of the peaks for the 
lithium sulfide indicated that the sulfur was reduced to form lithium sulfide. The 
strongest Li2S peaks of the cathode in LiNO3-based electrolyte implied the facile 
reaction kinetics for the Li2S deposition. Figure S7c and d showed the XRD patterns of 
the sulfur cathodes after recharging to 2.6 V. The absence of the Li2S peaks and the 
presence of the sulfur peaks indicated the dissolution of Li2S. The strongest sulfur peaks 
of the cathode in LiNO3-based electrolyte implied the facile reaction kinetics for the 
sulfur formation.
