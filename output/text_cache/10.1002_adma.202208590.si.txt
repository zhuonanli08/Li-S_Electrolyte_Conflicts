Experiment Section: 
Preparation of cathode materials 
Sulfur powders (Alfa Aesar) and carbon nanotube (Zhongke era) (mass ratio = 6:4) were ground well 
with a mortar, then sealed the mixture in an Ar-filled ampoule and placed in a muffle furnace (Hefei 
Kejing) for 48 hours at 300 ℃ (S/C). Secondly, the S/C, conductive black (TCL), and polyvinylidene 
fluoride (PVDF, Aladdin) (mass ratio = 7:2:1) were fully dispersed in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP, 
Aladdin) for half an hour, then scrape the slurry on aluminum (Al) foil with a 100-micron spatula. After 
drying, cut it into round slices (diameter = 1.1 cm) for later use, and the sulfur loading on every electrode 
is about 0.8~1.2 mg cm-2. 
 
Characterizations  
Use the thermogravimetric analyzer (TG/DTA7300, SII NanoTechnology) to measure the final sulfur 
content of S/C. Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS, Anton Paar SAXSess MC2) using Cu-Kα radiation 
was employed to test the electrolytes with different concentrations at varying temperatures. Raman 
spectra of electrolytes and lithium salt were conducted with a Horiba Jobin Yvon HR Evolution Raman 
spectrometer under a Raman laser wavenumber of 532 nm. 7Li NMR was performed on an Agilent 
DirectDrive2 600 MHz nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer. Each electrolyte sample (600 μL) was 
placed in an NMR tube along with a sealed capillary tube. This sealed tube contained 1 M LiCl in D2O 
and was used as an external reference.  
 
Electrochemical Measurements  
CR2025-type coin cells were assembled in an argon-filled glove box with the S/C materials as the 
working electrodes, Li foil as the counter/reference electrode to evaluate the electrochemical 
performance. Various amounts of LiTFSI dissolved in 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and ethylene glycol 
dimethyl ether (DME) (V:V =1:1) with 0.1 M LiNO3 were used as electrolytes. Celgard 2400 (Celgard, 
USA) was used as a separator. The batteries were first equilibrated for 24 h before the operation at 
different temperatures to ensure that the entire system reached the same ambient temperature. Then 
battery testing system (LAND CT 2001A, Wuhan, China) was used to test the charge/discharge 
performance at different temperatures. All the EIS and CV tests were conducted on a Chenhua CHI660E 
electrochemical station. The EIS data was recorded in the frequency range from 0.01 to 100000 Hz. For

For Li-S pouch cell evaluation, the well-mixed cathode slurry was double-side coated on Al foil 
and then dried at 60 ℃ overnight. Then a die-cutting machine was used to cut out 5 cm * 8 cm electrodes 
with a single-side areal sulfur loading of 5.6 mg cm-2. The ultra-thin lithium metal anode sheets were 
also cut with the same size as sulfur cathode and rolled on both sides of the copper foil. The cathode 
and anode were stacked layer by layer with PP separators and controlled all the electrodes in parallel. 
Aluminum and nickel electrode lugs were welded to the positive and negative electrodes by an 
ultrasonic welder. Finally, the cell was put into the aluminum-plastic film pouch, and encapsulating 
under negative pressure after being injected with electrolyte. 
 
Theoretical Simulations and Analysis 
Molecular dynamics simulations were performed with Gromacs 2019.6.[1] The simulation systems were 
composed of 964 DME, 1438 DOL, 20 NO3–, 80 S42–, and a certain amount of Li+ and TFSI– to reach 
LiTFSI concentrations of 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 mol L–1, with above species uniformly mixed by 
PACKMOL.[2] 

<<PAGE 5>>
4 
 
DME, DOL, NO3–, TFSI– and S42– were modeled by the GAFF force field,[3] with bonded 
parameters for S42– obtained from reported work,[4] and for Li+ the parameter was obtained from another 
reported work.[5] RESP2(0.5) partial charges were used for the species involved in this study, which 
were derived with the help of Multiwfn program by first optimizing the molecular geometry at the 
B3LYP/TZVP level of theory in Gaussian 16, and then fitting to electrostatic potential calculated at the 
B3LYP/ma-TZVP level of theory.[6,7]  
Van der Waals interactions were described by the Lennard Jones (LJ) potential with a cutoff of 1.2 
nm. The LJ parameters between different atom pairs were generated by Lorentz-Berthelot mixing rules. 
Electrostatic interactions were calculated with the particle-mesh Ewald method with a grid spacing of 
0.12 nm and a pme-order of 4.  
Initial configurations were first energy-minimized, followed by 50-ps equilibration at T=233.15 K 
and P=1 bar. Subsequently, 50-ns production runs were carried out at the same PT condition maintained 
by Nose-Hoover thermostat and Parrinello-Rahman barostat. The leap-frog algorithm with a time step 
of 1 fs was used to integrate the equations of motion. Three-dimensional periodic boundary conditions 
were applied during simulations. 
The solvation states of lithium ions are identified by analyzing the MD trajectories, and the most 
frequently occurring solvation structures are extracted. The de-solvation free energy of each solvation 
structure is determined by quantum chemical calculations via Gaussian 16, as described below. 
The structure of each solvation structure is optimized at the B3LYP-D3/TZVP level of theory, and 
vibrational analysis is conducted to ensure the local minima is achieved and to obtain the thermal 
correction to Gibbs Free Energy. Also, the single point energy based on the optimized structure is 
calculated at the B2PLYP/def2-TZVP level of theory. Assuming a solvation structure with formula 
LiAxBy, with A and B to be the solvent components around a Li+, and x/y to be the molecular number 
of each solvent type. The de-solvation free energy of LiAxBy is calculated according to equation: 
∆Gde-solvation(LiAxBy)=Ggas(Li+)+xGsolv(A)+yGsolv(B)–Gsolv(LiAxBy) 
Where Ggas and Gsolv denote the gibbs free energy of a species in gas state and solvation state, 
respectively. For the same structure, the difference between Gsolv and Ggas is the solvation free energy, 
which is obtained by subtracting the electronic energy calculated at M052X/6-31G* with SMD solvent 
model from that calculated without solvent model.
