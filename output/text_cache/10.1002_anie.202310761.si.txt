Experimental Procedures 
 
 
Materials 
Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) (99.98%) was received from Capchem without any treat. Dimethoxy ethane (DME) and butyl 
methyl ether (BME) (>99.0%, GC) were purchased from Dodochem and Aladdin, respectively. BME was dried with 4 Å molecular sieves 
for 48 h before use. Electrolytes were prepared by dissolving LiFSI in DME or BME. Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) (Mw=150,000) and sulfur 
were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. To synthesize sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN), polyacrylonitrile and sulfur were mixed in an 
agate mortar at a mass ratio of 1:4. The mixture was heated in an argon-filled furnace at 450 °C for 6 h with a ramping rate of 10 °C 
min-1.  
Preparation of electrodes 
SPAN, Super P, Poly(acrylic acid), and guar gum were mixed through a high-speed homogenizer at a mass ratio of 8:1:0.5:0.5 in 
deionized water. The slurry was coated on the aluminum collector and vacuum dried at 80 °C for 12 h. By controlling the coating 
thickness, the SPAN electrode with different loading can be obtained. 
Characterization 
The micromorphology of all samples was characterized by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (JEOL JSM-7900F) and. 
and a transmission electron microscope (TEM) (Talos F200X G2), respectively. The UV−vis spectroscopy was tested on the Cary 4000 
UV–vis spectrometer (Agilent Technologies). The chemical components of the samples were analyzed by X-ray photoelectron 
spectrometer (XPS) (Thermo Scientific K-Alpha). The 17O and 23Na nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra of electrolytes were 
acquired by NMR spectrometer (AVANCE III 400MHz). The solvation structure of the Li-ion in different electrolytes was measured by 
Raman spectrometer (Horiba LabRAM HR Evolution).  
Electrochemical Measurement 
The electrochemical performance was tested by CR2032-type coin cells on the battery testing systems (Land battery tester, Wuhan, 
China). All coin cells were assembled in a glove box filled with argon gas (H2O and O2 < 0.1 ppm). For Li||SPAN half cells, thick Li 
metal foils (500 μm) serve as the anode, and a low (~1.8 mg cm-2) loading SPAN electrode was used as the cathode. For Li||SPAN full 
cells, Li metal foils (50 or 100 μm) were used as the anode, and high (~7.1 mg cm-2) and ultrahigh (~13.3 mg cm-2) loading SPAN 
electrode was used as the cathode. The amount of electrolyte added to the entire battery was strictly controlled. All cells use Celgard 
2400 as a separator. It was worth mentioning that the capacity statistics of Li||SPAN cells were based on SPAN materials. For CE 
statistics of Li||SPAN cells, that is, the ratio of charge capacity to discharge capacity over a full cycle. If not otherwise specified, 
galvanostatic cycling of the Li||SPAN battery was conducted within the voltage range of 1–3 V under room temperature. The 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy tests were tested on the CHI660E (Shanghai Chenhua Instrument Co., Ltd) electrochemical 
workstation in the frequency range of 100 kHz to 10 mHz with amplitude of 5 mV. The ionic conductivity of different electrolytes was 
measured by a Swagelok cell. The specific value was calculated by the following formula:

In-situ UV−vis spectroscopy measurement 
A quartz cell was used for the in-situ UV−vis spectroscopy test. In detail, the SPAN electrode and Li foil were fixed to the electrode 
holder as cathode and anode respectively and the different electrolytes of 2.5 mL were added to the quartz cell before test. The quartz 
cell was placed on the holder of the spectrophotometer and then connected to LAND battery testing systems by wire. The cell was 
charged/discharged continuously for 12.5 h within a voltage range of 1–3 V at 1C (1C=800 mAh g-1) and UV−vis spectra were recorded 
the data every 30 minutes.

Preparation of three-electrode coin cells 
The preparation process refers to the reported work.[1] A Li4Ti5O12 (LTO)-coated copper wire (0.1 mm diameter, with polyimide insulating 
layer) was used as a reference electrode, which was placed between two separators and assembled into a coin cell with Li foil and 
SPAN used as counter and working electrodes, respectively. Afterward, LTO was lithiation to a stable potential plateau (~1.56 V vs. 
Li/Li+) before usage. 
Theory Calculations 
All the calculations based on density functional theory were carried out in Gaussian 16 software package. Geometric optimization and 
frequency calculations were performed at B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) level. The solvation effect was considered with the integral equation 
formalism variant of the polarizable continuum model (IEFPCM). The binding energy between Li-ion and solvents was obtained as 
follows: Eb = E(Li+-S) – E(Li+) – E(S) where E(Li+-S), E(Li+) and E(S) are the total energies of the Li+-S complexes, Li-ion and solvent, 
respectively. The binding energy between polysulfides and solvents was obtained as follows: Eb = E(Ps-S) – E(Ps) – E(S) where E(Ps-
S), E(Ps) and E(S) are the total energies of the polysulfides-solvent complexes, polysulfides and solvent, respectively. 
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted in GROMACS, utilizing the OPLS-AA force field for the electrolyte system.[2] 
The FSI- and Li-ion descriptions were obtained from previous publications.[3-4] The force field parameters for the solvent molecules were 
generated using the LigParGen web server.  The 1.2-scaling CM5 charges were based on DFT calculation by Gaussian program and 
Hirshfeld population analysis by Multiwfn.[5]  A cutoff of 1.2 nm was used for nonbonded Lennard-Jones interactions. Periodic boundary 
conditions were applied in all directions. For each system, initial energy minimization was performed using the steepest descent 
approach. Subsequently, NpT simulations with an A V-rescale thermostat and Berendsen barostat were conducted at 300 K for 40 ns. 
Finally, the last 20 ns of the production run were used for analysis.
