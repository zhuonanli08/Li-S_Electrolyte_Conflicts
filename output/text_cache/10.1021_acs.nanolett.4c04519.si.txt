Experimental procedures 
Preparation of electrolytes  
Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)-imide (LiTFSI, 99.5%) and lithium nitrate (LiNO3, 
99%) were obtained from Aladdin, and tris(perfluorophenyl)borane (TPFPB, 97%) was 
purchased from Xinhengyan. The solvents, namely 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, purity > 
99%) and 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, purity > 99.8%), were acquired from Macklin and 
MREDA, respectively. Prior to use, all solvents were treated with activated molecular 
sieves (4 Å pore size) for three days. The lithium salts were dried at 130 °C for 48 hours 
under vacuum conditions. The electrolytes were prepared inside an argon-filled glovebox. 
The blank electrolyte was formulated by dissolving 0.8 M LiTFSI and 0.6 M LiNO3 in 
DOL/DME (1:1, v/v). To create the TPFPB electrolyte, 25 mM TPFPB was added to the 
blank electrolyte. Additionally, electrolytes with varying TPFPB concentrations were also 
prepared.  
Preparation of polysulfide solutions 
Sublimed sulfur powder (S, 99.95%) and lithium sulfide (Li2S, 99.9%) were purchased 
from Aladdin and Macklin, respectively. The lithium polysulfide solution was prepared in 
an argon-filled glovebox by mechanically stirring stoichiometric amounts of Li2S and S 
powders to achieve the desired Li2S6 composition. The mixture solvent of DOL/DME (1:1, 
v/v) was used for the preparation of Li2S6 solution. These samples were stirred at 60 °C for 
24 hours. The Li2S6 catholyte solutions were prepared by adding 0.8 M LiTFSI and 0.6 M 
LiNO3 to the lithium polysulfide solution, with or without the addition of 25 mM TPFPB. 
Preparation of sulfur electrodes 
The Ketjen black/sulfur (KB/S) with 70 wt% sulfur composite was prepared by melt 
diffusion method by mixing the KB/S mixture at 155 °C for 12 h. The sulfur electrodes 
were prepared by casting the homogeneous slurry consist of KB/S, Super P, polyvinylidene 
fluoride (PVDF) with a weight ratio of 8:1:1 in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP) solvent on 
carbon coated Al foil, followed by 60 °C vacuum heating for 12 h. The dried electrode was

Electrochemical measurements  
The electrochemical characteristics of Li-S cells employing different electrolytes were 
evaluated in CR2032 coin cells. The electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio was 10 μL mg−1 for 
electrochemical cycling tests in each Li-S cell, unless otherwise specified. All experimental 
procedures were conducted inside an argon-filled glovebox. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) 
curves were recorded within a voltage window of 1.6-2.7 V, employing scan rates ranging 
from 0.1 to 2 mV s−1. Galvanostatic cycling experiments between 1.7 and 2.6 V were 
performed using the Neware system (CT-4008Tn). The electrochemical performance of 
Li-S cells at −25 °C was assessed within a voltage range of 1.5-2.9 V inside a low-
temperature chamber. GITT measurements were performed using the Neware system at 
−25 °C, the current pulse was set as 0.05 C, and the relaxation time was 2 hours. The Li|Li 
symmetrical cells were assembled with two identical Li metal electrodes separated with a 
Celgard 2500 separator and the charge/discharge tests under current density of 1 mA cm−2 
and capacity of 1 mA h cm−2 were carried out. The Li|Cu cells were assembled with a Li 
metal electrode and a Cu foil electrode separated with a Celgard 2500 separator and the 
charge/discharge tests under current density of 0.5 mA cm−2, capacity of 1 mA h cm−2 and 
voltage cutoff of 1 V were carried out. The EIS measurement of the Li-S cells was 
performed on a Gamry electrochemical station (IFC1010) with the frequency between 1-
100 kHz and 10 mV perturbation amplitude. 
Nucleation of lithium sulfide  
Li2S nucleation on carbon paper (CP, HCP020N) current collectors at 25 °C and −25 °C 
were investigated using the Li2S6 catholyte, with a piece of lithium foil serving as the 
counter electrode. The two electrodes were separated by a Celgard 2400 separator. A 
volume of 20 μL of Li2S6 catholyte was applied onto the CP, while 20 μL of corresponding 
electrolyte without Li2S6 was added to the anode compartment. At 25 °C, the cells were 
subjected to galvanostatic discharge at a rate of 0.05 C until reaching 2.09 V, followed by 
a potentiostatic hold at 2.05 V to allow for the nucleation and growth of lithium sulfide. At

Physical Characterizations 
The microstructure of the electrode was analyzed using field-emission scanning electron 
microscopy (FESEM, SU8220). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analyses were 
performed on the cycled Li electrodes. Prior to analysis, the electrodes were disassembled 
in an Ar-filled glovebox to prevent exposure to air or moisture, and then transferred to the 
XPS analysis system. The NMR measurements of the electrolytes was performed on a 
Bruker AV-400 NMR spectrometer using coaxial NMR tube with the 1 M LiCl-D2O 
adopted as the internal standard. The 7Li chemical shift was referenced to 1 M LiCl solution 
at 0 ppm. The UV-vis spectra of the Li2S6 solutions were collected on the Shimadzu 
UV3600Plus UV-VIS-NIR spectrophotometer. The ionic conductivities of the electrolytes 
were measured using electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) on a Gamry IFC1010 
electrochemical workstation. Swagelok cells, comprising two molybdenum electrodes and 
a polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) ring spacer (inner diameter: 6 mm, thickness: 2 mm), 
were assembled for the measurements. A volume of 400 μL of electrolyte was used in each 
cell. The ionic resistance was determined by the x-axis intercept (Rb) of the impedance

Computational simulations.  
Computational investigations were conducted to explore the structural characteristics of 
lithium polysulfides. The cluster-based density functional theory calculations were 
conducted using the Gaussian 16 software package. For all geometry optimizations, the 
hybrid functional B3LYP 2 and the 6-311+g(d, p) basis set were employed. Additionally, 
the Grimme's D3 dispersion correction (D3-BJ) 3 was applied. The binding energy (ΔE) 
between the two components was determined following the formula: ΔE = Etotal – m× 
ETPFPB – n×ELi2S4, where Etotal, ETPFPB, and ELi2S4 represent the total energy of the TPFPB-
Li2S4 complexes, TPFPB, and Li2S4, respectively. The variables m and n correspond to the 
numbers of TPFPB and Li2S4 molecules in each system. 
Pouch cell assembly 
The graphene/sulfur (G/S) with 80 wt% sulfur composite was prepared by melt diffusion 
method by mixing the G/S mixture at 155 °C for 12 h. We utilized a lab-synthesized three-
dimensional graphene material with a high S loading aims to enable the electrode 
manufacturing and battery-level energy density. The graphene was synthesized according 
to a previous report.4 The double-sided sulfur electrodes were prepared by casting the 
homogeneous slurry consist of G/S, carbon nanotube, Super P, and LA133 with a weight 
ratio of 85:4:6:5 in water on Al foil, followed by 50 °C heating for 12 h. The S loading of 
the double-sided sulfur electrode is 8 mg cm−2. The pouch cells consist of four sulfur 
cathodes (68×96 mm2) and five Li anodes (125 μm, 71×99 mm2) were assembled in an Ar-
filled glove box. The overall weight of the pouch cell was 17.82 g. The pouch cell was 
evaluated at a 0.05 C rate, employing a voltage window of 1.7-2.6 V at 0 °C after two 
cycles activation at 25 °C.

Figure S9. (a) Voltage profiles of the Li-S cells that are paused periodically for in-situ EIS testing and 
the corresponding Nyquist curves at the different stages during (b) discharge and (c) charge and their 
equivalent circuit (inset). The Nyquist plots exhibit two main semicircles at high frequency, representing 
the interphase contact resistance (R2) of the surface of sulfur cathodes, and the charge transfer resistance

the sulfur surface and faster charging process, respectively. These findings align with the results of the 
CV measurements, providing further evidence of accelerated sulfur redox kinetics in the cell with 
TPFPB electrolyte.

Figure S15. The effect of trimethyl borate (TMB) additive on the electrochemical performance of Li-S 
cells and the lithium metal plating/stripping behavior. (a) The first cycle voltage profiles and (b) the 
capacity retention of the Li-S cells using electrolytes with different TMB concentrations at 0.3 C at 
room temperature. (c) The Coulombic efficiency of the Li|Cu cells and (d) the voltage profile evolution 
of Li|Li symmetric cells using electrolytes with different TMB concentrations. Although the voltage 
profiles revealed no significant capacity enhancement with TMB additive, the cell using the electrolyte 
with 100 mM TMB shows a higher capacity retention than the one using blank electrolyte. In addition, 
Li|Cu and Li|Li cells show stable Li plating/stripping. We confirm that the cells with TPB and TMB 
additives show limited enhancement on the capacity and stability, which may be caused by the 2D film-
like Li2S deposition in TPB and TMB electrolyte and the poor Li deposition/stripping performance. In 
addition, the borates in where boron is fully coordinated with O can only bond with Li+ in the LiPSs 
because of the steric hindrance. This will further greatly hinder the following conversion of LiPSs.

Figure S16. Electrochemical performance of Li-S cell using different electrolytes at −25 °C. The cells 
measured in this Figure were subjected to two activation cycles at room temperature to exclude the 
possible adverse effect of bad SEI or CEI formation at low temperature, and to encourage sulfur 
activation at room temperature. (a) The voltage profiles at the third cycle (the first cycle of
