The reversibility of lithium (Li) metal anodes is highly susceptible to temperature, owing to the aggravated side reactions at high
temperatures and serious Li dendrite growth at low temperatures. Thus it is extremely challenging to simultaneously realize the
high Li reversibility in both low and high temperature scenarios. Herein, an oxygen-free solvent (n-hexane, HEX) assisted with
the hexyl methyl ether and 1 mol L−1 lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide is proposed to constitute an electrolyte for temperature-
immune lithium metal batteries. It demonstrates that the HEX not only greatly suppresses the solvent reduction even at high
temperatures but also weaken the Li
+–solvent interaction for the facile Li-ion desolvation, leading to high Li Coulombic
efficiencies (99.59% at 25 °C, 99.30% at 60 °C and 98.75% at −30 °C) and the dendrite-free Li plating from −30 °C to 60 °C.
Benefitting from the low density and temperature-immune properties of our electrolyte, the sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (3.8
mAh cm
−2)||Li (60 μm) pouch-cells deliver 278 Wh kg
−1 energy density and maintain the stable performance over 50 cycles, and
retain 248 and 320 Wh kg−1 energy density at −30 °C and 60 °C, respectively. This work provides a new perspective on the
electrolyte design for wide-temperature Li metal batteries.

Introduction
The pursuit of high energy-density batteries to increase the
operating time of portable electronic devices and the driving
range of electric vehicles has motivated the revival of the Li
metal anode in recent years [1,2]. However, this anode is
highly temperature-sensitive, exhibiting aggravated side re-
actions between Li metal anodes and the electrolyte as
temperatures rise [3] and exacerbating Li dendrite growth as
temperatures fall [4]. It has failed to achieve high Coulombic
efficiency (CE) and uniform Li deposition for Li metal an-
odes at extreme temperatures [5,6]. As reported by Climate
Change Institute [7], the global temperature is mainly con-
centrated in −30 °C–50 °C. Thus, designing an wide-tem-

perature Li metal battery (LMB) is crucial for broadening its
application scenarios and minimizing the thermal manage-
ment requirements [8]. Unfortunately, so far, the funda-
mental understanding and effective solutions for wide-
temperature-operative LMBs are insufficient.
It is widely accepted that temperature strongly influences
the reversibility of Li metal anodes because it dramatically
affects the reaction rate between Li metal anodes and elec-
trolytes, the solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) composition
as well as the interfacial charge-transfer resistances of the
anode [9–12]. On the one hand, high temperature aggravates
the side reactions and even destroys the SEI component [13],
usually resulting in the rapid depletion of the Li metal anodes
and electrolytes (Figure 1a). Low temperature, on the other
hand, results in not only the low Li+ desolvation rate but also
the low ion transport in both the electrolyte and SEI, thus
greatly disturbing the uniform deposition of Li metal [11,14–
16]. In such a situation, the enhanced tip effect accelerates
the Li dendrite growth, resulting in the internal short circuits
and battery failure (Figure 1b) [4]. Li metal CE and cycling
life are negatively affected due to either high or low tem-
perature, hindering the realization of wide-temperature-op-
erative LMBs.
Side reactions, SEI composition, and Li
+ solvation struc-
ture are all determined by the properties of electrolytes [17–
19]. Thus, improving the Li reversibility at extreme tem-
peratures can be realized by regulating the electrolyte for-
mula. For example, Liu et al. [4] realized the remarkable Li
reversibility operating at ultra-low temperatures (less than
−30 °C) by weakening the Li
+–solvent interactions, and Cui
et al. [10,12,20] demonstrated enhanced performance in
LMBs operated at elevated temperatures (60 °C) owing to
the special anion-derived SEI nanostructure. Such significant
progress has improved Li metal cyclability at either low or
high temperatures; however, achieving the high Li reversi-
bility over a wide temperature range, required in realistic
operation scenarios, remains a significant challenge.
Herein, we design a tailor-made electrolyte for tempera-
ture-immune LMBs by introducing an oxygen-free solvent
(n-hexane, HEX) assisted with a monoether (hexyl methyl
ether, HME) and 1 mol L−1 lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide
(LiFSI). Based on theoretical and experimental investiga-
tions, we discovered that the HEX can greatly suppress the
solvent/Li side reactions and significantly lower the Li
+
desolvation barrier, allowing the Li metal anode to maintain
more than 98.7% Li||Cu CE at −30 °C–60 °C. Furthermore,
this
electrolyte
possesses
a
much
lower
density
(0.824 g mL
−1) than that of the traditional electrolyte
(1.286 g mL−1). Appling our electrolyte to the sulfurized
polyacrylonitrile (SPAN)||Li pouch-cells can not only in-
crease the energy density by 25.8% compared with that of the
reference sample but also maintain the 73.8% capacity re-
tention compared with 56.6% of that in the reference sample
after 50 cycles (at room temperature). Besides, this electro-
lyte also enables the SPAN||Li pouch cell to retain 248 and
320 Wh kg
−1 energy density and maintain stable cycling at
−30 °C and 60 °C, respectively.
2
Results and discussion
2.1
Electrolyte design
The critical challenge of the temperature-immune electrolyte
for LMBs is to simultaneously solve the problem of
accelerated side reactions at high temperatures and severe
lithium dendrite growth at low temperatures. As a result, two
principles should guide the electrolyte design: on the one
hand, constructing a more stable SEI can achieve high
Coulombic efficiency of Li metal anodes. However, even the
most stable SEI at present is also difficult to completely
suppress the side reactions between the electrolyte and Li
metal. This is also the fundamental reason why it is difficult
to obtain the industry standard of >99.9% cycling Coulombic
efficiencies of Li metal anodes. Reducing the reaction rate
between Li metal and electrolytes under the similar SEI
conditions may hold the key to further improve the Cou-
lombic efficiencies of Li metal anodes, especially at high
temperatures. On the other hand, weakening the Li
+–solvent
interactions plays a key role in achieving the facile Li-ion
desolvation at low temperatures [4]. The side reactions be-
tween solvents and Li are mainly related to the reactivity of
various functional groups [21]. As shown in Figure 1c, ether-
based electrolytes with relatively low polar C–O bonds have
significantly better reductive stabilities than carbonates with
high polar C=O bonds [22–24]. Furthermore, the polarity of
C–O bonds primarily determines the Li-ion solvation struc-
ture because the cation of Li ions is solvated by coordinating
the O atom. The lower the polarity of C–O bonds, the weaker
Li+–solvent interactions. Given all of this, a low polar ether-
based solvent can provide a more stable electrolyte/lithium
interface as well as the easier Li-ion desolvation, resulting in
the much higher Li reversibility. Inspired by this realization,
we are considering reducing the carbon/oxygen (C/O) of
ethers to further alleviate solvent/Li side reactions while si-
multaneously weakening the Li
+–solvent interactions. Fol-
lowing the rule to extend the C/O to infinity (C/O = ∞), we
finally obtain oxygen-free alkanes, regarded as the most
stable solvent against lithium. As a result, the solvent/Li side
reactions and Li
+–solvent interactions could be minimized
once the electrolyte solvent was used [25]. However, due to
their nonpolarity and low dielectric constant, alkanes have
never been used as solvents in standard electrolytes in pre-
vious studies [26].
Guided by this principle, the HEX was selected as the
oxygen-free solvent. Hexyl methyl ether (HME), a mono-
ether with one oxygen atom in the molecular structure, high

2.2
Verifying
the
stability
of
oxygen-free
solvent
against Li metal
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were also
performed to understand the stability between various pure
solvents and Li metal under the most favorable conditions for
the chemical reaction [33]. As shown in Figure 2a–2d, all
molecules react with Li and are arranged in their most fa-
vorable configurations. The binding energy shows the fol-
lowing trends: G2>DME>HME>HEX, indicating that the
electrons of Li-metal is more difficult to donate to the HEX
than G2, DME and HME. In addition, no significant corro-
sion, especially in the HEX sample, was observed in the
scanning electron microscopic (SEM) images of the Li sur-
face after being soaked in the pure HME and HEX for more
than 100 h (Figure 2g and 2h). Severe chemical corrosions
were observed in both the optical experiment and SEM
images of G2 and DME samples (Figure 2e and 2f). These
findings support the high reduction resistance of HME and
HEX against Li metal. The frontier molecular orbital en-
ergies (Figure 2i) were also calculated to distinguish the
potential of pure solvent reduction at negative potentials,
rather than the electrochemical stability of electrolytes. It can
be seen that the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital
(LUMO) energy shows HEX>HME>DME>G2, further in-
dicating that HEX has better chemical compatibility with Li
metal than others.

Conclusions
In this study, a temperature-immune electrolyte for LMBs
was developed using the oxygen-free HEX as the solvent.
The addition of HEX to the electrolyte reduced the side re-
actions between the electrolyte and Li metal to a new low-
level and significantly promoted the Li
+ desolvation, re-
sulting in the high Li-metal reversibility and a dendrite-free
structure over a wide temperature range (from −30 °C to
60 °C). These unique properties of the designed electrolyte
contributed to the extremely high CE (99.59%) in bare Li||Cu
half cells and provided SPAN||Li cells with 2.5 mAh cm
−2
area capacity to maintain the >85% capacity retention with

99.9% CE over 100 cycles from −30 °C to 60 °C. Further-

SPAN||Li pouch cells to increase from 221 to 278 Wh kg

E/S = 3.2 μL mg

conclusion, this study developed a temperature-immune
electrolyte capable of sustaining a long-term cycle in LMBs
and achieved the high pouch-cell-level energy density over a
wide temperature range.
