Summary
In lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries, the discharge performance depends greatly on
a number of cell design parameters because of the complex reaction mecha-
nisms in the cathode. Electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio and carbon-to-sulfur (C/S)
ratio in the cell are key examples of these critical design factors that define the
Li-S battery performance. Here, a 1-D electrochemical model is reported to cal-
culate the dependence of the discharge behavior of a Li-S battery on the E/S and
C/S ratios. Proposed model describes the complex kinetics through two electro-
chemical and two dissolution/precipitation reactions. Concentration variations
in the cathode are also taken into account in the model. Characteristic aspects
of the discharge profile of a Li-S battery -the two distinct voltage plateaus and
the voltage dip in between- are captured in the predicted voltage curve. Similar
trends on the discharge performance of the Li-S cell with varying E/S and C/S
ratios are projected; both voltage and discharge capacity of the Li-S battery are
improved substantially with increasing C/S or E/S ratio up to a certain point,
whereas, the dependence of the discharge performance on these factors is less
substantial at higher ratios. This model offers a mechanistic interpretation of the
influence of cell design on the Li-S battery performance.

INTRODUCTION
Lithium-Sulfur (Li-S) batteries have gained major consid-
eration in recent years due to their high theoretical gravi-
metric energy density and high-capacity, inexpensive and
abundant cathode materials.1-6 In Li-S batteries, the bat-
tery performance depends vastly on cell design because
of the complex mechanisms taking place in the cell.1-5
Carbon-to-sulfur (C/S) and electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S)
ratios in the cell are commonly investigated examples of
these design factors that define the battery perfor-
mance.1-4 C/S ratio determines the electrochemical sur-
face area in the cathode along with its electronic
conductivity and thus has a key impact on the cathode
kinetics.1-3 Similarly, E/S ratio controls the reaction and
shuttle kinetics in the cell and therefore dictates the sul-
fur utilization.1,2,4 Consequently, both the cell voltage
and the discharge capacity of a Li-S battery are signifi-
cantly sensitive to these factors, specifically until a cer-
tain threshold. The influence of C/S and E/S ratios on
the discharge behavior of a Li-S cell has been experimen-
tally explored extensively in the literature.7-17 However,
mechanistic interpretations of the connection between
cell design and discharge performance are very limited.
Cathode kinetics in a Li-S battery is extremely compli-
cated with numerous electrochemical and precipitation
reactions. Subsequently, there is an extensive research effort
on

MODEL DEVELOPMENT
Herein, we present a 1D concentration- and time-
dependent electrochemical model of a Li-S cell. As stated
above, Kumaresan et al19 developed a detailed model for
the Li-S cell, which has been adopted subsequently by
many others in the literature. Therefore, the model pro-
posed in this study is also based on Kumaresan et al.19 As
shown in Figure 1, our modeling domain includes only
the porous cathode. This simplification is done to obtain
a less complicated model with fewer assumed parame-
ters. This approach has also been taken by the others pre-
viously since the Li-S cell overpotential is typically
dominated by the cathode.18,28 The assumptions in the
model are listed below:
• A 1-D concentration- and time-dependent electro-
chemical model of a Li-S cell is demonstrated for an
isothermal, constant-current discharge. The model
accounts for the porous cathode only; the contribu-
tions of the anode and separator overpotentials to the
cell overpotential are neglected.
• A reaction mechanism in which each of the high and
low voltage plateaus are represented by a single elec-
trochemical reaction is used to describe the cathode
kinetics.2,18,22,28 Consequently, the model discounts
the polysulfide shuttle mechanism or the side reac-
tions. The discharge-charge reactions in the cathode
are assumed to be symmetric and the transfer coeffi-
cients are taken as 0.5.
• The only polysulfide that can precipitate during dis-
charge is Li2S(s).
• Carbon
volume
fraction
controls
the
cathode
active area.
• The anode can supply unlimited amount of Li.

Discharge profiles are predicted using the proposed
model to explore the impact of C/S and E/S ratios on the
discharge behavior of a Li-S battery. Tables 2 to 4 report
the model parameters used in these analyses.
As shown in Table 2, the cathode specific surface area is
defined as a function of the carbon volume fraction. In the
previous models, the change in the area with changing
porosity as a consequence of the precipitation of discharge
products has been considered. However, as will be discussed
in the next section, the alteration proposed here is required
to include the impact of the C/S ratio on reaction kinetics.
Therefore, the equation proposed by Kumaresan et al
(Equation 16 in Reference 19) is modified here to define the
electrochemically active area based on the carbon volume
fraction in the cell. On the other hand, the impact of E/S ratio
on the discharge behavior can be realized inherently, most
probably due to the inclusion ofdissolution/precipitation
reactions in the reaction mechanism. Yet, for examining the
influence of E/S ratio on the discharge behavior exclusively,
both the carbon volume fraction and the sulfur loading were
kept constant in the model in this part of the analysis. In the
model any variation in the porosity leads to a change in both
carbon and sulfur volume fractions and consequently in sul-
fur loading at a given thickness. Keeping the carbon volume
fraction constant for each E/S ratio cancels the impact of εc
on the cell area thus on the cathode kinetics. Moreover, the
cathode thickness is changed with varying E/S ratio so that
the sulfur loading remains constant and thus its influence on
the discharge capacity is disabled in the model. Another pos-
sible way of investigating the impact of E/S ratio on the cath-
ode kinetics would be by keeping the C/S ratio constant.
However, in that case the electrochemically active area must
be defined in terms of the C loading rather than the carbon
volume fraction in the cathode so that changing porosity
does not affect the electrochemically active area additionally
via changing εc. This will be considered in our future work.

On the other hand, S8(s) volume fraction decreases drasti-

the end of discharge, almost all solid S is consumed in
the cell. It is apparent in Figure 4A that the decrease in
the porosity is greater near the separator/cathode inter-

again explained by the Li2S(s) volume fraction profile

near the separator/cathode boundary. Therefore, it may
be discussed that both the porosity and the Li2S(s) volume
fraction become less uniform in the cathode as the dis-

uniform distributions of the concentrations and current
densities within the cathode at higher discharge depths.

The proposed electrochemical model captures the
effect of C/S ratio on the cell performance by calculating
the cathode active surface area based on the C/S ratio.
Therefore, increasing the C/S ratio enhances the kinetics
through the available surface area and thus improves cell
voltage and capacity up to a certain point. However,
when the kinetic limitations in the cathode are no longer
significant, cell voltage becomes independent of the C
loading. At these high C/S ratios, the capacity worsens
slightly due to the reduction in the sulfur amount in the
cathode. These trends predicted here are consistent with
the previous experimental studies.3,9,10 Consequently, the
proposed model can successfully capture the influence of
the C/S ratio on the Li-S cell voltage and capacity.

Discharge profiles calculated with different E/S ratios are
presented in Figure 8. It is clear in the figure that the
lowest E/S ratio of 2.4 mL g S−1 leads to a much worse

As stated in Section 2, Li2S(s) has been accepted as the
only solid discharge product in our model. Herein, it
should be emphasized that at very low E/S ratios the pre-
cipitation of other solid discharge products could be
important. However, we believe that this assumption,
which has also been adopted by many others in the
literature,20-22,27,28 is acceptable for all E/S ratios investi-
gated here since the average S4
2−concentrations do not
exceed the maximum solubility limit at any discharge
capacity in our projections (Figure 10B). It should be
mentioned that there may be instances in which the solu-
bility limit is surpassed locally in the cathode since
Figure 10 presents the average concentrations. Yet, this
should not create a major divergence in the results.
Here, we should emphasize that even though the
predicted discharge profiles match the experimentally
observed trends, quantitative agreement of model pre-
dictions with experimental results is not straightfor-
ward. For example, even though a Li-S battery is
expected to be discharged in 1 hour at 1C in theory, the
discharge time of a battery at this rate is much lower in
practice. Likewise, an E/S ratio of 2.4 mL g S−1 may
cause a much worse performance in reality. These quan-
titative deviations between the experimental data and
the model predictions are most probably due to the exis-
tence of a number of assumed parameters in the model,
which cannot be measured individually. A detailed sen-
sitivity analysis is conducted (not given here) and it is
concluded that the model has high sensitivity to these
assumed parameters; consequently, fitting the predicted
discharge profile with an experimentally measured
curve is tricky.
One apparent disagreement between the model and
the experimental results is that the capacity ratio of the
upper to lower discharge plateau should be around 1:3,
whereas, this ratio is considerably lower in our model
predictions. As a result of the sensitivity analysis con-
ducted to investigate the reason of this disagreement, it is
seen that changing the exchange current densities for
either of the reactions ends up changing only the second

CONCLUSIONS
In this report, a 1-D electrochemical model is demon-
strated to predict the dependence of the discharge
behavior of a Li-S cell on the critical design factors, C/S
and E/S ratios. Cathode kinetics is defined via a simpli-
fied reaction mechanism, which considers two electro-
chemical and two dissolution/precipitation reactions.
Predicted voltage curves for the Li-S cell capture the key
features of a distinctive discharge profile including the
high and low voltage plateaus and the voltage dip in
between. The discharge voltage and capacity of the Li-S
battery are both enhanced substantially at higher C/S
ratios due to an enhancement in the cathode area.
