1.1 Preparation of electrolyte
The materials and solvents employed in this work were sourced commercially from suppliers including Aldrich, RHAWN, and Dodochem. The baseline electrolyte comprised a blend of two lithium salts：1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethane sulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI) and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3. along with two solvents: 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME)/1,3-dioxolane (DOL) (v/v = 1:1). Zinc(II) bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Zn(TFSI)2) and cobalt bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (Co(TFSI)2) were introduced into this baseline electrolyte at varying weight ratios (0.5 wt.%, 1 wt.%, 2 wt.%) and stirred to homogeneity. For the 1 wt.% concentration, 0.03 g of Zn(TFSI)2 was combined with 2.97 g of baseline electrolyte and thoroughly mixed to form the optimized electrolyte; other concentrations were prepared analogously. All preparation procedures in this study were conducted within an argon-filled glove box maintaining oxygen and moisture levels below 0.01 ppm.

1.2 Preparation of sulfur cathode
Sulfur-carbon composites were prepared by blending 3 g of conductive carbon (Super P) with 7 g of sublimated sulfur at a 3:7 weight ratio. The mixture was ground using a mortar and pestle for 30 minutes to achieve uniform blending. This homogenized mixture was transferred to a crucible and heated in an argon-filled tube furnace at 155 °C for 12 h. The resulting sulfur-carbon composite, Super P, and PVDF binder were mixed at a 7:2:1 mass ratio and ground. The blend was dissolved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), coated onto aluminum foil current collectors, and vacuum-dried at 60 °C for 8 h. The dried electrodes were punched into 12 mm diameter discs, with the positive electrode exhibiting a sulfur content of 49%. Celgard 2500 served as the separator, while lithium metal functioned as the anode. Button cells (Type 2016) were assembled within an argon-filled glove box using stainless steel casings.

1.3 Preparation of polysulfides
Sublimated sulfur (Aladdin, 99.5%) and lithium sulfide (Aladdin, 99.9%) were added to the baseline electrolyte in molar ratios of 5:8 and 7:8, respectively. For example, 1.283 g of sulfur and 0.368 g of Li2S were added to a 0.1 mol L-1 solution of Li2S6 in 10 mL of medium baseline electrolyte, followed by heating under stirring at 60°C for 72 hours. A 0.1 mol L⁻¹ Li2S8 solution was prepared via a comparable approach. All procedures were conducted in an argon-filled glove box.

1.4 Electrochemical measurements
During electrochemical testing, current rates and specific capacity were referenced to the sulfur mass in the cathode (1 C = 1672 mA g-1). Cycling and rate capability assessments of lithium-sulfur batteries were conducted on a Land CT2001A test system within a voltage window of 1.7-2.8 V. CV measurements employed the LANHE test system at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s⁻¹ (1.7-2.8 V), with variable scan rates of 0.1-0.5 mV s⁻¹. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and ionic conductivity were characterized using an Autolab workstation (frequency range: 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz; AC amplitude: 10 mV), employing Li|S cells for EIS. Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests were performed on a Neware system with 0.2 mA pulse current, 120 s relaxation intervals, and a voltage range of 1.6-2.8 V.

To examine the influence of Zn(TFSI)2 and Co(TFSI)2 on polysulfide conversion and Li2S deposition, CR2016 coin cells (Li|Li2S8, Li|Li2S8+Zn(TFSI)2, and Li|Li2S8+Co(TFSI)2) were assembled. The anode comprised lithium foil, the cathode employed carbon paper, and a Celgard 2500 separator was utilized. The Li|Li2S8 cell contained 20 μL of 0.1 M Li₂S₈ solution plus 15 μL of baseline electrolyte, whereas the modified cells incorporated 20 μL of 0.1 M Li₂S₈ solution with 15 μL of electrolyte containing 1 wt% Zn(TFSI)2 or Co(TFSI)2. Cells were discharged to 2.11 V at 0.112 mA, followed by constant-voltage discharge at 2.09 V to facilitate Li₂S nucleation and growth until current decayed below 10-5 A. CV tests were performed on a LANHE system (1.7-2.8 V). Multiple trials ensured data accuracy and reproducibility, with continuous stability monitoring throughout testing.

2. Characterization
The 1H NMR spectra of Li2S6 and its mixtures with Zn(TFSI)2 or Co(TFSI)2 were acquired on a Bruker 400 MHz nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer. These compounds were further characterized using a Thermo Fisher UV-vis spectrometer and a LabRAM HR Raman spectrometer. To verify the suppression of polysulfide/lithium anode side reactions by Zn(TFSI)2 or Co(TFSI)2 additives, lithium-sulfur cells were disassembled after 5 cycles. Recovered lithium foils were cleaned with dimethoxyethane (DME), dried in an argon-filled glove box, and subjected to surface morphology analysis by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Surface composition was examined via energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS), while cycled electrode chemistry was probed using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS).

3. DFT modeling
The spin-polarized DFT Were carried out using the Vienna Ab Initio Package (VASP) was employed to perform all the density functional theory (DFT) calculations within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew, Burke, and Enzerhof (PBE) formulation.The projected augmented wave (PAW) potentials were applied to describe the ionic cores and take valence electrons into account using a plane wave basis set with a kinetic energy cutoff of 450 eV. Partial occupancies of the Kohn–Sham orbitals were allowed using the Gaussian smearing method with a width of 0.01 eV. The electronic energy was considered self-consistent when the energy change was smaller than 10−5 eV. A geometry optimization was considered convergent when the force change was smaller than 0.05 eV/Å. Grimme’s DFT-D3 methodology was used to describe the dispersion interactions. The dissociation energy is also calculated. The Brillouin zone integral utilized the surfaces structures of 3×3×1 monkhorst pack K-point sampling.
