Synergistic regulation of polysulfide redox kinetics by 3R-2H phase-junction MoSe2 and DMDSe electrolyte additive

College of Materials Science and Engineering, Sichuan University, Chengdu 610064, China.

Experimental section
Preparation of cathode catalysts
(1) MoO2@rGO precursor: Graphene oxide (GO) was synthesized via modified Hummers method. 0.5 g Na2MoO4 was added to a sonicated GO dispersion (25 mL, 3.5 mg ml-1) under stirring. Separately, 1 g CTAB was dissolved in 50 mL water by heating, then mixed with the GO/Mo precursor solution. After the solution turned coffee-brown, the mixture was transferred to a stainless-steel autoclave for hydrothermal reaction at 200 °C for 10 h. The product was collected by vacuum filtration, washed three times, and lyophilized to obtain MoO2@rGO.
(2) H-MoSe2@rGO: MoO2@rGO was ground with Se powder and subjected to hydrothermal reaction at 155 °C for 12 h.
(3) C-MoSe2@rGO: MoO2@rGO mixed with Se powder was calcined at 550 °C for 4 h. The obtained sample is 3R-2H (R3m-P63) MoSe2.

Preparation of cathodes
The catalysts was uniformly mixed with elemental sulfur at a 1:8 mass ratio and calcined at 155 °C for 12 hours. The resulting powder was combined with a carbon nanotube dispersion (5 mL, 2 mg ml-1) and homogenized in an alcohol solution. The mixture was transferred to a ball-milling jar and processed for 48 hours, followed by ethanol rinsing. The dispersed slurry was thoroughly mixed and vacuum-filtered to obtain self-supporting cathode material. The cathode was placed in a freeze-drying chamber for 36 hours. After complete drying, it was cut into 12 mm diameter discs for coin cells and 6×18 cm sheets for pouch cells.

Coin cell assembly
The self-supporting material served as the cathode with a sulfur loading of 5.5–6.5 mg cm-2. Lithium metal was used as the anode, and a commercial polypropylene membrane (PP, Celgard 2400) as the separator. The electrolyte consisted of 1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) dissolved in a 1:1 (v/v) mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME), with either 2 wt% LiNO3 (control group) or 1 wt% dimethyl diselenide (DMDSe) as the additive. CR2032-type coin cells were assembled in a high-purity argon-filled glovebox (H2O < 0.01 ppm, O2 < 0.01 ppm).

Symmetric cell assembly
CR2032-type coin cells were configured with lithium foil symmetric electrodes, Celgard 2400 PP separator, and electrolyte comprising 1 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (v/v=1:1) with either 2 wt% LiNO3 (control group) or 1 wt% DMDSe additive.

Pouch cell assembly
The self-supporting material served as the cathode (6 mg cm-2) paired with a 110 μm-thick lithium strip anode, cut to 6 ×18 cm dimensions in an argon glovebox (H2O <0.01 ppm, O2 <0.01 ppm). A folded PP separator was sandwiched between copper foil/lithium strips and cathode/aluminum foil, then wound into a core. The core was sealed within 88 μm-thick aluminum laminate film after electrolyte injection.

Characterization
The morphology of the samples was examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS Gemini 300). The crystal structure and phase-junction interfaces were characterized by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM, JEOL JEM-F200), while the elemental composition was analyzed through high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) imaging and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). The specific surface area and pore structure were determined from N2 adsorption-desorption isotherms measured on a Micromeritics ASAP 2460 instrument. Crystal structure of the cathodes were identified using X-ray diffraction (XRD, Shimadzu XRD-7000) with Cu-Kα radiation (λ=1.5406 Å), scanning from 20° to 40° at a rate of 5°/min. UV-vis-NIR  spectra were acquired between 200 and 750 nm using a spectrophotometer (Jasco, Japan). Thermal stability was evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA, NETZSCH STA 449 F5) under nitrogen atmosphere with a heating rate of 10 °C/min. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) measurements were performed on a Thermo Fisher Scientific Nicolet iS20 spectrometer. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was conducted using a Thermo Scientific instrument equipped with an Al-Kα monochromatic source.

Electrochemical testing
CHI660E electrochemical workstation was used for cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements. CV scans were performed between 1.7 and 2.8 V, while EIS spectra were acquired over a frequency range of 10-2 to 105 Hz with a 5 mV amplitude. Galvanostatic charge/discharge, cycling, rate capability tests were carried out on a LAND CT2001A system, using a voltage window of 1.7 to 3.1 V, with specific capacities calculated based on sulfur mass (1 C = 1675 mA g-1). 
The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) was implemented by alternating 0.1 C constant-current discharge pulses (10 min duration) and relaxation periods (40 min), terminating at a cutoff voltage of 1.7 V. In situ EIS tests employed identical frequency and amplitude parameters (10-2 to 105 Hz, 5 mV), with spectra recorded at 0.1 V intervals during discharge (OCV to 1.7 V) and charge (1.7 to 2.8 V) processes. 
For Li2S nucleation experiments, a 0.5 M Li2S8 solution was first prepared by dissolving sulfur and Li2S in a 7:1 molar ratio in 1.0 M LiTFSI/DOL-DME (1:1 v/v) electrolyte under 24 h stirring. CR2032 coin cells were then assembled using prepared material as cathode, lithium foil as anode, and Celgard 2400 separator. Injected 10 μL Li2S8 electrolyte on cathode side and 10 μL Li-S electrolyte (1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL:DME=1:1 v/v with 2 wt% LiNO3 or 1 wt% DMDSe) on anode side. Discharged at constant current (0.112 mA) to 2.06 V, followed by potentiostatic discharge at 2.05 V for 20,000 s to induce Li2S nucleation.

Visual LiPSs adsorption experiments
A 5 mmol L-1 Li2S6 solution was prepared by dissolving sublimed sulfur and lithium sulfide (Li2S) at a 5:1 molar ratio in a mixed solvent of DOL and DME (v/v=1:1) under vigorous stirring at 70 °C for 10 hours. Equal masses of MoO2@rGO, H-MoSe2@rGO, and C-MoSe2@rGO samples were separately dispersed in 10 mL aliquots of the Li2S6 solution within an argon-filled glovebox. Time-dependent color variation monitoring provided qualitative assessment of polysulfide adsorption capabilities.

Energy level and electrostatic potential calculations
The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO), lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), and electrostatic potential isosurfaces of electrolyte additives were computed using Gaussian 09 software. Visualization renderings were generated from Multiwfn-exported data files processed with Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) software.

DFT calculations
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP). The spin-polarized Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA) functional was employed with an energy cutoff of 420 eV, using the projector augmented wave (PAW) method with all-electron plane-wave basis sets. A supercell with MoSe2 facets was constructed via VESTA. A (1 × 2 × 2) Monkhorst-Pack k-point grid sampled the Brillouin zone. A 15 Å vacuum layer minimized interlayer interactions. Geometric optimizations used the conjugate gradient algorithm with convergence criteria of 1 × 10-4 eV for total energy and 0.02 eV Å-1 for atomic forces.
