Experimental Section 
Preparation of Materials.  
Elemental sulfur (S, 99.99 %, Aladdin) and Ketjen Black (KB, Aladdin) were mixed 
at a weight ratio of 65/35 by mechanically ball milling for 5 h. The mixture was 
heated at 155 °C for 12 h. The S/KB electrodes were prepared by mixing 80 wt% 
KB/S composite powder, 5 wt% Super-P carbon, 5 wt% CNT additive, and 10 wt% 
LA133 binders in water. The slurry was cast onto an Al foil and then dried at 65 °C 
overnight. The dried sulfur electrodes were punched into 10 mm disks for use. The 
loading of sulfur was about 2.0 mg cm-2. 
Preparation of Electrolytes.  
1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), and 
lithium bisfluoromethane sulfimide (LiTFSI) were purchased from Duoduo Reagent 
Network. 1-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP), Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and N, 
N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) were purchased from Aladdin. The blank electrolyte is 
composed of 1 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (v/v, 1/1) with 2 wt % LiNO3. The high 
donor number solvent additives (NMP, DMSO, or DMF) was added into the blank 
electrolyte with various volume ratios (1-20 v%). The amount of liquid electrolyte

2 
 
used in each cell was about 30 μL. 
Synthesis of Polysulfides.  
Sulfur and lithium sulfide (Li2S) were mixed together in the proper stoichiometric 
ratio (the ratio of 7:1 for Li2S8, and the ratio of 5:1 for Li2S6) with DME or NMP as a 
solvent and then magnetically stirred for 48 h at 60 °C (DME) or room temperature 
(NMP). The solvent was evaporated to obtain a solid Li2S8.  
Electrochemical Measurements.  
The electrochemical performances were investigated in CR2032 coin cells with S/KB 
(65 %) as the working electrode, lithium foil as the anode, and Celgard 2400 film as 
the separator. The sulfur areal loading for regular cycling performance test was about 
2 mg cm-2. The E/S ratio for regular electrochemical test was 15:1 μL mg-1. All the 
coin cells were assembled in an argon-filled glove box (O2 < 0.01 ppm and H2O < 
0.01 ppm). The galvanostatic charge/discharge (between 1.8 V and 2.8 V) of batteries 
was tested by Land BT2001A battery system (Wuhan, China) at 25 °C. Cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) tests were performed by a VSP-3 workstation (Bio-logic, France) 
with a sweep rate of 0.1 mV s-1 between 1.7 V and 2.8 V. Electrochemical Impedance 
Spectroscopy (EIS) tests were carried out with the frequency range from 100 mHz to 
400 kHz on the same workstation. For the Li-Li symmetric cells, we used a protocol 
of 2 h of stripping followed by 2 h of plating with a current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 for 
each cycle. 
Li2S Nucleation Tests.  
Commercial carbon paper (CP) was used as a current collector for Li2S deposition.

onto the anode side. All assembled batteries were galvanostatic discharged to 2.10 V

In Situ Raman Spectroscopy. 
Cells with a quartz window, provided by Beijing Science Star technology Co. Ltd was 
used for the in-situ Raman spectroscopy analysis. The discharge rate of batteries was 
about 0.3 C (1 C= 1675 mAh g-1). Raman signals of the Li-S Batteries were 
simultaneously recorded at five-minute intervals during the discharge/charge process. 
Characterizations.  
UV-Vis measurements were carried out on a Cary 5000 UV-Vis spectrophotometry 
(Varian, America). Raman analyses were carried out on a bench Raman dispersive 
micro-spectrometer (InVia Reflex, Renishaw) using a laser (wavelength of 532 nm) at 
frequencies between 2000 cm−1 and 100 cm−1. The morphology of the sulfur cathode 

<<PAGE 5>>
4 
 
and lithium anode were obtained by a Scanning Electron Microscope (MIRA4 LMH, 
Tescan). Electrodes were gently washed with DME solvent to remove the electrolyte 
and dried for 12 h in vacuum prior to the characterizations. XPS spectra were 
obtained using a Thermo Scientific K-Alpha system with a monochromatic Al-Ka 
(1486.6 eV) X-ray source. Calibration of the binding energies was conducted relative 
to the C 1s at 284.8 eV. The XPS data were fitted with the asymmetric 
Gaussian-Lorentzian sum function in XPSPEAK software. Viscosity measurement 
was carried on an NDJ-1/5s Viscometer at room temperature with a rotating speed of 
60 rpm min-1. Blank electrolyte is 1 M LiTFSI, 2 wt% LiNO3 in DME-DOL (v/v,1/1). 
Computational Methods. 
MD simulations were performed within Forcite Package. The COMPASS force field 
was used for the calculations. The Ewald method and the atom-based method were 
employed for analyzing the Coulomb interactions and the van der Waals (VDW) 
interactions. In order to obtain a reasonable interaction configuration, a geometry 
optimization was performed by ABNR, and quasi-Newton methods with an energy 
convergence criterion of 2.0 × 10-5 kcal mol-1 and force convergence criteria of 10-3 
kcal mol-1 Å-1 to reach a global minimum energy configuration. To further equilibrate 
the model, the simulations were initially relaxed under the constant pressure and the 
constant temperature (NPT ensemble) for 1 ns at a room temperature and atmospheric 
pressure. During the simulation, the Nose thermostat and Berendsen barostat 
algorithm were applied in the temperature and pressure control. Later, the equilibrated 
simulations run at a constant NVT ensemble for 1 ns in order to get authentic data.
