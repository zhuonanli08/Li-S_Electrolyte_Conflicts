EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES 
1. Materials and methods 
1.1 Materials 
Lithium sulfide (Li2S), Ethyl viologen Dibromide (EtV2+), Sodium p-styrene 
sulfonate (SSNa), Ammonium persulphate were purchased from Macklin 
(Shanghai, China); Sublimed sulfur (S) and lithium nitrate (LiNO3) were 
purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. 1,3-diamethoxyethane 
(DOL), 1,2-dimethyl ether (DME), and bis(trifluoromethanesulfonimide) lithium 
salt (LiTFSI) were purchased from Suzhou DoDochem Co., Ltd, China. Lithium-
metal foil (thickness, 450 μm) was obtained from Xilong scientific Co., Ltd. All 
these materials were used without further purification. 
1.2 Synthesis of GO-g-PSSLi molecular brushes. 
The preparation process for GO-g-PSSLi was shown previously.[1] In detail, 50 
mg of GO dispersed in 40 mL of deionized water was added to 100 mL Schlenk 
and sonicated for 1 h following by adding 50 mmol of SSNa monomer. Under 
Ar flow protection and vigorous stirring, 0.25 mmol ammonium persulphate was 
added. The Schlenk was then immersed in oil bath at 75 ℃ for 48 h, the 
resultant solution was precipitated in methanol. The solution was transferred 
into centrifuge tubes (6 × 50 mL) and then centrifugated at the speed of 15 000 
rpm for 0.5−1 h to collect the bottom black solids. This centrifugation was 
repeated (at least four times) until the upper layer was colorless, obtaining the 
black solid GO-g-PSSNa. Then, GO-g-PSSNa were transformed into PSSLi 
side-chains via ion exchange in LiOH solution (1 M). The resulting GO-g-PSSLi 
molecular brushes were separated by centrifugation and washed with 
deionized water until the filtrate was neutral. The final product was obtained 
through freeze-drying. 
1.3 Preparation of GO-g-PSSLi@PP separators. 
The modified separators were fabricated by the slurry coating method with a 
doctor blade. Typically, 35 mg GO-g-PSSLi, 10 mg ketjen black (KB), and 5 mg 
of polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) were ground together in an agate mortar with 
N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) as solvent. Afterwards, the slurry was evenly 
coated on a polypropylene (PP) membrane (Celgard 2500) using a doctor blade. 
The coated separators were dried at 60 ℃ for 12 h and then were punched into 
round plates with a diameter of 16 mm.   
1.4 Preparation of sulfur electrodes. 
Sulfur/carbon (S/C) composite was firstly prepared by heating the mixture of 
sublimed sulfur and commercial MWCNTs in Teflon autoclaves at 155 ℃ for 12 
h under Ar atmosphere. The sulfur content in the obtained composite was 
determined by TGA measurement. Sulfur electrode was prepared by casting 
the slurry containing the S/C composite, VGCF, and PVDF in weight ratio of 
8:1:1 onto a piece of Al foil, and vacuum drying at 60 ℃ for 12 h. The sulfur 
loading was controlled at 1.2~1.4 mg cm-2 for regular electrodes, while higher 
sulfur loading electrodes were also prepared for electrochemical evaluations. 
The high sulfur loading electrodes (2.87 mg cm-2 and 5 mg cm-2) were prepared 
by using LA133 binder to ensure the mechanical integrity. 
2. Electrochemical measurement. 
2.1. UV-visible spectra experiments  
In the three-electrode system, with chronoamperometry method, the work 
electrodes were applied with 2.45 or 2.01 V vs. Li/Li+ to obtain the reduced state 

<<PAGE 4>>
of EtV+ or EtV0, respectively, in aprotic medium (TEGDME, with 1M LiTFSI and 
0.02 M EtV2+) under an Ar atmosphere. 
The mixtures of lithium sulfides (Li2S) and sulfur (S) with a molar ratio of 1:3, 
and 1:7 were added into TEGDME mixed solvents to verify the reaction with 
ethyl viologen. Then the powder was heated under an argon (Ar) atmosphere 
at 60 °C for 120 h to prepare Li2S4, and Li2S8. 
2.2. Cell assembly and electrochemical measurements. 
2.2.1 Electrolyte and polysulfide preparation 
The electrolytes were obtained by dissolving 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.3 M LiNO3 in 
DOL/DME (1:1 vol) mixed solvents with and without 0.02 M EtV2+, respectively. 
The mixtures of lithium sulfides (Li2S) and sulfur (S) with a molar ratio of 1:7 
were added into DOL/DME mixed solvents. Then the powder was heated under 
argon (Ar) atmosphere at 60 °C for 120 h to prepare Li2S8. 
2.2.2 Li-S batteries (CR2025) assembly 
Li-S batteries (CR2025) were assembled using different loading MWCNTs/S 
electrodes as the cathodes, lithium foil as anode, PP or GO-g-PSSLi@PP as 
separators, respectively. The E/S ratios were about 15, 13, 9 and 6 µL mg-1 for 
the electrodes with sulfur loadings of 1.2, 1.3, 2.87, 5 mg cm-2, respectively. 
2.2.3 Li-Carbon paper (CP) batteries (CR2025) assembly 
Specifically, carbon paper was punched into circular disks with diameters of 12 
mm as the cathode, and 10 µL of blank electrolyte was added into the anode 
side and 20 µL blank electrolyte containing 20 mM of EtV2+, 0.1 M Li2S8, or 20 
mM EtV2++0.1 M Li2S8 as catholyte. 
2.2.4 The galvanostatic discharge/charge measurements 
Li-S and Li-CP cells were cycled between 1.7 and 2.8 V (vs Li+/Li), which were 
conducted using a Neware battery test system (CT-4008Tn-5V20mA-HWX). 
The cathode specific capacity is normalized by the mass of sulfur. 
2.2.5 Symmetric cell measurements 
The performance of Li//Li symmetric cells were measured on a Neware battery 
test system. 
2.2.6 Electrochemical cyclic voltammetry (CV) 
The CV tests were conducted by using a CHI 660e electrochemical workstation 
with a potential range of 1.7-2.8 V (vs Li+/Li) at different scan rates. Blank 
electrolyte containing 20 mM of EtV2+, 0.1 M Li2S8, or 20 mM EtV2++0.1 M Li2S8 
as catholyte to assemble Li//CP cells to study the redox behavior of EtV2+ and 
lithium polysulfides. Li-S cells with and without EtV2+ to investigate the 
enhancement of sulfur redox kinetics by EtV2+. 
2.2.7 In-situ Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) 
The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy tests were carries out a CHI 
660e electrochemical workstation at room temperature. EIS measurements 
were made in the frequency range of 50 mHz to 1 MHz (swept from high to low 
frequency). Li-CP Cells were rested for 2 h post-fabrication to allow electrode 
wetting. EIS measurements were performed during discharge at different SoC 
(from 100% to 0%). The cells were brought to required SoC at a rate of C/5. 
The cells were kept at OCV for 30 min after bringing the cell to the desired SoC 
to allow the cell to achieve steady-state before EIS measurements were made. 
2.2.8 Nucleation of Li2S 
Li2S8 solution with and without EtV2+ were used as the catholyte. The 
assembled Li//CP cells were first discharged galvanostatically at 0.112 mA to 
2.06 V and then discharged potentiostatically at 2.05 V for Li2S nucleation and

2.2.12 Measurement of the shuttle current 
The shuttle current of cells in electrolyte without LiNO3 additive were measured 
according to previous reports.[2] Typically, LiNO3-free electrolyte was employed 
to prevent the passivation of lithium anode. Cells were firstly galvanostatically 
charged to 100 % state at 2.8 V under the current density of 0.5 C. Then the 
cells were discharged to 2.38 V, at which shuttle current reached maximum as 
we indicated previously, and switched to potentiostatic mode. After about 104 s, 
the potentiostatic current was stabilized at a steady state and can be regarded 
as shuttle current. 
3. DRT analysis 
The pyDRTtools (python version of DRTtools) was used to calculate DRT from 
the impedance data. The Tikhonov regularization was used to fit discrete 
experimental data in a non-linear least-square manner and the Gaussian 
method was used for data discretization. Second-order regularization derivative 
fitting parameters were used and the regularization parameter was set at 
0.0001 (residuals between real impedance and impedance derived from DRT 
are within the acceptably low for this value), while radial basis function (RBF) 
with FWHM of 0.5 was used; these parameters gave appropriate fitting of the 
EIS data. Measured DRT were fitted with a Gaussian non-linear curve fit using 
the Levenberg-Marquardt iteration method in Origin to calculate the polarisation 
resistance and time constants. For peak fitting, the number of peaks was 
manually selected and the base of the peak was fixed to zero whereas other 

<<PAGE 6>>
peak parameters such as position, area, and FWHM were not fixed and the 
iterations were performed until the convergence of fit (to reach R2 ~1). 
4. Material Characterizations. 
SEM analysis was performed with a field emission scanning electron 
microanalyzer (Hitachi SU8010). Elemental mapping was conducted using 
EDX detector attached on Oxford-Horiba Inca XMax50. FTIR spectra at room 
temperature were recorded on a FTIR spectrometer (VERTEX70). Contact 
angles were measured by using a DSA25 machine. BET test was conducted 
using KuboX1000. TGA was conducted by DSC3+ at a heating rate of 10 °C 
min-1 under N2 atmosphere to determine the sulfur content in the obtained C/S 
composite. UV-vis spectra were recorded by UV-5200PC spectrophotometer. 
The Raman spectra were recorded on an inVia Raman microscope (Renishaw) 
at 532 nm Laser. 
5. Theoretical computation. 
First, the geometries of LiPSs, and EtV, EtV⁺, EtV²⁺ molecules were optimized 
at the B3LYP/Def2-SVP level of theory. Frequency analyses were performed 
concurrently with the geometry optimizations to ensure that the optimized 
structures corresponded to true minima on the potential energy surface, with 
no imaginary frequencies observed. To enhance the accuracy of the calculated 
results, single-point energy calculations were subsequently performed at the 
B3LYP/Def2-TZVPP level on the optimized structures. Given the significant 
negative charge of the PSS molecule, the ma-def2-SVP and ma-def2-TZVPP 
basis sets were employed for geometry optimization and single-point energy 
calculations, respectively, to ensure computational accuracy. All calculations 
incorporated D3(BJ) dispersion correction and employed an implicit solvent 
model (DOL/DME, volume ratio 1:1) to simulate the real solution environment.  
It is important to note that the HOMO-LUMO gap calculated via DFT is 
significantly influenced by the choice of functional. Typically, functionals with a 
higher HF component yield larger HOMO-LUMO gaps. Therefore, the B3LYP 
functional was selected for this study, balancing accuracy with a lower HF 
component. 
The binding energy Eb between Li₂Sₙ/EtV⁺(²⁺) and PSS was defined as 
Eb=ELi₂Sₙ/EtV⁺(²⁺)-PPS- ELi₂Sₙ/EtV⁺(²⁺)-EPPS, where ELi₂Sₙ/EtV⁺(²⁺)-PPS denotes the energy 
of the interaction system between Li₂Sₙ and PSS; ELi₂Sₙ/EtV⁺(²⁺) and EPSS- 
represent the energies of the free Li₂Sₙ/EtV⁺(²⁺) and PSS molecules, 
respectively. 
All quantum chemical calculations were performed using the Gaussian 16 A.03 
software package, and Independent Gradient Model based on Hirshfeld 
partition (IGMH) analysis was conducted with the Multiwfn 3.8(dev) program. 
The VMD 1.9.4 software was employed for the visualization of the 
computational results.
