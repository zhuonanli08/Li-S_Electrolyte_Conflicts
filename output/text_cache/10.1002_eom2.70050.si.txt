Methods
Electrolyte preparation: All chemicals utilized in electrolyte preparation were of high purity (99.9%) and obtained from DodoChem, including lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), LiNO₃, 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), and 1,3-dioxolane (DOL). Electrolytes were formulated in an argon-filled glove box (Mikrouna; H₂O < 0.1 ppm, O₂ < 0.1 ppm) by dissolving specified concentrations of LiTFSI or LiFSI salts in DME and/or DOL solvents, with 0.1 M LiNO₃ added as an essential additive. Three distinct electrolyte compositions were designed: (1) 1 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO₃ in a 1:1 v/v mixture of DME and DOL (commercial electrolyte); (2) 0.4 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO₃ in DME (LiTFSI-ULE); (3) 0.4 M LiFSI and 0.1 M LiNO₃ in DME (LiFSI-ULE). For polysulfide-containing electrolytes, 0.1 M Li₂S₆ was incorporated by reacting sulfur (99.98%, Sigma-Aldrich) and Li₂S (99.98%, Sigma-Aldrich) at a molar ratio of 1:5 in the prepared base electrolytes, followed by stirring at 50 °C for 8 h under inert conditions.

Sulfur Cathode Preparation: The sulfur/carbon (S/C) composite materials were synthesized via a melt-diffusion method conducted at 155 °C for 12 hours, employing Ketjen Black (Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd.) as the carbon host with a fixed sulfur content of 10 wt%. The resulting S/C composites were then homogenized with 10 wt% Super P conductive carbon additive and 10 wt% polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) solvent (Shenzhen Kejing) through continuous stirring for 2 hours to form a uniform slurry. This slurry was subsequently coated onto commercial ultrathin carbon felt substrates (thickness ~80 μm; Suzhou Kangmai New Material) using a doctor-blading technique. The coated electrodes were dried in an oven at 55 °C for 24 hours to ensure complete solvent evaporation and structural integrity. By precisely adjusting the coating thickness, sulfur cathodes with a range of practical loadings—approximately 1, 2.6, 5, and 8 mg cm⁻²—were reproducibly fabricated to evaluate performance under varied conditions.

Battery Assembly: R2025-type coin cells were assembled in an argon-filled glove box using lithium metal anodes (14 mm diameter, 400 μm thickness; China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd.), Super P-coated Celgard 2325 separators (16.5 mm diameter, ~30 μm thickness; optimized for enhanced sulfur capacity utilization as reported previously)[1], and the prepared electrolytes. For thin sulfur cathodes with loadings of 1 or 2.6 mg cm⁻², 80 μL of electrolyte was used, while 25 μL was employed for thick cathodes (5 mg cm⁻²). For practical validation, pouch cells were fabricated by attaching Ni and Al tabs to a thin Li foil anode (100 μm thickness, 4 × 6 cm²) and a high-loading sulfur cathode (8 mg cm⁻², 4 × 6 cm²), respectively. The components—cathode, Super P-coated separator, and anode—were stacked into a jellyroll configuration. After injecting electrolyte at a lean electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio of 4 μL mg⁻¹, the cells were hermetically sealed with aluminum laminated films.

Electrochemical Measurement: Galvanostatic charge-discharge tests of Li-S cells were performed on a multi-range battery testing system (Neware, CT-4000) at 25 °C. Prior to cycling, two formation cycles were conducted at 0.1 C (1 C = 1000 mA g⁻¹) within a voltage window of 1.7~2.8 V. To investigate Li₂S deposition morphology and kinetics, Li-Li₂S₆ and Li₂S₆-Li₂S₆ cells were constructed using carbon felt conductive substrates (16 mm diameter). Cyclic voltammetry (CV), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), and galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) were carried out on a BioLogic VMP3 electrochemical workstation. The sulfur redox mechanism was probed via CV at scan rates of 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, and 1 mV s⁻¹. EIS measurements were conducted over a frequency range of 10 mHz to 7 MHz with a 5 mV amplitude to determine interfacial impedance, while Li⁺ ion conductivity was evaluated using stainless steel (SS||SS) cells at room temperature. GITT profiles were generated by applying 0.1 C current pulses for 30 minutes, followed by 3-hour relaxation intervals.  

Material characterizations: Post-cycling electrodes were carefully retrieved by disassembling the cells in an argon-filled glove box to prevent air exposure. The harvested electrodes were gently rinsed with anhydrous DME to remove residual electrolyte salts and soluble species, followed by drying at room temperature under an inert atmosphere. To maintain surface integrity during ex situ analyses, the samples were transferred using airtight holders—such as XPS vacuum transfer chambers—to avoid oxidation or contamination. Surface morphology and elemental distribution were examined using a field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM; Tescan MAIA3) equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) detector. Chemical composition and interfacial properties of the cycled electrodes were characterized by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) on a Thermo Fisher Scientific Nexsa system with an Al Kα radiation source (1486.6 eV). All XPS spectra were calibrated based on the adventitious carbon C 1s peak at 284.8 eV to ensure accurate binding energy alignment.
Note 1. Lithium salt selection
While the theoretical promise of salts like LiBr and LiI based on their individual DN and mass characteristics, we believe a successful ULE design requires a salt that delivers a balanced performance across interfacial compatibility with Li metal, chemical compatibility with solvents, and comprehensive ionic transport properties. LiFSI, despite its known challenge with DOL (which we engineered around), currently presents a more favourable overall profile.

Note 2. Electrolyte density measurement
The density of the formulated electrolytes, including the reported value of 0.89 g cm⁻³ for the LiFSI-ULE, was determined precisely using the gravimetric method (mass/volume). The measurement was performed under an inert argon atmosphere inside a glovebox (H₂O < 0.1 ppm, O₂ < 0.1 ppm) to prevent any moisture absorption or reaction that could alter the electrolyte composition and density. A clean, dry 1 mL volumetric flask was weighed using a high-precision analytical balance (Model: METTLER TOLEDO, readability: 0.01 mg). The mass of the empty flask was recorded. The flask was then filled with the electrolyte using a calibrated, high-precision micropipette (Eppendorf Research® plus, accuracy: ± 0.5%) to ensure the volume was accurately 1 mL. The mass of the flask containing the electrolyte was immediately measured again using the same analytical balance. To ensure the highest accuracy, both the analytical balance and the 1 mL volumetric flask were calibrated prior to use. The balance was calibrated using certiﬁed standard weights traceable to national standards. The volumetric flask was also certiﬁed (Class A) for its accuracy. The aforementioned measurement procedure was repeated independently five times for each electrolyte batch to obtain a statistically significant average value. The results for the LiFSI-ULE exhibited excellent reproducibility, with a very small standard deviation (< ± 0.005 g cm-3), confirming the reliability of our measurement.
