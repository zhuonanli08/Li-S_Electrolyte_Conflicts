1. Experimental Procedures 
1.1. Material preparation 
Raw materials: All raw materials, including elemental sulfur (S8), lithium sulfide (Li2S), Benzo-
15-crown-5 (B15C5), polypropylene/polyethylene (PP/PE) Separator, poly (vinylidene fluoride) 
(PVDF), N-methyl pyrrolidone (NMP), aluminum (Al) foil, lithium (Li) foil, 1,3-dioxane (DOL), 1,2-
diamethoxyethane (DME), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonic)imide 
(LiTFSI), carbon cloth (CC), carbon nanotubes (CNT) and Super P were all purchased from 
commercial sources. The lithium-sulfur (Li-S) electrolyte consists of 1 M LiTFSI and 1% LiNO3 
dissolved in DOL/DME (v/v = 1:1) solvent. 
Preparation of electrolytes: B15C5-based electrolytes with the concentration ranging from 0 
to 100 mM were obtained by adding corresponding quality of B15C5 into Li-S electrolyte and 
stirring at room temperature until the solution is clear. The Li2S8 solution was prepared by mixing 
S and Li2S at a molar ratio of 7:1 in Li-S electrolyte and the brownish-red Li2S8 solution (100 
mM) was obtained after ball-milling for 1 h. The Li2S8 solution with 50 mM B15C5 was obtained 
through the same method. The Li2S4 solution was prepared by mixing S and Li2S at a molar 
ratio of 3:1 in DOL/DME solvent and the brownish-red Li2S4 solution (100 mM) was obtained 
after ball-milling for 1 h. The Li2S4 solution with 50 mM B15C5 was obtained through the same 
method. 
Electrode preparation: The slurry of sulfur cathodes was prepared by mixing 80 wt% of 
compounds composed of carbon nanotubes (CNT) and sulfur (1:3 weight ratio), 10 wt% of Super 
P, and 10 wt% of polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP). And the 
obtained slurry was uniformly coated on the Al foil and dried at 80 ºC for 12 h. Electrodes were 
cut out by the MSK-T10 manual slicer. The sulfur loading of cathode in  coin cells ranges from 
1.0 mg cm-2 to 1.4 mg cm-2. The high sulfur loading in the coin battery is about 3.6 and 6.2 mg 
cm-2. The sulfur loading of cathode in  pouch cells is 4 mg cm-2. 
1.2. Electrochemical measurements

Equipment: The galvanostatic charge-discharge test, Li-Li symmetric cell test, galvanostatic 
intermittent titration technique (GITT) test and Li2S nucleation test were all carried out on 
NEWARE Battery Test System (CT-4008Tn 5V10mA-164, Shenzhen, China). Electrochemical 
workstation (Wuhan Corrtest Instruments Corp., Ltd.) was used to test cyclic voltammetry (CV), 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) and Li2S6 
symmetric cells. 
Battery measurement: We assembled Li-S batteries for electrochemical tests with Li foil as 
anodes, electrolytes with 0-100 mM B15C5, polypropylene/polyethylene (PP/PE) separators. All 
the assembly work were conducted in a glovebox under pure argon atmosphere. The voltage 
window of galvanostatic ranges from 1.7 to 2.8 V. The currents of 0.1 C, 0.2 C, 0.5 C, 1.0 C, 2.0 
C were set to investigate the rate performance, and the cycling test was carried out under the 
current of 0.2 C and 2.0 C. The GITT curves were obtained by setting a pulse time of 7.5 minutes 
with a current of 0.08 C and a relaxation time of 75 minutes respectively. CV test was conducted 
at the scanning rates of 0.1 mV s−1-0.5 mV s−1 with a potential range of 1.7-2.8 V. 
Linear sweep voltammetry: The CC/Electrolyte/Li batteries were assembled and the LSV test 
was conducted at a scanning speed of 3 mV s−1 to investigate the electrochemical stability of 
electrolyte with and without B15C5. 
Ion conductivities: we assembled the Stainless Steel/Electrolyte/Stainless Steel batteries and 
conducted the EIS test at 25 oC. The corresponding ionic conductivities were calculated as

Li-Li symmetric battery measurements: Two Li folis were assembled into a standard 2032-
coin cell with a 20 mm diameter separator, and 25 μL of electrolyte was dropped on each side. 
Electrolyte types include blank and B15C5 electrolytes. 
Li2S nucleation tests: We used Li foil as the anode, porous PP/PE as the separator, CC as 
the positive current collector and different types of electrolytes (the positive and negative 
electrodes were Li2S8/B15C5+Li2S8 electrolytes and ordinary Li-S electrolyte, respectively) to 
assemble the batteries. The batteries were galvanostatically discharged to 2.10 V at 0.112 mA, 
and then potentiostatic discharged at 2.09 V to promote Li2S nucleation and growth until the 
current was below 0.01 mA. 
Pouch cell assembly and measurements: The Li-S pouch cell was assembled using CNT@S 
as cathode, PP/PE as separator, 50 μm Li foil as anode, and electrolyte with B15C5 additive, 
with an E/S ratio of 8 μL mg−1. To assemble the pouch cell, the cathode and anode were stacked 
layer-by-layer with a separator. The Li-S pouch batteries were fabricated with a sulfur loading 
of 4.0 mg cm−2. The pouch cells were galvanostatically cycled at. 0.1 C within a voltage range 
of 1.7-2.8 V. 
1.3. Materials characterization 
7Li, 13C and 19F nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded on a JNM-ECZ 
spectrometer with deuterated chloroform as solvent. Electrospray ionization mass spectrometry 
(ESI-MS) used an Agilent 1260 Infinity HPLC system equipped with an Eclipse Plus C18 column 
(1.8 μm, 3.0 × 50 mm) under reversed-phase conditions, with a mobile phase gradient of 0.1% 
formic acid in acetonitrile/water (5% to 95% acetonitrile over 7 min) at a flow rate of 0.5 mL min−1. 
Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) analysis was performed on a Nicolet IS5 
spectrometer in the range of 400 to 4000 cm−1 to verify the covalent bonds of long-chain 
polysulfide polymers. Raman spectra (Renishaw inVia laser confocal Raman spectrometer) 
were conducted to verify the composition of Li-S electrolytes with and without B15C5. And 
viscosity data were collected using a Thermo HAAKE MARS 60 rheometer equipped with a 27 
mm diameter dual-slit coaxial cylinder, across a temperature of 25 oC.

To verify the manipulation of B15C5 toward Li2S deposition, the samples were prepared by 
grinding the carbon nanotubes and Li2S dissolved in absolute ethyl alcohol in a mortar and 
drying them to powder in a glove box purged with argon. Synchrotron X-ray total scattering 
measurements were performed at the BL13SSW beamline of the Shanghai Synchrotron 
Radiation Facility (SSRF). The center energy of the radiated X-rays was 50.00 keV, 
corresponding to a wavelength λ=0.2480 Å. The pair distribution function (PDF) was obtained 
from the raw total scattering data after corrections for environmental scattering, incoherent and 
multiple scattering, polarization and absorption using xPDFsuite software with Qmax=16.1 Å−1. 
The unit cell of Li2S was expanded to a 10×10×10 super-cell (about 57×57×57 Å3) with 
containing 12000 atoms (including 8000 Li atoms, and 4000 S atoms) as the initial model of 
reverse Monte Carlo (RMC) simulation. A big box fitting for PDF data was carried out by 
RMCprofile, combining atomic moves. The Li2S deposition morphologies and microstructures 
of the sulfur electrodes were characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). For TEM characterization of Li2S samples, the 
specimen was prepared through the following procedure: Li2S powder was first collected by 
scraping from the CC substrate surface. The obtained powder was then homogeneously mixed 
with graphene flakes and subjected to ultrasonic dispersion in DOL/DME, followed by vacuum 
drying at 60°C for 12 hours. 
1.4. In situ XRD patterns measurements 
In situ XRD tests were performed to directly monitor the sulfur evolution in the overall 
electrochemical reaction process. A special cell was assembled using a beryllium window for X-
ray penetration. CNT@S was used as a cathode, and Li foil as anode, electrolytes with and 
without B15C5 were added. In situ XRD measurement was determined by a XRD system during 
the discharge and charge between 1.7 and 2.8 V at 0.1 C. 
1.5. Theoretical simulation 
All calculations were carried out with the Gaussian 16 A 03 software. The B3LYP functional was 
adopted for all calculations in combination with the Grimmer’s D3(BJ) dispersion correction. For 
geometry optimization and frequency calculations, the def2-SVP basis set was used for all 
atoms. The singlet point energy calculations were performed with a larger basis set def2-TZVP.

The polarizable continuum model (PCM) implicit solvation model was used to account for the 
DOL/DME solvation effect when performing single point energy calculations. The static dielectric 
constant parameter of the solvent was set to 7.1 according to the mixture volume ratio (1:1) of 
the solvent. All wave function analyses (electrostatic potential, molecular surface quantitative 
analysis, electron density difference, and so on) as well as a drawing of various kinds of maps 
were finished via the Multiwfn code. Some isosurface maps were rendered by means of the 
VMD visualization program based on the files exported by Multiwfn.

Figure S13. CV curves of the Li-S batteries (a) with and (b) without B15C5 at different scanning 
rate from 0.1 mV s–1 to 0.5 mV s–1. 
Note: Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were conducted at scan rates of 0.1-0.5 mV s−1. During the 
negative scan, the C1 and C2 peaks correspond to the transformation of S8 into soluble LiPSs 
and the subsequent conversion of LiPSs into Li2S, respectively. In the positive scan, the A1 and 
A2 peaks correspond to the oxidation of Li2S back to LiPSs and the further conversion of LiPSs 
to S8, respectively. At a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1, the peak potentials for C2 and A1 shift from 
1.95 V and 2.33 V to 2.00 V and 2.31 V, respectively, when B15C5 is introduced. This smaller 
polarization indicates an improved conversion kinetics for the battery with B15C5. Moreover, 
the sharper C2 peaks for the battery with B15C5 also suggests an improved redox capability for 
the Li2S conversion. As the scan rate increases, the battery with B15C5 exhibits a higher current 
response in the second reduction region, and at a high scan rate of 0.5 mV s−1 the current 
response difference is most pronounced. These results indicate that the B15C5 effectively 
activates more sulfur species under high current density, thereby enhancing the overall redox 
kinetics.

Figure S21. Comparison of molecular orbital energy levels with the same sulfur chain length. 
The green and blue regions represent the positive and negative parts of the LUMO and HOMO 
isosurface, respectively. The pink, yellow, red, cyan, and white balls denote the Li, S, O, C, and

Note: In each [2B15C5·Li2Sx] (x=1, 2, 4) system, the energy difference between the lowest 
unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) 
level of 3.51 eV, 3.18 eV and 3.69 eV are significantly smaller than those of the free Li2Sx (x=1, 
2, 4) (4.38 eV, 4.03 eV, and 3.79 eV). A narrower energy difference indicates higher conductivity 
and faster charge transfer. In particular, B15C5 makes the LUMO and HOMO energy levels of 
Li2S/Li2S2 in the solid phase a significant positive shift. The lower a molecule’s LUMO energy 
level, the easier it is to accept electrons into a more stable state. The higher HOMO energy level 
of a molecule indicates a greater likelihood of losing electrons to a stable state. The results 
verified that the chemical properties of sulfur species are changed by coordination.
