In lithium-sulfur batteries, cell design, speciﬁcally electrolyte design, has a key impact on the battery per-
formance. The effect of lithium salt anion donor number (DN) (DN[PF6] = 2.5, DN[N(SO2CF3)2] = 5.4, DN
[ClO4] = 8.4, DN[SO3CF3] = 16.9, and DN[NO3] = 21.1) on the patterns of lithium-sulfur batteries and
lithium metal electrode performances with sulfolane-based electrolytes is investigated. An increase in DN
of lithium salt anions leads to an increase in the depth and rate of electrochemical reduction of sulfur and
long-chain lithium polysulﬁdes and to a decrease in those for medium- and short-chain lithium polysul-
ﬁdes. DN of lithium salt anions has weak effect on the discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur batteries and
the Coulomb efﬁciency during cycling, with the exception of LiSO3CF3 and LiNO3. An increase in DN of
lithium salt anions leads to an increase in the cycling duration of lithium metal anodes and to a decrease
in the presence of lithium polysulﬁdes. In sulfolane solutions of LiNO3 and LiSO3CF3, lithium polysulﬁdes
do not affect the cycling duration of lithium metal anodes.

1. Introduction
Lithium-sulfur batteries (LSBs), which have been intensively
studied in the last decade, are classiﬁed as one of the most promis-
ing energy storage systems beyond Li-ion batteries [1–3]. Lithium-
sulfur batteries are liquid cathode batteries, since the active mate-
rials of the positive electrode - elemental sulfur [4] and the inter-
mediate
products
of
its
electrochemical
reduction,
lithium
polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn) - are soluble in the electrolytes [5–7]. Electro-
chemical transformations of sulfur and lithium polysulﬁdes occur
into sorption layers on the surface of carbon particles that entering
in the LSBs sulfur electrode. Species of sulfur and lithium polysul-
ﬁdes into the sorption layers on the surface of carbon particles are
determined by the properties of the components of electrolytes
regarding solvents and lithium salts.
Lithium polysulﬁdes are salts of polysulfonic acids [8]. In solu-
tions of aprotic dipolar solvents, lithium polysulﬁdes undergo elec-
trolytic [9,10] and homolytic dissociation [11,12]. The presence of
lithium salts in the electrolytes of LSBs affects the degree of elec-
trolytic dissociation of lithium polysulﬁdes, and consequently,
their species and reactivity. The degree of inﬂuence of lithium salts
will be determined by their dissociating ability and concentration.
The high reactivity of lithium polysulﬁdes limits the list of sol-
vents and lithium salts applicable as components of LSB elec-
trolytes. Lithium polysulﬁdes are able to interact with organic
carbonates
(ethylene
carbonate,
propylene
carbonate,
etc.)
[13,14] and lithium salts with complex anions (LiPF6, LiBF4), which
are the most widely used components of electrolytes of lithium-ion
batteries [15,16].
Therefore, as solvents used in LSBs electrolytes, mixtures of 1,2-
dimethoxyethane (DME) with 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) in various ratios
are most often used (more than 40% of all scientiﬁc publications).
Solvents of the glyme family including dimethyl ethers of tri-
ethylene glycol (TRIEGDME), tetraethylene glycol (TEGDME), and
polyethylene glycol (PEGDME) are also quite widely used as elec-
trolyte solvents (in about 16% of scientiﬁc publications) [17]. The
main disadvantage of electrolytes based on DME, DOL, their mix-
tures, and other ethers is the formation of vapors of low-boiling
solvents or gaseous products during LSBs operation, which are
formed during their reductive degradation and adversely affects
the safety of LSBs [18].

Lithium bis(triﬂuoromethanesulfonyl)imide (more than 56% of
publications), lithium triﬂuoromethanesulfonate, and lithium hex-
aﬂuorophosphate (almost 17% of publications for each salt) are
widely used as lithium salts in LSB electrolytes. The degree of
knowledge of the properties of LSBs with electrolytes based on
lithium perchlorate and other lithium salts is signiﬁcantly lower,
accounting for 4.5% and 4.1% of publications, respectively. Lithium
tetraﬂuoroborate are the least frequently mentioned - only 1.2% of
publications [17], also LiSCN [19,20], LiFSI [21,22], LiBOB [23,24], Li
[B(OCH2CF3)4] [25], LiODFB [26,27], LiBr [28,29], LiI [28], and LiBETI
(LiN(SO2CF2CF3)2 [30].
To improve the electrochemical performance of LSBs, binary or
ternary mixtures of lithium salts are often used, which exhibit a
synergistic effect: LiFSI + LiTFSI [31], LiTFSI + LiODFB [32]. Lithium
nitrate is also used in the composition of LSB electrolytes as an
additive that increases the Coulomb efﬁciency and the duration
of the lithium metal anode cycling.
In the recent years, in the development of electrolytes for LSBs,
increasing attention has been paid to sulfones as the most promis-
ing solvents [13,33]. Among a number of sulfones, sulfolane [6,34–
36] has the greatest interest, since it has a high dielectric constant,
readily dissolves lithium salts of various nature, including lithium
polysulﬁdes, and has high oxidative stability (up to 6.3 V relative to
Li/Li+) and high ﬂash point (>165 C). In addition, sulfolane is non-
toxic and does not form gaseous products when interacting with
metallic lithium, which increases the ﬁre and explosion safety of
sulfolane-based electrolytes.
Despite the potential interest in sulfolane-based electrolytes,
there are no systematic studies of the inﬂuence of the nature of
lithium salt anions on the regularities of the electrochemical pro-
cesses in LSBs. From phenomenological considerations, it can be
assumed that both the nature of solvents and the properties of
lithium salt anions will affect the entire set of processes occurring
in lithium-sulfur cells (LSCs) during their charge, discharge, and
storage.
However, based on the published data, it is difﬁcult to reveal
the effect of the properties of lithium salts on the regularities of
the processes in LSCs, since experimental studies were often car-
ried out under different conditions [37]. Therefore, in order to elu-
cidate the fundamental regularities of the processes in LSCs, it is
desirable to carry out studies under standardized conditions [38].
The aim of this work was to study the inﬂuence of the donor
number of lithium salt anions on the performance of LSCs in
sulfolane-based
electrolytes
under
standardized
experimental
conditions.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials and preparation of electrolyte
1 M solutions of lithium salts in sulfolane were studied as LSB
electrolytes: lithium hexaﬂuorophosphate (LiPF6, battery grade,
99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich), lithium perchlorate (LiClO4, battery
grade,
99.995%,
Aldrich),
lithium

(LiSO3CF3, 99.995%, Aldrich), lithium bis(triﬂuoromethanesulfo

lithium nitrate (LiNO3, 99.99%, Sigma-Aldrich). The water content
in the electrolytes, which was determined by coulometric titration
in Fischer reagent medium using a Titroline7500 KF trace auto-
matic titrator (SI Analytics), was 50 ppm.
Physicochemical properties of sulfolane solutions of lithium
salts (1 M) have been taken from article [39]. The speciﬁc ion con-
ductivity of sulfolane solutions of lithium salts (1 M) was deter-
mined by the bridge method at a frequency of 1000 Hz using an
E7-8 ac bridge in glass two-electrode cells of a capillary design
with platinum electrodes. The viscosity of electrolyte solutions
was measured in suspended-level Ubbelohde viscometers. The
accuracy in determining speciﬁc ion conductivity and viscosity
was 0.1%–0.5%.
2.2. Preparation and characterization of lithium polysulﬁde solutions
Lithium polysulﬁdes in the studied electrolytes were prepared
by reacting sulfur (S, 99.5%, Acros) and lithium sulﬁde (Li2S,
99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich) in a molar ratio of 7:1 at 30 C. The weight
ratio of the mixture of sulfur and lithium sulﬁde to the electrolyte
was 1:9.2. The solubility of the lithium polysulﬁdes was calculated
using the concentration of the S2. Concentration of the S2 (mol/
kg) in the sulfolane solutions of lithium polysulﬁdes was deter-
mined by back iodometric titration (with potentiometric indication
of the end point of the titration) using a TitroLine5000 automatic
titrator (SI Analytics).
2.3. Electrochemical measurements
All electrochemical studies were performed in sealed two-
electrode disk cells of the Swagelok cell type, made of stainless
steel. The electrode pressure into assembled cells was controlled
using calibrated springs and amounted to LSCs was 0.3 kg/cm2
and that in lithium-lithium cells was 1 kg/cm2. Galvanostatic
polarization of LSCs was carried out using a BioLogic BCS-810 bat-
tery tester and lithium-lithium cells - using a Biologic SP-200
potentiostat/galvanostat. Before the start of the experiment, the
cells after assembly were thermostated for 24 h at 30 C.
The preparation of the electrolytes and lithium polysulﬁde solu-
tions, the assembly of LSCs and lithium-lithium cells were all car-
ried out in a glove box under argon atmosphere (the contents of
water and oxygen were <0.1 ppm).
2.3.1. Cycling of lithium-sulfur cells
In LSCs, sulfur electrode composition was 70 wt% of S (99.5%,
Acros), 10 wt% of Ketjenblack EC-600JD (Akzo Nobel), and 20 wt
% of polyethylene oxide (MW 4  106, Aldrich). The sulfur loading
in electrodes was 2 mA h/cm2, which is equivalent to a sulfur con-
tent of about 1.2 mg/cm2. Auxiliary electrodes (disc-shaped with a
diameter of 2.53 cm, A = 5.02 cm2) were made of a 100 lm thick
metallic lithium foil (99.9%, China Cell Energy, PRC). A single layer
of Celgard3501 was used as the separator. Disc-shaped sulfur
electrodes and separators had a diameter of 2.85 cm, A = 6.38 cm2.
The amount of electrolyte in the electrochemical cells was 4 lL/
mA h(S), which is equivalent to 1 lL/0.15 mg of sulfur (6.7 lL/mg
of sulfur). The electrolyte was injected into the cells using a
MICROLITERTM Syringe microsyringe (Hamilton Company, USA)
with a volume of 50 lL. The electrolyte dosing accuracy in the cells
was ±5%.
Galvanostatic cycling of LSCs was carried out in the voltage
range of 1.5–2.8 V (for LiPF6, LiClO4, and LiSO3CF3) and 1.7–2.8 V
(for LiN(CF3SO2)2 and LiNO3). The charge and discharge current
densities were 0.1 mA/cm2 (0.05 C, 84 mA/g(S)) and 0.2 mA/cm2
(0.1 C, 168 mA/g(S)), respectively. The cycling tests were per-
formed at a temperature of 30 C. The scatter of data did not
exceed ± 10%.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Effect of the lithium salt anion donor number on LSCs performance
in sulfolane-based electrolytes
The effect of the nature of the lithium salt anions, included into
electrolytes, on the patterns of electrochemical processes occurring
in the LSCs can most conveniently be judged by the shape of the
discharge and charge voltage proﬁles obtained in the ﬁrst cycle.
In the ﬁrst cycle there are no distortions, introduced by changes
in the properties of electrodes and electrolytes during long-term
cycling.
The charge and discharge voltage proﬁles obtained by us for
LSCs with sulfolane-based electrolytes with various lithium salts
are typical for LSCs with electrolytes with a different composition.
On the discharge and charge voltage proﬁles of the LSCs, two pla-
teaus are observed regarding a high-voltage plateau, related to the
electrochemical transformations of sulfur and long-chain lithium
polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn; n > 4) and a low-voltage plateau corresponding
to the electrochemical transformations of medium- and short-
chain lithium polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn; n < 4).
The obtained experimental data show that the properties of
lithium salt anions (Table 1) affect the depth of electrochemical
reduction of sulfur, the shape of the discharge and charge voltage
proﬁles, and the voltage in the high-voltage and low-voltage pla-
teaus of the charge and discharge voltage proﬁles (Fig. 2a and c,
respectively). The greatest depth of electrochemical reduction of
sulfur is achieved in sulfolane solution of 1 M LiSO3CF3, and the
smallest - in sulfolane solution of 1 M LiPF6. It should be noted that
a higher depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur is achieved
mainly due to the reduction of short-chain lithium polysulﬁdes.
Good linear correlations are observed between the discharge and
charge capacities of the LSCs and the donor properties of the
lithium salt anions (Fig. 2b and d).
The values of discharge voltages make it possible to judge the
rates of electrochemical processes. The magnitude of the discharge
voltage in the high-voltage plateau of the LSC discharge voltage
proﬁles was judged from the voltage during sulfur reduction to a
depth of 104.5 mA h/g(S), i.e. by the termination voltage of one-
electron reduction of sulfur (Fig. 3a). The discharge voltage of the
low-voltage plateau of the LSC discharge voltage proﬁles was taken
to be the maximum voltage in the low-voltage plateau, i.e. voltage
at the maximum rate of electrochemical reduction of medium- and
short-chain lithium polysulﬁdes.
The nature of the lithium salt anion also affects the shape of the
charge voltage proﬁles (Fig. 2c), to a greater extent, it affects the
charging voltage of LSCs at the low-voltage plateau. The LSCs with
sulfolane solution of 1 M LiSO3CF3 exhibit the lowest overvoltage
on the low-voltage plateau while the highest overvoltage is
observed with 1 M LiNO3. In the high-voltage plateau of the charg-
ing voltage proﬁles, the highest overvoltage is observed for LSCs
with sulfolane solution of 1 M LiNO3 and the smallest - with sul-
folane solution of 1 M LiN(SO2CF3)2.
Our results show that the donor properties of anions have the
strongest effect on the voltage of the high-voltage plateau of the
discharge voltage proﬁles. The voltage of low-voltage plateaus
depends on the nature of the anions of lithium salts to a lesser
extent (Fig. 3b).
Thus, the rate of electrochemical reduction of sulfur and long-
chain lithium polysulﬁdes increases (the discharge voltage at the
high-voltage plateau increases), while medium- and short-chain
lithium polysulﬁdes initially slightly increases and then decreases
with an increase in the donor properties of lithium salt anions
(Fig. 3b). The lowest rate of electrochemical reduction of lithium
polysulﬁdes occurs in sulfolane solutions of lithium salts with
high-donor anions (LiNO3 and LiSO3CF3).
During cycling, the discharge capacity of the LSCs decreases
(Fig. 4a,b). The rate and the depth of capacity depletion of LSCs dur-
ing cycling are determined by the donor properties of lithium salt
anions. The discharge capacity of the LSCs with a sulfolane solution
of lithium nitrate decreases most rapidly and profoundly (Fig. 4a).
In the case of LiSO3CF3, the capacity decreases by 20% during 50
cycles, in the case of lithium salts with weakly donor anions (LiPF6,
LiN(SO2CF3)2, LiClO4), the capacity of LSCs decreases by 50%–60%,
the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells decreases most strongly with
electrolytes based on LiNO3 - for 50 cycles by 70% (Fig. 4b).
The Coulomb efﬁciency of LSCs cycling is determined by the
intensity of interelectrode transfer of lithium polysulﬁdes and
the barrier properties of surface ﬁlms formed on lithium metal
anodes (SEI). The Coulomb efﬁciency of LSCs cycling decreases
most rapidly at the initial stages (up to approximately 30–40
cycles). With further LSCs cycling, the Coulomb efﬁciency increases
and then stabilizes (Fig. 4c). The highest Coulomb efﬁciency of
cycling is observed for the LSCs with sulfolane solution of 1 M
LiNO3 and the lowest - for 1 M LiSO3CF3. The inﬂuence of the nat-
ure of the supporting salt anion on the Coulomb efﬁciency of
cycling indicates that the chemical properties of the anions deter-

electrolytes based on other lithium salts. In general, the resistance
change rates of LSCs with electrolytes based on lithium salts with
weakly donor anions during long-term cycling are lower than
those with electrolytes based on lithium salts with high donor

3.2. Effect of the lithium salt anion donor number on lithium-lithium
cell cycling life in sulfolane solutions
For a deeper understanding of the effect of the donor properties
of lithium salt anions on the patterns of processes on the lithium
electrode of LSBs, we studied the patterns of cycling of lithium-
lithium cells with sulfolane solutions of lithium salts that were
unsaturated (Fig. 6a) and additionally saturated with lithium poly-
sulﬁdes (Fig. 6b).
Studies have shown that the effect of the donor properties of
lithium salts on the duration of lithium metal electrode cycling
in sulfolane solutions with and without lithium polysulﬁdes is dif-
ferent (Fig. 6). In sulfolane solutions of lithium salts without
lithium polysulﬁdes, the duration of cycling of a lithium metal
electrode increases with an increase in the donor properties of
anions (Fig. 6a and c), and with lithium polysulﬁdes it decreases
(Fig. 6b and d). The extent to which lithium polysulﬁdes inﬂuence
the cycling duration of the lithium metal electrode is determined
by the properties of the lithium salt anions (Fig. 6c and d). Thus,
lithium polysulﬁdes increase the duration of cycling of a lithium
metal electrode in sulfolane solutions of 1 M LiPF6 by 85 cycles,
of 1 M LiN(CF3SO2)2 by 60 cycles, and of 1 M LiClO4 by 20 cycles.
Lithium polysulﬁdes do not affect the duration of cycling of the
lithium metal electrode in sulfolane solutions of 1 M LiNO3 and
1 M LiSO3CF3.
To analyze the regularities and mechanism of the inﬂuence of
the solvation properties of lithium salt anions on electrochemical
processes in LSBs, we used the values of speciﬁc ion conductivity
and dynamic viscosity of 1 M solutions of studied lithium salts in
sulfolane at 30 C [39]. Studies have shown that with an increase
in the donor number of anions of lithium salts, the speciﬁc ion con-
ductivity, dynamic viscosity, and corrected ion conductivity (ion
conductivity corrected for viscosity) of their sulfolane solutions
decrease almost linearly (Fig. 7a–c, respectively). A decrease in
the value of the corrected ion conductivity indicates that an
increase in the donor number of lithium salt anions leads to a
decrease in the degree of their electrolytic dissociation (Fig. 7c).
An analysis of the results showed that there are no clear corre-
lations between the duration of LSCs cycling and any physicochem-
ical properties of electrolytes or lithium salt anions. However,
there is a general trend towards an increase in the discharge capac-
ity and duration of the LSCs cycling as the value of the corrected
ion conductivity of the electrolytes decreases (Fig. 7d). The excep-
tion is LSCs with a sulfolane solution of 1 M LiNO3.

The main problems hindering the commercialization of LSBs are
the high rate of capacity depletion during cycling and the low
depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur, especially at low elec-
trolyte/sulfur ratios.

The donor properties of the lithium salt anions also have a sig-
niﬁcant effect on the shape of the dependences of the capacity
depletion of the LSCs on the number of charge–discharge cycles
(Fig. 4a and b). The effect of the DN of lithium salt anions on the
change in the capacity of the LSCs during cycling manifests itself
in a complex way. For the salts with weakly donor anions (LiPF6,
LiN(SO2CF3)2, LiClO4), the discharge capacity rapidly decreases at
the initial stages of cycling, and with an increase in the donor prop-
erties of anions, the degree of discharge capacity depletion
increases (from 40% to 50%).

 and NO3

the shapes of the dependences of the discharge capacity of the LSCs

The nitrate anion has the highest donor properties among the
studied anions; it can be easily reduced by metallic lithium and
can interact with lithium polysulﬁdes [48,49]. Probably, due to
the high chemical activity of the NO3
, the capacity of LSCs with sul-
folane solutions of LiNO3 decreases most rapidly.
Signiﬁcant differences in the nature of the change in the capac-
ity of LSCs with electrolytes based on lithium salts with weakly
donor anions (LiPF6, LiN(SO2CF3)2, LiClO4) and lithium salt with a
high donor anion (LiSO3CF3) are probably due to the features of
the processes occurring into the positive electrode.
A rapid capacity depletion of the LSCs at the initial stage of

The positive electrode of LSBs is a porous electrode. Electro-
chemical reduction of sulfur is carried out on the surface of carbon
particles included in sulfur electrode. The electrochemical reduc-
tion of sulfur and lithium polysulﬁdes occurs with the greatest rate
in the front areas of the positive electrode (electrode areas facing
the negative electrode). Due to differences in the rates of electro-
chemical transformations in the front and back areas of the posi-

which are generated at the initial stages and dissolved in the elec-
trolyte, arises. As a result, during the electrochemical reactions,
sulfur is transferred as lithium polysulﬁdes from the back to the
front areas of the positive electrode.
The ﬁnal products of electrochemical transformations regarding
elemental sulfur during the charge of the LSCs and lithium sulﬁde
during the discharge are poorly soluble in the electrolyte and, as
they form, are deposited on carbon particles, which ultimately
leads to the passivation of their surface. With a high degree of pas-
sivation of the surface of carbon particles, a part of depolarizer is
excluded from electrochemical processes.
The uneven distribution of electrochemical reactions over the
volume of the positive electrode also leads to an uneven distribu-
tion of solid-phase reaction products regarding sulfur and lithium
sulﬁde. To the greatest extent, the formation and accumulation of
insoluble products, leading to the passivation of the surface of car-
bon particles, will occur in the front areas of the positive electrode.

4. Conclusions
Herein, we investigated the impact of sulfolane-based elec-
trolytes with ﬁve lithium salts (1 M) with various donor number
of anion (LiPF6, LiN(SO2CF3)2, LiClO4, LiSO3CF3, and LiNO3) on the
electrochemical
performance
of
lithium-sulfur
and
lithium-
lithium cells. The studies performed have shown that the electro-
chemical behavior of LSCs with sulfolane solutions of LiPF6
(DN = 2.5), LiN(SO2CF3)2 (DN = 5.4) and LiClO4 (DN = 8.4) were sim-
ilar. On the contrary, the patterns of cycling of LSCs with sulfolane
solutions of LiSO3CF3 (DN = 16.9) or LiNO3 (DN = 21.1) as the elec-
trolytes differed signiﬁcantly from those of LSCs with electrolytes
based on LiPF6, LiN(SO2CF3)2, and LiClO4. The lower rate of capacity
depletion of lithium-sulfur cells with LiSO3CF3-based electrolyte
can be explained by the slower rate in passivation of the surface
of particles of carbon materials entering sulfur electrode. The high
rate of capacity depletion of LSCs with LiNO3-based electrolyte is
possibly associated with a decrease in the concentration of lithium
nitrate as a result of interaction with metallic lithium and lithium
polysulﬁdes.
In sulfolane solutions of lithium salts, the duration of cycling of
a lithium metal electrode increases with an increase in the donor
properties of anions. Introduce of lithium polysulﬁdes increases
the duration of cycling of lithium metal electrode in sulfolane solu-
tions of LiPF6, LiN(CF3SO2)2, and LiClO4, but does not affect the
duration of cycling in sulfolane solutions of LiSO3CF3 and LiNO3.
