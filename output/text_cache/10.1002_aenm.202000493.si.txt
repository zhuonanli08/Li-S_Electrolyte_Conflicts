Figure S1. 7Li chemical shifts of the co-salt electrolytes versus a standard solution. 7Li 
nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were measured as a function of the electrolyte’s 
lithium nitrate (LiNO3) concentration. The chemical shift values were calibrated with the Li 
chemical shift of a 1 M lithium chloride (LiCl) in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)-d6 standard 
solution correspondent to 0 ppm.[1] The 1.0 M lithium ion (Li+) concentration was fixed and 
the anion ratio was only varied for each electrolyte. The salt concentrations for the 
electrolytes were provided using the notation of x:y. For example, the electrolyte labelled 
“95:5” contained 0.95 M lithium bistriflimide (LiTFSI) and 0.05 M LiNO3. 
 The 7Li NMR chemical shift reflects changes in the chemical environment around Li nuclei. 
Since molecules in the first solvation shell are the most adjacent environment around Li+, the 
species in the first solvation strongly affects the Li chemical shift either by de-shielding 
(moving to positive chemical shifts/downfield) or shielding (moving to negative chemical 
shifts/upfield) of the Li nucleus.[2] In liquid electrolytes, the first solvation constitutes a 
stronger de-shielding environment when with the higher number of anionic coordination or 
electronegative ligands, thus the NMR peaks will shift to downfield under the circumstances 
of the higher coordination.[3]

Figure S2. An enlarged optical image of polysulfide coloration under the different electrolyte 
compositions. 1 mM of lithium polysulfide (LiPS) solutions were prepared using the four 
electrolyte samples varying the LiTFSI:LiNO3 ratio. It was reported that S4
2− displays a 
yellow color in a 1,3-dioxolane (DOL):1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) solution, whereas S3•−

number (DN) anion amount increases, the color turns from yellow to green, which supports

concentrations show deeper color gradients, expecting the higher concentrations of the

polysulfide anions’ distribution and their solvated amounts, which we observed from the UV-

Figure S6. Current response observation for choosing the two-step voltages. A preliminary 
pre-discharge step is required before collecting the current signals from the 
chronoamperometry (CA) analysis. It is because the reduction current of the long-chain 
polysulfides may overlap with the current from the lithium sulfide (Li2S) electrodeposition 
process, interfering an accurate observation and a well-matched model fitting. a) Cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) (0.1 mV s-1) of the Li-S cells gives brief information on the applicable step 
voltages for the pre-discharge and Li2S electrodeposition process. Current response data were 
recorded by applying different voltages of b) 2.15 V and c) 2.05 V to confirm that 2.15 V is in 
an appropriate range for the pre-discharge step. 
 The reduction current of the soluble polysulfide species should follow an exponentially 
decaying behavior.[5] However, when the potentiostatic discharge operation occurs with the 
process of electrodeposition, as the electrode areas change, the current response no longer 
follows the exponential decay function. The discharge current showed a clear exponentially 
decaying behavior at 2.15 V. It proves that 2.15 V is a reasonable step voltage to proceed the 
pre-reduction of the long-chain polysulfides to short-chain polysulfides without any 
perturbation on the electrode surface area.

Figure S7. A representative current plot and schematic illustration for the potentiostatic 
discharge of a lithium-sulfur cell. Applied voltage data and their corresponding current 
response were obtained at 2.05 V and are shown in a–b). In addition, schematic illustrations 
of electrode interface reactions are provided in c). 
 The initial current drops until the reduction of the residual long-chain polysulfides are 
completed. Subsequently, the current response is reversed, reflecting that a nucleation and 
growth process has initiated (marked as “1” in the figures). At the beginning of the 
electrodeposition step, the current rises because the electrode area increases with the 2D or 3D 
growth of the Li2S nuclei. (“2”) Then, the current re-drops (“3”) when either or both of the 
two following phenomena are proceeded on the electrode interface:[6] 
(1) The impingement of neighboring nuclei by surface growth of electro-crystallization 
(Bewick, Fleischmann, and Thirsk models) 
(2) The overlap of the 3-dimenisonal diffusion zone developed from the nuclei surface to an 
infinite radius of the concentration gradient (Scharifker–Hills models). 
Based on the theoretical background, we extracted the peak current (Im) and its 
corresponding time (tm) from the chronoamperograms of the four electrolytes. Because tm 
reflects the moment when nuclei impingement starts on the electrode (“3”), we can interpret 
that the increasing tm values as a function of the LiNO3 concentration comes from the delayed 
electrode passivation by the Li2S precipitates.
