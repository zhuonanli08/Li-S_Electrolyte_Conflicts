Experimental Section 
Preparation of SPAN cathode 
The PAN/CNT (PAN, Mw=150000, Sigma-Aldrich) precursor nanofibers were prepared via 
an electrospinning method. The PAN/CNT precursor films were vulcanized at 500 °C for 3 h 
with a ramping rate of 5 °C min−1 under a flowing nitrogen atmosphere, the specific 
preparation process has been described in detail in our previous work.[1] 
Preparation of electrolyte 
The electrolyte of I2-LiNO3-LiTFSI-DOL/DME was prepared by adding 1 M lithium 
lithiumbis(trifluoromethyl)sulfonateimide (LiTFSI, ≥99%, Sigma-Aldrich), 0.2 M lithium 
nitrate (LiNO3, ≥99%, Sigma-Aldrich), 0.01 M iodine (I2, Yonghua Chemical Technology Co., 
Ltd.) into 1,3-dioxolane/1,2-dimethoxyethane (DOL/DME, 1:1 by volume) in a glovebox 
filled with Ar (<0.1 ppm of H2O and O2). The iodine-free electrolyte of LiNO3-LiTFSI-
DOL/DME was also prepared as a control.  
Material characterizations 
The ultraviolet and visible spectrophotometry (UV–Vis, TU-1901, Beijing Purkinje General 
Instrument Co., Ltd.) was identify electrolyte components. The 1H and 13C unclear magnetic 
resonance (1H NMR, 13C NMR) spectrum was recorded on an AVANCE III 600 MHz NMR 
spectrometer using CDCl3 as the solvent. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra 
(Nicolet6700, Thermo Fisher) was detected in the range of 400–4000 cm–1 with the attenuated 
total reflectance accessory (ATR). Laser raman spectrometer (inVia Reflex, Renishaw) was 
performed in the range of Raman shift 100-2000 with excitation wavelength at 532 nm. The 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was performed with a JSM (7500F, JEOL) equipped 
with an energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS) attachment. X-ray diffraction (XRD) was 
measured on an X-ray diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (D/max–2550VB+/PC, Rigalcu). 
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was detected on a spectrometer (Escalab 250Xi,

Thermo Fisher) with Al Kα X-ray radiation. In-situ optical microscopy was obtained using an 
in-situ optical battery with an optical microscope camera (Beijing Scistar Technology Co., 
Ltd., LIB–MS–II).  
Electrochemical measurements 
The 2025 type coin cells were assembled in an argon-filled glove box (<0.1 ppm of H2O and 
O2) with polypropylene/polyethylene microporous membrane (Celgard 2325) as the separator. 
The LiǀǀLi symmetric cells were assembled with two symmetric Li-metal electrodes. The 
galvanostatical charge–discharge measurements were evaluated by the LAND battery tester 
(Wuhan, China). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was measured in the range 
of 0.1 Hz to 1000 kHz at an amplitude of 5 mV on a CHI660E electrochemical workstation 
(Shanghai Chenhua) and fitted with Zview. The ionic conductivity was measured with a 
symmetric stainless steel (SS) symmetrical cell and calculated by the following equation, 
σ=L/RS, in which the thickness (L), bulk resistance (R) and area (S) of electrolytes were 
available. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) measurement was performed to evaluate the 
oxidation potential in Li||SS cell in the voltage range of 1.0-5.0 V and reduction potential in 
Li||Cu cell in the voltage of 3.0-1.0 V with a scanning rate of 5 mV s-1. The open-circuit 
voltage (OCV) was tested by Li||Cu cell on LAND battery tester. The galvanostatic cycling of 
Li||SPAN cell was evaluated in the cut-off voltage of 1.0-3.0 V. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) 
of Li||SPAN full cells was tested at a scanning rate of 0.1 mV S–1 on a CHI760E 
electrochemical workstation.  
Computational methods 
Quantum chemistry calculations were performed using the density functional theory (DFT) 
method with the Dmol3 module in Material Studio software, employing the B3LYP 
functional, custom Grimme DFT-D2 parameters, and DNP 4.4 basis set. [2,3] All molecules 
were simulated in a solvent environment using the conductor-like screening model (COSMO), 
with acetone as the implicit solvent. The k-points were set at Gamma (1×1×1), and
