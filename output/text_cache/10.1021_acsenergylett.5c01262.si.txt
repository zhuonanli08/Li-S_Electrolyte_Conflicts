Materials and methods
1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME, 99.5%, anhydrous, inhibiter free, Aldrich) and 1,3-
dioxolane ((DOL, 99.5%, anhydrous, ~75 ppm BHT as inhibitor, Aldrich) were dried for 
several days over freshly activated molecular sieves (type 4 Å, Aldrich) without further 
purification. Lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, 99.95% trace metal 
basis, Aldrich) was dried at 120 °C under vacuum for 72 hours. Lithium nitrate (LiNO3 
99.99% trace metal basis, Aldrich) was dried at 90 °C under vacuum for 72 hours. 
Sulfur (325 mesh, Alfa Aesar) was dried at room temperature under vacuum and 
transferred to a N2-filled glovebox. Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) and carbon black 
were dried at 70 °C and under vacuum overnight. Gas diffusion layer carbon paper 
(GDL) was dried at 120 °C under vacuum overnight. NEI commercial cathodes (3.46 
mg cm-2) were dried under vacuum and punched into 12 mm diameter disks inside an 
Ar-filled glovebox (MBraun, O2 ≤0.1 ppm, H2O ≤0.1 ppm). All other cell components 
were dried at elevated temperatures under vacuum then transferred to an Ar-filled 
glovebox. All electrolytes were prepared in a N2-filled glovebox (MBraun, O2 ≤0.1 ppm, 
H2O ≤0.1 ppm).

Commercial NANOMYTE® BE-70E S/C cathodes were purchased from NEI 
corporation (Somerset, NJ, USA) and dried under vacuum overnight. The cathodes 
contain 70% active material (sublimed sulfur), 10% CMC-SBR binder, 20% carbon 
black composition and had an active material loading of 3.46 mg cm-2 on a carbon-
coated aluminium current collector.
In-house sulfur cathode fabrication was completed in a N2-filled glovebox. A ratio 
of 7:2:1 sulfur:carbon:binder was used for the in-house cathodes. Sulfur and carbon 
black were hand-ground using a mortar and pestle for 15 minutes under air-free 
conditions prior to mixing overnight with PVDF dissolved in N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone 
(NMP). 40 µL of the slurry was dropcast onto 10 mm diameter GDL disks and left to 
dry. All cathodes had a sulfur loading of ~2.2 mg cm-2.
Polysulfide synthesis followed literature procedure wherein solution of ‘Li2S8’ was 
synthesised using a 7:8 ratio of sulfur to lithium sulfide. Li2S and S8 were mixed in 
standard voltammetry electrolyte (1:1 DOL:DME, 1 M LiTFSI) at 60°C for 72 hours 
under an N2 atmosphere. The solution was allowed to stir for a further 48 hours at 
room temperature before filtering off the remaining solid, resulting in a 0.1 M stock of 
‘Li2S8’.1
Three-electrode electrochemical measurements were performed in a N2-filled 
glovebox using an SP300 potentiostat (Biologic). Measurements were taken at room 
temperature using iR correction. The analyte (2 mM) was dissolved in 1M LiTFSI in 
DOL:DME (1:1 v/v). For cyclic voltammetry, a coiled platinum wire was used as the 
counter electrode and glassy carbon (3 mm diameter) served as the working electrode. 
Measurements were taken versus a reference electrode based on LiFePO4 (LFP), 
which was pre-charged (20% of total capacity) to LixFePO4. For chronoamperometry, 
GDL was used as the working electrode and LFP as the counter electrode. Another 
LFP served as the reference electrode. A correction of 3.0 V was applied to correct 
between LFP and Li/Li+.
Ultraviolet-visible spectroscopy DPP measurements were recorded on a UV-vis 
NIR Agilent Cary spectrometer using air-free quartz cuvettes. The standard 
voltammetry electrolyte was used (1 M LiTFSI in DOL:DME 1:1 v/v) with an additive 
concentration of 0.022 mM. For the polysulfide concentration study, an Ocean Optics 
fibre optic UV-vis spectrometer was used under an N2 atmosphere.
Galvanostatic cycling measurements were performed using BCS-805/810 cyclers 
(Biologic) at 40 °C. CR2032 coin cells were constructed in an Ar-filled glovebox. 
Lithium ribbon was punched into 14 mm diameter to serve as the negative electrode. 
A 15 mm diameter Celgard2400 disk was used as the separator. A standard cell 
electrolyte was optimised (1 M LiTFSI, 0.5 M LiNO3 in DOL:DME 1:1 v/v), with 2 mM 
additive where appropriate.  An electrolyte to sulfur ratio of 20 was utilised in all coin 
cells. All capacities were normalised to the theoretical capacity of sulfur (1675 mA h g-
1).2
