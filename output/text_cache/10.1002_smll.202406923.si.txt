Replenish the electron-deficient state of carbonyl carbon atoms to achieve stable Li-S Batteries 
Experimental section:
Raw materials:
Bistri(fluoromethanesulfon)imide lithium (LiTFSI), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), 1,3- dimethyl-2-imidazolidinone (DMI), Tetramethylurea (TMU), 3-methyl-2-oxazolidinone (MOX), dimethyl carbonate (DMC), Ethylene carbonate (EC) and lithium sulfide (Li2S) were purchased from Macklin, China. 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) were purchased from DoDoChem, China. Elemental sulfur and Ketjen Black (S, 99.99 %, Aladdin). All the materials were kept in an Ar-filled glove box with oxygen and water content below 0.1 ppm.
Synthesis of Polysulfides:
Sulfur and Li2S were mixed with the ratio of 7:1 for Li2S8, and the ratio of 5:1 for Li2S6 in a DME solvent and stirred for 120h at 60 °C. The solvent was evaporated to obtain a solid Li2S8.
Characterizations:
The content changes of polysulfides were evaluated by UV-vis spectrophotometry. Raman spectra were obtained using a Raman spectrometer (LabRAM Hr800, Horida, USA) at frequencies between 1500 cm−1 and 500 cm−1. The morphology of the sulfur cathode 4 and Li anode were obtained by a Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM, HITACHI, SU8010, Japan). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to identify chemical compositions on the Li anode surface area after cycling. The 1H NMR spectra were recorded on Bruker 400 M (600 MHz) at room temperature.
Preparation of sulfur cathode:
Elemental sulfur and Ketjen Black were mixed at a weight ratio of 7/3 by ground with a mortar for 1h to make the mixture well mixed. The mixture was heated at 155 °C for 12 h. The S/KB electrodes were prepared by mixing 80 wt% KB/S composite powder, 10 wt% Super-P carbon and 10 wt% LA133 binders in water. The slurry was cast onto an Al foil and then dried at room temperature. The dried sulfur electrodes were punched into 12 mm disks for use. The loading of sulfur was about 1.0 mg cm -2.
Electrochemical characterization:
The electrochemical performances of Li-S coin cells were investigated in CR20 CR2025 with S/KB (70 %) as the working electrode, lithium foil as the anode, and Celgard 2400 film as the separator. The areal sulfur loading was fixed at about 1 mg cm−2and the E/S ratio for regular electrochemical test was 20:1 μL mg-1. As for the high sulfur loading, the electrolyte was kept at 7 μL mg-1. The cells were cycled at 1.7–2.8 V (versus Li/Li+) on the NEWARE test system (ShenZhen, China). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were measured on the electrochemical workstation CHI600D with a sweep rate of 0.1 mV s-1 between 1.7 V and 2.8 V. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test was investigated by PARSTAT 2273 electrochemical workstation in the frequency range from 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz. For the Li-Li symmetric cells, we used a protocol of 1 h of stripping followed by 1 h of plating with a current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 for each cycle. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was carried out using an aluminum current collector with a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1. For high temperature test, the cell was tested at 0.3C when temperature was 30,60 and 80 ℃, respectively. The cell was tested at 0.1C when temperature was 0 ℃.
Theoretical simulations:
The molecular dynamics simulations were used the Gromacs 2019 with all-atom optimized potential liquid simulation (OPLS-AA) force field.[1] Force field parameters for TFSI- and NO3- were obtained directly from the literature,[2] while force field parameters for other electrolyte components were generated using the LigParGen web server.[3] Calculate the 1.2-scaling CM5 charges using Mulitwfn program.[4] The PACKMOL program was used to construct the initial simulation boxes with dimensions of 70 × 70 × 70 Å3 and filled with electrolyte components.[5] First, the temperature is allowed to slowly ramp up from 0 K to 298 K in 1 ns, then, 10 cycles of quench-anneal kinetics were performed from 298 K to 698 K within 4 ns to eliminate the persistence of the metastable state. Finally, MD simulations were performed in the NPT system for 5 ns at a temperature of 298 K and a pressure of 1 bar. The final 5-ns NPT simulation was output every 5000 steps and used to analyze the radial distribution function (g(r)) and the solvation structure. 
The particle-mesh Ewald (PME) method with a cut-off distance of 12 Å was applied to treat the electrostatic interactions and the van der Waals forces. During the simulation, the temperature is controlled by the V-rescale algorithm and the pressure is controlled by the Parrinello-Rahman algorithm. Visualization of simulated structures using the Visual Molecular Dynamics program (VMD).[6] 
The Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were calculated by the Gaussian package. The geometry optimization was conducted at B3LYP/6-31+g (d) level and a higher level of B3LYP/6-311++g (d, p) was applied for the single point calculation. D3 dispersion correction was also adopted. The solvation effect was considered with the universal solvation model of PCM.

Fig.S17. Comparison of activation energies. (a) DOL/DME and (b) DMI-TMU-0.5M LiNO3. The corresponding activation energies of charge transfer (EaCT) before and after the cycling of Li-S cells are calculated according to Arrhenius equation. As shown in figure S17, the EaCT of Li-S battery in DOL/DME and DMI-TMU-0.5M LiNO3 electrolyte is 43.23 kJ mol−1 and 38.49 kJ mol−1, respectively. After 50 cycles, the EaCT of Li-S battery in DOL/DME and DMI-TMU-0.5M LiNO3 electrolyte is 40.98 kJ mol−1 and 34.51 kJ mol−1, respectively. Obviously, the DMI-TMU-0.5M LiNO3 electrolyte has a lower EaCT than DOL/DME, indicating that the designed electrolyte has a faster charge transfer process at all temperatures.
