1. Raw materials. 
1,3-dioxolane (DOL, battery grade, DoDoChem), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, battery 
grade, DoDoChem), phenethylamine (PEA, INNOCHEM), benzylamine (BA, Meryer), 
1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (TTE, 95%, TCI), lithium 
bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)imide (LiTFSI, battery grade, DoDoChem), lithium nitrate 
(LiNO3, metals basis, Aladdin), polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard 2500), nickel 
(Ni) foil, aluminum (Al) foil, lithium (Li) foil (50 μm thick), elemental sulfur (S), 
lithium sulfide (Li2S), carbon nanotubes (CNTs), dimethyl sulfoxide-d6 (DMSO-d6, 
99.9% purity), N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP, Sinopharm), poly(vinylidene fluoride) 
(PVDF, HSV-300), and carbon paper (CP) were all obtained from commercial suppliers 
and utilized without additional purification. The materials were stored in an argon-
atmosphere glove box with H2O and O2 levels below 0.01 ppm. 
2. Preparation of sulfur cathodes. 
A S/C composite was prepared by blending S and CNTs at a 7:3 weight ratio. This 
composite was then ball-milled with a PVDF binder at a ratio of 9:1 (by weight) in 
NMP. The slurry was spread onto Al foil and dried at 60°C for 12 h. The resulting S/C 
cathodes with 63% S content were cut into 13.0 mm diameter disks. The areal sulfur 
loading was set as 4.3 mg cm−2.

The TTE-based EPSE was chosen as the baseline electrolyte. The baseline electrolyte 
was obtained by dissolving 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 in TTE/DOL/DME 
(1:2:2, by vol.) mixed solvents (marked as w/o PEA). 1 vol.% PEA was added to the

4. Preparation of inactive Li. 
Inactive Li was prepared on Ni foils by disassembling charged-state Li | Ni batteries. 
Li | Ni batteries firstly underwent 4 charge/discharge cycles at a current density of 1.0 
mA cm−2 and a capacity of 3.0 mAh cm−2 (Figure S2). Then the last charging process 
was conducted at a current density of 0.1 mA cm−2 to ensure the complete stripping of 
active Li. 
5. Material characterizations. 
1H, 7Li, and 13C NMR spectra were captured at 400 MHz utilizing a Bruker AVANCE 
NEO 400 system, with measurements taken at room temperature. The absorbance 
properties of solutions were examined using an ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) 
spectrometer (AvaSpecULS4096CL-EVO, Avantes, China). 0.4 mM LiPEA, O-LiPEA, 
or R-LiPEA was dissolved in 3.0 mL DMSO-d6 for NMR and UV–vis. The mixture of 
S and Li2S in the molar ratios of 1:5 was added in DMSO-d6 to prepare Li2S6 
electrolytes for NMR and UV–vis. The concentration of Li2S6 was 0.4 mM.

(FEI-Apreo C FEI Ltd., Hillsboro state, United States) operated at 5.0 kV. Cycled 
anodes from Li–S batteries were rinsed with DME three times to remove any remaining 
electrolyte solution and then dried in a glove box with H2O and O2 content below 0.01

6. Battery assembly and electrochemical measurement. 
All the batteries were assembled in the configuration of a 2032-type coin cell and tested 
on a LAND multichannel battery cycler at an ambient temperature of 25°C. 
In the case of Li–S batteries under demanding conditions, an S loading of 4.3 mg cm−2 
was applied. The fresh Li was utilized as anodes, with a diameter of 15.0 mm and a 
thickness of 50 μm. PP membrane was used as the separator. The amount of electrolyte 
was controlled to an electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio of 6.6 μL mgS−1. The cycle 
performance was evaluated between 1.7–2.6 V (1 C = 1672 mA g−1) at 0.1 C, following 
2 activation cycles at 0.05 C. 
In the case of i-Li–CP and i-Li–S batteries, the inactive Li on Ni was 16.0 mm in 
diameter. The CP cathode was cut into disks measuring 13.0 mm in diameter. The 
diameter of inactive Li on Ni was 16.0 mm. PP membrane was used as the separator. 
35 μL baseline electrolyte is used in each i-Li–CP, and i-Li–S battery. The i-Li–CP and 
i-Li–S batteries are tested at the current density of 0.3 mA cm−2. 
Cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves were performed on a Solartron 1470E electrochemical 
workstation over a voltage range between 1.7 and 2.8 V at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1. A 
carbon paper was used as the working electrode, and a Li metal anode was used as the 
counter and reference electrode.
