Siloxane Electrolyte Molecular Design for Lithium-Sulfur Batteries










Experimental Methods
Raw materials
Synthesis of SPAN: PAN (Sigma-Aldrich, Mw=150,000) is mixed uniformly with S (Sigma Aldrich) by ball milling at a weight ratio of 1:3 for 2 h with ethyl alcohol. Then the dried mixture is annealed at 350 ℃ for 6 h with a heating rate of 5 ℃ in Ar atmosphere.
Electrolyte and electrode preparation: Battery-grade LiFSI, DME, TTE and DOL are used as received from DodoChem. The electrolytes of 1 M LiFSI DME: DOL (1:1), 1 M LiFSI PTTS, 2M LiFSI PTTS, 3M LiFSI PTTS, 4M LiFSI PTTS, 4M LiFSI PTTS: TTE (7:3) are prepared by dissolving the Li salts into the selected solvents. The SPAN cathodes about 2.01 mg cm-2 are prepared by slurry coating on Al foils, with SPAN, CNT and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in a mass ratio of 80:10:10 mixed in N-methyl pyrrolidinone (NMP). The SPAN cathodes with high are comprised of SPAN, CNT, poly (acrylic acid) (PAA, Sigma-Aldrich, Mw=450,000), guar gum (GG, Aladdin) in a mass ratio of 90:5:2.5:2.5 mixed in deionized water. After drying in a vacuum oven at 60 ℃ overnight and calendaring three times, the SPAN cathodes are cut into 11 mm discs for coin cells and 43 mm×56 mm rectangles for pouch cells.
Preparation of lithium polysulfide solutions:
To prepare the Li2S6 solutions, Li2S and S8 were mixed in the HME solutions with a molar ratio of 8:5 and stirred at room temperature. The Li2S6 concentration was set to be 1.0 mol [S] L-1. To prepare the Li2S8 solutions, Li2S and S8 were mixed in DME solvents with a molar ratio of 8:7 and stirred at room temperature. The Li2S8 concentration was diluted to 0.20 mmol [Li2S8] L-1 and the Li2S8 solutions were utilized in adsorption tests
Computational Method
MD: MD simulations were performed in GROMACS using the General Amber Force Field (GAFF). Topology files and bonded and Lennard-Jones parameters were generated by using the Autoff while the RESP atomic charges from Multiwfn3.8 program were used [J. Comput. Chem. 2012, 33, 580-592]. The cutoff for the Lennard-Jones potential was set to 12 Å. The long-range Coulombic interactions were counted by a particle–particle particle-mesh. The initial periodic systems were set up using PACKMOL [J. Comput. Chem. 30,2157–2164 (2009)]. All ions and molecules were inserted in an initial cube box with 8 nm sides. In the equilibrium stage of the system, the energy of the simulated system was minimized by the conjugate gradient method first, then the equilibrium simulation was carried out under NPT ensemble for 10 ns. Finally, the simulation was continued for 40 ns with NVT ensemble and data were collected. Only the final 10 ns trajectory was sampled for the analysis of radial distribution function and solvent-solute interaction environments.
DFT: The binding energy was calculated by subtracting the reactant energies from the complex energy. All the molecules were first optimized using Gaussian with the three-parameter empirical formulation B3LYP [A. D. Becke, J. Chem. Phys., 1993, 98, 5648.] for the exchange-correlation density-functional energy in conjunction with the basis set of 6-311 G’s(d,p). A dispersion correction was also considered using the Becke-Johnson damping function. The solvent-solute interaction was considered with the universal solvation model of SMD [ J. Phys. Chem. B 113, 6378–6396 (2009)]. Frequency analysis was performed to ensure the ground state of molecular structures. The energy calculations were carried out in a 4-zeta grade Ma-QZVP basis set with diffuse functions to avoid the effects of basis set superposition error (BSSE).
Battery fabrication and electrochemical measurements

Cell Assembly. All CR2032 coin cells were assembled and disassembled in an argon-filled glovebox, where the concentrations of water vapor and oxygen were maintained below 0.01 ppm. Each cell was injected with 60 μL of electrolyte, and a Celgard 3501 separator (17 mm in diameter) was used as the separator material. Li||Cu half cells were fabricated to evaluate the feasibility of the electrolyte additive strategy and the Coulombic efficiency (CE) of lithium stripping/plating. The cells were constructed by stacking a lithium chip, a Celgard 3501 separator (1.7 cm in diameter), and a copper foil (1.1 cm in diameter). Li||Li symmetric cells were assembled using two identical lithium foils, and cycled under a specified current density and capacity per half cycle to assess electrolyte compatibility with the lithium anode. Li-SPAN full cells were assembled to evaluate electrolyte compatibility with both the SPAN cathode and metallic lithium anode. The cells were cycled at 0.5 C (1 C = 1675 mAh g-1) within a voltage window of 1-3 V. In this study, the CR2032 coin cells adopted lithium metal anodes with a diameter of 14 mm and SPAN cathodes with a diameter of 11 mm, with an electrolyte dosage of 60 uL per cell, the electrolyte mass is 61.2 mg. For the pouch cells, the electrolyte dosage was 150 uL per cell, the electrolyte mass is 153 mg. Based on the above corresponding sulfur mass, the calculated results show that: for the CR2032 cell with a sulfur loading of 2.01 mg cm-2, the E/S mass ratio is optimized to about 32:1 and the volume-to-mass ratio is about 31.4 uL·mg-1, with an N/P ratio of about 31:1. For the CR2032 cell in with an increased sulfur loading of 4.4 mg cm-2, the E/S mass ratio is optimized to about 14.6:1 and the volume-to-mass ratio is about 14.3 uL·mg-1, with an N/P ratio of about 14:1. The pouch cell (cathode areal capacity of 6.16 mAh cm-2), with an E/S mass ratio of about 20.8:1 and a volume-to-mass ratio of about 20.4 uL·mg-1, as well as an N/P ratio of about 1.7:1.
Electrochemical Testing. Battery cycling performance: All cycling performance tests were conducted using the Neware MIHW-200-160CH-B battery testing system. A small activation current of 0.05 mA was first applied to Li||Cu half-cells within the voltage range from 0 to 1 V for 5 cycles to stabilize the SEI and clean surface impurities. After the activation process, 1 mA h cm-2 Li was plated on the Cu current collector and then stripped away at a specified current density with a cutoff voltage of 1 V. For the Aurbach’s CE test, a standard protocol was followed: (1) performing one formation cycle with Li metal deposition of 5 mAh cm-2 on Cu with 0.5 mA cm-2 and stripping Li to 1 V; (2) deposited 5 mAh cm-2 Li on Cu with 0.5 mA cm-2 as a Li reservoir; (3) repeatedly stripping/platting Li 1 mAh cm-2 Li with 0.5 mA cm-2 for 10 cycles; 4) stripping all Li to 1 V. Cyclic voltammetry (CV): CV measurements were performed on Li-SPAN full cells at a sweep rate of 0.1 mV s⁻¹ over a voltage range of 1-3 V, using the Corrtest CS310X electrochemical workstation. Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT): GITT measurements were carried out using the Land CT2001A battery testing system. The tests involved 0.2 C current pulses for 20 min, followed by a 60 min rest period, within a voltage range of 1-3 V. Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed using the Corrtest CS310X electrochemical workstation. Room-temperature EIS: Conducted over a frequency range of 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz with a voltage amplitude of 10 mV. Temperature-dependent EIS: Carried out on Li||Li symmetric cells across a temperature range of 263-313 K, with a voltage amplitude of 5 mV and a frequency range of 10-2 to 105 Hz. The activation energy was calculated from temperature-dependent Nyquist plots using the Arrhenius equation. Tafel plots were obtained using the Corrtest CS310X. Curves were generated by plotting the overpotential of galvanostatic lithium stripping/plating against the natural logarithm of the current density.

Material Characterizations
The in-situ optical microscope characterization of lithium deposition behavior was recorded by a digital camera (Yuescope) in Li||Li symmetrical battery at current density of 5 mA cm-2 with fixed deposition capacity of 5 mAh cm-2. Raman microscope (HORIBA XploRA PLUS) was carried out to detect the evolution of Li+ solvation structure and identify the electrolyte components. The solvated structural changes at different electrolytes were measured with FTIR (Bruker Tensor 27, Germany). In-situ Raman test. Li-SPAN cells were assembled with a customized device, which was galvanostatically discharged at 0.05 C. In the meanwhile, Raman spectra were recorded at 20 points during the discharge process. The morphology and microstructure were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Hitachi SU3500). Cryo-TEM were performed using Thermo Scientific Titan Krios G3i (USA) operated at 300 kV, which is equipped with automatic injection system of frozen sample. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha+) using mono Al 𝐾𝛼 was employed to analyze the chemical composition on the surface of cycled Li anodes or SPAN cathodes. Atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker Dimension Icon) is employed to test the morphology and mechanical strength of the Cathode Electrolyte Interphase (CEI) on the surface. Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy (ToF-SIMS) analysis was performed on PHI nanoTOF II Time of-Flight SIMS (ULVAC-PHI Inc., Japan), using a Bi3 ++ beam (30 keV, 2 nA) for detection and an Ar+ beam (3 kV, 100 nA) for depth profiling. The sputter area was 400 × 400 μm, and the area of analysis was 80 × 80 μm. The UV-vis detector continuously recorded the intensity of characteristic Li2S4 signals in electrolyte for 5min. The peak intensity at the wavelength of 410 nm was linearly related to the adsorption capacity. NMR spectroscopy was employed to probe the dynamic evolution of the Li⁺ solvation structure. All measurements were conducted at a constant temperature of 298 K. The DOSY experiments utilized pulsed field gradient (PFG) NMR, implemented via Bruker’s standard dstebpgp3s pulse sequence. To determine diffusion coefficients, a double stimulated echo pulse sequence incorporating bipolar gradient pulses, eddy current delay periods, and convection compensation was applied. Corresponding signal attenuation profiles and diffusion coefficient values were derived from the T1/T2 analysis module of the NMR software. The detailed calculation equation is presented in the following section. 

E denotes the echo signal attenuation during free diffusion, I/I0 corresponds to the normalized signal intensity at different gradient amplitudes, γ stands for the gyromagnetic ratio of the target isotope, δ refers to the duration of the bipolar gradient pulse, g represents the gradient amplitude, Δ is the interval between consecutive pulses, and τ signifies the gradient ringdown delay. Individual diffusion coefficients were calculated by fitting the attenuated signal traces from 32 separate NMR spectra, with integration performed over a carefully selected chemical shift range.

T1 relaxation describes the recovery of longitudinal net magnetization (MZ) toward its equilibrium maximum value (M0), and is formally defined as the time needed for MZ to recover to 63% of M0.
