Detailed description for Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulations
Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using the LAMMPS (Large-scale 
Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator) software package [1], employing the non-polarizable OPLS-AA 
(Optimized Potentials for Liquid Simulations All-Atom) force field [2]. Molecular topology files, Lennard-Jones 
parameters, and bonded parameters were generated using the LigParGen server [3–5] and the fftool package [6]. To 
account for electronic screening effects and enhance the accuracy of interionic interaction predictions, a scaling 
factor of 0.8 was applied to the partial charges of all ions, following common practices for non-polarizable force 
fields [7–8].
Force field parameters for the anions were adapted from their corresponding neutral counterparts due to the 
absence of direct parameters for these specific ions; i.e., TDI (4,5-dicyano-2-(trifluoromethyl)imidazolide ion) was 
parameterized based on TDI0 (4,5-dicyano-2-(trifluoromethyl)imidazole)–and the same for PDI and HDI. Bond 
lengths, angles, and dihedral parameters were transferred from the neutral analogues, assuming minimal structural 
changes upon deprotonation. van der Waals (vdW) parameters were similarly retained, given that they are largely 
dependent on atom types, which do not change with ionization. Partial atomic charges for each anion were 
recalculated using density functional theory (DFT) at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level, employing the CHELPG 
(CHarges from ELectrostatic Potentials using a Grid-based method) to capture the effects of deprotonation 
accurately.
To examine the influence of salt concentration on the coordination environment of the lithium cations, 
simulations were carried out for 0.3, 0.6, 1.0, and 2.0 M electrolytes. Each within a cubic simulation box containing 
appropriate ratios of DME, DOL, Li+ and anions to achieve the desired concentration.
The simulation protocol began with an initial equilibration in the isothermal-isobaric ensemble (NPT) at a 
high temperature of 800 K for 2 ns. This high-temperature equilibration step was used to overcome potential 
pseudo-equilibrium states and ensure thorough sampling of conformational space. The system was then cooled to 
300 K and equilibrated for an additional 5 ns in the NPT ensemble to reach the desired temperature and pressure. A 
further equilibration was performed in the canonical ensemble (NVT) at 300 K for 5 ns, resulting in a total 
equilibration time of 12 ns. Temperature and pressure control were maintained using a Nosé-Hoover thermostat and 
barostat, with damping constants of 100 fs for temperature and 1000 fs for pressure, respectively. Following 
equilibration, an NVT production run of 15 ns was performed at 300 K to collect data for structural and dynamic 
analyses.
All simulations were conducted at a constant temperature of 300 K and a pressure of 1 atm. The Nosé-
Hoover thermostat (temperature damping of 100 fs) and barostat (pressure damping of 1000 fs) were employed to 
maintain the target conditions during the NPT phases. Electrostatic interactions were calculated using the particle-
particle-particle-mesh (PPPM) method, with periodic boundary conditions applied in all directions.
To investigate the coordination environment of lithium ions, radial distribution functions (RDFs) and 
coordination numbers were calculated between lithium ions, the anions, and the solvent molecules. These properties 
were obtained using LAMMPS subroutines, allowing for a detailed examination of how varying electrolyte 
concentrations affect the solvation structure and ion pairing tendencies in the mixed DME solvent system.
