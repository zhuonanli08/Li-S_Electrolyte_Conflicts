Supplementary Note 1:
Determination of the molar concentration of polysulfides in the electrolyte considering Li2S6 
as the representative polysulfide species under the given conditions:
Sulfur loading = 5 mg/cm2
Electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio = 5 µL/mg
Step 1: Determining total sulfur quantity (in mg) per cell
Total sulfur = Sulfur loading × Electrode area
With a diameter of 13 mm, the total electrode area becomes 1.32665 cm2
Hence, total sulfur = 5 mg/cm2 × 1.32665 cm2 = 6.63325 mg of S per cell
Step 2: Calculating the moles of sulfur (in mmol) per cell
Moles of sulfur = Total sulfur mass / Molecular weight of sulfur
Moles of sulfur = 6.63325 mg / 32.07 g/mol = 0.207 mmol of S per cell
Step 3: Converting to the moles of sulfur to moles of Li2S6 (in mmol)
Since one Li2S6 molecule contains 6 sulfur atoms, the moles of Li2S6 are:
Moles of Li2S6 = Moles of sulfur / 6 = 0.207 mmol / 6 = 0.0345 mmol of Li2S6 per cell
Step 4: Calculating electrolyte volume (in µL) in the cell
Electrolyte volume = Total sulfur mass × E/S ratio
Electrolyte volume = 6.63325 mg × 5 µL/mg = 33.2 µL
Step 5: Determining the molar concentration (in M) of Li2S6
Molar concentration = Electrolyte volume / Moles of Li2S6
Molar concentration = 33.2 µL / 0.0345 mmol = 1 M of Li2S6
Hence, the cell tested with a S-loading of 5 mg/cm2 and E/S ratio of 5 µL/mg contains 1 M of 
Li2S6 in the electrolyte as the presentative polysulfide. The molar concentration of Li2S6 used 
in the UV-Vis experiments in Fig. 1 was 1.5 M, which closely aligns with high-loading and 
lean electrolyte conditions.
