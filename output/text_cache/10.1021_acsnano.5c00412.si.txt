Note S2. The lithium morphology changes by HE electrolyte. 
13 
As shown in Figure S24-S25, the changes in Li deposition behavior by HE electrolyte can be 
14 
attributed to the robust anion-derived solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) layer and enhancement 
15 
of Li+ flux due to the LiPS anti-clustering effect.  
16 
 
17 
Anion-derived SEI layer of HE electrolyte 
18 
During the repeated cycling, the Li anode undergoes continuous volume expansion and 
19 
contraction.5 As a result, the solvent-derived SEI layer which has poor mechanical properties 
20 
tends to develop cracks.6 These cracks lead to inhomogeneous Li+ flux,7 causing the growth 
21 
of lithium dendrites.8 Therefore, the anion-derived SEI layer with the HE electrolyte, which is 
22 
based on inorganic materials with superior mechanical properties, can suppress the formation 
23 
of cracks in the SEI layer, thereby inhibiting the growth of lithium dendrites. The inhibited 
24 
Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​ancac3/​article-supplement/​3755261/​pdf/​nn5c00412_si_001/​ by TSINGHUA UNIV user on 24 September 2026

<<PAGE 5>>
 
 
5 
 
cracking and reconstruction of the SEI layer with HE electrolyte can be verified by 
1 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analysis of cycled Li symmetric cells. The 
2 
lower resistance of the SEI layer (RSEI) observed after 200 cycles with the HE catholyte 
3 
compared to that with the TFSI catholyte verified that SEI cracking and reconstruction were 
4 
effectively suppressed by robust anion-derived SEI layer (Figure 4b). 
5 
 
6 
Improvement of Li+ mobility through LiPS anti-clustering by HE electrolyte 
7 
LiPS clustering leads to strong electrostatic interactions between Li+ and polysulfide anions, 
8 
resulting in reduced mobility of Li+.9 Therefore, the anti-clustering behavior of LiPS in the 
9 
HE electrolyte promoted the mobility of Li+, resulting in the uniform Li+ flux by the decrease 
10 
of Li+ concentration gradient near the Li metal anode.10 Consequently, uniform Li+ flux with 
11 
the HE electrolyte induced compact and bulk Li deposition.11

Note S3. The nucleation and growth behavior of Li2S by four classical models of 
14 
Bewick–Fleischman–Thirsk (BFT) and Scharifker–Hills (SH). 
15 
The nucleation and growth behavior of Li2S can be analyzed by comparing tm vs im curves 
16 
obtained from the potentiostatic chronoamperometry with the BFT and SH model.12 (im 
17 
represents the maximum current, and tm refers to the time required for the current to reach im). 
18 
Two-dimensional (2D) models of instantaneous nucleation (2DI) and progressive nucleation 
19 
(2DP) were investigated by BFT models.13 Three-dimensional (3D) models of instantaneous 
20 
nucleation (3DI) and progressive nucleation (3DP) were investigated by SH model.14 The 
21 
deposition of Li2S consisted of two steps: 1) Formation of nuclei by nucleation; 2) Growth of 
22 
Li2S by surface diffusion (2D) or bulk diffusion (3D) of depositing substances onto the 
23 
nuclei.12 Depending on the nucleation rate, the deposition model of Li2S can be classified into 
24 
either the instantaneous or the progressive nucleation model.15 If the nucleation rate is 
25 
sufficiently fast, the deposition of Li2S follows the instantaneous nucleation model, leading to
