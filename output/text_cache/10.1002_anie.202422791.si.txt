1. Experimental Details 
1.1 Electrolyte preparation  
The LiFSI salt was purchased from DodoChem (Suzhou) (purity ≥ 99.9%). The tetrahydrofuran (THF, ≥ 
99.9%) was purchased from Aladdin. The 2,5-dimethyltetrahydrofuran was purchased from Adamas (2,5-
THF, ≥ 98.0%, GR). The benzotrifluoride (BTF, ≥ 99.0%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. In this work, 
the “m” stands for molar salt per liter solvent (+diluent). The high-concentration electrolytes of 3.0 m LiFSI 
THF and 3.0 m LiFSI 2,5-THF were prepared by dissolving 0.5612 g (3.0 mmol) LiFSI in 1.0 mL THF and 
1.0 mL 2,5-THF, respectively. The localized high-concentration electrolytes of 1.5 m LiFSI THF-BTF and 
1.5 m LiFSI 2,5-THF-BTF were prepared by dissolving 0.5612 g (3.0 mmol) LiFSI in 1.0 mL THF and 1.0 
mL 2,5-THF, respectively, followed by the addition of 1.0 mL BTF as diluent. All prepared electrolytes are 
stirred with a magnetic stirrer at room temperature for 4 hours. All the above steps were carried out in an 
Argon-filled glove box (H2O ≤ 0.01 ppm, O2 ≤ 0.01 ppm). 
 
1.2 Electrode preparation 
The sulfur powder and polyacrylonitrile (PAN. Mw~150 kDa) were purchased from Aladdin.  
The sulfur powder (4.0 g) and PAN (1.0 g) were mixed and ball-milled for 2 hours under an air atmosphere 
to obtain a homogenous powder. Then the above powder into a tube furnace and heated at 450 ℃ for 6 
hours with a temperature rising rate of 2 ℃/min under an Ar atmosphere. After the temperature cooled 
down, the SPAN powder was collected and prepared for SPAN electrodes. The S content of our SPAN 
powder was 41.0% by elemental analysis (Table S6). 
The SPAN electrodes can be prepared by SPAN powder, Super p, and PVDF binder at a mass ratio of 6: 
2: 2 following adding the NMP solvent to mix and stir for 4 hours to form a slurry. The slurry was cast on 
Al foil and dried for 8 hours at 60 ℃ under vacuum. The electrode was applied by cutting into 12 mm-
diameter discs. The areal mass loading of SPAN is ~0.7 mg cm-2 for half batteries. 
The electrode for pouch batteries was prepared by mixing SPAN powder, Super p, and PAA-Li binder at 
a mass ratio of 8: 1: 1 following adding deionized water to mix and stirring for 8 hours to form a slurry. The 

<<PAGE 4>>
3 
 
slurry was cast on Al foil and dried overnight at 60 ℃ under vacuum. The electrode was used by cutting 
into 4 cm*4 cm square pieces for pouch batteries. The areal mass loading of SPAN is 6.0~7.0 mg cm-2 for 
pouch batteries. The theoretical capacity of the SPAN electrode is 500 mAh g-1. 
1.3 Electrochemical performance 
1.3.1 Coin battery test 
All batteries were assembled in the Argon-filled glove box (H2O ≤ 0.01 ppm, O2 ≤ 0.01 ppm).  
For Li||SPAN half batteries, the pre-weighted SPAN electrode, and 16 mm-diameter Celgard PP separator 
are sequentially placed into a 2032-type coin battery, followed by the addition of 80 μL electrolytes. Then 
100 μm lithium metal was placed on a PP separator. The assembled Li||SPAN half batteries were rested for 
6 hours before the electrochemical test. The cut-off voltage was from 1.0 V to 3.0 V. 
1.3.2 Pouch battery test 
For Li||SPAN pouch batteries, the pre-weighted SPAN electrode and Celgard PP separator are sequentially 
assembled. Then 50 μm Li was placed on the PP separator, followed by the addition of 11 mg cm-2 
electrolytes. The Li||SPAN pouch batteries were rested for 10 hours before the electrochemical test. The cut-
off voltage was from 1.0 V to 3.0 V.  
1.3.3 Li||Cu battery test 
The 12mm-diameter Li foils and 19 mm-diameter Cu foils were assembled for testing the Coulombic 
efficiencies of Li||Cu batteries. The long-cycling Li||Cu batteries were cycled at 0.5 mA cm-2 with a capacity 
of 1.0 mAh cm-2. Meanwhile, the Aurbach measurement of Li||Cu batteries was firstly deposited 10.0 mAh 
cm-2 Li on Cu foils at 1.0 mA cm-2 (step 1) and then stripped to 1.0 V (step 2) to ensure the complete 
passivation of the Cu surface. Afterward, another 10.0 mAh cm-2 Li was deposited (step 3) and cycled at 
1.0 mA cm-2 with a capacity of 1.0 mAh cm-2 for 10 cycles (step 4). Finally, the Li was stripped till 1.0 V 
(step 5), and calculated the CE of Li||Cu batteries the following equation: 
CE = (1 mAh cm-2*10 + Q5) / (10 mAh cm-2 + 1 mAh cm-2*10) 
Where Q5 is the amount of stripped Li in step 5 (unit: mAh cm-2)1.

1.3.4 Other electrochemical test 
The galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD) test was operated using the NEWARE Battery Testing System 
(Shenzhen, China). The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) was operated using the 
LANHE Battery Testing System (Wuhan, China). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was 
tested by CHI 760e (Shanghai, China). 
1.4 Characterization 
The Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectra were obtained by Bruker AVANCE III 400M. The 
magnitude of the coupling constant can be calculated by multiplying the separation of the lines in chemical 
shift δ (ppm) by the resonance frequency of the spectrometer in megaHertz. J Hz = δ ppm * νo MHz (νo: 
400 MHz)2.  
The Raman spectra were obtained by HORIBA LabRAM HR Evolution with a laser wavelength of 785 
nm and wavenumber from 600-900 cm-1. A scanning electron microscope (SEM), ZEISS Merlin was used 
to acquire the morphologies of anodes and cathodes. The time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy 
(TOF-SIMS) tests with high ion detection sensitivity were carried out by TOF-SIMS V (ION-TOF GmbH), 
with a sputtering time of 800 s, within an area of 300 μm*300 μm. The data of TOF-SIMS were further 
processed and visualized by ION-TOF software. Cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (cryo-TEM, 
Krios G3i) was applied to acquire the details of the SEI layer on lithium metal.  
Use a compression plate to press the pouch battery and pressure sensor array (model: M1616) tightly. The 
pouch battery undergoes a charge/discharge process through the NEWARE battery testing system. The 
pressure sensor test data is collected using a signal collector and plotted (Figure S20). 
 
2. Calculation Details 
All molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted using the GROMACS 2022.5 simulation 
package with the amber99sb-ildn force field3, 4. Atomic charges for solvents and anions were generated 
through the restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) fitting procedure via the Multifwn software5. 
Electrolyte topologies were created using the Sobtop software6. Both equilibrium and production 

<<PAGE 6>>
5 
 
simulations were performed in the NPT ensemble under constant pressure (1.01325 bar) and temperature 
(298.15 K) within a cubic simulation box with periodic boundary conditions in all xyz Cartesian directions. 
For equilibrium processes, the temperature is maintained by V-rescale coupling with a time constant of 0.2 
ps. The Berendsen barostat was adopted for controlling pressure, which is with a coupling constant of 0.5 
ps. Equilibrium simulation ran 5 ns. For production simulation, the V-rescale coupling and parrinello-
rahman barostat were used to control the temperature and pressure, respectively. Production simulation ran 
20 ns (1fs per step, simulating 2*107 steps) for obtaining the equilibrium Li+ coordination shell structure at 
room temperature for electrolytes. Electrostatic interactions were treated using the Particle-Mesh-Ewald 
(PME) method7. Solvation shell statistics were analyzed based on the MDAnalysis Python module8. All 
visualizations of MD simulation were implemented by VMD software and Quantum chemistry calculations 
parts were performed using the Gaussian 16 package9. The structures of molecules were optimized at the 
B3LYP/6-311+G(d,p) level. Binding energy (𝐸𝑏𝑖𝑛) between two components is defined as: 
𝐸𝑏𝑖𝑛= 𝐸𝑎/𝑏−𝐸𝑎−𝐸𝑏 
In which 𝐸𝑎/𝑏, 𝐸𝑎and 𝐸𝑏 were the total energy of the a and b complexes, component a and component b, 
respectively. All the visualizations of quantum chemistry calculations were implemented by GaussView 
software.
