Synthesis of 6,6'-Thiobis(2,4-di-tert-butylphenol) (TBPS)
2,4-Di-tert-butylphenol (5.16 g, 25.00 mmol) was dissolved in 50 mL of dichloromethane under ambient conditions with stirring, and the solution was transferred into a three-necked flask. Sulfur dichloride (SCl2, 1.42 g, 13.75 mmol) was dissolved in 10 mL of dichloromethane and slowly added dropwise to the flask under N2 atmosphere while stirring at 0 °C. The reaction was allowed to proceed for 2 h. Thin-layer chromatography (TLC) was employed to monitor the reaction progress. Upon completion, the reaction mixture was quenched with deionized water. The organic phase was separated, washed three times with saturated brine, and the solvent was removed under reduced pressure to afford a yellow crude solid. The crude product was purified by column chromatography, eluting with hexanes/EtOAc 50:1 (v/v) to obtain the purified TBPS, which was dried, weighed, and stored for further use.
Preparation of Nitrogen-doped Porous Carbon (NFC)
1 g of peptone was dissolved in 5 mL of deionized water and sonicated for 5−10 min to ensure complete dissolution. The solution was then frozen at −20 °C for 12 h and subsequently subjected to freeze-drying for 12 h to obtain a dry solid. The dried sample was mixed with 10 g of NaCl and ball-milled at 550 rpm for 3 h. The resulting mixture was placed in a tubular furnace under N2 atmosphere and heated to 900 °C at a rate of 5 °C min-1, followed by high-temperature pyrolysis for 1 h. After cooling to room temperature, the carbonized product was washed thoroughly to remove residual salts and dried in an oven at 60 °C. The obtained nitrogen-doped porous carbon was denoted as NFC and stored for further use.
Preparation of Sulfur-Loaded NFC (S@NFC)
NFC and sublimed sulfur were mixed at a mass ratio of 2:3 and ground thoroughly for 30 min. The mixture was then transferred into a glove box under an argon atmosphere and heated in an oven at 155 °C for 12 h to allow sulfur impregnation. The resulting sulfur-loaded nitrogen-doped porous carbon composite was denoted as S@NFC and stored for subsequent use.
Preparation of Li2S6 Electrolyte
Lithium sulfide (Li2S) and sublimed sulfur were mixed at a molar ratio of 1:5 in a solvent mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME) (v/v = 1:1). The mixture was stirred at 60 °C for 24 h to obtain a 0.2 M Li2S6 solution, which was subsequently used as the catholyte in Li-S battery tests.
Preparation of Electrolyte Additive (TBPS) Solution
The blank electrolyte was prepared using a conventional Li–S battery (LSB) electrolyte as the base, consisting of 1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and 0.1 M LiNO3 dissolved in a 1:1 (v/v) mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME). 6,6'-Thiobis(2,4-di-tert-butylphenol) (TBPS) was then dissolved in this base electrolyte at concentrations of 20, 30, and 40 mM. Electrochemical performance tests indicated that 30 mM TBPS exhibited optimal performance; hence, all subsequent experiments were conducted using this concentration.
Assembly and Electrochemical Testing of Li–S Batteries
The cathode was prepared by mixing S@NFC (70 wt.%), Super P conductive carbon (20 wt.%), and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF, 10 wt.%) binder in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) to form a uniform slurry. The slurry was coated onto aluminum foil current collectors and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h to remove the solvent. The dried electrodes were punched into 12 mm diameter disks, with a typical sulfur loading of 1.0−1.4 mg cm-2. Celgard 2400 membranes were used as separators, lithium metal as the anode, and CR2032-type stainless steel cases were employed for coin cell assembly in an Ar-filled glove box.
Electrochemical performance was evaluated with reference to the sulfur mass in the cathode (1 C = 1675 mAh g-1). Cells were tested in the voltage range of 1.7−2.8 V using a Neware battery testing system. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were performed on a CHI 650D electrochemical workstation. CV measurements were carried out at scan rates of 0.1−0.5 mV s-1 within 1.7−2.8 V. EIS tests were conducted over a frequency range of 100 kHz to 10 mHz. All electrochemical tests were performed at room temperature.

Assembly of Li2S6 Symmetric Cells
The cathode was prepared by mixing NFC, Super P, and PVDF in a mass ratio of 8:1:1, following the same procedure as described above. For cell assembly, 30 μL of 0.2 M Li2S6 solution and 20 μL of either the blank or TBPS-containing electrolyte were added to both sides of the separator. The previously prepared cathode disks were used to replace the lithium anode, and the symmetric cells were assembled following the same sequence as for the Li-S coin cells.
Assembly of Li/Li₂S₈ Cells
The cathode was prepared by mixing NFC, Super P, and PVDF in a mass ratio of 8:1:1, following the procedure described above. For cell assembly, 20 μL of 0.2 M Li2S8 solution in tetraglyme was added onto one side of the working electrode, while 20 μL of blank or TBPS-containing electrolyte (without Li2S8) was added to the lithium counter electrode side. The Li/Li2S8 coin cells were then assembled following the same sequence as described for the Li-S cells.
Cyclic Voltammetry (CV) Testing of Li2S6 Symmetric Cells
The assembled Li2S6 symmetric cells were characterized on a CHI 650D electrochemical workstation. CV measurements were conducted within a voltage window of -0.8 V to 0.8 V at scan rates of 1 and 50 mV s-1.
Li2S Deposition Testing of Li/Li2S8 Cells
The assembled Li/Li₂S₈ cells were initially discharged under a constant current of 0.112 mA to 2.06 V using a Neware battery testing system. Immediately afterward, the cells were subjected to potentiostatic discharge at 2.05 V on a CHI 650D electrochemical workstation until the current decreased below 0.01 mA, ensuring complete nucleation and deposition of Li2S.
Physical Characterization
All chemicals and solvents used in this study were obtained from commercial sources (e.g., Aldrich, Canrd, Dodochem) and used without further purification. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were obtained using a Carl Zeiss Ultra Plus microscope (Germany). Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were recorded on a Nicolet is 50 spectrometers (USA) in the range of 4000−400 cm-1. Raman spectra were measured using a Lab Ram HR spectrometer (France) with a 532 nm excitation wavelength. Ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectra were recorded on a UV-5800PC spectrophotometer (Shanghai Yuanxi Instruments Co., Ltd., China) over the wavelength range of 200–600 nm.
Theoretical Calculations
All density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Gaussian 09 software package. The B3LYP functional with the 6-31G(d) basis set was employed, along with the empirical dispersion correction keyword (empirical=gd3bj) in Gaussian 09. All molecular structures were fully optimized and confirmed to have no imaginary frequencies. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO), lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), and electrostatic potential (ESP) distributions were analyzed and visualized using Multiwfn and Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) software.
