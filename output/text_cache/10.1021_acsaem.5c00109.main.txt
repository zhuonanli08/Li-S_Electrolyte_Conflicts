ABSTRACT: Lithium−sulfur (Li−S) batteries continue to
encounter challenges related to the shuttle effect and interfacial
issues associated with lithium metal anodes in practical
applications. In this study, the multifunctional electrolyte organic
nitrogen−fluorine additive 2,5-difluoropyrazine (2,5-DFP) was
proposed for Li−S batteries, highlighting its merits from several
perspectives. The additive not only facilitates the formation of
energy-reducing intermediates during the reaction of sulfur
cathode with lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) but also effectively
modulates the molecular orbital energy levels of LiPSs and
enhances their redox kinetics. The additive can also suppress
dendrite growth by forming a dense and smooth organic−
inorganic hybrid solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) on the lithium anode such as lithium fluoride. Consequently, the developed
cell shows a high capacity of 1215.6 mAh g−1 and maintains a capacity of 588.1 mAh g−1 even at a high rate of 4 C. This study
demonstrates that 2,5-DFP can serve as a functional electrolyte additive, offering insights for the design of electrolytes for Li−S
batteries.
KEYWORDS: nitrogen−fluorine compounds, electrolyte additive, Li−S batteries, sulfur kinetics, Li anode
■INTRODUCTION
As an emerging energy storage system, lithium−sulfur (Li−S)
batteries utilize lithium metal as the anode material and
elemental sulfur as the cathode active material, achieving an
energy density of 2600 Wh kg−1 and a theoretical specific
capacity of 1675 mAh g−1.1−3 This high theoretical specific
capacity has garnered significant attention in the field of energy
storage. However, the challenges associated with Li−S
batteries cannot be overlooked. During charging and
discharging, the sulfur cathode undergoes a conversion where
solid sulfur forms into lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) and
dissolves in the electrolyte.4−6 These LiPSs shuttle between
the cathode and anode, resulting in the loss of active sulfur and
a rapid capacity decay. Upon contact with metallic lithium,
LiPSs react with the lithium, resulting in an unstable solid
electrolyte interphase (SEI) and dendritic growth on the metal
surface, which can cause separator punctures and potential
short circuits.7−10
The electrolyte serves as a crucial medium for the transport
of lithium ions. Therefore, its regulation affects the cathode
and lithium anode simultaneously. To address the aforemen-
tioned challenges, the introduction of additives into the
electrolyte has been widely adopted. Suitable additives can not
only mitigate the shuttle effect but also accelerate the
nucleation and oxidation of Li2S.11−14 More importantly,
additives can help form a more stable SEI on the lithium
anode, thereby protecting the lithium metal from corrosion.15
Among all of the additives, nitrogen-containing additives are
particularly effective. It has been proved they can interact with
LiPSs and create a nitrogen-containing SEI at the lithium
anode, enhancing its stability.9,16,17 For example, Wang et al.17
introduced a nitrogen-rich azole additive that forms lithium
bonds with lithium-containing nitrogen heterocycles, anchor-
ing and adsorbing lithium, which suppresses the shuttle effect
and leads to the formation of inorganic−organic hybrid SEIs
including lithium nitride. This process also results in uniform
lithium deposition and the generation of lithium dendrites. To
achieve an inorganic-rich SEI that inhibits dendritic lithium,
fluorine-containing additives are often incorporated into
lithium metal batteries, with lithium fluoride (LiF) being a
key component for effective SEI formation.14,17 Gao et al.19
added tris(2,2,2-trifluoroethyl) borate (TFEB) to the electro-
lyte, forming a stable and robust SEI on the lithium anode,
thereby improving the cycle stability of Li−S batteries.

In this study, 2,5-difluoropyrazine (2,5-DFP) was employed
as a multifunctional organic electrolyte additive to mitigate the
shuttle effect and facilitate the formation of dendrite-free
lithium anodes. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations
and electrochemical results demonstrate that the introduction
of 2,5-DFP significantly reduces the binding energy to
polysulfides and promotes the formation of lithium bonds
between nitrogen-containing heterocycles and polysulfides.16,20
This enables 2,5-DFP to anchor polysulfides in the sulfur
cathode, thereby mitigating the shuttle effect. Additionally, the
introduction of 2,5-DFP leads to the formation of a smooth,
dense, and stable inorganic−organic hybrid SEI on the lithium
surface, effectively inhibiting dendritic lithium growth and
preventing dendrites from penetrating the separator and
causing short circuits.18−21 Beneficial from this additive, the
coin cell with 2,5-DFP exhibits a reversible capacity of 588.1
mAh g−1 even at a high rate of 4 C. Furthermore, the capacity
retention is approximately 72.8% after 150 cycles at 0.2 C and
the decay rate is only 0.066% per cycle after 800 cycles at 1 C.
Therefore, this study presents a straightforward and
fundamental approach to suppress the shuttle effect and
protect the lithium anode using a small amount of electrolyte
additives without altering the battery configuration. This
method shows significant potential for advancing the practical
application of Li−S batteries.
■EXPERIMENTAL METHODS
Preparation of Electrolyte. The blank electrolyte consisted of a
solution of 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO3 in a mixture of DOL
(dioxolane) and DME (dimethoxyethane) in a volume ratio of 1:1.
The 2,5-DFP was completely dissolved in the ether-based electrolyte
at different concentrations of 40, 50, and 60 mM. To further
investigate the effect of electrolytes, organic additives of 2,5-DFP, 2-
FP, and PZ were dissolved in an ether electrolyte at a concentration of
50 mM. The prepared electrolyte was placed in a glovebox at room
temperature.
Sublimated sulfur (60 wt %) was mixed with carbon nanotubes (20
wt %), Super P conductive carbon black (10 wt %), and PVDF (10 wt
%) and dissolved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The mixture
was applied to the aluminum foil manifold. To remove the NMP
solvent, it was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h. Finally, the
dried electrodes were cut into 12 mm diameter discs, which are the
sulfur cathode electrodes.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Electrochemical Properties of Li−S Batteries. In this
study, we introduced pyrazine (PZ), 2-fluoropyrazine (2-FP),
and 2,5-DFP (structural formulas shown in Figure 1a) as
additives to thoroughly investigate the effects of nitrogen (N)
and fluorine (F) containing molecules on sulfur redox
processes. The optimal concentration of 50 mM for the
additives was determined by comparing the capacity of cells
with different additive concentrations (Figure S1 in the
Supporting Information). At first, we calculated the LUMO−
HOMO orbitals of LiPSs (Li2S, Li2S4, Li2S6, and Li2S8) with
PZ, 2-FP and 2,5-DFP based on density functional theory. As
shown in Figure 1b and Figures S2−S5, due to the strong
electronegativity of nitrogen and fluorine atoms, they can alter
the electron distribution of LiPSs through electron attraction,
thereby modulating their molecular orbital energy levels.19,20
Compared to Li2Sx (x = 2, 4, 6, and 8), the addition of
nitrogen-based additives and nitrogen−fluorine-based additives
results in a narrowing of the energy gap and a reduction in the
binding energy. This suggests that the intermediates PZ-Li2Sx,
2-FP-Li2Sx, and 2,5-DFP-Li2Sx exhibit higher conductivity and
faster charge transfer than LiPSs, with 2,5-DFP being
particularly effective (this can be demonstrated by the lower
resistance and lower voltage hysteresis below). Consequently,
during the electro-transformation of 2,5-DFP-SxLi2, the
activation energy is lower and the redox reaction kinetics are
faster compared to Li2Sx, thereby enhancing the charging and
discharging efficiency and stability of Li−S batteries.3,22
Effect of 2,5-DFP on the Kinetics of Polysulfides. To
visually assess the interaction between additives and poly-
sulfides, a visual adsorption experiment of a Li2S6 solution was
conducted in glass vials. Equal amounts of 0.2 M Li2S6 solution
were added to electrolytes containing additives and to a DME/
DOL solution, respectively, and left to stand for 12 h. The
color gradient observed, from darkest to lightest, was: 2,5-DFP
< 2-FP < PZ < Li2S6, visually demonstrating that 2,5-DFP has a
stronger adsorption capacity for LiPSs. This result was also
corroborated by UV/vis absorption spectroscopy (Figures S6
and S7). To investigate the modulating effect of 2,5-DFP
additives on the kinetics of liquid−liquid conversion,3,13
symmetrical batteries containing PZ, 2-FP, and 2,5-DFP
additives with Li2S6 were assembled. As shown in Figure
2a−c, CV curves at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1 exhibit almost no
response current in the absence of Li2S6. In the presence of
pure Li2S6, no significant peaks are observed in the curves
without the additive. In contrast, the cell with 2,5-DFP + Li2S6
exhibits larger peak areas and peak intensities, indicating that
the conversion kinetics of polysulfides was improved.23 This
demonstrates the highest catalytic effect of 2,5-DFP.
Furthermore, the Nyquist plots obtained from electrochemical
impedance spectroscopy (EIS) of Li2S6 symmetric cells, shown
in Figure S8, indicate that the cell with the 2,5-DFP additive
has the lowest internal resistance and interfacial charge transfer

The formation of solid Li2S from liquid LiPSs is regarded as
the conversion bottleneck during the redox processes of
cathode sulfur.25,26 Effects of various additives on the
deposition of Li2S were evaluated by potentiostatic electro-
chemical deposition, which is shown in Figure 2d−f and Figure
S9. The curve of 2,5-DFP cell exhibits an earlier and higher
response current and a higher precipitation capacity (445 mAh
g−1) than 2-FP (400.3 mAh g−1), PZ (386.5 mAh g−1), and
without (303.3 mAh g−1) based on Faraday’s law. The results
indicate that 2,5-DFP exhibits an accelerated nucleation effect.
The nitrogen atoms form chemical bonds with LiPSs, lowering
their reduction potential and promoting the conversion of
LiPSs to Li2S.27 The fluorine atoms, with their strong
electronegativity, regulate the electron distribution of LiPSs
through electrostatic interactions, further reducing the energy
barrier for Li2S nucleation.28 This is consistent with the
theoretical calculations, demonstrating that the combined
action of nitrogen and fluorine significantly enhances the
efficiency of the liquid−solid conversion reaction, thereby
promoting the nucleation of LiPSs.20,25 Furthermore, the trend
in precipitation capability aligns with the number of F atoms in
the additives, providing sufficient evidence to infer that
nitrogen and fluorine in the additives exhibit a synergistic
effect during the liquid−solid conversion process.

As expected, the cell with 2,5-DFP demonstrated the best
rate capacities at each current density. As is shown in Figure
3d, the 2,5-DFP cell exhibits a higher initial capacity of 1215.6
mAh g−1 at 0.1 C and 588.1 mAh g−1 at 4 C, compared with
cells with PZ and 2-FP as additives. For the long-term cycling
performance, 2,5-DFP delivers the initial 931 mAh g−1 and the
retained discharge capacity of 678 mAh g−1 after 150 cycles at
0.2 C (Figure 3e). At a higher current at 1 C, the cell with 2,5-
DFP also shows an enhanced capacity retention, maintaining
360 mAh g−1 after 800 cycles, which is higher than the cell
with 2-FP (230 mAh g−1) and the PZ cell (150 mAh g−1)
(Figure 3f). The decay rate is only 0.066% for the 2,5-DFP cell,
lower than that for the 2-FP cell (0.084%), the PZ cell
(0.097%) and the control cell (0.129%), respectively. The
improved cyclability and Coulombic efficiency of the cell are
majorly rooted to enhance the redox kinetics and suppressed
shuttle effect by 2,5-DFP.25,39 It is speculated that further
engineering to the electrolytes will encourage better cycling
performance with interactions between N and F in 2,5-DFP
and LiPSs.

The surface composition of the control and lithium anode
with introduced additives in Li−S batteries after 50 cycles was
investigated by using XPS characterization. Panels g−i of
Figures 5 and Figure S23 display the Li 1s, F 1s, C 1s, and S 2p
spectra obtained from cyclic lithium with 2,5-DFP, 2-FP, PZ,
and blank LiTFSI electrolytes. In the Li 1s spectra, peaks at
54.6, 55.4, 56.1, and 57.3 eV correspond to Li−S, Li−N, Li−O,
and Li−F, respectively. Li−N, Li−O, and Li−F are derived
from the decomposition of LiTFSI/LiNO3 and the 2,5-DFP/2-
FP/PZ products. The peaks at 55.4 eV (Li−N) and 57.3 eV
(Li−F) were enhanced compared to the blank cell, suggesting
that Li3N and LiF are key components of the SEIs.19,47 Among
them, the Li−N bond formed between the nitrogen atoms of
pyrazine and the lithium atoms of Li2Sx.17,18,20 The surface of
Li−N is lithophilic and can guide the electrochemical
deposition of lithium ions along its surface through the
interaction of Li−Li3N.48 This guiding effect promotes the
formation of a uniform and dense deposition layer, further
reducing the contact between LiPSs and lithium metal and
inhibiting the shuttle effect.20,48 Additionally, Li−F exhibits an
extremely high mechanical strength and chemical stability. It
forms a dense SEI that effectively prevents direct contact
between the electrolyte and lithium metal, thereby reducing
the occurrence of side reactions.49 Therefore, Li−F and Li−N
work synergistically to not only inhibit the growth of lithium
dendrites but also reduce the polysulfide shuttle effect. This
cooperative interaction significantly enhances the safety and
cycling stability of Li−S batteries.
Furthermore, the F 1s spectra using 2-FP and 2,5-DFP
showed the presence of LiTFSI and LiF at 688.7 and 685.0 eV,
along with a gradual enhancement of co-ionic C−F at 690.5 eV
(Figure 5h).49,51 The co-ionic C−F in the C 1s spectrum at
292.9 eV was also gradually strengthened by the addition of 2-
FP and 2,5-DFP, confirming their participation in SEI
formation, which benefits the protection of lithium ano-
des.50−55 For the S 2p deconvolution results, the ratio between
high-valence sulfur-containing species (Li2SO3/Li2SO4) to
low-valence sulfur-containing species (Li2S2/Li2S) in SEI is
obviously different. The higher content of Li2SO3/Li2SO4 in
SEI with LiTFSI in the 2,5-DFP cell can induce uniform Li
deposition and superior cycle performance (Figure 5i).56 The
more intensive Li2S peak in the absence of a cell shows the
corrosion of the Li anode and explains the rapid cycling
degradation.
Based on SEM, EDS, and XPS analyses, the enriched hybrid
SEI effectively avoids the formation of lithium dendrites, thus
enabling the battery to achieve stable operation. The
introduction of 2,5-DFP is a useful additive that might show
its potential for a practical cell. To prove that, we fabricated
coin cells with high sulfur loading (Figure 5j). The sulfur
cathode battery with an area loading of 3.8 mg cm−2 could
maintain 79.2% capacity retention after 120 cycles. The
excellent electrochemical performance of the cell indicated its
high development potential and commercial prospect. (The
capacity retention of the without cell is 57.5%, Figure S24).
This work revealed the performance of the organic additive
2,5-DFP for Li−S batteries, contributing to a re-examination
electrolyte design requirements and principles.
■CONCLUSION
In summary, we have introduced 2,5-DFP as an effective
electrolyte additive for Li−S batteries. This molecule,
containing bis-nitrogen and bis-fluorine atoms, lowers binding
energy and reduces charge transfer impedance compared to
pure Li2Sx, thereby accelerating liquid−liquid conversion and
Li2S precipitation. By comparing it with two other additives, 2-
FP (one fluorine atom) and PZ (no fluorine atom), we
elucidated the role of fluorine in protecting the lithium anode.
The nitrogen-rich and fluorine-rich 2,5-DFP facilitate the
formation of stable and uniform organic−inorganic hybrid
SEIs containing Li−F and Li−N, which protect the lithium
anode from continuous corrosion by dissolved LiPSs.
Consequently, the coin cell with 2,5-DFP exhibited a high
initial discharge capacity of 1215.6 mAh g−1 at 0.1 C and
demonstrated stable long-term cycling performance with 800
cycles (0.066% capacity decay per cycle) at 1 C. Notably, the
high-loading cell (3.8 mg cm−2) with low electrolyte usage (10
μL/ms S) maintained 80% capacity over 120 cycles. This study
reveals the significant impact of 2,5-DFP on LiPSs conversion
and lithium anode protection, and it inspires the development
of new nitrogen−fluorine compound additives for high-
performance Li−S batteries.
