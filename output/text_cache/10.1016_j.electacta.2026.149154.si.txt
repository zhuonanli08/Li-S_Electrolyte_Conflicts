Supplementary Information 2/7
Morphological and Elemental Analysis of the C-S Composite
Figure S2(a) displays the SEM image of the C-S composite cathode. The composite particles were observed to have a diameter of approximately 2 µm, exhibiting a surface morphology nearly identical to that of the pristine Cnovel carbon.
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of the C-S composite revealed a weight loss of approximately 65 wt% when heated to 500 °C under an inert atmosphere. This weight loss corresponds to the sublimation of sulfur. The absence of bulk sulfur agglomerates on the particle surface in the SEM images, combined with the significant sulfur content confirmed by TGA, suggests that the sulfur is effectively infused within the internal mesoporous structure of the Cnovel matrix rather than accumulating on the exterior surface.
To further verify the distribution of sulfur, elemental mapping was performed using EDX, as shown in Figure S2(b). The mapping reveals that the spatial distribution of sulfur (S) coincides well with that of carbon (C), indicating that both elements are present in the same regions. This result confirms the successful formation of a composite where sulfur is homogeneously composited with the Cnovel framework.
