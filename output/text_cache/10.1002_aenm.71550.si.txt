Experimental
1.1 Materials preparation
The blank commercial electrolyte (named TMA-free) was purchased from Dodo Chem. It is composed of 1 M LiPF6 dissolved in EC : DEC (1:1 by volume) + 10 vol% FEC. 1 M LiPF6 in EC : DEC (1:1 by volume) with 10 vol% FEC and 1 wt% TMABr was used as the TMA electrolyte. Tetramethylammonium bromide (TMABr, C4H12NBr, 98%) powder was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical. The electrolytes were prepared in an argon-filled glovebox (<0.01 ppm for both H2O and O2) and stirred overnight. Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology provided sulfur (S) and polyacrylonitrile (PAN, Mw = 150000) powder. The SPAN cathode preparation method was based on a previously reported method with minor modifications[1]. S and PAN powders were ball milled with a mass ratio of 4:1. The homogeneous mixture was heated at 300 °C in a tube furnace for 10 h with a ramp rate of 2 °C min−1. The SPAN slurry was prepared by mixing SPAN powder, carbon nanotubes (CNTs, Macklin), and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF, Alfa Aesar) in a mass ratio of 7:2:1 in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, Sigma-Aldrich) solvent, which was uniformly spread on a carbon-coated aluminum foil and dried at 50 °C in a vacuum oven over 12 h. The SPAN film was punched into 10 mm discs as cathode electrode for use (active loading mass was about 2 mg cm−2).

1.2 Electrochemical measurements
All the coin cells (CR2032-type) were assembled and disassembled in an argon-filled glove box (O2 < 0.1 ppm, H2O < 0.1 ppm). Approximately 45 µL of electrolyte was added to each coin cell to ensure complete wetting of the separator and electrodes, and Celgard 2400 was used as the separator. For testing Coulombic efficiency (CE), copper foil was used as the working electrode and lithium foil as the counter electrode. Li|Li symmetrical cells were assembled from two identical lithium electrodes and tested under specified current densities and capacities. To verify the compatibility of the electrolyte with the SPAN cathode and metallic lithium anode, Li|SPAN batteries were assembled and evaluated using a NEWARE battery testing system (CT-4008Tn-5V10mA-164) at current densities of 0.5 C and 1 C within the voltage range of 1–3 V. Rate performance testing was conducted at current densities ranging from 0.1 to 2 C. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves of Li|Cu batteries with TMA-free and TMA electrolytes were obtained at the scanning rate of 0.1 mV s−1. Tafel curves were obtained from Li|Li symmetric cells by scanning the voltage from −0.2 to 0.2 V at a scan rate of 1 mV s−1. CV, Tafel and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were performed on the CHI-760E electrochemical workstation (CH Instruments, Inc., Shanghai). Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) was carried out at 0.1 C between 1 and 3 V with 10 min galvanostatic pulse and a relaxation step of 30 min.

1.3 Material characterization
Raman spectra were collected on a Raman spectrometer (Horiba LabRAM HR800) using a laser 532 nm, 50 x microscope objective (Olympus, MLPlanTL N). For in-situ Raman spectroscopy, Li|Li battery was assembled with an in-situ Raman battery mold (LIB-Raman, Beijing Scistar Technology Co., Ltd). In-situ Raman experiments were conducted during the discharging cycle at a current density of 1 mA cm−2, with diffraction patterns collected every 2 min. A Bruker Avance 600 spectrometer was used to measure the 1H (600 MHz), 13C (151 MHz) and 19F (565 MHz) NMR spectra. The TMA-free and TMA-containing electrolytes were diluted with CDCl3 using the same electrolyte/CDCl3 ratio. X-ray diffraction (XRD) was performed on Bruker D8 Advance instrument with Cu Kα radiation at a scan rate of 2° min−1 and a 2θ range of 5-80°. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, SU-8000, acceleration voltage 10 kV) was used to characterize the Li deposition morphology of the cycled Li electrodes with the TMA-free and TMA electrolytes. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were carried out on an Escalab 250 XI (Thermo Scientific) using a 200 W monochromatic Al Kα radiation. The solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) and cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) were characterized using a high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) (JEOL-2100F). In-situ Fourier Transform Infrared (FT-IR) testing was performed using a FT-IR (Nicolet iS50, Thermo-Fisher Scientific) equipped with an attenuated total reflectance (ATR) probe. The lithium (Li) electrode was plated at -0.15 V for 15 min and stripped at 0.15 V for 15 min in two different electrolytes: the TMA-free electrolyte (1.0 M LiPF6 in EC:DEC (1:1 by volume) with 10% FEC) and the TMA electrolyte (1.0 M LiPF6 in EC:DEC (1:1 by volume) with 10% FEC with 1 wt% TMABr). The Young's modulus of the SPAN electrode after cycling was measured using atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker).
1.4 Computational details
Quantum chemistry calculations for the electrolyte components and the TMA+-PF6- cluster were performed using the ORCA 6.1.1 program[2]. Geometry optimizations and vibrational frequency calculations (to confirm true local minima) were carried out using the B3LYP functional with the def2-TZVPD basis set[3,4]. Grimme’s D3 dispersion correction with Becke-Johnson damping (D3BJ) was applied to accurately describe non-covalent interactions. The RIJCOSX approximation with the def2/J auxiliary basis set was utilized to accelerate the computation. The Solvation Model based on Density (SMD) was employed with acetone as the reference solvent. The Multiwfn software was used for the post-processing of the electrostatic potential (ESP) and to derive the Restrained Electrostatic Potential (RESP) charges[5,6]. Additionally, the energy levels of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), along with the binding energy of the TMA+-PF6- complex, were evaluated. Molecular orbitals and ESP isosurfaces were visualized using GaussView 6.0[18].
Spin-polarized periodic density functional theory (DFT) calculations for the surface slab models were performed using VASP 5.4.4 with the projector augmented-wave (PAW) method and the GGA-PBE exchange-correlation functional[7,8]. The DFT-D3 method was applied for empirical dispersion corrections[9]. The plane-wave cutoff energy was set to 500 eV, and the Brillouin zone was sampled using a -centered 221 k-point mesh with a Methfessel-Paxton smearing of 0.1 eV. Energy and force convergence criteria were set to 10-6 eV and 0.01 eV/Å, respectively.
The Li (001) surface was modeled as a 6-layer slab within a 13.0013.0035.83 Å3 supercell, incorporating a vacuum region of 25 Å. During structural relaxation, the bottom two layers were fixed, while the remaining upper layers and the TMA adsorbate were fully relaxed. A dipole correction along the z-axis was applied. The isolated gas-phase TMA molecule was simulated in an identical orthogonal box to ensure the cancellation of systematic errors.

The macroscopic electrostatic potential for the work function calculation was extracted using VASPKIT 1.5.1[10]. Charge density difference (CDD) maps were visualized using VESTA with an isosurface level of 0.001 Å3. Finally, the quantitative electron transfer (fractional charge) between the Li surface and the TMA adsorbate was evaluated using the grid-based Bader charge analysis algorithm[11]. 
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of the liquid electrolytes were conducted using the GROMACS package[12]. The intermolecular interactions were described by the all-atom OPLS-AA force field[13,14,15], with partial atomic charges assigned using the QM-derived RESP charges. Two distinct simulation boxes were constructed using the PACKMOL[16] and fftool[19] utilities: a baseline electrolyte containing 90 Li+, 90 PF6-, 618 EC, 342 DEC, and 120 FEC molecules, and a modified electrolyte system incorporating an additional 90 TMA+ and 90 Br-.
For the equilibration phase in total 10 ns, the systems were heated from 0 K to 348.15 K over 500 ps, maintained at 348.15 K until 750 ps, and subsequently cooled to the target temperature of 298.15 K by 1000 ps. The V-rescale thermostat and the Berendsen barostat were employed during equilibration. Following this, a 100 ns production run was performed at 298.15 K, during which the pressure coupling was switched to the Parrinello-Rahman barostat for rigorous ensemble sampling. 
For structural analyses, the 100 ns production trajectory was sampled at an interval of 10 ps, yielding a statistically robust ensemble of 10,000 frames. The radial distribution functions (RDFs, g(r)) were calculated to investigate the local solvation structure of the Li+ cations. The average coordination numbers (CN) were quantitatively obtained by integrating the RDFs. Furthermore, the Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD 1.9.3) software was employed to visualize and extract the TMA+-PF6- ion pairs within the simulation box[17], applying a strict distance cutoff of 3.0 Å to define the associated coordination complexes.

Comparative partial 1H, 13C, and 19F NMR spectra of the TMA-free and TMA-containing electrolytes are shown in Figure S4. The carbonate-related 1H resonances at approximately 4.4–4.7 ppm exhibit a discernible line-shape variation after TMA addition, including attenuation of the shoulder near 4.60 ppm. Consistent variations are observed in the carbonate-related 13C signals at approximately 63.6–65.0 ppm, indicating a modification of the local solvent environment.
The FEC-related 19F resonance at approximately −121.6 ppm becomes sharper and more symmetric in the TMA-containing electrolyte. The characteristic PF6−-related doublet at approximately −73.6 and −74.9 ppm is retained in both electrolytes, accompanied by subtle line-shape variations after TMA addition.[S21] These spectra were obtained under identical CDCl3-diluted conditions and are therefore used as qualitative comparative evidence rather than for quantitative determination of SSIP, CIP, or AGG populations. Together with the Raman and MD results, the NMR variations support a TMA-induced modification of the local electrolyte coordination environment.
