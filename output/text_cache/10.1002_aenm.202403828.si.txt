Supplementary Note 1. Minimum volume of electrolyte required to wet the cathode and separator
The minimum volume of electrolyte required to wet the cathode and separator can be calculated using the volumes of the voids in the cathode and separator.

To calculate the void volume of the cathode, the porosity of the cathode was first calculated using the following equation.

To calculate the volumes of the components, the composition over 1 cm2 of the electrodes is used, as summarized in Table S1.

Table S1. Materials included in the composite cathode.
An electrode with 120-µm thickness was coated on a 10-µm-thick Al foil. The volume of the electrode was 0.00269 cm-3, and the calculated porosity was 78%. The void volume corresponding to a Φ size of 14 mm with 78% porosity was 0.0128 cm3, i.e., 12.8 μL.
The void volume of the Li metal anode can be determined by multiplying the change in thickness by the area of this anode. According to Figure 1, the thickness of the degraded Li metal increases by 16 µm, yielding a final thickness of 56 µm. The area considered for this calculation, which matches the size of the anode, was 1.53 cm². Consequently, the product of the thickness change and the anode area indicates that 2.5 µL of electrolyte was required.
Thus, the combined void volume of the cathode and the separator, representing the minimum electrolyte volume required to wet both components, was 15.4 µL. This minimum electrolyte volume is then divided by the theoretical capacity of the sulfur-loaded cathode (3.8 mg), yielding a capacity of 9.78 mAh. Therefore, the minimum electrolyte amount was calculated to be 1.57 µL mAh⁻¹. Additionally, when considering the electrolyte trapped in porous Li, the required volume increased to 1.83 µL mAh-1.

Figure S2. 1H NMR and 19F NMR data used for calculating the decomposed amounts of the electrolyte components. 1H NMR spectroscopy conducted on the electrolytes in the Li-S batteries (a) before and (b) after operation. 19F NMR spectroscopy conducted on the electrolytes (c) before and (d) after operation.
To measure the amount of electrolyte present before operation, 29.3 µL of the electrolyte was diluted in 1.5 mL of tetrahydrofuran (THF). To measure the amount of electrolyte present after operation, all of the cell components were washed with the same amount of THF. The peaks were normalized using the –CH2 group, i.e., δ = 1.8 ppm farther from the O in THF. To quantify the amount of DME, the peak corresponding to the H in the –OCH3 group (δ = 3.15 ppm) was compared with that corresponding to THF. To quantify the amount of DOL, the peaks corresponding to the –CH2 groups between two O atoms (δ = 4.902 ppm) were compared. The peak areas were integrated and subsequently compared. The decomposed amounts of DME and DOL were calculated from the relative peak areas corresponding to DME and DOL before and after the operation.

To quantify the amount of BTFE solvent, the peak area corresponding to F in the –CF3 group was calculated. The peak areas were integrated and then compared. The decomposed amounts of FSI and the fluorinated solvents were calculated from the relative peak areas of fluorobenzene before and after operation.
