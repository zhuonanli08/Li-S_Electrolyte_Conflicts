1. Experimental Section 
1.1. Raw materials 
Lithium (Li) metal foils (China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd.), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, 
DoDoChem), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, DoDoChem), di-isopropyl sulfide (DIPS, TCI), 1,3,5-
trioxane (TXA, TCI), lithium bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, DoDoChem), 
LiNO3 (Aladdin), Li2S (Alfa), elemental sulfur (S8, Alfa), aluminum (Al) foils, carbon 
nanotubes (CNTs, Cnano), poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, HSV-300), N-methylpyrrolidone 
(NMP, Aladdin), and polypropylene (PP) membranes (Celgard 2400) were used as purchased 
without further purification. 
 
1.2. Preparation of electrolytes 
The conventional electrolyte composed of DOL and DME (1:1, by vol.) with 1.0 M LiTFSI 
was employed as the control electrolyte, denoted as CVE. The experimental electrolyte was 
composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in the mixture of TXA/DME (1:2, by mol.) mixed with DIPS (4:1, 
by vol.), denoted as TWE. 5 wt.% LiNO3 was added into the electrolytes to improve the 
performance of Li–S full cells. To prepare the electrolyte with Li2S8 or Li2S6, Li2S, and S8 were 
mixed with a molar ratio of 8:7 or 8:5 in the above electrolytes, respectively. All electrolytes 
were prepared in an argon-filled glove box with H2O and O2 contents below 0.1 ppm. 
 
1.3. Preparation of sulfur cathodes 
Both CNTs and S8 were mixed with a weight ratio of 7:3 and then ball-milled with PVDF 
binders with a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a homogeneous slurry. The slurry was then 
cast onto Al foils by doctor blades and dried at 60°C for 12 h to obtain sulfur cathodes. The

SI2 
 
sulfur cathodes were prepared with the areal sulfur loading of 1.3, 4.0, or 7.9 mg cm−2 and 
punched into disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm for electrochemical measurements. 
 
1.4. Electrochemical measurements 
For the cathode kinetic evaluation, Li2S6 symmetric cells were assembled with carbon-
coated Al foils as electrodes (13.0 mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 
40 µL electrolyte with 1.0 M[S] Li2S6. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was 
performed at the open circuit voltage with a sinusoidal voltage amplitude of 10 mV and a 
frequency range from 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed with a scan 
rate of 50 mV s−1 and a voltage window of −0.4–0.4 V. Both tests were conducted on a Solartron 
1470E electrochemical workstation. 
To verify the mitigation of the parasitic reactions, Li | Li symmetric cells were assembled 
with 50 μm Li (15.0 mm in diameter) as electrodes, PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 
40 μL electrolyte containing 0.5 M[S] Li2S8 and tested at a current density of 1.0 mA cm−2 and 
an areal capacity of 4.0 mAh cm−2. Furthermore, Li–S cells were assembled with 600 µm Li 
anodes (16.0 mm in diameter), 1.3 mg cm−2 sulfur cathodes (13.0 mm in diameter), PP 
separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 25 µL electrolyte without the LiNO3 additive. The Li–S 
cells were firstly galvanostatically charged to 2.90 V and then discharged to 2.60, 2.45, 2.43, 
2.40, 2.39, 2.38, 2.37, 2.36, 2.35, 2.34, 2.25 and 2.20 V step by step to measure the shuttle 
current. The cells were kept potentiostatically for 1.0 h at each voltage step and the shuttle 
current was determined by the steady-state current at each different potentiostatic voltages. 
Moreover, the Li–S cells were cycled at 0.1 C (1 C = 1672 mA g−1) with a voltage range of 
1.70–2.60 V to investigate the Coulombic efficiency (CE). The Li–S cells were tested on a 
Neware battery cycler (CT-4008T, Shenzhen, China) at room temperature.

1.5. Immersion test of Li metal 
For the visual experiment, 600 μm Li foils were immersed into 2.0 mL of CVE or TWE 
with 2.5 mM[S] Li2S8 to evaluate the stability of the lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) with Li metal. 
The color of the electrolyte was detected before and after 4 h soaking. 
 
1.6. Material characterization 
Li metal anodes were disassembled from the cycled Li–S cells and washed 3 times by 
DME to remove residual electrolytes and dried in the glove box. The Li deposition morphology 
was characterized by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM 7401F, JEOL Ltd., Japan) 
operated at 3.0 kV. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) experiments were carried out on a 
scanning X-ray microprobe (Axis Supra+, Kratos Analytical) operated at 250 kV with 
monochromated Al Kα radiation as the X-ray source. The Ar+ sputtering rate was ~130 nm min–
1 calibrated on the Ta2O5 surface. Furthermore, time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry 
(ToF-SIMS) was performed (PHI nano ToF II, ULVAC-PHI, Inc.) using a special transfer 
vessel to prevent samples from being exposed to the ambient air. The sputter etching was 
performed using an Ar+ beam (3 kV 100 nA) to obtain depth profile. 
 
1.7. Li–S full cell assembly and battery performance evaluation 
The performance of Li–S full cells was evaluated in standard 2032 coin-type cells. Li–S 
coin cells were assembled with 50 μm Li anodes (15.0 mm in diameter), 4.0 mg cm−2 sulfur 
cathodes (13.0 mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 35 µL electrolyte 
containing 5 wt.% LiNO3 additives. In addition, Li–S coin cells with ultrahigh sulfur loading 
were assembled with 50 μm Li anodes (15.0 mm in diameter), 7.9 mg cm−2 sulfur cathodes 
(13.0 mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 75 µL electrolyte containing

5 wt.% LiNO3 additives. The cells were galvanostatically cycled at 0.1 C after activation at 0.05 
C with a voltage range of 1.70–2.60 V for cycling performance evaluation on the Neware 
battery cycler. 
For Li–S pouch cell evaluation, the sulfur cathodes were double-side coated on Al foils 
following the method in section 1.3. The sulfur cathodes and Li metal anodes were cut into a 
size of 4 × 7 cm2. The areal sulfur loading was around 7.9 mg cm−2 and the thickness of Li metal 
was 75 µm on a single side. The pouch cell was assembled with stacks of double-side Li metal 
anodes, double-side sulfur cathodes, and PP separators in a dry room with a dew point below 
−40°C. A low electrolyte to sulfur (E/S) ratio of 3.0 or 3.5 g gS−1 was used for 2.5 Ah-level and 
3.0 Ah-level pouch cells, respectively. The 2.5 Ah-level and 3.0 Ah-level pouch cells were 
cycled at 0.05 C and 0.025 C (1 C = 1000 mA g−1), respectively, with a voltage range of 1.70–
2.55 V on a LAND battery cycler. The energy density was calculated based on the total mass 
of the pouch cell including current collectors, package, and tabs. 
 
1.8. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations 
MD simulations were performed with the LAMMPS code1 and OPLS-AA force field.2 
The force field parameters were generated by the LigParGen web server,3 except that restrained 
electrostatic potential (RESP) atomic partial charges were assigned to atoms based on 
electrostatic potential (ESP) charges using the Multiwfn program.4 The parameters for Li+, 
TFSI−, and S82− were obtained from Jensen et al.,5 Koddermann et al.,6 and Rajput et al.,7 
respectively. Two models were constructed, one with DIPS and the other without. The model 
with DIPS contains 229 TXA, 462 DME, 110 DIPS, 10 Li2S8, and 80 LiTFSI molecules. The 
model without DIPS contains 572 DOL, 385 DME, 10 Li2S8, and 80 LiTFSI molecules. The 

<<PAGE 5>>
                                                                                           
 
SI5 
 
initial atomic coordinates were generated with the Packmol8 program. The final configurations 
were visualized with the VESTA9 program. 
The time step was fixed to be 1 fs for all simulations. All systems were first equilibrated 
in the NPT ensemble using the Parrinello–Rahman barostat10 for 2 ns at 298 K and 1 atm with 
time constants of 0.1 ps for temperature and 1 ps for pressure. Subsequently, the models were 
heated from 298 to 420 K over 2 ns and maintained at 420 K for 4 ns. This was followed by 
cooling from 420 to 298 K over 2 ns and equilibration at 298 K for 4 ns. Then, the ensemble 
was changed to NVT for additional equilibration over 7 ns under the Nosé–Hoover 
thermostat.11,12 Finally, a production run of 5 ns at 298 K in the NVT ensemble was conducted, 
where samples were taken every 1 ps for solvation structure analyses.
