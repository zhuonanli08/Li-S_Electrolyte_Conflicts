Experimental section:  
 
Materials: 
Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, 99.95%, Sigma), Lithium nitrate (LiNO3, 
99.99%, Sigma), Lithium sulfide (Li2S, 99.98%, Sigma), Sulfur (99.998%, Sigma), Tetraethylene glycol 
dimethyl ether (TEGDME, 99%, Sigma), Bis(cyclopentadienyl)cobalt (Cobaltocene, Alfa Aesar), 
Chloroplatinic acid hydrate (H2PtCl6. xH2O, 99.9%, Sigma) were used without any further purification. 
Glassy carbon (5 X 4 mm, Ted Pella) was polished with alumina paste and was washed thoroughly with DI 
water before each experiment.  
Lithium Polysulfides (catholytes) preparation 
Stock solutions of Li2S8 (0.2 M) and Li2S6 (0.2 M) were prepared, for further dilution, using 
stoichiometric amounts of Li2S and sulfur in TEDGME solvent and were heated at 90o C overnight. A blank 
solution containing 0.1 M-LiTFSI and 0.1M- LiNO3 in TEGDME solvent was also prepared similarly 
without heating. For electrochemical imaging 1 mM Li2S8 in blank solution was used. For approach curve 
studies, 2 mM Cobaltocene in 0.1 M-LiTFSI and 0.1M- LiNO3 in TEGDME solvent was used. 
AFM-SECM cell assembly 
A Li-S cell was assembled in a specially designed four-electrode electrochemical cell. The AFM-
SECM tip was used as working electrode-2, a glassy carbon circular disk (~4 mm2 area) was used as 
working electrode-1, and a lithium strip acted as reference cum counter electrode. Based on existing 
literature and our own prior studies on understanding the effects of electrolytes with varied Li-salt 
concentration, solvent composition, and protective additives, the concentration of Li salt was pinned as 
0.1M LiTFSI with 0.1M LiNO3 as an additive and TEGDME as the solvent.1-5 The entire electrochemical 
cell was assembled inside an Argon filled glove box (oxygen and moisture levels maintained below 0.1 
ppm) using a specially designed transfer chamber. The chamber was equipped with a provision to drain or 
fill the cell with electrolyte outside the glovebox, without exposing it to the outer atmosphere. The chamber 
was thoroughly sealed inside the glove box and was then transferred to the AFM-SECM work station. A 
continuous flow of Argon gas was maintained in the chamber throughout the experiments, in order to 
preserve a controlled atmosphere for Li-ion battery experiments.  
AFM-SECM and Raman instrumentation 
All the measurements in this study were carried out using a Nanonics Multiview 2000 system 
(Nanonics, Israel) coupled with CHI 760E (CH Instruments, Austin, TX) bipotentiostat electrochemical 
work-station. AFM-SECM tip functioning was governed by NSW software provided by Nanonics Ltd, and 
the resulting current was monitored simultaneously using both NSW and CHI softwares. The AFM-SECM 
platinum (Pt) nanoprobes used in this study were procured from Nanonics company and had a diameter of 
100 nm with an Rg factor of 2 and Q-factor of 1600. A frequency of 34.32 kHz was used for tuning the 
AFM probe to obtain approach. The AFM-SECM setup was placed on an i4 series active vibration isolation 
table (Accurion Ltd.) to eliminate any possible external vibrations from the surroundings. Raman 
spectroscopy of Li2S samples was carried out with 532 nm green laser (Nd-YaG source) in ANDOR 
Shamrock 500i model. All the Raman spectrum band intensities were previously baseline corrected.

SECM Approach curve measurements: 
Redox mediator (cobaltocene) response: The calibration approach curve measurement was carried 
out with a conical shaped Pt tip of 100 nm diameter, in a four-electrode setup; where tip and substrate act 
as two working electrodes with Li metal foil as a counter and reference electrodes. Initially, the tip was 
positioned in the vicinity of the substrate, immersed in a solution containing 2 mM-Cobaltocene in the 
blank electrolyte. Next, to confirm proper approach, the tip was moved towards the surface using a stepper 
motor at 300 Hz, and the approach was confirmed by an error generated by the AFM feedback.  
The tip was then retracted and the steady-state current was recorded at the AFM-SECM Pt tip far 
away from the substrate (iT,∞) and the corresponding current can be given as the following equation6.

Also, in the same position, cyclic voltammetry was performed both on tip and substrate (Figure S1) 
in the potential range of 1.5 V to 3.0 V vs Li/Li+.  
Next, in the SECM-feedback mode, the tip and the substrate were biased at 2.1 V and 2.4 V 
respectively. The positive feedback approach curve, in terms of tip current (iT) versus distance from the 
substrate (d), was then obtained at a preset approach speed of 0.0003 µm/ms. The experimental tip current

Competition mode control experiment  
Initially, platinum (Pt) nanoparticles were electrodeposited using a method described elsewhere9. 
Briefly, a typical three-electrode system was set up using glassy carbon, platinum wire, and Ag/AgCl as 
working, counter and reference electrodes respectively. An aqueous electrolyte containing 1 mM 
chloroplatinic acid and 0.1 M sulfuric acid was used. Pt was electrodeposited by performing cyclic 
voltammetry in the potential region between 0.2 V to 1.2 V at a scan rate of 50 mV s-1. The substrate 
containing electrodeposited Pt was used as a working electrode for the control experiment.   
Next, for scanning, a cell setup as described earlier was made with 1mM Li2S6 in 0.1 M LiTFSI 
and 0.1 M LiNO3 in TEGDME as the electrolyte. Scanning was performed in an intermittent mode, using 
amplitude signal as feedback for AFM-SECM tip approach. Scan area of 2 x 2 µm and a constant scan rate 
of 32 nm/ms was used for all the mappings. While scanning, to establish competition, the substrate was 
biased at different potentials from 2.5 V to 2.7 V vs Li/Li+, whereas the tip was held at a constant potential 
of 2.6 V. Then, at each potential simultaneous topographical (height) and electrochemical (current) and 
phase images were obtained at a resolution of 256 pixels. All the images were processed using WsxM 
software10 (Nanonics Ltd.), in an identical method to avoid any discrepancies. Figure S3 shows 
simultaneously recorded phase-contrast images.

In situ AFM-SECM mapping of Li2S/Li2S2 on carbon surface:  
Prior to oxidation studies, the cell containing 1mM Li2S8 was initially discharged (as described 
earlier in the main manuscript) to ensure the formation of Li2S/Li2S2 on the carbon, and the electrolyte 
containing catholyte was removed. A fresh blank electrolyte without polysulfides was then introduced into 
the cell. AFM-SECM scanning was then performed in an intermittent contact mode, using amplitude signal 
as feedback for AFM-SECM tip approach. Scan area of 10 X 10 µm and a constant scan rate of 32 nm/ms 
was used for all the mappings. For scanning, the tip was biased at a potential of 2.6 V while the substrate 
was biased with potentials of 2.5 V to 2.8 V vs. Li/Li+ and simultaneous topographical (height), AC phase 
and electrochemical (current) images were recorded at a resolution of 256 pixels. All the images were 
processed using WsxM software10 (Nanonics Ltd.), in an identical method to avoid any discrepancies. 
Figure S5 presents the changes in the height and volume of the Li2S/Li2S2 particle as a function of applied 
substrate potential. Figure S6 shows AFM-SECM mapping of the same particle while the substrate was 
biased at an oxidation potential of 2.8 V vs Li/Li+. The Raman measurements performed on the single 
particle, at different oxidative potentials are presented in Figure S7.  
 
Ex situ X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) studies: 
 
For ex situ XPS studies, typical CR-2032-coin cell was fabricated using carbon gas diffusion layer 
(GDL) as cathode and  Li metal as anode; which were sandwiched with porous polypropylene separator 
(Celgard) in between them using a catholyte containing 0.2 M Li2S8. Initially, the cell was galvanostatically 
discharged from open circuit potential to 1.9 V at a C-rate of C by 20, to nucleate Li2S/Li2S2. Then a constant 
potential of 1.9 V vs. Li/Li+ was applied for 10 hours, to grow Li2S. The cell was then immediately 
disassembled, and X-ray photoelectron spectra were collected using a PHI Quantera spectrophotometer. 
Open source XPS peak fit 4.1 software was used for deconvoluting the spectra.  Figure S4 shows XPS 
spectra of S2p core level obtained for Li2S/Li2S2 deposited on the carbon cathode surface. It can be clearly 
observed that the S2p spectrum consists of two peaks at 162.4 and 161.4 eV corresponding to Li2S2 and 
Li2S respectively. This result indicates the existence of both Li2S and Li2S2 in the reduction product on the 
carbon surface.

Li2S/Li2S2 deposition on carbon surface for in situ-AFM studies:  
Initially, the cell containing 1mM Li2S8 in the blank electrolyte was discharged from open circuit 
potential 2.4 V to 2.05 V11, using a CHI 700E bipotentiostat, to nucleate Li2S/Li2S2. Then constant potentials 
of 2.00 V, 1.95 V, and 1.90 V vs. Li/Li+ were applied to the substrate, each for 30 minutes to grow Li2S/Li2S2 
and AFM-imaging was done after applying each potential. During the reduction, at 2.0 V, the formation of 
several scattered particles with different sizes was observed throughout the surface. Further, with a 
subsequent decrease in reductive potentials to 1.95 and 1.90 V, accumulation of non-uniformly grown solid 
particles with a gradual increase in size and height was observed through AFM height profile (Figure S8).

Galvanostatic charge/discharge experiments:  
The galvanostatic charge-discharge experiments were carried out in a similar cell setup as described above. 
Initially, the cell was discharged at a current density of 20 µA/cm2. In situ AFM scanning was performed 
at two different reduction potential regions (as shown in Figure S10a,b). Then oxidation studies were carried 
out at a similar current density of 20 µA/cm2, and scanning was performed in two different oxidation 
potential regions (Figure S10 c,d).
