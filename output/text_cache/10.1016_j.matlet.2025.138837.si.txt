1.Experimental Procedures
1.1 Materials
Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI,99.9%) was obtained from the DoDoChem official website. Dimethoxyethane (DME,99%) and 1,3-dioxolane (DOL,99%) were purchased from Meryer Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Lithium nitrate (LiNO₃,99.999%) was acquired from Macklin Chemical Reagent. Polypropylene separators (PP,16±2 μm) were sourced from the Canrd official website. Lithium metal (Li,Diameter is 14 mm) was supplied by China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. A commercial electrolyte with 1M concentration (1M LiTFSI in DME:DOL = 1:1 vol% with 2 wt% LiNO₃) was procured from the DoDoChem official website.
1.2 Electrolyte Preparation
Six concentrations of electrolytes (0.2M, 0.5M, 0.8M, 1.3M, 1.6M, 2.0M) were prepared in 5 ml quantities in a glove box (Mikrouna, Super (1220/750/900)) under an Ar atmosphere. The preparation process involved weighing the corresponding masses of LiTFSI (moles × molar mass) and LiNO₃ (2% of the total mass of LiTFSI, DME, and DOL), placing them into a 20 ml glass vial, and adding a 1:1 volume ratio of DME and DOL (The total volume of electrolyte was adjusted to 5 mL by subtracting the volumes of LiTFSI and LiNO3.). The vial was sealed with a cap and sealing tape, then stirred at 50°C on a heating stirrer for 4 hours. The entire process was conducted inside the glove box(Figure S1).
1.3 Materials characterization
Raman spectra were acquired using a HORIBA LabRAM HR Evolution spectrometer.
1.4 Preparation of sulfur cathode
The sulfur cathode (KB/S) was prepared by mixing Ketjen Black (30 wt%) and sublimed sulfur (70 wt%) in a melt impregnation method. Subsequently, a slurry by mixing KB/S, and binder (LA133, sourced from the Canrd official website and used after dilution) at a weight ratio of 9:1 into NMP solvent was made and coated on carbon-coated aluminum foil to prepare the sulfur cathode. The loading mass of sulfur is about 1.0 mg cm-2 for normal electrochemical tests, and the high sulfur loading is also made (4~6 mg cm-2 sulfur loading) by enhancing the coating thickness of doctor blade.
1.5 Electrochemical measurements
CR2032 coin cells were assembled in a glove box (Mikrouna, Super (1220/750/900)) to evaluate electrochemical performance. The glove box was maintained under an argon (Ar) atmosphere. The coin cells consisted of the following components: a sulfur cathode (working electrode, diameter 12 mm), a PP separator, a bare lithium foil anode (diameter 14 mm, serving as both counter and reference electrode), and the electrolyte (six self-prepared concentrations and a commercial 1.0M electrolyte). Electrochemical performance (EIS) was evaluated using an electrochemical workstation (CHI 760E) through A.C. Impedance (IMP). Constant current charge-discharge tests were conducted using a LAND battery testing system (CT3002A).The low and high temperature environments were provided by an AMOi semiconductor thermal chamber (LL-19WL) for tests under varying temperature conditions. All assembled batteries were maintained at the designated temperature for at least 2 hours to ensure thermal equilibrium between the cells and the set temperature of the chamber. To ensure consistency across tests conducted at different temperatures, sulfur cathodes were used for all full-cell assemblies, ensuring uniform sulfur loading. The same batteries were used for charge-discharge curve tests across room, high, and low temperature conditions.

At low temperatures (e.g., –5 °C), conventional electrolytes typically suffer from reduced ion mobility due to increased viscosity. In contrast, dilute electrolytes can significantly reduce the overall viscosity of the system, thereby enhancing ion mobility. This corresponds to an increase in the rate constant k, and in the context of the Arrhenius relationship, reflects a reduction in the activation energy Ea​. As a result, dilute electrolytes exhibit superior ionic transport performance under low-temperature conditions, making them more suitable for low-temperature electrochemical applications.
