Materials and methods 
Synthesis of fluorinated solvents 
  For PPEO-CF3 solvent, 0.44 mol tosyl chloride (TsCl) and 0.5 mol KOH were 
dispersed in 250 mL dichloromethane (DCM) under an ice bath. The 0.4 mol 2-
propoxyethanol (PPEO) was dropwise added in the mixture and kept stirring for 4 h 
and the resultant white suspension was dissolved via adding 100 mL deionized water 
(DIW). The mixture was extracted using DCM for three times. The organic layer was 
collected and dried using anhydrous Na2SO4. Then DCM was removed using rotary 
evaporator to obtain intermediate product Ts-PPEO. Furthermore, under N2 atmosphere 
and -15 ℃, 0.34 mol NaH was dispersed in 160 mL Tetrahydrofuran (THF) and then 
0.33 mol 2,2,2-Trifluoroethanol was dropwise added to obtain sodium 2,2,2-
trifluoroethanolate. After total reaction without bubbles producing, 0.3 mol Ts-PPEO 
was added and the mixture was heated to 65 ℃ to reflux for 6 h. After cooling to room 
temperature, 90 mL DIW was added to dissolve the brown suspension. The THF was 
removed using rotary evaporator and then DCM was added to extract. The collected 
organic layer was dried using anhydrous Na2SO4 and further purified via distillation 
under reduced pressure. Finally, the PPEO-CF3 was dried using a Li+ exchange 4 Å 
molecular sieve and saved in glove box. 
  For PXEO-CF3 solvent, 2-isopropoxyethanol (PXEO) was used to replace PPEO. 
Electrolyte preparation and characterization 
The LiFSI and DME were purchased from Tansoole and the solvent was further 
purified with a Li+ exchange 4 Å molecular sieve for 24 h. 2 M LiFSI was dissolved in 
different solvents to prepare electrolytes marked in 2 M-DME, 2 M-PPEO-CF3 and 2 
M-PXEO-CF3 respectively. The ionic conductivity was measured with a DDB-303A 
conductivity meter (INESA Instrument) and the viscosity was measured on a 
programmable rheometer (USANTV-CAP1, Nirun). The contact angel was tested by 
DSA 30 from Kruss Inc. Raman spectrum were recorded with a DXR Raman 
microscope (Thermo Scientific) using a HeNe laser with a wavelength of 780 nm as the 
excitation source. FTIR was conducted using a Perkin-Elmer Spectrum 100. The NMR 

<<PAGE 4>>
spectrum of the electrolytes were determined without a deuterated reagent using an 
AVANCE III HD 500 (Bruker 500).  
Electrochemical measurements 
For coin cells, 200 μm lithium foil was used and the electrolyte amount was 
controlled at 30 μL except where otherwise noted. The CE of Li-Cu half-cells was 
calculated using an optimized Aurbach protocol[1] according to the equation: CEavg. = 
(n QC + QS) / (n QC + QT), where QT indicates an initial Li plating capacity of 5 mAh 
cm-2, QC is a fixed capacity of 1mAh cm–2 in specific cycles (n = 10), and QS is the 
capacity retention during final stripping. The cathodes consist of S@pPAN composite 
with sulfur content of 48 wt.%, Super P, and crosslinked carboxylic styrene butadiene 
rubber-guar gum binder[2] in the mass ratio of 9 : 0.5 : 0.5 and the S@pPAN loading 
was 1.8 mg cm-2. The pouch cell was prepared using higher-loading cathode (5.65 mg 
cm-2 SPAN) and thin lithium foils (50 μm). The cycling performance was tested in a 
voltage range of 1.0-3.0 V (vs. Li/Li+) using a LANDCT 2001A Cell Test System 
(Wuhan, China). A CHI760E Electrochemical Workstation (Shanghai, China) was 
adopted to test the impedance (100 kHz to 0.1 Hz with an amplitude of 5 mV) and 
exchange current density. The lithium morphology after cycling was observed using 
SEM (FEI Nova Nano-scanning electron microscope). The XPS was conducted using 
a Kratos Axis UltraDLD spectrometer (Kratos Analytical, a Shimadzu Group 
Company). The cathode interphase was analyzed using TEM (JEM-2100, JEOL Ltd.). 
 
Theoretical calculations 
For DFT calculations, the ESP was calculated using the Lee-Yang-Parr gradient-
corrected correlation functional (B3LYP) method and the 6-311G (d,p) basis set of the 
Gaussian package.
