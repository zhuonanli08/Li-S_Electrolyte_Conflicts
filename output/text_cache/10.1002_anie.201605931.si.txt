S1. Experimental Section  
Preparation of electrolytes. Lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF6), dimethyl 
carbonate (DMC), ethylene carbonate (EC), and fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) (all 
in battery-grade purity) were obtained from Capchem. Co. Ltd. and used as-received. 
LiODFB was synthesized by the reaction of boron trifluoride etherate and Li2C2O4 in 
a 1:1 molar ratio and further purified through extraction and recrystallization using 
ethyl acetate/methylbenzene mixed solvents for three times, and dried under vacuum 
overnight at 70 °C. The electrolytes of 1 M LiPF6 in EC-DMC (1:1, by volume) and 1 
M LiODFB in EC-DMC-FEC (4.5:4.5:1, by volume) were obtained by blending 
adequate amounts of salts and carbonate solvents in a PP flask, and further stirring for 
12 h at room temperature. The experimental work was carried out and the materials 
were stored in an Ar-filled dry glove box (MB-10 compact, MBRAUN) containing 
less than 1 ppm water and O2. 
Electrochemical measurements. Cyclic voltammetric studies of the electrolyte 
solutions were conducted in a three-electrode configuration inside the glove box using 
a CHI604A electrochemical workstation. The working electrode was a Pt disc 
electrode (area: 0.0314 cm2) and Li metal was used as both the counter and the 
reference electrodes. The Pt disc electrode was polished with Al2O3 powder and rinsed 
with alcohol before use. Electrochemical cycling tests were carried out using 
CR2016-type coin cells of a two-electrode configuration with Celgard 2400 separator. 
Cu|Li cells were assembled in the glove box with Li foil and Cu foil as electrodes. 
During this experiment, a constant deposition current density of 0.28 mA cm−2 was 
passed through the cell for 2.5 h, and then the same dissolution current density was 
applied until the cut-off voltage of 1.2 V vs. Li/Li+. Li|Li symmetric cells were 
assembled with Li metal used as the working and counter electrodes. The A.C. 
impedance spectroscopy of Li|Li symmetric cells and Li-S cells with Celgard separator 
and different electrolyte solutions were measured using AUTOLAB PGSTAT302N 
(Metrohm) in the frequency range from 1 MHz to 0.01 Hz. Conductivity 
measurements were carried out using a FE30 conductivity meter and an InLab 710 

<<PAGE 3>>
conductivity measuring cell (Mettler Toledo, Switzerland) by inserting the InLab710 
conductivity electrode in solution after calibration. 2016-type coin cells (Li-S batteries) 
were assembled in the glove box using lithium foil anode and S@pPAN composite 
cathode. 1 M LiPF6 in EC-DMC (1:1, by volume) and 1 M LiODFB in EC-DMC-FEC 
(4.5:4.5:1, by volume) solutions were used as electrolytes. The thickness of the 
pressed coating layers of cathodes is about 13 μm (ca. 1.7 mg composite) and the 
electrolyte volume is ca. 75 μL. The v/v ratio of electrolyte to the coating layer is ca. 
51. Specific capacity values were calculated based on the mass of sulfur or S@pPAN 
composite and the charge/discharge cycles were carried out with a cut-off voltage limit 
of 1.0~3.0 V on a LAND-CT 2001A Battery Test System (Wuhan, China). 
Characterization. The morphologies of lithium metal electrodes were observed by 
FEI Nova Nano-scanning electron microscope (SEM). Firstly, cells were 
disassembled in the glove box and then the obtained electrodes were immersed in 
EC-DMC mixed solvents and then washed by anhydrous DMC to remove any 
electrolyte salt residuals. To avoid exposure to air, the dried samples were sealed in an 
air-isolating container and transferred quickly into the SEM equipment under the 
protection of Ar flow. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was 
performed using a Kratos Axis UltraDLD spectrometer (Kratos Analytical-A 
Shimadzu Group Company) with monochromatic Al Ka source (1486.6 eV). The 
samples of electrode after cycling were pretreated with the same as the SEM 
measurement. Before transferring the samples to the equipment, an air-isolating 
container with protective Ar flow was used to avoid moisture/air exposure. Under slot 
mode, the analysis area was 700 × 300 μm and analysis chamber pressure was less 
than 5 × 10−9 Torr. The binding energy was calibrated according to the C 1s peak 
(284.8 eV) of adventitious carbon on the analyzed sample surface. 
Computation details. Geometry optimizations of the radicals were carried out with 
the all-electron density functional program DMol3 in Materials Studio 5.5 (Accelrys) 
using the Becke-Lee-Yang-Parr (BLYP) functional and the double numerical plus 
polarization (DNP) basis set. The optimization was considered converged when the

following convergence criteria were met: 1.0 ×10−5 Ha for the total energy, 0.002 
Ha/Å for the maximum force on atoms, and 0.005 Å for the maximum atomic 
displacement.

Figure S2. Partial Li cycling test with 0.28 mA cm−2 in LiODFB/EC-DMC-FEC 
electrolyte for average cycling efficiency calculation. 
Methods: a Cu|Li cell with LiODFB/EC-DMC-FEC electrolyte was assembled to 
measure the average Coulombic efficiency (ACE). An excess of lithium was 
galvanostatically plated on the surface of the copper foil followed by galvanostatic 
cycling of a fraction of the original excess. The number of cycles required to consume 
the original excess (indicated by a sharp change in dissolution potential) was used to 
calculate the ACE of the electrolyte. 
ACE = 100 × NQps/(NQps + Qex) 
where N is the number of cycles, Qex is the plated excess (1 C cm−2), and Qps is the 
cycled fraction (0.25 C cm−2). All cycling efficiency values were determined at room 
temperature. In this case, N = 353, ACE = 98.88%.

Figure S8. The first charge/discharge profiles at 1 C for Li-S batteries using 
LiODFB/EC-DMC-FEC and LiPF6/EC-DMC electrolytes. 
All the initial discharge capacities exceeded the theoretical value, which might 
mainly derive from lithium insertion into the anionic conjugated backbone in pPAN, 
as is known to occur in electrically conducting polymers as described previously.[4] 
For LiODFB/EC-DMC-FEC electrolyte, FEC would be oxidized and decomposed to 
form a protection layer on the cathode surface during the first discharge as reported in 
the literature,[5] which increases the irreversible capacity. Thus, the initial efficiency is 
slightly lower than that in LiPF6/EC-DMC electrolyte.

Figure S9. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra of Li anode (top) and 
S@pPAN composite cathode (bottom) in Li-S cells with LiODFB/EC-DMC-FEC 
electrolyte after 50 cycles at 1 C. 
F 1s spectrum in Figure S9 reveals that LiF exists not only on the surface of Li 
anode, but also on the surface of the cathode. Meanwhile, other products generated on 
the surfaces of the anode and cathode, such as B-F species and minor high-valent 
sulfur containing species (ca. 168 eV). The similar XPS response near 168 eV on 
sulfur based cathode has been observed by another group.[6] We conjecture that these 
new sulfur species may arise from the reactions between active sulfur products and 
organic ODFB anion during cycling test. These organic sulfur species may be 
partially soluble from the cathode and then re-deposit on Li anode. Therefore, a 
similar S 2p peak at 168 eV is also observable on Li electrode surface. All these 
detectable components, including LiF, B-F species and minor high-valent sulfur 
species, may be favorable for the formation of a compact and thin SEI layer on both 
the electrodes. On the other hand, undetectable Li2S and Li2S2 on the Li anode 
confirms that the final discharge products of Li-S@pPAN cathode are insoluble in 
carbonate electrolytes[7] and the shuttle reaction does not take place in this cell 
system.
