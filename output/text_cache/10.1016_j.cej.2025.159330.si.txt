Experimental section
Electrochemical measurements: All the cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box below 1.0 ppm. CR-2025 coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box using the HCG/S-coated aluminum foil as the cathode, lithium foil as anode, and Celgard 2400 as the separator. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were measured in the CHI660E electrochemical work station. CV was carried out at a scan rate of 0.1-0.5 mV s-1 within a voltage window of 1.7-2.8 V. The frequency range of the EIS test is between 100 kHz and 0.01 Hz with 5 mV amplitude. The frequency cut-off of in-situ EIS measurements 100 kHz-0.01 Hz with 5 mV amplitude. The value of each discharge depth is shown in Figure S18, each point corresponds to the specific stages that were conducted with EIS measurement in different electrolytes.
The Li2S deposition experiment was measured with assembled cells using carbon paper as the cathode and lithium foil as the anode. In the cell assembly process, 20 μL electrolyte containing 0.2 M Li2S6 was added on the carbon paper side and another 20 μL electrolyte CE/STE was dropped on the Li foil side. The cell was first 3 discharged at constant current to the 2.06 V and then discharged with the constant voltage at 2.05 V until the current was below 10−5 A for the nucleation and deposition of Li2S.

Materials Characterizations: The morphologies of Li depositions and Li2S depositions were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7001F). The SEI components of Li-S cells were further characterized through X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, AXIS, SUPRA+). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded in the range of 2θ = 5-50° on a desktop X-ray diffractometer (RIGAKU-Miniflex II) with Cu Kα radiation (λ= 1.5406 Å). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was measured at a heating rate of 10 °C min-1 using a TAQ600 thermo-gravimetric analyzer. The Raman measurements of the electrolytes were conducted on a Renishaw inVia device between 200 and 2000 cm-1. The electrolytes of DME/MPE (1 : 1, by vol.), 0.2 M LiTFSI in DME and 0.2 M LiTFSI in DME/MPE (1 : 1, by vol.) were configured for testing. NMR analyses were conducted on Varian VNMRS 500 MHz spectrometers. 0.2 M Li2S8 was added to the configured CE and STE electrolytes for testing. The viscosity of the electrolytes was measured on an TA DHR2 with a controlled shear rate and shear stress at 25 ℃.

Ex-situ XRD measurements: HCG/S electrodes were firstly activated at 0.2 C for three cycles. The cycled electrodes were extracted from coin cells in an Ar-filled glove box and then dried in Ar-filled glove box after washing by DOL/DME mixture. The cycled electrodes at different charge/discharge state were sealed before the XRD measurement.

Classic MD simulation: Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using the GROMACS MD simulation package version. The initial configurations were obtained by randomly packing molecules in a periodic cubic box of size 70×70×70 Å3 using PACKMOL. The initial configuration was first minimized using the steepest descent method, employing a convergence criterion of 1000 kcal/(mol Å), and then using the conjugate-gradient energy minimization scheme, employing a convergence criterion of 10 kcal/(mol Å). The systems were equilibrated in an isothermal-isobaric ensemble (constant NPT) using a Berendsen barostat to maintain the pressure of 1 bar with a time constant of 2 ps for 2 ns. All systems were then melted at 400 K for 2 ns and subsequently annealed from 400 to 298 K in three steps for 3 ns. Finally, production runs of 20 ns were then obtained in the canonical ensemble (NPT) using an improved velocity-rescaling algorithm proposed by Bussi et al. with a time constant of 0.2 ps at 298 K. The viscosity simulation was performed using another box of 42×42×126 Å3 according to the perturbation method of the reported works.

AIMD simulation: For the CE simulation, single monomer Li2S6 molecules were placed at the center of 16×16×16 Å3 boxes with a 1 : 1 mixture of 10 DME and 10 DOL molecules around occupying the remaining volume of the box. Likewise, a 3 : 3 : 4 mixture of DME, DOL, MPE was placed around Li2S6 in the other box to simulate the STE environment. Each of the three Li2S6 monomer systems was solvated with their respective DOL/DME mixtures using the PACKMOL program. The CP2K software was used to perform optimizations, NVT simulations, and NPT simulations in the same fashion (i.e., same types of basis sets, pseudopotentials, NVT/NPT thermostat, NPT barostat, NVT/NPT temperature, and NPT pressure). The SHAKE algorithm was used for hydrogen-bearing bonds (1.1 Å for C-H bonds) to extend the time step of the NVT and NPT simulations to 1 fs. The simulation time for all of the NVT and NPT DOL/DME-solvated Li2S6 system simulations was 5 ps.
Following the NPT simulations, NVT metadynamics simulations were performed on each of the DOL/DME-solvated Li2S6 monomer systems using the final NPT trajectory frame structures and cell lattice parameters. Each of the metadynamics simulations used two collective variables to scan (1) the monomer sulfur coordination number and (2) the DOL/DME solvent oxygen coordination number of one of the two lithium atoms in each of the Li2S6 monomer systems. A harmonic wall potential constraint of 1000 kcal/mol was placed on the Li-S distance of the other Li atom (with r0 = 2.7 Å) to allow for the scanning of the free energy of a single Li migration to and from the sulfur chain monomer. Small repulsive Gaussian hills with a height of 2 × 10-3 Hartree and a width of 0.2 Hartree were added at a frequency of every 50 time steps for both the Li-S and Li-O coordination number collective variables.

Quantum chemical calculation: Quantum chemical calculations were performed using the ORCA 5.0.1 program package. For the frontier molecular orbital energy, electrostatic energy, the overall reaction energy and the binding energy, B97-3c was first used to optimize the molecular structure for a stable configuration and then r2SCAN-3c was performed to calculate the single-point energy and output molecular orbital state, electrostatic energy state.

The parameters of COMSOL Multiphysics: The modeling of Li+ electrodeposition was performed by using COMSOL Multiphysics 5.4 based on the finite element method. Nernst-Planck-Possion equation was adopted to describe the behaviors of charged ions. The concentration-dependent Bulter-Volmer equation was employed to introduce the reaction of electrode surface. Deformed geometry interfaces were applied to the anode and cathode interface and the moving speed of meshes was equal to the deposited rate. The initial concentration of Li+ was set to 1.0 mol L-1. The exchange current density was set to be 30 A m-2. The time of deposition was 2000 s.
