Experimental Details
Cell Assembly. First, the Bucky paper was cut into 12 mm diameter discs and placed 
in a vacuum oven at 100 °C for 24 h to completely remove water. Then, a certain amount 
of sulfur was dissolved in CS2. 20 μL of the solution was dropped onto the Bucky paper 
and placed in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h to remove the solvent. The mass of sulfur 
on each  obtained was about 1.0 to 1.2 mg. 20 μL electrolyte with or without 
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 was dropped on a Li-metal anode, then a Celgard 2400 membrane 
separator was placed on the Li-metal anode, followed by another 20 uL of the 
electrolyte with or without Co(bpy)3(PF6)2. All the aforementioned operations were 
conducted in a glove box filled with an Argon atmosphere. Carbon fiber paper was used 
to test the working potential of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2. Electrochemical measurements were 
conducted after resting for 2 h.
Symmetric Cell Measurements. The symmetrical cells were all assembled in a glove 
box using carbon paper as the cathode and anode with a 0.5 mol L-1 of Li2S6 solution as 
the electrolyte. The Li2S6 solution was prepared by dissolving Li2S and S in a molar 
ratio of 1:5 in a commercially available electrolyte (1M LiTFSI in DME/DOL(1:1 in 
volume) with 1% LiNO3 as an additive). Cyclic voltammetry was tested with a voltage 
range from 1 V to -1 V at the scan rate of 50 mV s−1.
Li2S Nucleation Measurement. 0.5 mol L−1 Li2S8 solution was prepared by stirring 
Li2S and S with a molar ratio of 1:7 in tetraglyme through vigorous mixing at 60 ℃ for 
24 h, with 1 M LiTFSI and 1% LiNO3 additives. Using lithium as the anode, carbon

Li/Li Symmetric cells. 20 μL electrolyte with or without Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 was dropped 
on the lithium metal electrode. Subsequently, a Celgard 2400 separator was placed on 
the top of lithium anode, followed by adding 20 μL electrolyte with or without 
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 on the top of the separator. Finally, a lithium metal electrode was placed 
on the separator. All the aforementioned operations were completed in a glove box 
filled with an Argon atmosphere. Electrochemical measurements were conducted after 
resting for 2 h.
H-Cell Test. 15 mL DME/DOL(1:1 in volume) with 1 M LiTFSI and 1% LiNO3 was 
used for the anode, and 2 wt.% Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 was added to the cathode electrolyte. A 
Celgard 2400 membrane separator was placed between the compartments.
Theoretical Calculations. We have employed the first-principles23, 24 to perform 
density functional theory (DFT) calculations within the generalized gradient 
approximation (GGA) using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE)25 formulation. We 
have chosen the projected augmented wave (PAW) potentials26, 27 to describe the ionic 
cores and take valence electrons into account using a plane wave basis set with a kinetic 
energy cutoff of 520 eV. The GGA+U method was adopted in our calculations. The

Kohn−Sham orbitals were allowed using the Gaussian smearing method with a width 
of 0.05 eV. The electronic energy was considered self-consistent when the energy 
change was smaller than 10−5 eV. A geometry optimization was considered convergent 
when the energy change was smaller than 0.05 eV Å−1. The Brillouin zone integration 
is performed using 2×2×1 Monkhorst-Pack k-point sampling for a structure. Finally, 
the adsorption energies(Eads) were calculated as 
                   
Eads = Ead/sub - Ead – Esub

where Ead/sub, Ead, and Esub are the total energies of the optimized 
adsorbate/substrate system, the adsorbate in the structure, and the clean substrate, 
respectively. The free energy was calculated using the equation:
                       
G = Eads + ZPE – TS                       
(S2)
where G, Eads, ZPE and TS are the free energy, total energy from DFT calculations, 
zero point energy and entropic contributions, respectively, where T is set to 300K.
