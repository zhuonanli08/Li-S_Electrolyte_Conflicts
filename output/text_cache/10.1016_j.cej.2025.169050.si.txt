Experimental Section
Synthesis of S8 microspheres: 5 g Na2S2O3 and polyvinyl pyrrolidone K30 (500 mg) are dissolved in 500 mL of deionized water with continuous magnetic stirring. Later, 4 mL HCl is dropwise added. After stirring for 2 h, the resulting powder is collected via filtration and washes three times with deionized water. Finally, the powder is  dried under vacuum at 60 oC for 12 h.
Synthesis of S8@PAN: S8 microspheres (500 mg) and PAN (1 g, molecular weight = 150,000) are ultrasonically dispersed in 10 mL of DMF (Ra = 10.2) for 2 h, followed by magnetic stirring at 60 oC for 12 h. The homogeneous mixture is then transferred into a high-pressure spray gun and sprayed into stirred deionized water, resulting in the formation of S8@PAN particles through a phase-inversion process. Subsequently, these particles are washed five times with deionized water, filtered, and freeze-dried to obtain dispersed powders.
Synthesis of S8@S7Se/PAN: S7Se powder and S8@PAN particles are thoroughly mixed and ground at a mass ratio of 4:1 for 30 min. The mixture is then placed in a tube furnace under nitrogen atmosphere and annealed at 350 oC for 210 min with a heating rate of 5 oC min-1. After naturally cooling to ambient temperature, the S8@S7Se/PAN composite material is obtained.
Li-Sulfur Battery Assembly and Testing: The SPAN cathodes with sulfur mass loadings below 4.0 mg cm-2 are fabricated by mixing active materials with carbon black (Lion, Japan) and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF, Aladdin) in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) at a mass ratio of 8:1:1 to form a homogeneous slurry, which is subsequently cast onto aluminum foil. For cathodes with sulfur mass loadings exceeding 4.0 mg cm-2, the active materials were blended with carbon black, poly(acrylic acid) (PAA, Sigma-Aldrich, Mw = 450,000) and guar gum (GG, Aladdin) in deionized water at a mass ratio of 8: 1: 0.5: 0.5 to prepare a uniform aqueous slurry, which is also coated onto aluminum foil. All cathodes are vacuum-dried at 60 oC overnight and then punched into 12 mm diameter discs for coin cell assembly. The CR2032 Coin cells are assembled using the prepared cathodes and lithium metal anodes, with a polypropylene membrane (Celgard 2400) serving as the separator. The assembly is performed in an argon-filled glovebox (O2 < 0.5 ppm, H2O < 0.5 ppm). The electrolyte (LB-007, DoDoChem) consists of 1.0 M LiPF6 dissolved in a 1:1 volume ratio mixture of ethylene carbonate (EC) and dimethyl carbonate (DMC). Galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD) measurements were carried out using a LAND battery testing system at 25 oC and 60 oC. For pre-potentiostatic treatments, SPAN||Li cells are initially discharged to preset voltages (1.75 V, 2.0 V, or 1.4 V vs. Li+/Li) and hold for 4 h to induce electrolyte decomposition. For galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) analysis, cells are cycled at 0.1 C with 30 min current pulses followed by 2 h rest intervals under open-circuit conditions. Other electrochemical characterizations are performed using an EC-Lab electrochemical workstation. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) is conducted in the potential range of 1.0-3.0 V at scan rates ranging from 0.1 to 0.5 mV s-1 vs. Li+/Li.Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is measured in the frequency range of 105-10-2 Hz with a 5 mV amplitude. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) is employed to assess the oxidative/reductive stability of the electrolyte using Li//stainless steel (SS) or SPAN//Li cells.
Preparation of Li2S7Se and Li2S8 solutions: A 0.1 M Li2S7Se solution was prepared by dissolving selenium and Li2S precursors in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at a molar ratio of 1:2.3. Similarly, a 0.1 M Li2S8 solution is obtained by dissolving sulfur and Li2S in DMSO at a molar ratio of 1:8. All solutions are stirred continuously at 80 oC for 72 hours to ensure complete dissolution and equilibrium.
Materials Characterization: The sample morphology is observed by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM; JEOL JSM-7800F) with high-precision energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM; JEM 2010F). The crystalline phase is characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD; Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer, Cu Kα radiation). The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS; Thermo Scientific K-Alpha) and Raman Spectrometer (Horiba Scientific) are used to detect the surface compositions and chemical bond information. The involved sulfur content is confirmed by the thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and element analyzer (EA; Elementar Vario EL).
Theoretical computations: The Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) is employed for Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, utilizing the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) framework to describe electron exchange and correlation effects. In the amorphous unit cell constructed via the Materials Studio module, the projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotential is applied to model electron-ion interactions within a slab geometry measuring 17.01 × 17.01 × 37.728 Å. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 520 eV is adopted to ensure computational accuracy. The energy convergence is rigorously controlled with a force threshold of 0.02 eV/Å, while the electronic self-consistency criterion is set to 1×10-6 eV. A Monkhorst-Pack (MP) k-mesh sampling scheme centered at the Γ-point is employed for Brillouin zone integration. The optimal k-grid dimensions of 3 × 3 × 1 for the Li2S8 (001) and Li2S7Se (111) surface orientations are determined by monitoring energy convergence with respect to k-point density.
The Li2S8 (001) and Li2S7Se (111) surface slab models consist of two atomic layers, with the bottom layer fixed to mimic bulk-like behavior. A vacuum/solvent region of 15 Å thickness is introduced along the z-direction to minimize spurious interactions. For ion and solvent decomposition simulations, the implicit solvation model - encoded in VASPsol - is implemented via the joint DFT (JDFT) method, which fills vacant regions with a dielectric continuum. The solvent permittivity (ε) is set to 56.0, and the ionic concentration is fixed at 1 M, while maintaining default solvation model parameters. In charged systems, electron count variations in JDFT are compensated by the implicit solvation framework through the numerical solution of the linearized Poisson-Boltzmann equation.

A 1 M LiPF6 EC:DMC electrolyte is selected due to the reduction ability of LiPF6 at SPAN cathode surface, forming LiF within the relevant voltage window.[1, 2] To demarcate the redox potential of the electrolyte, linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) is conducted using Li//stainless steel (SS) half-cells. As shown in Figure S6(a), the current exhibited a pronounced decline from approximately 1.9 V to 1.75 V, where a peak current is observed.[3] Accordingly, the Li//SS half-cells are cathodically swept to 1.75 V and maintained at this potential for 4 hours until a steady state is reached (current approaching zero) in Figure S6(b). This reductive pre-potentiostatic treatment is denoted as PS. During this voltage holding process, the rapid decline in current is attributed to the formation of a passive interfacial layer resulting from electrolyte reduction. In contrast, a redox scan without voltage hold is performed across a 1.0-3.0 V range, during which limited reductive transformation of fluorine species occurred; this procedure is denoted as Base. A control process without any electrochemical pre-treatment is designated as Blank. The anodic stability of the cells subjected to these treatments is evaluated via LSV. In the Blank group, anodic current increased significantly beyond 2.7 V, with additional peaks observed at 3.0 V and 4.2 V, corresponding to fluorine oxidation and deposition processes. The Base group exhibit notable fluorine deposition features, whereas the current response of the PS group is minimally affected, indicating the formation of sufficient oxidation products passivated on the surface. This also corresponded to the enhanced voltage stability up to 4.2 V, consistent with the characteristics of a fluorine-rich interfacial coating as Figure S6(c).

Pre-potentiostatic method is adapted to S8@S7Se/PAN|Li coin cells to facilitate robust LiF-rich CEI growth on cathode derived from electrolyte. These cells comprise an S8@S7Se/PAN cathode with an areal capacity of 1.7 mAh cm-2, paired with an excess lithium anode (~500 μm) and 120 μL electrolyte. During the initial discharge at a current density of 0.05 C, trace LiPS is generated and reacted with the limited quantity of carbonate solvent at 2.25 V through a nucleophilic reaction in Figure S7(a). Organic products are deposited to form prototype CEI, incurring a minimal irreversible specific capacity loss.[4] The nascent CEI mitigates the deleterious interfacial reaction between sulfur species and the carbonate solvent. Subsequently, electrolyte reduction occurred at 1.75 V on the interface of the S8@S7Se/PAN cathode (also denoted as PS) contributing a insignificant capacity of 79.1 mAh g-1 during the pre-potentiostatic discharge.[5, 6] To achieve complete lithiation and activate long-term reaction kinetics, a final galvanostatic discharge is conducted down to 1.0 V. The influence of pre-potentiostatic voltage on CEI formation is investigated by holding cells at 2.0 V (PS-2 V), 1.75 V (PS-1.75 V), or 1.4 V (PS-1.4 V). Figure S7(b) illustrates the time dependent capacity curves over a 4 hours potentiostatic period. The capacity responsed to PS-2 V are low reactivity, indicative of negligible electrolyte decomposition. The lower constant potential of 1.4 V (PS-1.4 V), the accumulated capacity corresponding reached 537.9 mAh g-1, primarily reflecting lithium-ion insertion behavior. Ultimately, CEI formed through pre-potentiostatic at 1.75 V inhibites LiPS dissolution and associated parasitic reactions.[7, 8] As a result, the S8@S7Se/PAN cathodes follow a solid-solid conversion pathway, evidenced by the emergence of a single discharge plateau during the second discharging process. 
Interfacial evolution are monitored via electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), as illustrated in Figure S7(c-e). At both open-circuit voltage and 1.75 V, the AC impedance spectra exhibite a single semicircle, indicating a relatively simple interfacial structure. However, upon further discharge to 1.0 V, two distinct semicircles corresponding to the emergence of an additional interfacial layer appeares. This dual semicircle feature persistes until the completion of the first cycle, signifying the establishment of a stable CEI.[9] During the pre-potentiostatic (PS) treatment, both the ohmic resistance (Rb) and interfacial resistance (RCEI) exhibited moderate increases, with Rb rising from 5.26 Ω to 11.59 Ω. This is ascribed to transient charge accumulation at the cathode surface, induced by ongoing LiPF6 decomposition, which also contributes to an increase in RCEI from 49.6 Ω to 68.3 Ω.[10, 11] Continued electrolyte decomposition alters the CEI composition, temporarily hindering efficient electron and ion transport across the interface.
