Experimental Section
Cathode fabrication: 
S@KB compositions were prepared via a melting-diffusion method. The sublimed sulfur powder (99.5% purity, Aladdin) and Ketjenblack (ECP-600JD, Lion Specialty Chemicals) were first mixed at a mass ratio of 7 : 3. Then, the composition was transferred into a sealed glass bottle and heated to 155 °C for 12 h at 5 °C min−1 at argon atmosphere and the S@KB composition with a S content of 70 wt% was collected after the powder was cooled to room temperature. With the same method, the S@KB powder with a content of 80 wt% of S was prepared. The S cathodes composed of 80 wt% S@KB powders (70 wt% S), 12 wt% Super P carbon (DodoChem), and 8 wt% aqueous polyacrylic latex binder (LA133, Chengdu Indigo Power Sources Co., Ltd., Chengdu, China) were blended into an automatic mixer (AR-100, THINKY). The slurry obtained was blade-cast onto a carbon-coated aluminum foil and dried at 60 °C for 12 h. S cathodes with an active material loading of 0.8 to 1.0 mg cm−2 were obtained. The high loading S cathodes (＞ 3 mg cm−1) are composed of 90 wt% S@KB powders (80 wt% S) and 10 wt% LA133.
Electrolyte preparation:
The blank electrolyte was prepared by solving 1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethane sulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, DodoChem) and 2 wt% LiNO3 (Adamas reagent, ltd.) in mixed dioxolane (DOL, DodoChem) and dimethoxyethane (DME, DodoChem) with a volume ratio of 1 : 1. The electrolyte with additive was prepared by adding 0.1 M 2-Bromoacetamide (BA, Adamas reagent, ltd.) in blank electrolytes. Different ether-based electrolytes used for phase graph were formulated as follows: a 7 : 1 molar ratio of S and Li2S with E/S ratios of 20 or 10 μL mg−1) in electrolyte without and with BA additive, respectively. The above procedure was done in an argon-filled glove box. 
Electrochemical tests: 
The electrochemical test of Li-S batteries was carried out in 2025-type coin cells at 25 °C. 40 μL electrolyte was added for each cell. The coin cells were assembled with the S@KB cathodes and lithium metal anodes (15.6 mm in diameter, 450 µm in thickness, China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd., Tianjin China) separated by polypropylene (PP) separators (Celgard 2500) in an Argon-filled glove box. The Li||Li2S8-CNT cells were assembled with the CNT paper, 40 uL 0.5 M Li2S8 with and without BA additive and lithium metal anodes separated by PP separators in an argon-filled glove box. Galvanostatic measurements were conducted in the voltage range of 1.8 to 2.8 V vs. Li/Li+ at a current density of 0.2 C on a Neware Battery test station (CT-4008T) and all the specific capacity values were calculated based on the weight of S in the cathodes. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) analyses were carried out in the frequency range of 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz with an amplitude of 10 mV at the open-circuit voltage. The in-situ EIS test was performed on the cells discharged at 0.1 C for every 30 minutes. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1. CV and EIS were performed on an electrochemical station (VMP3, BioLogic). For the analysis of the dissolution of the intermediates, the assembled cells were firstly discharged to 2.12 V at 112 mA, and subsequently potentiostatically discharged at 2.05 V for the transformation of active materials into soluble intermediates. The potentiostatic discharge was stopped when the discharge current was below 10−5 A. The Li plating/stripping curves of the symmetrical cells in blank electrolytes with Li2S and BA electrolytes with fresh Li electrodes at a current density of 1 mA cm−2 and an areal capacity of 1 mAh cm−2. 
Assembly of pouch cell: 
The S cathodes are composed of 80 wt% S@KB powders (80 wt% S), 12 wt% Super P carbon , and 8 wt% LA133 were blended into an automatic mixer. The well-mixed slurry was single-side coated on Al foil and dried at 60°C for 12 h. Then, 4×3 cm-2 electrodes with a S loading density of around 3.2 mg cm-2. The Li anode sheets were also cut with the same size as sulfur cathodes. The thickness of the Li anode was 100 μm on Cu current collectors. A Celgard 2500 membrane was used as the separator. The composition of the electrolyte was the same as the coin cells, and the E/S ratio is around 4 µL mg-1. The cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.8–2.8 V.
Titration of the actual content of sulfur in LPSs: 
The Li2Sx solutions were stirred for a week to ensures fully proceed of the redox reaction, and then clear liquid is taken after settling. H2O2 (China National Pharmaceutical Group, ltd) and LiOH (Adamas reagent, ltd) are added into the clear liquid to oxidize Sx2- into SO42-, and the content of the SO42- is titrated by adding BaCl2 (Adamas reagent, ltd) to form BaSO4 precipitate. By filtering, washing, and drying the precipitate, and the actual stoichiometric ratio of sulfur in LPSs was calculated based on the mass of the BaSO4 precipitate.
Characterizations:
The conductivity of the electrolytes was measured with a conductivity tester (FE38, METTLER TOLEDO) and a matched platinum electrode (Inlab 710, METTLER TOLEDO) at 20 °C. The 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) spectrum (Bruker, Avance III 600 MHz) of BA and BA with Li2S was performed by solving BA and BA in CDCl3, respectively. The 7Li nuclear magnetic resonance (7Li NMR) spectrum (Bruker, Avance NEO 600) of Li2S and BA with Li2S was performed by solving Li2S and BA with Li2S in CDCl3, respectively. The Raman spectra (HORIBA, LabRAM HR Evolution) of blank and BA electrolytes were performed by sealing the solutions in capillary tubes in the range of 100 to 4000 cm−1. The UV quartz cuvette cells were assembled in an argon filled glovebox and tested in a Lambda 35 UV-vis spectrometer with the transparent window facing to the UV light in the wavelength range of 200 to 800 nm. The morphologies of fresh and cycled lithium electrodes were characterized by using a field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM, JEOL, JSM-7610FPLUS). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi) of the cycled lithium electrodes was carried out using Al Kα (1,486.7 eV) source. X-ray diffraction (XRD) on a multipurpose X-ray diffractometer (SmartLab SE, Rigaku) with a scanning speed of 5º min−1 in the range of 10 to 50º.

The dot lines represent the phase boundary, which divide the dash line into three parts. The red part in the two phase (solid sulfur and Li2Sx solution) is corresponding to the upper plateau of the discharge process, during which the sulfur is reduced as polysulfides. The blue part in the single phase is corresponding to the slope of the discharge process, during which long-chain polysulfide is reduced as short-chain polysulfides. The orange part in the two phase (solid Li2S and Li2Sx solution) is corresponding to the lower plateau of the discharge process, during which polysulfide is reduced as Li2S.
