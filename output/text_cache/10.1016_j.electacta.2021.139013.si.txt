2. Experimental
S@C was prepared using a melt-diffusion method. Briefly, sulfur was mixed with Ketjenblack EC-600JD first and then heated at 160 °C for 16 h. The sulfur content was determined to be 90 wt% based on mass analysis before and after heating. The morphology of the material was analyzed by a JOEL JSM-6340F microscope.
The working electrode contained S@C and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in a weight ratio of 9 : 2.  A lithium disc was used as a counter electrode. A Celgard 2325 membrane was used as a separator. A porous carbon membrane (TORAY, TGP-H-060, 190 µm thick, porosity: 78%) was used for comparison. The electrolyte was 1 M LiTFSI/1 M LiNO3 in 1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME). The cell components were assembled into a 2032-type coin cell in an Argon-filled glove box. The cells were galvanostatically cycled in a voltage window of 1.5 V to 2.8 V at a current density of 0.5 A g-1. The current density and the capacity were calculated based on the mass of sulfur in the electrodes. All measurements were performed using Neware battery testers (Neware Electronic Co., China) at room temperature.
Parameters used for energy density calculations: the specific capacity of sulfur is 1000 mAh g-1; the sulfur electrode contains sulfur (70 wt%, density: 2.07 g cm-3), conductive carbon (20 wt%, density: 2 g cm-3), and polyvinylidene fluoride (10 wt%, density: 1.7 g cm-3); the cathode current collector is aluminum (double-side coated, thickness: 15 µm, density: 2.7 g cm-3); the anode is pure lithium (3861 mAh g-1, 100% excess, density: 0.534 g cm-3) without an additional current collector; the density of electrolyte is 1 g mL-1; the separator is Celgard2325 (thickness: 25 µm, porosity 39%, areal density: 1.4 mg cm-2, the pores are filled with electrolyte); the pouch cell casing is not considered; and the voltage is 2.1 V. “Sulfur loading” refers to single-side sulfur loading density. 
Based on the parameters above, the equations used for specific energy (Wh kg-1) and volumetric energy density (Wh L-1) calculations are 2.1m×Q/(3.425+1.42857m+0.000518m×Q+m×n) and 0.0021m*Q/(0.002275+0.00071m+0.00000097m*Q+0.001m*n), respectively, where Q (mAh g-1) is the specific capacity of sulfur, m (mg) the single-side sulfur loading density, and n (µL mg-1) the E/S ratio. 
Both m and n are >=1 to make sure that there is sufficient electrolyte to completely fill the pores of the separator. A capacity of 1000 mAh g-1 for sulfur is used for the calculation because it may be difficult to reach and maintain such a specific capacity for long cycles at a high sulfur loading and low E/S ratio,[1, 2] which are required for high-energy LSBs.
