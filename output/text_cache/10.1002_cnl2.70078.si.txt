Experimental section
Material
The chemicals obtained from Aladdin Reagent Company in all experiments included: 6-fluoro-1H-indole-2,3-dione (95%), sulfur powder (>99%), N-methylpyrrolidone (≥ 99.5%, chromatographic grade), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, 99.5%, containing ~75 ppm BHT stabilizer), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, 99.5% (GC), AR), and lithium sulfide (99.98%). On the other hand, the chemicals acquired from Duoduo Reagent Co., Ltd. comprised polyvinylidene fluoride (AR), aluminum foil, PP separator (Celgard 2400), and lithium sulfur battery electrolyte (1 mol L−1 lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) in DOL and DME with 1 wt% LiNO3) . Additionally, the experiment used lithium sheets from Tianjin Zhongneng Lithium Industry Co., Ltd., and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (CNT, >95%) sourced from Harbin Wanxin Graphite Valley Technology Co., Ltd.

Material characterization: The morphology of the electrode after cycling was observed using a scanning electron microscope (NanoSEM2000, ThermoFisher Scientific, USA). The occurrence of clusters in the electrolyte was studied using a UV visible spectrophotometer (UV-1800, Shimadzu Corporation), scanning within the range of 1100-200 nm. Furthermore, the surface information of the electrode after cycling was analyzed through the X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (PHI (China) Limited).


Preparation of positive electrode: Sulfur powder and carbon nanotubes were combined in a mass ratio of 8:2 and homogenized through grinding. The resulting mixture was then subjected to vacuum oven treatment at 155 ℃ for a duration 8 hours. Following cooling to ambient temperature, the material underwent thorough grinding and sieving for subsequent utilization. The sulfur content within the sulfur-carbon composite material was sustained at approximately 80%.The homogeneous slurry comprising screened carbon sulfur composite material (CNT-S), CNT conductive additive, and polyvinylidene fluoride adhesive in a 7:2:1 mass ratio was prepared using high-purity N-methylpyrrolidone as the solvent. The slurry underwent continuous stirring at an appropriate rate for over 8 hours to achieve homogeneity. Subsequently, the clean aluminum foil was uniformly coated using a coating machine, followed by treatment in a vacuum oven at 55 ℃ for 8 hours. Upon removal from the oven and subsequent cooling, the coated aluminum foil was stamped into hairless circular pole plates with a 14 mm diameter. It is imperative to note that the sulfur loading for a single positive electrode disc must exceed 1.2 mg cm−2.


Preparation of electrolyte: Quantities of 0.1, 0.5, and 1 wt% of 6-fluoro-1H-indole-2,3-dione electrolyte additives were precisely weighed within a glove box environment using an electronic balance. The glove box environment was maintained at a water oxygen content of <0.1 ppm. Subsequently, the accurately measured electrolyte additives were introduced into a mixed ether solution (comprising 1:1 (v/v) of 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 1 wt% of LiNO3) of DOL/DME) under argon atmosphere conditions. The resulting electrolyte was then sealed and stirred until achieving clarity, after which it was stored in an aluminum bottle for future utilization.


Battery assembly: The battery assembly process was conducted within a glove box environment with a water oxygen content below 0.1 ppm and under argon atmosphere conditions, employing the CR2025 battery casing model. Specifically, positive electrode plates, separators, and lithium plates were meticulously aligned and stacked in a sandwich configuration. The interlayers between the positive and negative electrodes, as well as separators, were impregnated with the prepared electrolyte or a control without group electrolyte. Following assembly, a button-type battery press was utilized to compress the components. Subsequently, the assembled battery was allowed to stand for approximately 8 hours to attain thermodynamic equilibrium. The sulfur loading of the battery was approximately 1.2 mg cm−2, with an E/S ratio control of approximately 20 μL mg−1. Additionally, Li-Li and Li-Cu symmetric cells were assembled using identical procedures for other electrochemical assessments.


Electrochemical tests: Electrochemical testing procedures were carried out using electrochemical workstation (CHI 760E), encompassing cyclic voltammetry (CV) within a voltage window spanning 1.60-2.80 V (vs. Li/Li+), and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) conducted at open circuit voltage, employing an AC amplitude of 5 mV across a frequency spectrum ranging from 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz. Voltage thresholds of 2.80 V and 1.60 V were set on the NEWARE Battery Test System (Shenzhen,China) for subsequent constant current charging and discharging evaluations. Low-temperature experimentation was conducted within a controlled environment maintained at −20 ℃ using a low-temperature refrigerator. Prior to commencing the test regimen, batteries underwent a preconditioning period of no less than 7 hours within the low-temperature refrigerator to attain thermal equilibrium, ensuring optimal conditions for subsequent charge and discharge assessments.


Li2S deposition experiment: In this experimental setup, the positive electrode comprised a 14 mm carbon cloth circular plate fabricated from uniformly dispersed conductive material (CNTs). The active material within the positive electrode consisted of a self-prepared 0.5 mol L−1 of Li2S8 solution in a solvent mixture of DOL and DME in a ratio of 1:1 (v/v). The negative electrode was constituted by lithium plate. The electrolyte used in the lithium-sulfur battery configuration consisted of the 1 mol L−1 LiTFSI dissolved in DOL and DME solution with 0.1 wt% LiNO3. This electrolyte formulation was prepared in-house and procured from Duoduo Reagent Network. Following the assembly of the battery using the aforementioned components, an 8-hour stabilization period was allowed. Subsequently, a discharge process was initiated at a constant current of 0.112 mA until reaching 2.11 V, facilitating the complete discharge of Li2S8 to its short-chain counterpart Li2S4. The discharge continued at a fixed voltage of 2.10 V until the current tapered to 0.01 mA or below.


Li-Li or Li-Cu symmetrical battery measurements: Li-Li or Li-Cu batteries were subjected to testing under conditions characterized by a capacity surface density of 1 mAh cm−2 and varying current densities of 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 mA cm−2. The Coulombic efficiency of metallic lithium deposition and dissolution was assessed utilizing Li-Cu batteries, with the deposition charge of metallic lithium being modulated through precise control of the deposition duration during each individual deposition process.


Galvanostatic Intermittent Titration Technique (GITT) measurements: The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT), introduced by the esteemed German scientist W. Weppner, represents a transient measurement method utilized not only for the determination of ion diffusion coefficients but also for the acquisition of microscopic kinetic insights pertaining to electrode reactions. The specific procedural sequence entails the application of a continuous current pulse of 0.05 C (1 C =1675 mAh g−1) for 30 minutes within the voltage span of 1.60-2.80 V. Subsequently, a 2-hour relaxation period is observed to attain quasi open circuit voltage conditions, and this cycle is repeated thrice. Shuttle current measurements: Following assembly and equilibration, the battery underwent two charge-discharge cycles at a rate of 0.2 C, followed by a third charging session up to 2.70 V, after which it was allowed to rest under open circuit conditions for 10 minutes. Subsequently, an i-t test was performed at the workstation, maintaining an open circuit voltage for a minimum duration of 1 × 104 seconds. During this period, the current stabilized to a steady state, which was documented as the shuttle current.


In-situ electrochemical colorimetric observation: For in-situ observation experiments, an H-shaped electrolytic cell was meticulously assembled. The positive and negative electrodes were secured using threaded nails, while a diaphragm was carefully positioned between the two individual H cells to effectively impede the migration of polysulfides. Throughout the testing procedure, potential control was administered using the CHI760E electrochemical workstation. The discharge protocol encompassed a voltage range of 2.80-1.60 V, with a scanning rate set at 0.5 mV s−1. Digital photographs were captured at regular intervals, with a control interval of 30 minutes. The sealed electrolyte after testing is used for UV spectroscopy testing to check the solvation of clusters.
