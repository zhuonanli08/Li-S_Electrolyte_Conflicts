Synthesis of Co-NC sample:
To prepare the Co-NC precursor, 0.03 mmol of cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O) and 1 g of peptone were dissolved in 2 mL of deionized water. The resulting solution was frozen at −20 °C for 12 h to form a solid ice block. Subsequently, it was subjected to freeze-drying for another 12 h. The dried sample was then thoroughly mixed with 10 g of NaCl using ball milling at 550 rpm for 3 h. The obtained composite was pyrolyzed in a tubular furnace under a nitrogen atmosphere by heating to 900 °C at a ramp rate of 5 °C min⁻¹ and maintaining that temperature for 1 h. After natural cooling to room temperature, the carbonized material was washed to remove residual salts and dried for further use.
Preparation of S@Co-NC Composite:
The sulfur–carbon composite (S@Co-NC) was synthesized by mechanically mixing the Co-NC with sublimed sulfur in a mass ratio of 2:3 for 30 min. The mixture was then transferred into a glovebox filled with argon and heated at 155 °C for 12 h to facilitate sulfur infiltration, yielding the final S@Co-NC composite.
Preparation of Electrolyte Additives:
A baseline electrolyte was formulated by dissolving 1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and 0.1 M lithium nitrate (LiNO₃) in a DOL/DME mixture (1:1 by volume). Adiponitrile (ADN) was then introduced into thiselectrolyte at varying concentrations of 40, 60, and 80 mM to prepare the modified electrolytes with additive.

Physical Characterization:
Morphological analysis was carried out using a Carl Zeiss Ultra Plus scanning electron microscope (SEM, Germany), with images captured from multiple regions to ensure representative structural features. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted on a VG Escalab 210 instrument (UK) to investigate the surface chemical composition of the sulfur cathode after electrochemical cycling.

Electrochemical Performance Testing:
The sulfur cathode was prepared by mixing S@Co-NC, conductive carbon black (Super P), and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder in a mass ratio of 7:2:1. The mixture was dispersed in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) and stirred uniformly before being coated onto aluminum foil current collectors. The electrodes were then dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h. Lithium metal was used as the counter/reference electrode, and a Celgard 2400 membrane served as the separator. The baseline electrolyte consisted of 1.0 M LiTFSI dissolved in a 1:1 (v/v) mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME), with 1.0 wt% LiNO3. For the experimental cells, varying concentrations of ADN were added to the baseline electrolyte. All CR2032-type coin cells were assembled in an argon-filled glovebox. The lithium anode and separator have a diameter of 15.6 mm, while the sulfur cathode has a diameter of 12 mm. Unless otherwise stated, the sulfur loading of the cathode was approximately 1.0–1.2 mg cm-2, and the electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio was maintained at around 40 μL mg-1. For Li||Li symmetric cells, the cathode was replaced by a lithium foil.
Galvanostatic charge–discharge measurements were conducted using a Neware battery test system within a voltage window of 1.7–2.8 V. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were performed on a CHI 650D electrochemical workstation. EIS measurements were carried out over a frequency range of 100 kHz to 10 mHz. All electrochemical tests were conducted at room temperature. Theoretical Calculations:
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Gaussian 09 software package, employing the B3LYP functional in conjunction with the 6-31G(d) basis set. Empirical dispersion corrections were applied using Grimme’s D3 method with Becke–Johnson damping (keyword: empiricaldispersion=gd3bj). All molecular geometries were fully optimized and verified to be true minima by confirming the absence of imaginary vibrational frequencies. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) were visualized using the Multiwfn program in combination with Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD).
