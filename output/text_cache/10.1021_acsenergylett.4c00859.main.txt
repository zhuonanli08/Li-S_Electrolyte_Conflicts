A Coordinated-Anion-Enriched Electrolyte for
Lean-Electrolyte Li−S Batteries

ABSTRACT: The solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) on Li and cathode
electrolyte interphase (CEI) at sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN)
formed in the commonly used liquid electrolytes cannot accommodate
the large volume change of both Li anode and SPAN cathode, resulting
in severe electrolyte and Li consumption and fast capacity decay under
high mass loading and lean electrolyte conditions. Herein, a LiF-rich
SEI and a LiF−LixNyOz coenriched CEI are simultaneously formed by
adding ionic liquid (Py13TFSI) in the localized high-concentration
electrolyte (LHCE), which forms a coordinated-anion-enriched
solvation structure. The LiF-rich interphase results in less stress/strain
during large volume changes of Li and SPAN, therefore achieving
obviously improved stability even at a high areal capacity.
Consequently, Li (50 μm)||SPAN (6 mg cm−2) pouch cells under the
lean electrolyte conditions (E/S ratio of 5 mL gSPAN
−1) deliver a stable cycle life of 120 cycles with 79.2% capacity retention,
demonstrating great promise for high-energy-density lithium−sulfur batteries.
L
ithium−sulfur batteries (LSBs) are promising energy
storage devices for electric transportations and smart
devices due to the high theoretical energy density (2567
Wh kg−1), low cost, abundance, and high sustainability of
sulfur resources.1−3 However, commercialization of LSBs has
been restricted by several severe technological challenges from
both Li metal anodes and S cathodes, such as Li dendrite
growth, infinite volume change of the Li metal anode during
cycling, continuous consumption of the electrolyte, and the
shuttle effect of soluble lithium polysulfides (LiPSs).4−7
So far, a LiF-rich solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) with high
interface energy and weak bonding to Li has been identified as
a key enabler for achieving high Coulombic efficiency (CE)
and superior cyclic stability of Li metal anode.8−11 Increasing
the number of coordinated anions in the electrolyte is
beneficial for uniform construction of the anion-derived LiF-
rich SEI layer.12−18 The preparation of localized high-
concentration electrolytes (LHCEs) is an effective approach
to in situ construction of the LiF-rich SEI on Li metal.19−26
Meanwhile, short-chain sulfur (S2−3) cathodes are promising
substitutes for elemental sulfur due to their high cyclic stability
attributed to the unique solid-state reaction mechanism, which
can be operated under lean electrolyte conditions.27−29 Among
various S2−3 cathodes, sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN), in
which the short-chain sulfur is covalently bonded with a
conjugated pyridine skeleton, has been widely investigated as a
cathode material for LSBs due to its exceptional electronic
conductivity and structural stability.30−33 Though LiPS
dissolution behavior in SPAN can be effectively mitigated,
the organic−inorganic cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI)
with strong bonding to SPAN will break during the large
volume change of lithiated SPAN, especially at a high S loading
in SPAN, resulting in continuous consumption of the
electrolyte for the formation of fresh CEI on the SPAN
cathode.5,34,35 Thus, constructing a LiF-rich CEI on the high-
capacity SPAN cathode is highly desired.
Herein, we designed a coordinated-anion-enriched LHCE by
adding ionic liquid as an additive, which can simultaneously
construct a LiF-rich SEI on the Li metal anode and a LiF−
LixNyOz coenriched CEI on the SPAN cathode, enabling Li||
SPAN full cells to achieve a long cycle life under high mass
loading and lean electrolyte conditions. The coordinated-

(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) in diethyl ether (DEE) with
ethyl 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl ether (ETFE) diluent, and 1-
methyl-1-propylpyrrolidinium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)-
imide (Py13TFSI) additive (denoted as LDEFE-IL). The
addition of the Py13TFSI increases the portion of contact ion
pairs (CIPs) in the electrolyte solvation structure by forming
Li+ and TFSI−interactions. Both TFSI−and FSI−interact with
Li+ in the LDEFE-IL, forming a coordinated-anion-enriched
solvation structure and facilitating the reduction of anions to
enhance LiF content in the interphases. The LiF-rich SEI layer
formed by the LDEFE-IL electrolyte endows the Li metal
anode with high CE of 99.4% up to 900 cycles. Meanwhile, the
LiF−LixNyOz enriched CEI effectively accommodates the
volume change of the SPAN cathode and minimizes the
shuttle reaction, enabling Li (50 μm)||SPAN (6 mgSPAN cm−2)
pouch cells to deliver capacity retention of 79.2% after 120
cycles under lean electrolyte conditions (E/S ratio of 5 mL
gSPAN

The electrolyte solvation structures of LDEFE-IL, 1.5 M
LiFSI in DEE/ETFE (1:3 by volume, denoted LDEFE) and
1.5 M LiFSI in DEE (denoted LDEE) were studied by Raman
spectroscopy. As shown in Figure 1a, Raman peaks of the DEE
solvent at 840 cm−1 and FSI−anions at 774 cm−1 shifted to
longer wavenumbers after coordinating with Li+ cations.9,16,32
Among these three electrolytes, LDEE contains three solvation
structures, which are free FSI−, contact ion pair (CIP), and
aggregate (AGG) structures with the ratio of 7.57%, 32.71%,
and 59.72% (Figure 1b,c, Figures S2a and S3).15,36−40 The free
FSI−disappeared in the LDEFE after adding ETFE diluent,
and the portions of CIP and AGG increase to 14.97% and
85.03%, respectively. After further adding the ionic liquid in
LDEFE electrolyte (LDEFE-IL), the portion of the CIP
structure increases to 40.25%, while the portion of AGG
decreases to 59.75%, indicating the change of the solvation
structures after introducing the ionic liquid additive
(Py13TFSI).41−43
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed to
understand the impact of the ionic liquid on the electrolyte
solvation structure. Figure 1d−f show the radial distribution
functions (RDFs) of these electrolytes. In LDEE, the
coordinated shell of Li+ cations is dominated by O in the
DEE and FSI−(Figure S4a, Table S1). In LDEFE, the inner
solvation shell of Li+ cations is comprised of DEE and FSI−,
while the ETFE stays outside as the second solvation shell
(Figure 1e, Figure S4b, Table S1).44,45 After adding the
Py13TFSI, the smaller Li+ cations more readily coordinate with

Figure 1g−i shows the snapshots of each electrolyte system.
All three structures are presented and randomly distributed
across the whole system in the LDEE electrolyte. In contrast,
only CIPs and AGGs are presented and locally distributed in
the LDEFE electrolyte since Li+ cations do not coordinate with
the ETFE diluent. In the LDEFE-IL electrolyte, the addition of
Py13TFSI enlarges the distribution of the coordinated-anion-
dominated solvation structures (CIP and AGG) to a larger
region across the whole system, which can effectively facilitate
the construction of a uniform and robust anion-derived SEI
layer on the Li metal anode.

that in LDEFE (99.2%) and LDEE (98.5%) (Figure 2a). In

The chemical compositions of SEI layers constructed on the
surface of Li metal anodes in these electrolytes were
investigated by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) after
repeated Li plating/striping for 10 cycles with 5 mAh cm−2
(Figure 2d−f). The observation of an intense F 1s XPS peak at
684.6 eV (LiF) demonstrates the formation of LiF-rich SEI
layers in all three electrolytes, which is consistent with the
elemental analysis results (Tables S4−S6).9,14 The highest
ratio of LiF to organic fluorides in LDEFE-IL indicates the
lowest organic components in the LiF-rich SEI in the LDEFE-
IL electrolyte. Furthermore, the weakest intensity of the C 1s
XPS peak at 289.5 eV (Li2CO3) and the O 1s XPS peak at 531
eV (Li2O) among these three electrolytes demonstrates that
the LiF dominated SEI layer was constructed on the Li metal
anode by LDEFE-IL.47−51 The highest interface energy of LiF
(73.28 meV/Å2) ensures the highest CE and longest cycle life
of Li anode in the LDEFE-IL electrolyte.
The morphology of the Li anodes after 20 cycles at 0.5 mA
cm−2 with 5 mAh cm−2 was investigated using scanning
electron microscopy (SEM). In LDEE, the deposited Li
displays a small and loose granular chunk structure with F
content of 21.32 atom % (Figure 2g and Table S4).
Meanwhile, “dead Li” can be observed on the surface of the
Li metal. After addition of ETFE, the size of deposited Li
dramatically increases without any dendritic Li formation
owing to the enhanced F content (28.68 at%) in the SEI layer
(Figure 2h, Table S5). Moreover, flat Li metal with the densest
structure and highest F content (34.06 at%) was observed in
the LDEFE-IL, demonstrating the further improvement of the
stability and mechanical strength of the constructed SEI layer
in the optimized electrolyte (Figure 2i, Figure S7 and Table
S6).52

dispersive X-ray (EDX) analysis, and SEM (Figures S8−S11,

The components of the CEI layer constructed in these three
electrolytes were analyzed using XPS. The intense F 1s XPS
peak at 684.6 eV in LDEFE-IL demonstrates the construction
of a LiF-rich CEI layer on the SPAN cathode (Figure 3a). In
contrast, the low intensity of the F 1s XPS peak for LiF
indicates the LiF-deficient CEI formed in the LDEE and
LDEFE electrolytes. In addition, the C 1s XPS spectra indicate
that a small amount of Li2CO3 is observed in the CEI layer
formed in the LDEFE-IL electrolyte (Figure S21). The S 2p
XPS spectrum of the CEI formed in the LDEE electrolyte
shows an obvious peak of Li2S2 at 162.6 eV, further confirming
that the CEI was cracked (Figure 3b).18,26 As expected, the
absence of the S 2p XPS peak for Li2S2 demonstrates the stable
CEI formed in LDEFE and LDEFE-IL electrolytes. In addition,
a strong N−SOx peak signal at 399.5 eV generated by the
decomposition of FSI−can be identified in the N 1s XPS
spectra in LDEE and LDEFE electrolytes, implying the
formation of an organic CEI layer on the SPAN cathode
(Figure 3c).19,53 In contrast, the reduced intensity of the N-
SOx peak and the intense peak of LixNyOz in the N 1s XPS
spectrum in the LDEFE-IL electrolyte proves that inorganic
compounds dominated the CEI layer on the SPAN cathode.
The high ionic conductivity of LixNyOz species endows the
SPAN cathode with excellent rate capability.54 The above
results clearly prove that the LDEFE-IL electrolyte with a
coordinated-anion-enriched solvation structure constructs a
robust LiF and LixNyOz coenriched CEI layer on the SPAN
cathode, which weakly bonds to SPAN and suffer from less
strain/stress during the volume change of SPAN and thus is
more robust to protect the SPAN cathode.
To understand the exceptional performance of SPAN in the
LDEFE-IL electrolyte, CEI components in the LDEFE-IL
electrolyte were further investigated using X-ray absorption
spectroscopy (XAS). The total electron yield (TEY) model of
the F K-edge XAS spectra of the SPAN cathode at different
charge/discharge stages is shown in Figure 3d. Obviously, LiF-
related peaks are observed in the cycled SPAN cathodes at
different charge/discharge stages, and they do not show
apparent differences at different charge/discharge stages,
further confirming the construction of the LiF-rich CEI layer
on the SPAN cathode. Meanwhile, the peaks related to
carbonyl CO bonds and semicarbonate in the C K-edge
XAS spectra intensified and emerged during cycling,
corresponding to CO3
2−formation (Figure 3e). As shown in
Figure 3f, the peak assigned to pyridine N in the bulk SPAN
disappears after cycling, further demonstrating the high
strength and integrity of the constructed CEI on the SPAN
cathode. Moreover, the peaks assigned to LixNyOz are observed
after cycling and do not show obvious differences at different
charge/discharge stages, confirming the formation of LixNyOz
in the CEI layer on the SPAN cathode.
To further exploit the performance of the LDEFE-IL
electrolyte, the electrochemical behaviors of Li-SPAN batteries
with high-mass-loading SPAN cathodes (12 mg cm−2) were
investigated using coin cells under lean electrolyte conditions
(E/S is 5 mL gSPAN
−1, 10.5 mL gS
−1). As shown in Figure 4a,
the high-mass-loading SPAN cathode delivers an initial charge
capacity of 7.18 mAh cm−2 in the LDEFE-IL electrolytes under
the current density of 0.5 mA cm−2 and retains good cyclic
stability with capacity retention of 80.4% after 110 cycles due
to the robust LiF-enriched dual passivation layers on both Li
metal anode and SPAN cathode. This is much better than the
LDEFE-based full cell, where the capacity decreases to 2.17
mAh cm−2 after 100 cycles. In sharp contrast, the LDEE-based
full cell suddenly died after 20 cycles due to the short circuit
caused by low CE and rapid Li dendrite growth (Figure S22).

for practical applications, a single-layer Li-SPAN pouch cell
was assembled and tested under harsh conditions in the LDEE,

In summary, we developed a LHCE with an ionic liquid
additive to generate a coordinated-anion-enriched solvation
structure for the in situ formation of a robust LiF-rich SEI layer
on the Li metal anode and a LiF/LixNyOz coenriched CEI layer
on the SPAN cathode. The formed LiF-rich interphases with
weak bonding to the Li anode and SPAN cathode effectively
suppress Li dendrite growth and stabilize the SPAN cathode.
The electrolyte endows the Li metal anode with high average
CE of 99.4% for 900 cycles. In addition, 50 μm thin Li||high-
mass-loading SPAN (6 mg cm−2 based on SPAN) pouch cells
under lean electrolyte conditions (E/S ratio of 5 mL gSPAN
−1)
were fabricated and exhibited a stable cycle life of 120 cycles
with 79.2% capacity retention. This work offers a reliable
approach to electrolyte design for the commercialization of
low-cost, long-lifetime, and high-energy LSBs.
