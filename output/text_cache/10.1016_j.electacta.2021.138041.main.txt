Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries with high theoretical speciﬁc capacity and ascendancy of raw materials are 
considered as a potential candidate for next-generation energy storage system. However, some intractable 
challenges, such as low sulfur utilization caused by the loss of active materials and surface passivation 
resulted from the deposition of insulating products, still hinder the practical application of lithium-sulfur 
batteries. Herein, various types of thioamides with different molecular structures are investigated as elec- 
trolyte additives to enhance the electrochemical performance of Li-S cells by adjusting the solubility of 
the ﬁnal product, Li 2 S. It is demonstrated that the thioamides that contain hydrogen in primary-amine 
or secondary-amine groups are prone to generate recyclable active materials. Speciﬁcally, thioacetamide 
(TAA) as an electrolyte additive not only provides extra reversible capacity, but also boosts the solubil- 
ity of Li 2 S by intermolecular hydrogen bonds, alleviating the passivation of the electrode and enhancing 
kinetics for the conversion of polysulﬁde to Li 2 S. Therefore, the cells with TAA additive exhibit superior 
cycle performance and rate performance.

1. Introduction 
As energy crisis makes people have to develop renewable en- 
ergy such as solar energy and wind energy, high-energy-density 
rechargeable batteries as high-eﬃciency energy storage devices 
therefore attract much attention from academic communities 
[1–3] . Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) get extensively utilization in 
portable electric devices, e-bike and other energy storage equip- 
ment [ 4 , 5 ]. However, due to the limited theoretical capacity of 
cathode and anode, further enhancing the practical energy den- 
sity of LIBs is extremely diﬃcult. It’s more achievable to de- 
velop new cathode and anode materials with high speciﬁc energy 
[6] . Lithium-sulfur batteries (LSBs) coupling sulfur cathode with 
lithium anode possess high theoretical energy density (~2600 W h 
kg −1 ) and high theoretical speciﬁc capacity (1675 mA h g −1 ) [ 7 , 8 ]. 
The potential practical energy density of LSBs can reach 600 W 
h kg −1 , which is several multiples of advanced lithium ion bat- 
teries [ 9 , 10 ]. In addition, LSBs employ abundant, environmentally 
friendly and low-toxicity sulfur cathode, making them one of the 
most promising secondary battery systems [11–13] .

Many challenging issues still hamper the practical application 
of lithium-sulfur batteries. One is that, long-chain polysulﬁdes dis- 
solved in liquid electrolyte not only increase the viscosity, but 
also diffuse to the lithium anode and react with metallic lithium 
to form short-chain polysulﬁdes, resulting in so-called shuttle ef- 
fect. It brings about self-discharge, the loss of active materials, low 
coulombic eﬃciency and lithium corrosion [14–17] . On the other 
hand, the insoluble and insulated discharge products (Li 2 S 2 /Li 2 S) 
deposit on the surface of cathode, leading to the passivation of 
electrode surface. Huge volume effect during charging-discharging 
processes can easily cause the collapse of cathode structure [18–
20] . Furthermore, lithium dendrite resulting from uneven deposi- 
tion of lithium ions is likely to penetrate the separator, causing 
short circuit and thermal runaway [21–23] . 
Recently, considerable tactics from different aspects have been 
proposed to resolve these issues mentioned above. One of the most 
effective ways is accommodating sulfur into nanostructured host 
materials, such as porous carbon spheres [24] , carbon nanoﬁbers 
[25] , carbon nanotubes [26] and metal organic framework [27] , 
which not only increase the conductivity of the electrode but also 
constrain polysulﬁdes into the nanopores, making them hard to re- 
act with lithium anode and thus inhibiting notorious shuttle effect. 
The migration of polysulﬁdes can be further inhibited by doping or 
co-doping nitride [ 28 , 29 ], oxides [ 30 , 31 ] and sulﬁdes [32] into the

porous carbon matrix, through physical and chemical interaction

From the electrolyte aspect, tailoring the solubility of polysul- 
ﬁdes is one kind of effective method to restrain polysulﬁdes shut- 
tling, which involves concentrated salt [33] , sparingly solvating 
electrolytes [34] and ionic liquid-based co-solvents [35] . Numerous 
electrolyte additives have also been proposed to improve the elec- 
trochemical performance of LSBs. For instance, John B. Goodenough 
used bis(4- nitrophenyl) carbonate to capture polysulﬁdes, forming 
sulfur-containing intermediate to block migration of polysulﬁdes 
[36] . Cobaltocene is applied as an electrolyte additive, which acts 
as a redox mediator to promote the conversion of sulfur species 
from long-chain polysulﬁde to short-chain polysulﬁde, thus im- 
proving the utilization of active substances [37] . Glutamate is also 
investigated as an electrolyte additive to provide anions to re- 
pel negatively charged polysulﬁdes away from lithium anodes by 
charge effect [38] .Unfortunately, there is limited work on address- 
ing the passivation of electrodes. The deposition of non-conductive 
sulfur-containing species on the surface of cathode slow down the 
reaction kinetics, causing that some sulfur-containing species can- 
not be further converted to Li 2 S. 
To address this issue, herein, thioamides are proposed as elec- 
trolyte additives to enhance the solubility of Li 2 S, minimizing the 
passivation of cathode and improving the electrochemical perfor- 
mance, inspired by Yang et al. [39] , who introduced ε-caprolactam 
and acetamide as eutectic solvent to obtain high solubility of Li 2 S 
in electrolyte. Due to the contained sulfur atom is in the molec- 
ular of thioamide, by choosing a proper molecular structure of 
thioamide, it can provide extra capacity for the lithium sulfur cells, 
which is beneﬁcial to enhance the energy density. It is found that 
sulfur atoms in thioamides cannot be activated in the case that 
the hydrogen in the amino-group of thioamide functional group is 
completely substituted. Compared with other thioamides, thioac- 
etamide (TAA) not only enhances the dissolution of Li 2 S to acceler- 
ate the electrochemical kinetics, but also act as an active material 
to provide extra capacity for lithium sulfur batteries. With TAA as 
a functional electrolyte additive, the cell delivers a discharge ca- 
pacity of 738.9 mA h g −1 after 200 cycles at the current density of 
0.5 C (1 C = 1675 mA g −1 ). 
2. Experimental section 
2.1. Preparation of sulfur-carbon composite 
Porous carbon materials was synthesized as depicted in our 
previous work [40] . Sulfur-carbon composite materials was pre- 
pared by thoroughly mixing sublimated sulfur and porous carbon 
in a ratio of 65:35 with CS 2 as dispersant. After fully evaporation 
of CS 2 , the mixture was then heated to 160 °C and held for 2 h in 
a muﬄe furnace. The sulfur cathode was prepared by mixing the 
sulfur-carbon composite, acetylene black and polyvinylidene ﬂuo- 
ride (PVDF) in N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP) with homogenizer 
to acquire homogeneous slurry at the ratio of 8:1:1, then casting 
on aluminum current collector and drying at 60 °C for 12 h in a 
vacuum oven. The electrode is punched into disks with a diameter 
of 13 mm and the sulfur loading is ~1.2 mg cm −2 .

2.3. Electrochemical measurements 
Lithium-sulfur batteries (CR2025) with sole carbon cathode 
were assembled with metallic lithium sheet as anode and 
polypropylene separator where 40 μL of thioamide-based elec- 
trolytes was injected in Ar-ﬁlled glove box. The cells were ex- 
amined in a range of potential 1.7~2.8 V (vs. Li/Li + ) at the cur- 
rent density of 0.5 C by a LAND-CT2001A test system (Wuhan, 
China) at room temperature. The batteries with thioacetamide- 
based electrolyte were installed with similar components except 
for the amount of electrolyte (E/S = 20) and conducted in sim- 
ilar condition at 0.5C or 1C. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was exe- 
cuted at a scanning speed of 0.1 mV s −1 and the voltage range of 
1.5~3 V. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was applied 
at open-circuit potential over the frequency range of 10 5 ~0.01 Hz 
with the amplitude of 5 mV. CV and EIS abovementioned were 
measured by an electrochemical workstation (Solartron 1470E) at 
room temperature. 
2.4. The dissolution of Li 2 S 
To verify the enhancement of the solubility of lithium sulﬁde, 
visual experiment was collected in which equal amount of lithium 
sulﬁde was added to blank electrolyte and thioacetamide-based 
electrolyte and the mixture was agitated at 50 °C for 48 h until 
no precipitation existed or solution was invariable, and stood still 
at room temperature. 
2.5. Characterization 
Nuclear magnetic resonance spectra (NMR, JEOL 400 M) were 
obtained after dissolving pure TAA and the mixture of TAA and 
lithium sulﬁde in tritiated solvent. Viscometer (Lovis 20 0 0 M) was 
applied to detect the viscosity of electrolyte with different mass 
fraction of TAA. Ultraviolet-visible light absorption spectrum (UV- 
Vis, U-4100) was used to identify species of TAA electrolyte after 
dissolving lithium sulﬁde. The phase composition of the cathode 
surface was recorded by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, 
Thermo Kalpha). The surface morphology of the cycled sulfur cath- 
ode was acquired by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JEOL JSM- 
7900F) and mappings of sulfur element were collected by energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). 
2.6. Computational parameters 
All quantum chemical calculations were carried out with den- 
sity functional theory (DFT) method. The B3LYP functional and 
triple-zeta 6–311G(3df,3dp) with Grimme’s DFT-D3 dispersion cor- 
rection [41] were adopted for all the geometry optimizations. The 
HOMO/LUMO orbitals and atomic charge were analyzed by Multi- 
wfn [42] combined with VMD program.

The nitrogen-containing functional groups linked with C = S dou- 
ble bonds, whose hydrogen atoms are not substituted by alkyl, are 
denoted as primary-amine group, such as those in TAA and TBA. 
The nitrogen-containing functional groups with hydrogen atoms 
replaced by alkyl are named as tertiary-amine group, such as those 
in DMTA and DTFA. Thiourea species contain two primary-amine, 
secondary-amine or tertiary-amine groups, such as TU, DMTU and 
TMTU. 
To investigate the electrochemical activity of those thioamides, 
coin cells with sulfur-free cathode coupled with lithium anode are 
cycled in thioamide-based electrolyte. The charge-discharge volt- 
age proﬁles are presented in Fig 1 a–c. From Fig. 1 a it can be seen 
that the voltage proﬁle of the cell with TAA containing electrolyte 
is different from that of the conventional lithium-sulfur battery 
during the ﬁrst discharge process, where a relatively bound dis- 
charge platform appears at around 1.9 V instead of 2.3 V and 
2.1 V, indicating that the conversion pathway is distinguished 
from elemental sulfur. In the subsequent cycles, the discharging- 
charging voltage proﬁles are in consistence with those of conven- 
tional lithium sulfur cells. It can be speculated that active materi- 
als that continue to undergo redox reactions in the electrochem- 
ical window of lithium-sulfur batteries are formed after the ﬁrst 
discharging-charging process. The voltage proﬁles of the cell with 
TBA containing electrolyte, as presented in Fig. S1a, are basically 
the same with those of the cell with TAA containing electrolyte. 
The sulfur of thioamide species with primary-amine group pos- 
sesses electrochemical activities, tending to participate in electro- 
chemical reactions in lithium-sulfur batteries. Thioamide additives 
with tertiary-amine groups, such as DMTA and DTFA show nearly 
no reversible electrochemical activity as demonstrated by the volt- 
age proﬁles displayed in Fig. 1 b and S1b, especially after several 
cycles. For the blank cell, the voltage proﬁles ( Fig. 1 d) exhibit the 
property of an electric double layer capacitor. The voltage proﬁles 
of the cells with DMTU ( Fig. 1 c), where one hydrogen exists in 
secondary-amine group, and TU (Fig. S1c) containing electrolyte 
are unanimous in those of the cell with TAA containing electrolyte, 
while those of the cell with TMTU containing electrolyte (Fig. S1d) 
are identical to those of the cell with DTFA containing electrolyte, 
nearly without reversible electrochemical activity. To investigate 
electrochemical performance of thioamide species as active ma- 
terials, cells with sulfur-free cathode are cycled at the same cur- 
rent density. Fig. 1 e pictures the cycle performance of the batteries 
with thioamide electrolyte. As seen, TAA, TBA, TU and DMTU with 
primary-amine group or secondary-amine group show relatively 
high capacity compared to TMTU, DMTA and DTFA with tertiary- 
amine group. Among TAA, TBA, TU and DMTU, TAA and TU pro- 
vide highest capacity, which is beneﬁcial to enhancing the overall 
energy density of lithium sulfur batteries. The coulombic eﬃciency 
( Fig. 1 f) of TMTU, DMTA and DTFA is over 100% and higher than 
other thioamides, which means that greater irreversible reaction 
occurs in those cells. The coulombic eﬃciency of the TAA at ﬁrst 
cycle is 112% and maintains above 99% after several cycles. To fur- 
ther investigate the electrochemical behaviors of TAA, CV curves 
are obtained as seen in Fig. S2. During the ﬁrst scanning cycle, only 
one cathodic peak centered at 1.8 V is observed, which is in con- 
sistent with the discharge-charge curves of the cell with TAA con- 
taining electrolyte. In the following cathodic process, two reduced 
peaks appear at 2.05 and 2.3 V, where reduction reaction of ac- 
tive material take place. The anodic process is more complicated. 
The anodic peaks keep moving towards lower potential, indicat- 
ing the decrease of polarization. These results suggest that, hydro- 
gen atoms in primary-amine group and secondary-amine group are 
crucial for the reversible electrochemical activity of thioamides. 
Since TAA can provide the highest extra capacity, herein, the 
electrochemical behaviors and performance of the Li-S batteries 
with TAA containing electrolyte is ﬁrstly investigated. It is found 
that, 2 wt.% TAA in electrolyte can provide stable cycle perfor- 
mance, moderate enhancement on capacity and moderate viscosity 
as demonstrated in Fig. S3. Therefore, in this report, the dosage of 
TAA is ﬁxed at 2 wt.% in ether electrolyte, unless mentioned. As 
shown in Fig. S4, the CV curves of the cell with blank electrolyte 
show two characteristic cathodic peaks located at 2.0 and 2.3 V 
in cathodic process, corresponding to the conversion of elemental 
sulfur to long-chain polysulﬁde S n 2 −(4 ≤n ≤8) and the reduction 
of long-chain polysulﬁde to Li 2 S 2 and Li 2 S, which are generally in- 
soluble in conventional electrolyte respectively [ 43 , 44 ]. Only one 
characteristic anodic peak related to the oxidation of lithium sul- 
ﬁde to long-chain polysulﬁde and elemental sulfur appears at the 
range of 2.43~2.47 V [45] . However, the Li-S cell with TAA (2 wt.%) 
containing electrolyte shows an additional reduction peak at 1.7~
1.85 V in CV curves during the ﬁrst cathodic scan and it disap- 
pears in the following scanning ( Fig. 2 a). This result is in accor- 
dance with that of cell with sulfur-free cathode. The comparison 
on CV curves of the batteries with blank electrolyte and TAA con- 
taining electrolyte is presented as Fig. 2 b shown. It is clear that the 
cathodic peak moves to higher potential and the oxidation poten- 
tial decreases with TAA containing electrolyte. The potential gap of 
the batteries with TAA containing electrolyte is 420 mV less than 
that (470 mV) with blank group, indicating less polarization in the 
cell with TAA additive [46] . In addition, the intensity of the peaks 
is enhanced distinctly and the slope of Tafel plots (Fig. S5) is re- 
duced when TAA is utilized in electrolyte. The above results sug- 
gest that the precipitated discharge products accumulated on the 
electrode can be dissolved by TAA to alleviate electrode passiva- 
tion and improve the reaction kinetics [ 37 , 47 ].

There is no doubt that some enhancement on the electrochem- 
ical performance is contributed from the redox of TAA at the ﬁrst 
cycle, forming active materials to provide extra capacity. To under- 
stand the reason why thioamides containing hydrogen in primary- 
amine group or secondary-amine group can produce active mate- 
rials, the charge of sulfur of thioamides was received by density 
functional theory calculation as presented in Fig. 3 a. It is obvious 
that the charge of sulfur in TAA , TBA , TU and DMTU is more neg- 
ative than that of other thioacetamides, which means that elec- 
trons concentrate more on the sulfur atom, and sulfur tends to 
be electrochemically active during discharge process. For a spe- 
ciﬁc thioamide, from Fig. 3 b, LUMO orbitals are distributed at sul- 
fur, carbon and nitrogen atoms in the TAA molecule. The LUMO 
orbital of sulfur occupies the highest energy, indicating that sul- 
fur is prone to obtain electrons to undergo reduction reaction and 
become electrochemically active to provide extra capacity com-

explore the discharge and charge products formed on cycling TAA 
as shown in Fig. S9 by XPS. The peaks of Li 2 S 2 and Li 2 S can be seen 
in the XPS spectrum of the discharged state, which are also the ﬁ- 
nal products of conventional lithium-sulfur batteries. In the spec- 
trum of the oxidation state, the characteristic peaks of sulfur can 
be distinguished. Since the cathode does not contain sulfur, most 
of the sulfur in the sulfur-containing material comes from the sul-

the oxidation products are elemental sulfur, which also explains 
why TAA exhibits sulfur-like electrochemical behavior. 
The other enhancement on the capacity may be related to the 
enhancement on the solubility of Li 2 S, minimizing passivation of 
cathode and elevating the utilization of active material [39] .To 
demonstrate that, equal amount of Li 2 S is added to blank and 
TAA electrolyte and stirred for 48 h. As Fig. 4 b depicts, the TAA 
containing electrolyte is still transparent and the color changes 
to light yellow, demonstrating that lithium sulﬁde has been com- 
pletely dissolved. However, the blank electrolyte becomes a turbid 
liquid, revealing the negligible solubility of lithium sulﬁde in the 
blank electrolyte. To understand the enhancement of Li 2 S dissolu- 
tion with TAA additive, we performed nuclear magnetic resonance 
(NMR) measurements on pure TAA and the mixture of TAA and 
lithium sulﬁde. The result is shown in Fig. 4 c. Compared with pure 
TAA, the introduction of Li 2 S weakens the N-H bond of primary- 
amine group in TAA and forms S-H bond, which reveals the in- 
teraction between Li 2 S and N-H bond of primary-amine group, 
as the hydrogen is highly electropositive as hydrogen bond donor 
[ 39 , 51 , 52 ]. What’s more, no characteristic peak of polysulﬁdes ap- 
pears in UV-Vis absorption spectra in Fig. S10, which eliminates the 
possibility of lithium sulﬁde being oxidized to polysulﬁdes. Lithium 
sulﬁde dissolved in the electrolyte is more susceptible to oxidation 
than that deposited on the electrode surface. This means that in 
the case of high sulfur loading (4.5 mg cm −2 ), the oxidation kinet- 
ics of lithium sulﬁde is accelerated and the activation overpotential 
is lower, as described in Fig. 4 d. Considering that the actual Li- 
S battery application requires higher sulfur loading and less elec- 
trolyte, we assembled Li-S batteries with 4.5 mg cm −2 sulfur load- 
ing and 10 μL mg −1 E/S. Fig. S11 shows the cycle performance of 
Li-S batteries with/without TAA at 0.2C. The discharge capacity of 
the battery with blank electrolyte presents rapid attenuation, espe- 
cially after 20 cycles. The discharge capacity decreases to 663 mA 
h g −1 after 45 cycles, while the discharge capacity of experiment 
group maintains 775 mA h g −1 . 
To further explore the interaction between TAA and lithium sul- 
ﬁde, the electrodes extracted from cycled batteries at discharged 
state are tested by XPS to analyze phase composition of sulfur 
species. Evidenced from Fig. 5 a, two species that account for the 
largest proportion are Li 2 S at 161.5 eV and 162.4 eV and Li 2 S 2 at 
162.7 eV and 163.3 eV [53–55] which are stemmed from the re- 
duction of polysulﬁde and the deposition on cathode in the blank 
electrolyte. Besides, the peaks at 164.2 eV and 165.4 eV correspond 
to the incomplete conversion of elemental sulfur [ 53 , 56 ]. In addi- 
tion, the side reaction product, thiosulfate, locates at 166.7 eV and 
167.8 eV due to the presence of LiNO 3 to oxidize polysulﬁde and 
the S-O bond appears at 168.6 eV and 169.3 eV from the decom- 
position of lithium salt [57] . As for the S 2 p spectra of TAA elec- 
trolyte, the intensity of Li 2 S and Li 2 S 2 peaks decreases to varying 
degrees and furthermore the atomic concentration of element sul- 
fur in survey spectra (Fig. S12) of cathode with additive is less than 
that without TAA, which proves directly more insoluble discharge 
products are dissolved in the electrolyte to alleviate the electrode 
passivation. Furthermore, the EIS plots of lithium-sulfur batteries 
with two electrolytes before and after 200 cycles are displayed in

Organosulﬁdes are used as cathode materials or electrolyte ad- 
ditives in lithium-sulfur batteries to regulate the behavior of sul- 
fur during charging and discharging by interacting with polysul- 
ﬁdes or catalysts [ 20 , 23 , 59 ]. In this paper, the organic sulfur in 
thioacetamide is transformed into inorganic sulfur, which is the 
discharge product of conventional lithium-sulfur batteries during 
the discharge process, and participates in the subsequent cycle 
process. At the same time, thioacetamide increases the solubility of 
lithium sulﬁde, so that more polysulﬁdes can contact the conduc- 
tive substrate, which is more conducive to the conversion to the 
ﬁnal product. It is concluded from schematic diagram ( Fig. 6 ) that 
the dissolution of Li 2 S can be enhanced in thioacetamide-based 
electrolyte compared to blank electrolyte through intermolecular 
hydrogen bonds, leading to less deposition of discharge products 
on the surface of cathode and the achievement of weakening the 
passivation of electrode [60] . At the same time, the liquid-solid 
conversion between insoluble discharge products in the reaction 
process is altered into liquid-liquid conversion and the reaction 
kinetics of the lithium-sulfur battery are improved so that excel- 
lent rate performance and superior cycle performance is obtained. 
On the other hand, the sulfur atom in thioacetamide has suﬃcient 
electrochemical activity to provide active material. 
4. Conclusions 
In this work, thioamides are proposed as electrolyte additives 
for lithium sulfur battery to minimize the passivation of cathode 
caused by Li 2 S deposition and enhance the electrochemical per- 
formance by adjusting the solubility of Li 2 S. The inﬂuence of var- 
ious thioamides with different molecular structure as electrolyte 
additives towards the electrochemical performance of Li-S cell is 
investigated. It is found that TAA, TBA, TU, DMTU with primary- 
amine group or secondary-amine group show good reversible elec- 
trochemical activity while other thioamides with tertiary-amine 
group are not. With primary-amine group or secondary amine- 
group, the thioamide additives can offer extra capacity which is 
beneﬁcial to enhancing the energy density. It is found that hy- 
drogen atom in primary-amine group or secondary-amine group is 
crucial for the sulfur atoms of thioamide additives and TAA, as the 
best-performing thioamide additive, can promote the dissolution of 
Li 2 S through intermolecular hydrogen bonds, alleviating the passi- 
vation of the electrode and enhancing redox reaction kinetics to 
encourage conversion of polysulﬁde to Li 2 S. Therefore, Li-S batter- 
ies with 2 wt.% TAA convey speciﬁc discharge capacity of 738.9 mA 
h g −1 and 667 mA h g −1 at the current density of 0.5 C and 1 C 
respectively after 200 cycles. Compared with blank electrolyte, the 
cells containing TAA express superior cycle performance and rate 
performance.
