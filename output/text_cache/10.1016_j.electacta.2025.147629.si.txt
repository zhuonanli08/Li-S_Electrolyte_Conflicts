NOTE:
The S@C||Li half-cell employing the 2 M DOL+MDOL electrolyte exhibits two distinct discharge plateaus at approximately 2.3 V and 2.1 V, with a relatively low polarization. In contrast, the cell with the 3 M DOL+MDOL electrolyte shows a more sloped discharge profile around 2.1 V (Figure S12a and b), suggesting slower polysulfide conversion kinetics. As seen in Figure S12c and d, the cell with 2 M electrolyte delivers an initial discharge capacity of 1020.8 mAh g-1 at 0.05 C. After 100 cycles at 0.1 C, it retains a reversible capacity of 588.0 mAh g-1, corresponding to a capacity retention of 64.9%, along with an average Coulombic efficiency of 85.6%. By comparison, the cell using 3 M electrolyte achieves a much lower initial capacity of 514.4 mAh g-1 at 0.05 C. After 100 cycles at 0.1 C, it maintains a reversible capacity of 622.4 mAh g-1 (75.0% retention) and an average Coulombic efficiency of 89.4%.
Notably, the discharge capacity of the cell with 3 M electrolyte initially increases before decreasing. This behavior can be attributed to the limited amount of free solvent in the highly concentrated electrolyte, which slows polysulfide conversion kinetics and results in initially low sulfur utilization, so a certain activation period is required to reach maximum utilization. Although the 3 M electrolyte offers higher capacity retention and Coulombic efficiency, its slower reaction kinetics, need for a prolonged activation process, and higher cost due to increased salt content make it less favorable. Therefore, we selected the 2 M electrolyte as the optimized system for this study.
