Experimental Section 
Preparation of electrolyte and electrodes. Trimethyl(trifluoromethyl)silane (TFMTMS) and 
other 
general reagents (DME, DOL) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, and all the solvents were 
dried with molecular sieves before use.  
Lithium 
bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide 
(LiTFSI) and the conventional electrolyte (1.0 M LiTFSI, 2 wt% LiNO3 in DME:DOL=1:1 Vol%) 
were purchased from DodoChem. Lithium 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3-hexafluoropropane-1, 3-disulfonimide 
(LiHFDF) and Lithium nitrate (LiNO3) were purchased from Aladdin. The LFE electrolyte was 
prepared as follow: LiTFSI, LiNO3 and LiHFDF were dissolved into the DME completely, and 
then added the DOL and TFMTMS based on the volume ratio of DME:DOL:TFMTMS =48:17:35. 
For the sulfur electrode, mixed the sulfur and Carbon ECP-600JD at the mass ratio of 4:1 by ball 
mill, and then the mixture was added in a 50 ml Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave, heated at 
155 °C for 4 hours and cooled naturally at room temperature, resulting in S/C composite. The S/C 
composite, Ketjenblack, CMC and SBR were mixed at a weight ratio of 8:1:0.5:0.5 by milling. 
The corresponding slurry was cast onto a carbon-coated aluminum (Al) foil and dried at 60 °C for 
4 hours and further dried at 80 °C for 12 hours under vacuum.  
Characterization. The morphology of the electrode was investigated by an SEM (Hitachi S-4800 
field emission), operated at 10 kV. The cross-section of lithium metal was cut by a cross-section 
polisher (IB-19510 CP). The surface composition of the electrode was characterized by 
ESCALAB 250 XPS with monochromatic Al kα radiation. For the XPS of the lithium metal, the 
samples were disassembled from the coin cell or pouch cell configuration, and washed three times 
by DME and held in a vacuum of 25 °C overnight to get rid of electrolyte residue. 
Electrochemical Measurements. All 2032 coin-cell and pouch-cell configurations were 

<<PAGE 3>>
fabricated in an argon-filled glovebox. PP membrane was used as the separator in the coin cell, 
and the carbon-coated membrane was used in the pouch cell. For Li|Cu CE tests, deposited 1 mAh 
cm-2 capacity with a current of 0.5 mA cm-2 onto the Cu followed by charging to 1 V. All the Li-S 
cells were activated with a low rate for two cycles, and cycled between 1.7 and 2.8 V. The 
electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were tested by a CHI 600E electrochemical workstation, 
and the frequency window was set in the range of 0.01 Hz to 100 000 Hz. 
Preparation of Pouch Cells and the corresponding Energy density calculation. The Li-S 
pouch cells were assembled using S/C composite (30 mm * 40 mm) with 4.0 mg/cm2 as the 
cathode and 30 or 60 μm ultrathin Li as anodes. Carbon coated membrane (with a layer of Al2O3) 
as the separator, Al-plastic film as packaging. The pouch cells were clamped by two steel plates 
for applying some pressure onto the cycling pouch cell. The energy density was evaluated based 
on the following equations:

Eg stands for the gravimetric energy density (Wh/kg), C is the specific capacity (mAh/g1), S is the 
sulfur loading (mg/cm2), V stands for the output voltage (2.1 V), ms, mc, mb, ma, mLi and mse 
stand for sulfur mass, carbon mass, binder mass, Al mass, Li mass and the separator mass per unit 
area (mg/cm2). E/S is the electrolyte-volume/sulfur (μL/mg), 𝑝 stands for the electrolyte density 
(mg/μL). The density of the electrolytes was measured by 5 mL pipette, and the average of three 
tests was took to be the final result. 
Theoretical calculations. Theoretical calculations. We used DFT calculations to study the 
absorption of solvent molecule TFMTMS and LiTFSI onto Li, a four-layer Li 4×4 slab of 15 Å 
vacuum spacing was constructed to simulate the electrode surface, with the bottom 1.5 layers 
fixed during structural relaxation. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof methods[1] implemented in the 
VASP software[2] were employed. The energy cutoff for expanding electronic wave function and 
Monkhorst-Pack[3] k-point mesh are 400 eV and 1×1×1, respectively. The structures were relaxed 
until all atomic residual forces were no greater than 0.02 eV Å-1. The incorporation of the van der 
Waals effect was through the D3 correction[4]. The binding energy (𝐸b) used to determine the 
adsorbing ability was defined as the difference between the total energy of the molecule-adsorbed 
system energy (𝐸total) and the sum of isolated molecule (𝐸molecule) and a clean Li substrate 

<<PAGE 4>>
(𝐸substrate), i.e.,𝐸b = 𝐸total −(𝐸molecule + 𝐸substrate). Thus, the more negative the binding energy is, 
the higher the adoptability of the Li surface to the molecular adsorbate. 
Simulation of primary current distribution. The electrochemical model of the lithium metal 
half-cell was mathematically developed via the Nernst-Planck model (Electrochemical Methods, 
Fundamentals and Applications, Wiley, N.Y., 1980) in the commercial software package COMSOL 
Multiphysics V5.6. Balance equations for the species Li+, TFSI−, e− and Li were solved in the 
entire simulation domain. Concentrated solution theory, electro-neutrality and Ohm’s law were 
implemented to describe mass transport in the electrolyte[5].
