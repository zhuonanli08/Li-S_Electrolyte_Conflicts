I. Supplementary Text 
1. Raw materials 
Li metal foils (China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd.), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, 
battery grade, DoDoChem), 1,3-dixolane (DOL, battery grade, DoDoChem), lithium 
bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI, battery grade, DoDoChem), N-methyl-
2-pyrrolidinone (NMP, Sinopharm), poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, HSV-300), 
sulfur (S), multi-walled carbon nanotubes (CNTs), aluminum (Al) foil, and lithium 
nitrate (LiNO3, metals basis, Aladdin) were used as purchased without purification. 
1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (HFE, 98%, TCI) was purified 
by molecular sieve for 24 h. 
2. The preparation of sulfur cathode 
S powder and CNTs (7:3 by wt.) were mixed to form S/CNT composite. Then, the 
S/CNT composite and PVDF binder (9:1, by wt.) were ball-milled in NMP. The 
obtained slurry was cast onto Al foil and further dried at 60°C for 12 h. The S/C cathode 
was cut into disks (Φ13.0 mm) with a S content of 63%. The S loading is 1.3, 4.4, and 
6.6 mg cm−2 for coin cells. The S cathode was kept in an argon-filled glove box with 
O2 and H2O contents below 0.1 ppm. 
3. The preparation of electrolytes 
The control electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (1:1, by vol.) 
and was denoted as DOL/DME. The electrolyte composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in 
DOL/DME/HFE (2:2:1, by vol.) was denoted as HFE-EPSE. LiNO3 was employed in 
the electrolytes to improve the performance of the Li–S coin cells with high-loading S

SI2 
cathodes and ultrathin Li metal anodes. Li2S and S (1:7, by mol.) were added into the 
DOL/DME or HFE-EPSE electrolyte to prepare the Li2S8 electrolyte. Li2S and S (1:5, 
by mol.) were added into the DOL/DME or HFE-EPSE electrolyte to prepare the Li2S6 
electrolyte. All electrolytes were prepared in an argon-filled glove box with O2 and H2O 
contents below 0.1 ppm. 
4. Electrochemical measurements 
Li–S batteries were assembled in standard 2032-coin cells with polypropylene (PP) 
separators (Celgard 2400). Li–S batteries under mild conditions were assembled with a 
Li metal anode (600 μm), a regular-loading S cathode (1.3 mg cm−2), and 25 μL 
electrolyte without LiNO3. Li–S batteries under demanding conditions were assembled 
with an ultrathin Li anode (50 μm), a high-loading S cathode (4.4 or 6.6 mg cm−2), and 
electrolyte (35 or 60 μL, respectively) containing 2.0 wt.% LiNO3. The cells were tested 
between 1.7–2.6 V at different C-rates (1 C = 1672 mA g−1) on a Neware battery cycler 
(CT-4008t, Shenzhen, China). 
For the shuttle current test, Li–S batteries under mild conditions without LiNO3 
were galvanostatically charged to 2.9 V and then discharged potentiostaticlly to 2.90, 
2.60, 2.45, 2.43, 2.40, 2.39, 2.38, 2.37, 2.36, 2.35, 2.34, 2.25, and 2.20 V step by step. 
The shuttle current was determined by the steady-state current at different potentiostatic 
voltages. 
5. Material Characterizations 
The morphologies of Li metal anodes were observed by a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM, JSM 7401F, JEOL Ltd., Japan) operated at 3.0 kV. Cycled Li anodes

SI3 
from Li–S batteries were washed three times by DME to remove residual electrolyte 
and dried in the glove box. 7Li nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra (JNM-
ECA600) were recorded at 233.2 MHz at room temperature. X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS) were conducted on scanning X-ray microprobe (Axis Supra+, 
Kratos Analytical) operated at 250 kV with monochromated Al Kα radiation. The Ar+ 
sputtering rate was ~130 nm min–1 calibrated on Ta2O5 surface. 
6. Li–S pouch cells 
Li–S pouch cells were fabricated in a dry room (dew point < −40°C). The size of 
electrodes was 7 × 4 cm2. The S loading was 7.2 mg cm−2 for each side with S content 
of 63% (by weight). The thickness of ultrathin Li foils was 75 μm for each single side. 
The electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio was 3.5 g gS−1. The Li–S pouch cells were cycled at 
1.8–2.5 V and 0.05 C (1 C = 1000 mA g−1). The energy density was evaluated as follws: 
E = Ed/(mLi + mS + mCu + mAl + msep + mtap + mely + mpack) 
where E is the gravimetric energy density, Ed is the initial discharge energy, mLi, mS, 
mCu, mAl, msep, mtap, mely, and mpack stand for the mass of Li metal anodes, S cathodes 
(S, carbon, and binder), Cu current collector, Al current collector, separator, tap, 
electrolyte, and package, respectively. The pouch cells with HFE-EPSE delivered an 
initial discharge energy of 5.04 Wh. The total mass of the battery core (Li metal anodes, 
S cathodes, Cu current collector, Al current collector, separator, and tap) was 5.88 g. 
The mass of electrolyte and package were 7.12 and 2.10 g, respectively. The initial 
energy density based on the total mass of the pouch cell was 334 Wh kg−1. 
7. MD simulations 

<<PAGE 5>>
 
SI4 
MD simulations were performed based on the LAMMPS code[1] and OPLS-AA 
force field.[2] The force field parameters were generated by the LigParGen web server[3] 
except that restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) atomic partial charges were 
assigned to each atom, which was obtained based on electrostatic potential (ESP) 
charges using the Multiwfn program.[4] The parameters for Li+, TFSI−, and S82− were 
obtained from Jensen et al.,[5] Koddermann et al.,[6] and Rajput et al.,[7] respectively. 
Two models with and without HFE were constructed. The model with HFE contains 
458 DOL, 308 DME, 80 LiTFSI, 10 Li2S8, and 105 HFE molecules while the one 
without HFE contains 572 DOL, 385 DME, 80 LiTFSI, and 10 Li2S8 molecules. The 
initial atomic coordinates were generated with Packmol[8] program and the final 
constructions were visualized by VESTA[9] and VMD.[10] 
The time step was fixed to be 1 fs for all simulations. All systems were first 
equilibrated in NPT ensemble using the Parrinello–Rahman barostat[11] for 2 ns to 
maintain a temperature of 298 K and a pressure of 1 atm with time constants of 0.1 and 
1 ps, respectively. Subsequently, the models were heated from 298 to 420 K for 2 ns 
and maintained at 420 K for 4 ns, followed by annealing from 420 to 298 K in 2 ns and 
equilibrated at 298 K in NPT ensemble for another 4 ns. A production run of 12 ns at 
298 K in NVT ensemble under Nose–Hoover thermostat[12] was finally conducted. The 
last 5 ns NVT simulations were used for the solvation structure analyses.
