Experiments 
Preparation of LCDE 
N-hexane (NH, 99%) and tetrahydrofuran (THF, 99.5%) were purchased from 
Aladdin. 4 Å molecular sieve was used to remove the water. Lithium 
bis-(trifluoromethylsulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI) and lithium nitrate (LiNO3) were 
purchased from DodoChem. The lithium salts were dried in an argon-filled glove box 
at 60 ° C for 6 hours before used. The 0.1 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO3 dissolved 
THF/NH solution (v/v=65/35) was defined as LCDE, while the 0.1 M LiTFSI and 0.1 
M LiNO3 dissolved THF/NH (v/v=85/15) and (v/v=50/50) solutions were defined as 
LCDE- and LCDE+, respectively. The 0.1 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO3 dissolved 
THF solution was regarded as LCE baseline. The ionic conductivity (σ) was tested 
according to the equation of σ=L/(R*A); where L is the distance between two 
platinum electrodes (5 mm), R is the electrolyte resistance which is tested by an 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), and A is the area of a platinum 
electrode (5 mm×5 mm), respectively. 
Preparation of cathodes 
The commercial sulfur (S) powder was mixed with Ketjenblack (C) at a mass 
ratio of 4:1, following by being ball-milled for 6 h. The obtained composite (C/S) was 
kept at 155 ℃ for 12 hours under argon protection. Afterwards, the C/S composite was 
mixed with acetylene black (AB) and PVDF in a mass ratio of 8:1:1. The as-prepared 
composite was then dispersed in an NMP solvent with a solid/liquid mass ratio of 1/6. 
The slurry was casted onto the aluminum foil and then dried at 60 ° C for 12 hours. 
The obtained electrode was cut into discs with a diameter of 12 mm. The cathodes 
with different sulfur loadings (1.5, 2.4, 3.9 and 5.8 mg cm-2) were obtained by 
adjusting the thickness of the doctor blade. In particular, the C/S composite, AB and 
PVDF were mixed in a mass ratio of 84:8:8 for Li-S pouch cells. LiFePO4 (LFP) 
cathode was prepared by mixing LFP powder, AB and PVDF in a mass ratio of 8:1:1. 
The subsequent experimental process was consistent with the preparation of sulfur 
electrode discs. The mass loading of LFP was 7.0 mg cm-2.

Solubility tests of sulfur species 
The solubility tests were carried out in an argon-filled glove box. In LCE case, 
1.5 M Li2S8 (sulfur and Li2S in mole ratio of 7:1), 1.5 M Li2S6 (sulfur and Li2S in 
mole ratio of 5:1), and 1 M Li2S4 (sulfur and Li2S in mole ratio of 3:1) were dissolved 
in 10 mL LCE. In LCDE and LCDE+ cases, 50 mM Li2S8 (sulfur and Li2S in mole 
ratio of 7:1), 50 mM Li2S6 (sulfur and Li2S in mole ratio of 5:1), and 30 mM Li2S4 
(sulfur and Li2S in mole ratio of 3:1) were dissolved in 10 mL LCDE and LCDE+. 
The above solutions were stirred at 50 °C for 6 hours. The precipitates were filtered 
through a microporous membrane and then they were dried at 50 °C for 12 hours. The 
solubilities of sulfur species ([S], concentration of atomic sulfur) were calculated by 
the mass difference before and after dissolution. 0.5 M Li2S6-THF solution was 
prepared by dissolving 2.5 M sulfur and 0.5 M Li2S in THF solvent, which was stirred 
at 50 °C for 6 hours. 
Li-S battery button-cell assembly and electrochemical measurements 
The 2025-type button cell was used for electrochemical performance analysis. 
The button cell was assembled in an argon filled glove box, in which the water and 
oxygen concentrations were less than 0.5 ppm. Lithium foil with a diameter of 12 mm 
and a thickness of 100 μm was used as an anode. C/S electrodes with different sulfur 
loadings were applied as cathodes. Polypropylene membrane (Celgard 2400) was 
used as a separator. LCDE and LCE were used as electrolytes. Typically, 1 M LiTFSI 
dissolved 1,3-dioxolane/1,2-dimethoxyethane (DOL/DME) solution (v/v=1/1) with 2 
wt.% LiNO3 was used as a conventional ether electrolyte (CVE). The 
electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio was fixed at 10 μL mg-1. The galvanostatic 
charge-discharge (GCD) tests were performed on a Neware system (5V, 20mA). 
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), cyclic voltammetry (CV), linear 
voltammogram (LV) and chronoamperometry were performed on an electrochemical 
workstation (Chenhua, chi660d).

Materials characterizations 
The scanning electron microscopy (SEM, SIRION-100) equipped with an energy 
dispersive spectrometer (EDS, Oxford) attachment was applied to detect the 
morphology and structure. A Horiba JY LabRAM HR Evolution instrument (532 nm) 
and a Bruker Avance III (500 MHz) instrument were employed to analyze solvent 
chemistry. Rotary viscometer (Brookfield DV-2 pro) was used to analyze the viscosity 
of electrolyte with lithium anode. The contact angle measurements were performed in 
a Dataphysics OCA20 system. In-situ optical tests were realized under an optical 
microscope (Giorgione). The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Escalab 250Xi) 
with Al Kα radiation was used to identify the sulfur chemistry. A thermal gravimetric 
analyzer (TGA, Netzsch STA409PC) was employed to detect the sulfur content. In 
situ Raman test was realized in a closed container with a quartz window (Hefei 
Kejing), in which a 2025-type button cell was placed. The Φ2 mm holes were opened 
in negative shell as well as lithium foil to ensure that the Raman laser was injected 
into electrolyte in the anodic side. The surface and depth distributions of sulfur were 
identified by a time of flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS, 
5-100/ION). The sputtering and analysis areas are 250×250 and 50×50 μm2, 
respectively. 
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation 
The MD simulations were realized on the forcite programs (Accelrys Software 

<<PAGE 6>>
Inc.) with COMPASS II force field.[1] The geometries of LiTFSI, LiNO3, THF and 
NH species were optimized in forcite module. LCE is established by constructing an 
amorphous cell (59 Å×59 Å×59 Å) containing 10 LiTFSI, 10 LiNO3 and 1234 THF 
species, while LCDE contains 10 LiTFSI, 10 LiNO3 802 THF and 268 NH species. 
The ratio of various species corresponds to the actual stoichiometric ratio in 
electrolytes. All the systems were initially optimized for 50,000 steps applying smart 
algorithm with an energy convergence tolerance of 0.005 kcal mol−1 Å−1. Afterwards, 
the systems were equilibrated for 1 ns in the MD simulations using a time step of 1 fs 
in the isobaricisothermal (NPT) ensemble. The Berendsen barostat was used with 
maintaining a pressure of 1 atm. Particle-particle particle-mesh (PPPM) method was 
applied to describe the electrostatic interactions. Ultimately, the systems were run for 
1 ns in a canonical NVT ensemble with a time step of 1 fs at 298 K. The stable stages 
during 300-800 ns were applied to calculate the mean square displacements (MSDs). 
The MSDs of Li+ (<[x(t)-x(0)]2>) were recorded as functions of time. The diffusion 
coefficient was calculated by linearly fitting the MSDs-time curves.
