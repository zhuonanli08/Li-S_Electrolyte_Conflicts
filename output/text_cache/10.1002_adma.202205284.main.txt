Long cycling lifespan is a prerequisite for practical lithium–sulfur batteries yet 
is restricted by side reactions between soluble polysulfides and the lithium-
metal anode. The regulation on solvation structure of polysulfides renders 
encapsulating polysulfides electrolytes (EPSE) as a promising solution to sup-
press the parasitic reactions. The solvating power of the solvents in the outer 
solvent shell of lithium polysulfides is critical for the encapsulation effect of 
EPSE. Herein, 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (HFE) 
is demonstrated as a superior outer-shell solvent to construct EPSE. Based 
on the large steric hindrance of the fluorohydrocarbon chains, the electron-
withdrawing perfluoro segment (CF2 further endows HFE with promi-
nently weak solvating power. The HFE-EPSE improves the lifespan from 54 to 
135 cycles for lithium–sulfur batteries with an ultrathin lithium-metal anode 
(50 µm) and high-areal-loading sulfur cathode (4.4 mg cm−2). Furthermore, 
a 334 Wh kg−1 lithium–sulfur pouch cell (2.4 Ah level) with HFE-EPSE stably 
undergoes 25 cycles. This work demonstrates the role of weakening solvating 
power of outer-shell solvents to construct superior EPSE and inspires the 
significance of the solvation chemistry of polysulfides to achieve practical 
lithium–sulfur batteries.

in dictating the effect of EPSE and the underlying mechanism 
is indistinct. In order to further improve the electrochemical 
performance of practical Li–S batteries, EPSE with superior 
performance is necessary and the design of the outer solvent 
shell of LiPSs is crucial to encapsulate LiPSs.
Herein, 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether 
(HFE) is demonstrated as a superior outer-shell solvent to con-
struct EPSE to inhibit the side reactions of LiPSs on working 
Li-metal anodes. Both the large steric hindrance of fluorohydro-
carbon chains and the electron-withdrawing CF2 segments

power. HFE only distributes in an outer solvent shell around 
LiPSs, barely changing the inner solvent shell of LiPSs. HFE-

anode and a high-areal-loading S cathodes of 4.4  mg cm−2. 
Moreover, a 2.4 Ah pouch cell with HFE-EPSE undergoes stable 
25 cycles with an initial energy density of 334 Wh kg−1.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Solvation Structure of HFE-EPSE
According to previous reports, large steric hindrance enables 
molecules to possess weak solvating power which can be used 
as the outer-shell solvents of LiPSs to construct EPSE.[17] Hydro-
fluoroether not only has large steric hindrance of fluorohy-
drocarbon chains but also has abundant electron-withdrawing 
CF segments (such as CF2 and CF3).[18] The electron-
withdrawing effect of CF bond is considered to endow hydro-
fluoroether with extremely low solvating power compared with 
DIPS and DIPE. Therefore, hydrofluoroether is expected to 
have the potential to regulate the LiPS solvation structure, espe-
cially as a promising candidate for the outer-shell solvent of 
LiPSs. Herein 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl 
ether (denoted as HFE)[19] is chosen as a representative hydro-
fluoroether to demonstrate the potential to construct EPSE. 
HFE-EPSE consists of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL)/1,2-dimethoxy­
ethane (DME)/HFE (2:2:1, by vol.). DOL/DME (1:1, by vol.) is 
used as the typical control solvent due to its wide application in 
Li–S batteries since the 2000s.[20] Both the electrolytes contain 
1.0 m bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI) to provide 
basic ionic conductivity.[21]
The solvation structure of 1.0 m [S] Li2S8, that is the distribu-
tion of solvents around LiPSs in DOL/DME or HFE-EPSE, was 
decoded by molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. Li+ interacts 
with S82− tightly both in DOL/DME and HFE-EPSE, implying 
the dissociation of Li2S8 is weak (Figure 1a,d).[22] In DOL/
DME, DME emerges at 4.4 Å from the center of S82− through 
S82−–Li+–solvent interactions and the inner solvent shell (shell 
1) is formed. Following DME, DOL appears at 6.6 Å to form the 
outer solvent shell (shell 2). The solvation structure of 1.0 m [S] 
Li2S8 is similar to that in our previous research.[16] The coordi-
nation number of Li+, DME, and DOL begins to increase at 2.1, 
4.0, and 4.8 Å, respectively, which also reflects the difference 
in solvating power of the solvents around LiPSs (Figure 1b,c). 
The introduction of HFE into DOL/DME dramatically changes 
the LiPS outer solvent shell other than the inner solvent shell. 
Specifically, the inner solvent shell around LiPSs consists of 
DME (4.4 Å), which is similar to that in DOL/DME to ensure 
suitable dissolution of LiPSs.[23] HFE emerges at 6.4 Å which 
is recruited in the second solvent shell (Figure 1d). The coordi-
nation number of Li+, DME, DOL, and HFE around centered 
S82− begins to increase at 2.1, 4.0, 4.8, and 5.5 Å, respectively, 
indicating HFE merely influences the structure of the outer sol-
vent shell yet encapsulates centered S82− and the inner solvent 
shell (Figure 1e,f).
Experiments including nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) 
and Raman spectroscopy were further used to probe the sol-
vation structure of LiPSs. Considering that solvents interact 
with LiPSs by S82−–Li+–solvent interactions,[22] 7Li-NMR was 
used to infer the distribution of solvents around LiPSs. 1.0 m 
[S] Li2S8 dissolving in DOL/DME or HFE-EPSE is chosen as 
the electrolyte to imitate the electrolyte formulation used in 
MD simulations. The chemical shift of 7Li is 3.20  ppm both 
for DOL/DME and HFE-EPSE, indicating HFE barely interacts 
with Li+ and does not change the inner solvent shell of LiPSs 
(Figure  1g). Compared with previous research that the intro-
duction of DIPS and DIPE has a little influence of deshielding 
effect on the chemical shift of 7Li, the decoded solvation struc-
ture of HFE-EPSE indicates that the solvating power of HFE is 
much weaker than DIPS and DIPE, which is beneficial from 
the large steric hindrance of fluorohydrocarbon chains and 
electron-withdrawing effect of CF2 segments. There is a 
similar conclusion for Raman spectroscopy. The signal of poly-
sulfides (392, 448, and 510 cm−1), DME (845 cm−1), and DOL 
(945 cm−1) nearly overlaps for DOL/DME and HFE-EPSE,[24] 
indicating the inner solvent shell around LiPSs maintains 
with the introduction of HFE (Figure 1h and Figure S1, Sup-
porting Information). A similar conclusion is obtained for 
1.0 m [S] Li2S6 (Figures S2 and S3, Supporting Information). 
Therefore, it is concluded that HFE successfully constructs 
EPSE (Figure 1i).

batteries, where the introduction of hydrofluoroether aims to 
reduce the apparent concentration of Li salts but enable the 
electrolyte to exhibit the solvation structure of high-concen-
tration electrolytes.[25] Hydrofluoroether is not involved in the 
solvation of Li+ due to its poor solvating power. Almost all

2.3. Li–S Batteries with HFE-EPSE under Practical Conditions
Specifically, it should be mentioned that the test conditions 
of Li–S batteries highly influence the evaluation objectivity 
of the strategies. Although Li–S batteries can deliver a long 
lifespan under conventional conditions of regular-loading S 
cathodes of less than 2  mg cm−2 and enough Li supply with 
a thickness of more than 500 µm, the mild conditions conceal 
the problems of Li-metal anodes and exaggerate the effective-
ness of anode protection strategies because only low propor-
tion of Li-metal anodes (<4%) is used in each cycle. Therefore, 
the practical effect of HFE-EPSE was verified in Li–S batteries 
under demanding conditions of high-loading S cathodes 
(4.4  mg cm−2), ultrathin Li anodes (50  µm), and an electro-
lyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio of 6.0 µL mgS−1 with LiNO3 additives 
(Figure 3a and Figure S4, Supporting Information). The Li–S 
batteries were cycled at 0.1 C after initial two cycles of activation 
at 0.05 C (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The Li–S battery 
with DOL/DME electrolyte exhibits an initial specific capacity 
of 957 mAh g−1 (Figure  3a). However, the specific capacity 
sharply decreases after 40 cycles. The Li–S battery with DOL/
DME only delivers 54 cycles based on 70% capacity retention. 
In contrast, the battery with HFE-EPSE undergoes stable 135 
cycles based on 70% capacity retention. The cycling lifespan of 
the Li–S batteries improves 1.5 times with the employment of 
HFE-EPSE. The cycling stability of batteries with HFE-EPSE 
is superior compared with other advanced electrolyte strate-
gies (Table S1, Supporting Information). The discharge median 
voltage of the battery with HFE-EPSE maintains stable above 
2.06  V even after 135 cycles, while that with DOL/DME dra-
matically decreases after 20 cycles and reaches only 2.00 V after 
40 cycles (Figure  3b and Figure S6, Supporting Information). 
The more stable and higher discharge median voltage enabled 
by HFE-EPSE indicates the internal resistance of batteries is 
reduced and the dead Li accumulation is alleviated. Specifically, 
the galvanostatic charge and discharge profiles nearly overlap 
of the Li–S batteries with DOL/DME and HFE-EPSE at the 
second cycle except for a slight difference in discharge capacity 
(Figure 3c). Moreover, the discharge profiles of the Li–S battery 
with HFE-EPSE maintain stable at the 40th cycle, while that with 
DOL/DME delivers a dramatical slope of the second discharge 
plateau. The increased polarization with DOL/DME indicates

2.4. Practical Li–S Pouch Cells with HFE-EPSE
Compared with coin cells, pouch cells can demonstrate the 
high-energy-density advantage of Li–S batteries from the device 
scale and provide much demanding conditions to verify the 
effectiveness of strategies. A 2.4 Ah-level pouch cell is employed 
to verify the effect of HFE-EPSE (Figure 5a). The active mate-
rials in electrodes are double-layer and the size of the electrodes 
is 7 × 4 cm2 (Figure S14, Supporting Information). High-loading 
S cathodes (7.2  mg cm−2), ultrathin Li-metal anodes (75  µm), 
and lean electrolytes (3.5  g gS−1) are used to build a full cell. 
The initial discharge energy density is 334 Wh kg−1 (Figure 5b). 
After 25 cycles, the pouch cell with HFE-EPSE delivers a high 
capacity retention of 60% with a relatively stable CE (Figure 5c).

3. Conclusions
HFE is used as a superior outer-shell solvent to construct 
EPSE to inhibit the intrinsic side reactions between LiPSs 
and Li-metal anodes. There are two long fluorohydrocarbon 
chains with large steric hindrance and abundant electron-
withdrawing CF2 segments in the HFE molecule, which 
enables HFE to have much weak solvating power. The weak 
interaction of HFE with LiPSs makes HFE only distribute in 
the outer solvent shell of LiPSs. The HFE with high reduc-
tion stability ensures the stable preservation of the encap-
sulation structure during repeated cycling. HFE-EPSE 
dramatically improves the cycling life span of Li–S batteries 
from 54 to 135 cycles based on 70% capacity retention with 
ultrathin Li-metal anodes (50  µm) and high-loading S cath-
odes (4.4 mg cm−2). Moreover, a 2.4 Ah-level Li–S pouch cell 
with HFE-EPSE delivers stable 25 cycles with an initial energy 
density of 334 Wh kg–1. This work demonstrates that HFE is 
a superior outer-shell solvent to construct EPSE and inspires 
further effective regulation of solvation structures of LiPSs to 
achieve long-cycling Li–S batteries.
