Thermodynamic analysis of excess properties 
 
In addition to the van’t Hoff analysis as for the binary electrolytes, the excess enthalpy HE for the mixing process of 
cyclo-S8 in both the neat organic solvents and the electrolytes was computed. By definition, HE is the difference 
between the enthalpy of component i in the mixture and in the pure state.

This can be used to distinguish between the different types of interaction and their contributions to the overall 
intermolecular interactions. 
HE as a function of the cyclo-S8 mole fraction (xS8) was computed for both the neat organic solvents and the binary 
electrolytes using (A) and (B) (Figures S2–S7). In general, the mixing of cyclo-S8 is endothermic (HE > 0) for all solvents 
and electrolytes. This suggests that in total the attractive forces between molecules and ions of the pure components 
(solvent/solvent, Li+/solvent, Li+/TFSI, TFSI/solvent, Li+/Li+, TFSI/TFSI and cyclo-S8/cyclo-S8) are stronger than of those 
corresponding to the mixture (cyclo-S8/solvent, cyclo-S8/Li+, cyclo-S8/TFSI).  
Similar to the binary electrolytes, dissolving cyclo-S8 into the ternary electrolytes is endothermic (Figure S2). With 
increasing content of TMS HE increases, indicating the cyclo-S8/TMS interactions to be weaker than the cyclo-S8/DME 
interactions, quite consistent with the overall trends in cyclo-S8 solubility (Figure 5). In more detail the addition of 
TMS increasess HEMF whereas HEvdW decreases.

hydrogen bonding (HEHB) for mixing cyclo-S8 in TMS:DME:1.0 M LiTFSI of different volume ratios at 303.15 K.  
The maxima of HE were obtained for an xS8 to be between 0.45 and 0.55. For a more organized discussion, we divide 
the binary electrolytes in three groups:

1. PC and Pyr: in both neat solvents, negative values for HEvdW were obtained when mixed with cyclo-S8 which 
indicates that both solvents interact with cyclo-S8 via van der Waals interactions. However, due to less attractive 
electrostatic interactions (HEMF > 0), the total HE is positive. For Pyr, the enthalpies obtained in neat solvent and 
with 0.1 M LiTFSI are very similar. In the 1.0 M LiTFSI electrolyte, marginal negative values for HEvdW are observed 
and HEMF becomes even more positive, which indicates that the presence of LiTFSI already weakens Pyr/Pyr van 
der Waals interactions and induces electrostatic repulsion. For PC, even the addition of small amounts of LiTFSI 
overcomes the energetic win of PC/PC van der Waals interactions resulting in HEvdW > 0 and a significant increase 
in HEMF and HE, respectively, which increases even more with higher LiTFSI content. The differences due to the 
implicit and explicit solvation model are marginal for Pyr, but for PC consistently lower values for all excess 
properties were obtained with the implicit model.  
2. DME and G2: for both ether-compounds, with about 1.5 kJ mol-1, the major contribution to HE is a weakened 
van der Waals interaction upon mixing, HEvdW > 0. For the implicit solvation model in 0.1 M LiTFSI electrolytes, 
increased electrostatic attraction (HEMF < 0) was obtained for a cyclo-S8 content up to xS8 ≈ 0.5 in DME and xs 
≈ 0.7 in G2. In the corresponding explicit models, electrostatic repulsion is observed instead, as well as in the 
electrolytes with 1.0 M LiTFSI. As a result, starting with the neat ether-compound, HE decreases with addition of 
0.1 M LiTFSI and increases again for 1.0 M LiTFSI. 
3. DMF and DMSO: in both neat solvents the major contribution to HE is a weakened electrostatic attraction upon 
mixing, HEMF > 0. Apparently, small amounts of LiTFSI reduce HEMF in the implicit and explicit solvation model 
which indicates that potential cyclo-S8/Li+, cyclo-S8/TFSI as well as the cyclo-S8/Li+-solvent cluster and cyclo-
S8/Li+-TFSI cluster interactions are in average electrostatically more attractive. Thereby, values HEMF < 0 are 
obtained for the implicit solvation approach at xS8 < 0.2 for either solvent. Further, for DMSO, addition of LiTFSI 
destabilizes van der Waals interactions, whereas in DMF, HEvdW is unaffected by the lithium salt.
