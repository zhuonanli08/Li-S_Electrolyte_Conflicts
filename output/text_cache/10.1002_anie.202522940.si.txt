Experimental Section
Electrodes Preparation: Ketjenblack (KB, EC-600JD) was first spray-dried following the reported procedure to make the carbon host.[1] Sulfur (75 wt%, ~ 100 mesh, Thermo Scientific) were ground with the spray-dried KB (25 wt%) and melt-diffused in an autoclave at 155 °C for 12 h to obtain the S/KB composite. The particle sizes of S/KB were further refined with a 45 µm sieve to remove any agglomeration. To prepare sulfur cathodes, a slurry was produced by mixing the S/KB composite (85 wt%) with carbon nanotube (CNT, 5 wt%, XFNANO), super C65 (5 wt%), polyacrylic acid (PAA, 4 wt%, average MW ≈ 450,000, Sigma-Aldrich), and sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (Na-CMC, 1 wt%, MAC-350) in water in a Thinky mixer. The slurry was then doctor-blade cast onto carbon-coated aluminum foil and dried at 50 °C under vacuum. The electrodes were punched into 7/16-in. diameter disks before use. For pouch cell cathode with a loading of ≥ 4.5 mg cm−2, S/KB composite with 70 wt% sulfur was prepared and mixed with super C 65 (5 wt%) and Na-CMC (4 wt%, MAC-800) in water to obtain the slurry.

Electrolyte Preparation: All electrolytes were prepared in an Ar-filled glovebox. Solvents were dried with molecular sieves before use. The baseline electrolyte was composed of 1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, Solvionic) and 0.2 M LiNO3 (Sigma-Aldrich) in 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, Thermo Scientific) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, Oakwood Chemical) (v/v = 1: 1). Moderately solvating electrolytes (MSEs) were prepared by dissolving 1 M LiTFSI and 0.2 M LiNO3 in a mixture of DOL, DME, and a moderately solvating solvent (v/v/v = 3.5: 3.5: 3). The moderately solvating solvents tested include methyl tert-butyl ether (MtBE, Thermo Scientific), cyclopentyl methyl ether (CPME, Thermo Scientific), sec-butyl methyl ether (SBME, Sigma-Aldrich), and tert-amyl methyl ether (TAME, Sigma-Aldrich). 0.1 M Li2S6 was added to the electrolytes for assembling Li2S6 symmetric cells.

Electrochemical Measurements: All coin cells were assembled with CR2032 cell cases inside an Ar-filled glovebox. One layer of Celgard 2325 separator was utilized in each cell. The electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio was controlled (as specified). 20 μL of the electrolyte was added on each side for symmetric cells. The cells were rested for 5 h before being cycled. Low loading Li || S cells (~ 2 mg cm−2) were activated with one C/20 cycle and one C/10 cycle. High loading cells (≥ 3.5 mg cm−2) were first discharged at a C/30 rate, followed by one C/20 formation cycles. Extra C/10 formation cycles were performed for cells running the C/5 test. Galvanostatic cycling tests were then performed at specified rates between 1.7 and 2.8 V at 21 °C. High-temperature cycling test was performed at 45 °C with two C/20 and two C/10 rate formation cycles. All the cycling data were collected on a Landt or an Arbin cycler.

Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests were performed at a C/10 rate. A constant discharge current was applied for 15 min, followed by a 75 min relaxation. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was employed during the rest step (3 min after discharge). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were conducted in the potential range of 1.7 to 2.8 V for Li || S cells and −1 to 1 V for Li2S6 symmetric cells. EIS measurements were conducted in the frequency range of 1 MHz to 0.05 Hz. Distribution of relaxation time (DRT) analysis was performed with the pyDRTtools package.[2] GITT, CV, and EIS data were collected on a Biologic VMP-3 system.

Pouch Cell Assembly and Testing: Li-S pouch cells were fabricated with Li foil anode and blade-cast sulfur cathode. The Li anode was pressed onto a Ni current collector. The cathode was punched into 5.6 × 4.3 cm (~ 24 cm2) sheet, while the anode had a slightly larger dimension (5.8 × 4.5 cm). Ni and Al tabs were welded onto the anode and cathode current collector, respectively. The E/S ratio was controlled to be 5.5 or 4.5 µL mg−1. The pouch cells were put in pressure holders to ensure uniform pressure. The cells with a sulfur loading of 3.5 mg cm−2 were cycled between 1.7 and 2.8 V at a C/20 rate for 2 formation cycles, followed by cycling at a C/10 rate. The cells with a loading of 4.6 mg cm−2 were discharged once at a C/30 rate before cycling at a C/20 rate.

Materials Characterization: 1H, 7Li, and 19F nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy was carried out with a Bruker Avance III 500 MHz NMR spectrometer or an Agilent MR400 spectrometer. Two-dimensional (2D) diffusion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY) spectra were collected on a Varian 600 MHz NMR spectrometer. A coaxial insert with 0.1 M LiPF6 dissolved in the deuterated dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO-d6) was applied as the reference. Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) was performed with a Xenocs Ganesha instrument. Operando XRD analysis was carried out on a Rigaku Ultima IV X-ray diffractometer with a specially designed cell (MTI Corp). Raman spectra were collected with a Renishaw Raman spectrometer. Ionic conductivity of electrolyte was measured with a Mettler-Toledo SevenCompact S230 conductivity meter and an InLab 752 probe. The surface morphology of the materials was imaged with an Apreo 2C LoVac SEM. A PHI VersaProbe 4 X-ray Photoelectron Spectrometer was utilized for XPS analysis. The cycled Li samples were sputtered for various amounts of time with Ar cluster ions.

Computational methods: Density functional theory (DFT) calculations of binding energies were performed with the Gaussian 16 software package. B3LYP functional and the 6-311+G(d, p) basis set were used for structure optimization of solvent molecules and complexes.[3, 4] Vibrational frequency calculations were conducted to confirm that a local minimum was reached. M06-2X functional and the ma-def2-TZVP(-f) basis set were used for single point energy calculations.[5, 6] DFT-D3(BJ) and DFT-D3 correction were applied, respectively, for B3LYP and M06-2X to correct the vdW-dispersion energy.[7] The integral equation formalism variant of the polarizable continuum model (IEFPCM) was applied to describe the solvent effect. Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of the electrolytes were performed with the GROMACS 2024 software package. OPLS-AA force field was chosen for the simulations and Auxiliary Tools of Force Field (AuToFF) tool was applied to parametrize all atoms. 1.2*CM5 charge model was applied to the solvent molecules, while parameters from 0.8*OPLS-2009IL force field were derived for TFSI− and NO3−.[11, 12] The initial coordinates of the molecules were generated by the Packmol program in a 6.5 × 6.5 × 6.5 nm3 simulation box.[13] The numbers of molecules in each simulation cell are listed in Table S3. The simulation process consists of an initial energy minimization at 0 K with the conjugate gradient method, an initial heating process to 298 K for 1 ns, and a 15 ns run in the NPT ensemble (at 1 bar). Last 5 ns of the NPT simulation data was used for statistical analysis. A timestep of 1 fs was used in the simulations. The C-rescale barostat and V-rescale thermostat were used to control the pressure and temperature, respectively. 

Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations for electrolytes with polysulfide were conducted with Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) 6.4.2. Exchange-correlation potentials were parameterized with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) employing the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) functional.[14] The projector augmented wave (PAW) method was used to represent the core electrons and a plane-wave energy cutoff of 550 eV was chosen.[15] The k-point sampling was performed at Gamma point only because of the high computational demands. The number of molecules in the simulation box was determined based on the electrolyte density. The initial coordinates of the molecules were generated by the Packmol program. For MSE-MtBE, 15 DOL, 10 DME, 7 MtBE, 3 LiTFSI, 1 LiNO3, and 1 Li2S6 were placed in a 1.8 × 1.8 × 1.8 nm3 simulation box; for DOL/DME, 20 DOL, 14 DME, 3 LiTFSI, 1 LiNO3, and 1 Li2S6 were placed in a 1.75 × 1.75 × 1.75 nm3 box. AIMD simulations were conducted at 300 K in the NVT ensemble for 15 ps with last 5 ps of data extracted for statistical analysis. Temperature was controlled by a Nosé−Hoover thermostat. Tritium masses on solvents were substituted for protons to allow a timestep of 1 fs. Snapshots of the simulation trajectories are visualized in VESTA.[16]
