Figure S5. Voltage profiles of the Li-S optical cell using base electrolyte with LiNO3 at (A) 0.2 M, cycled between 1.5–2.8 V; and at 0.02 M, cycled between (B) 1.7–2.8 V and (C) 1.5–2.8 V.

Note for Figure S5
To examine whether these trends persist at deeper discharge, we extended the cut-off voltage to 1.5 V. To accentuate LiNO3 decomposition below 1.6 V, we employed a carbon paper current collector instead of Al foil (active material mass loading = 2 mg cm-2). With 0.2 M LiNO3, lowering the cut-off voltage from 1.7 to 1.5 V produced an additional sub-1.6 V plateau. Operando images showed uniform stripping and cohesive, electrochemically active deposits—similar to 1.7–2.8 V—indicating that residual LiNO3 remained effective. No pronounced shuttling was observed. To probe additive depletion, the LiNO3 concentration was reduced to 0.02 M to accelerate its depletion and assess whether deposition regulation persists once LiNO3 is exhausted. Figures S5B and S5C present cells with 0.02 M LiNO3 operated between 1.7–2.8 V and 1.5–2.8 V, respectively. During discharge, stripping remained uniform, similar to that in Figure S5A. By contrast, during charge, cells cycled at 1.7–2.8 V maintained growth behavior comparable to the 0.2 M case, whereas those cycled at 1.5–2.8 V exhibited severe PS shuttling, active Li loss, accumulation of corrosion-derived byproduct layers, and substantial dead Li formation. These results indicate that once LiNO3 is exhausted, it no longer regulates deposition or prevents detachment, and the system reverts to additive-free behavior.
