1. Experimental section 
1.1. Raw materials 
All the raw materials including elemental sulfur (S8), multi-walled carbon nanotubes 
(MWCNTs), poly (vinylidenefluoride) (PVDF), N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP), 1,3-dioxolane 
(DOL), 
1,2-dimethoxyethane 
(DME), 
lithium 
nitrate 
(LiNO3), 
lithium 
bis(trifluromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), lithium bis(fluorsulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), dimethyl 
diselenide (DMDSe), lithium sulfide (Li2S), copper (Cu) foils, aluminum (Al) foils, 
polypropylene (PP) membranes (Celgard 2400), lithium (Li) foils, 2025 and 2032 coin cell 
shells were purchased from commercial sources and directly used without further purification. 
 
1.2. Assembly and electrochemical test of Li–S pouch cells 
1.2.1. Fabrication of sulfur cathodes and Li anodes 
To fabricate the sulfur cathodes, a mixture of S8 and MWCNTs with a mass ratio of 7:3 
was ball-milled with PVDF at a mass ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a slurry. Then the slurry was 
coated on both sides of Al foils with doctor blades and dried at 60°C for 24 h. Subsequently, 
the sulfur cathodes were cut into 4.0 × 7.0 cm2 electrodes, with a sulfur content of 63 wt.% and 
an areal sulfur loading of 7.5 mgS cm−2 on a single side. Li anodes were fabricated by rolling 
Li anode foils on both sides of Cu foils and then cut into electrodes of the same size as the sulfur 
cathodes. The thickness of the Li anode was 75 μm on a single side. 
 
1.2.2. Assembly of Li‒S pouch cell 
1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 5 wt.% LiNO3 were dissolved in mixed solvents of DOL and DME 
(v/v=1/1) as the basic electrolyte. The density of the  basic electrolyte is 1.03 g mL−1. The 
separator used in the pouch cells was Celgard 2400 PP membranes. To assemble a Li‒S pouch 
cell at 3 Ah level, 5 pieces of sulfur cathodes and 6 pieces of Li anodes were stacked layer-by-
layer separated by the PP membranes in a dry room with a dew point below −40°C. All the

S2 
electrodes were set in parallel connection through an electrode lug terminal and then put in a 
package. Electrolyte was then injected into the pouch cells.  The electrolyte to sulfur ratio (E/S 
ratio) of Cell 1, Cell 2, and Cell 4 was 2.8 gele gS−1. The E/S ratio in cell 3 was 5.0 gele gS−1. 
After that, the pouch cell was vacuumized (‒65 kPa) and sealed for further electrochemical tests. 
 
1.2.3. Electrochemical tests of Li‒S pouch cell 
The as-assembled Li–S pouch cell was galvanostatically discharged and charged between 
1.70‒2.55 V on a Land multichannel battery tester (Wuhan Land Electronic Co., Ltd.) after 
resting for 12 h. For Cell 1, the cycling rate was 0.025 C (1 C = 1000 mA gS−1). For Cell 2 and 
Cell 3, the cells were initially discharged and charged at 0.025 C for one cycle then cycled at 
0.2 C in the following cycles. For Cell 4, the cell was first discharged and charged at 0.025 C 
for one cycle, then discharged and charged at 0.2 C for one cycle, and then reverted back to 
cycle at 0.025 C in the subsequent cycles. The cycling test was stopped at the charged state 
(2.55 V) when the Li‒S pouch cell exhibited rapid capacity decay. 
 
1.3. Disassembly and evaluation of Li–S pouch cells 
1.3.1. Disassembly of Li‒S pouch cells 
Cell 2 was disassembled using ceramic scissors after cell failure, and the process was 
completed within two hours by the end of the electrochemical test. All the procedures were 
conducted in an Ar-filled glove box with water and oxygen concentrations below 0.1 ppm. 
 
1.3.2. Characterization of the cycled electrodes 
The morphology of the cycled electrodes was observed on a FEI-Apreo C (FEI Ltd., 
Hillsboro state, United States) scanning electron microscope (SEM) operated at 3.00 kV. To 
confirm the presence of the sulfur species, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Kratos 
Analytical Inc., Japan) was employed using monochromatized Al Kα radiation (hν = 1486.8 eV) 

<<PAGE 4>>
S3 
as the X-ray source and a base pressure of 10−7 Pa. The XPS spectrometer was directly 
connected to the Ar-filled glove box to avoid direct contact of the cycled electrodes with the 
atmosphere. The binding energies of the XPS results were calibrated by referencing the carbon 
1s peak at 284.8 eV. To ensure that any residual electrolyte and precipitated species on the 
cycled electrodes were removed for further characterization, the electrodes were washed with 
DME until the washing solvent was colorless. 
 
1.3.3. Coin cell evaluation of the cycled electrodes 
Standard 2025 or 2032 coin cell configuration was used to evaluate the electrochemical 
performance of the cycled electrodes. One side of the unwashed electrodes was scraped off and 
the electrodes were punched into small disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm. Fresh PP membranes 
were punched into disks with a diameter of 19.0 mm and used as separators. 
To test the residual capacity of the cycled Li anodes, the cycled Li was matched with fresh 
600 μm-thick Li foils (16.0 mm in diameter) as the counter electrode in 2025 coin cells. To 
simulate the electrolyte conditions of the pouch cell, 4.0 mol[S] L−1 Li2S8 electrolyte was utilized. 
Specifically, S8 and Li2S powders in a molar ratio of 7:8 were added to a DOL/DME (v/v=1:1) 
solution. In addition, the electrolyte contained 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3. 15 or 35 
μL Li2S8 electrolyte was dropped onto the cycled anode while 5 μL electrolyte was dropped 
onto the fresh Li counter electrode. The as-assembled coin cells were charged galvanostatically 
to 0.10 V at a current density of 1.5 mA cm−2 to ensure that the residual active Li in the cycled 
anodes was fully stripped. In order to evaluate the polarization of the cycled anode, the cells 
were galvanostatic cycled at 1.5 mA cm−2 and 7.5 mAh cm−2. 
Similarly, the cycled cathodes were matched with fresh 600 μm-thick Li anode foils as the 
anode (16.0 mm in diameter) to test the residual capacity and cycling performance of the cycled 
cathodes in 2032 coin cell shells. In order to test the remaining capacity of the cycled cathodes, 
30 μL of basic electrolyte (E/S ratio= 3.5 μL mgS−1, the pouch cell electrolyte) was dropped

onto the unwashed cathodes while 5 μL electrolyte was added to the Li anode side. The as-
assembled coin cells were galvanostatically cycled under a cycling rate of 0.2 C within a voltage 
range of 1.70–2.60 V. In addition, tests under mild conditions (E/S ratio = 8.0 μL mgS−1, 0.2 C 
or E/S ratio = 3.5 μL mgS−1, 0.025 C) were performed in the same cell configuration. All the 
above electrochemical tests were performed on a Neware battery test system (Shenzhen, China). 
 
1.4. Polarization decoupling of the cathodic kinetic 
1.4.1. Polarization decoupling based on coin cell configuration 
The coin cells were assembled following the same parameters as the cycled cathodes in 
section 1.3.3. EIS measurement was conducted to obtain the bulk resistance (R1). EIS was 
performed using the potentiostatic mode at the open-circuit voltage on a Solartron 1470E 
electrochemical workstation. A sinusoidal voltage with an amplitude of 10 mV and a frequency 
range from 0.1 Hz to 100 kHz was applied. The intercept at the Z’ axis was regarded as ohmic 
resistance. In the EIS Nyquist plot, the internal bulk resistance (R1, by extrapolating the 
frequency to the real part of the resistance at infinity) was chosen, and its value after being 
multiplied by the test current (I) was regarded as ηohm.  
For the GITT test, specifically, the cell was discharged at a constant current of 0.2 C for 
15 min, accompanied by the generation of polarization, and then followed by a 120 min rest 
period to eliminate polarization and reach a thermodynamic equilibrium state.  
 
1.4.2. Polarization decoupling based on pouch cell configuration 
The Li–S pouch cell was assembled following the same parameters as section 1.2.2. It is 
worth noting that the assembled pouch cell was cycled for one cycle at 0.025 C before the GITT 
test at 0.2 C. The parameters of GITT involved a series of galvanostatic discharge processes of 
10 min at a current density of 0.2 C followed by 90 min rest at each step.

1.5. Performance regulation of pouch cell by adopting DMDSe electrolyte 
20 mM DMDSe was added to the mixed solvents of DOL/DME (v/v= 1:1) with 1.0 mol 
L−1 LiFSI and 5.0 wt.% LiNO3 as the DMDSe-based electrolyte. The regulated pouch cell (Cell 
5) using the DMDSe-based electrolyte was assembled with the same electrode pile and the E/S 
ratio of 2.8 gele gS−1 as Cell 2. The as-assembled Cell 5 was galvanostatically discharged and 
charged between 1.70‒2.55 V on the Land multichannel battery tester after resting for 12 h. 
Cell 5 was also initially discharged and charged at 0.025 C for one cycle then cycled at 0.2 C 
in the following cycles as Cell 2.
