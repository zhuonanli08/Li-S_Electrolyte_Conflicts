Supplementary Notes 
1. XPS analyses of the glass fibres 
Supplementary Fig. 2 summarizes the results of the XPS analyses carried out on the pristine 
glass fibres separator soaked with either the electrolyte or Li2S6 solution and those of the 
glass fibres separators extracted from the cells after cycling at the end of lithiation and 
delithiation processes. It is important to note that due to the significant charging of the 
studied samples especially those soaked with the electrolyte and the polysulphide solution, 
all core levels are drastically shifted in binding energies (BE). Thus, in the following 
discussion we will only refer to the peak intensity and/or additional contribution(s) appearing 
in some of the core levels, but we will not comment on the shift.  
Interestingly, the response of the Si2p core level (Supplementary Fig. 2) can be directly 
correlated to the TEM images shown in Fig. 4. A significant decrease in the intensity of the 
Si2p for the separator soaked with polysulphide solution and the separators extracted from 
the cells after cycling indicates the formation of a surface layer covering the glass fibres (the 
layer is less than 8 nm thick according to the detection limit of our apparatus, well in line with 
the TEM measurements). In addition, after cycling a new contribution appears at lower BE, 
which could indicate a chemical interaction between the PSs species and the SiO2. 
As for the sulphur S2s, the intensity of this core level is rather similar for all measured 
samples. This observation is not surprising since the adsorbed layer should be located only 
at the surface of the glass fibres and hence, regardless of the studied glass fibres, the 
sulphur should be always visible in the spectra. A closer look at the spectra of the separators 
extracted from the cells after cycling reveals the presence of new contributions. For the

As already demonstrated by SEM and TEM post-mortem analyses, at the end of lithiation, 
the glass fibres are covered by “crystal-like” precipitates, probably mostly Li2S solid 
products. Thus, at this state of charge, the sulphur S2s spectrum is most probably a 
signature of sulphur originating from Li2S and from the long-chains polysulphides 
permanently adsorbed at the surface of the glass fibres. In contrast, the S2s spectrum (as 
well as Si2s-S2p) recorded for the sample extracted at the end of delithiation is very rich in 
contributions, which cannot be attributed only to Li2S but could also correspond to i) the 
elemental sulphur (higher BE) and/or ii) to the Li2Sn species adsorbed on the glass fibres 
(lower BE, Supplementary Fig. 2). Both explanations seem reasonable since the main peak 
of the elemental sulphur is located at 237-233 eV, whereas that related to the adsorbed 
lithiated species is expected at lower BE. A similar trend is observed for the O1s spectra.  
2. Comparison to other operando studies 
Our results obtained using the in situ XRD cell (Supplementary Fig. 5) are similar to those 
reported in the literature. As for other operando studies (Supplementary Fig. 5), we can 
clearly differentiate the upper- and the lower-voltage plateaus during the lithiation process, 
and two plateau-like features during the subsequent delithiation process of the Li–S cell.  
The cycling rate we applied (C/50) is, however, much slower than those used for the 
operando XAS (C/5) and operando UV-Vis studies (C/10), yet comparable to the cycling rate 
applied for operando Raman spectroscopy (C/60). The reason for a slow cycling rate in our 
case is a data collection time, which was roughly 1 h per XRD pattern. Such a long collection 
time was chosen intentionally to maximise precision in tracking the evolution of the chemistry 
inside the cell from the beginning to the end of each pattern. However, it is known that the 
slower the cycling rate, the more active the polysulphide shuttle, which is nicely illustrated by 
the operando Raman spectroscopy study3. This simple relationship seems to be not entirely

even at a low applied current, still higher than 80%. Regarding the amount of electrolyte 
used, although our electrolyte/sulphur ratio is lower than that used for the operando Raman

coating of the sulphur particles and/or advanced engineering including the confinement of 
sulphur in various carbon matrices. We do not expect that the simple mixture of as-received 
sulphur, conductive carbon and polymeric binder have an impact on the electrochemistry of 
the cell, at least for “short-term” cycling, that is, for about 50-100 cycles.
