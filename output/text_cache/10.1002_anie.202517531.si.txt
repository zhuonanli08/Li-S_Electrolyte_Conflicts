Experimental section
Materials
Dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), tetrahydrofuran (THF), 1,2-diethoxyethane (DEE), butyl methyl ether (BME), diethyl carbonate (DEC), dimethyl carbonate (DMC), lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), and lithium nitrate (LiNO3) were purchased commercially. All solvents used in this work were dried with 4 Å molecular sieves for two days in the Ar-filled glovebox at 25 °C before use. The ‘M’ in this work means molar salt in liters of solvent. The sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) was synthesized through the thermal treatment of the mixture of polyacrylonitrile and sulfur (mass ratio: 1:4) at 450 °C.[1-2] The synthesized SPAN was characterized by elemental analysis, which determined a sulfur content of approximately 36.3 wt.%. The SPAN electrode was prepared by casting the slurry consisting of SPAN, Ketjenblack, poly (acrylic acid) (PAA), and guar gum at a weight ratio of 8:1:0.5:0.5 on carbon-coated aluminum foil.
Material characterization
The dielectric constant of solvents used in this work was referred to published works.[3-5] The 7Li NMR (using 0.5 M LiCl in DMSO-d6 as deuterated reagent) and 23Na (using 50 mM NaFSI in DMSO-d6 as deuterated reagent) spectra of the electrolytes under ambient conditions were collected on a Bruker AVANCE III 400 MHz instrument. Raman spectroscopy was conducted with HORIBA XploRA PLUS with 785 nm excitation wavelength. Ionic conductivity measurements of various electrolytes were tested with a Swagelok cell in a thermostatic chamber. The use of focused ion beam scanning electron microscopy (FIB-SEM, CARL ZEISS Crossbeam340) allowed for detailed information on the surface and cross-sectional of the deposition Li layer. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted by Thermo Scientific ESCALAB 250Xi and Thermo Scientific K-Alpha. All peaks were charge referenced using the C 1s binding energy of (284.8 eV). The cathode electrolyte interphase was observed by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM, Talos F200X G2). The homogeneity and mechanical stability of interphase were investigated through atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker icon). Synchrotron-based wide-angle X-ray scattering (WAXS) measurements were conducted at beamline BL02U2 of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). Monochromatic X-rays (10.75 keV, 1.1534 Å) were focused to a 160 × 80 μm2 beam spot using Kirkpatrick-Baez mirrors. Liquid samples were loaded into 1.0 mm inner diameter quartz capillaries (Hampton), hermetically sealed, and mounted in the sample stage. Scattering patterns were acquired using a Pilatus 2M detector (1475 × 1479 pixels; 172 × 172 μm² pixel size) positioned at 183.9 mm sample-to-detector distance, with 10 s exposure time per measurement. Two-dimensional diffraction images were azimuthally integrated using Fit2D[6] software (v18.002) to produce intensity-vs-wavevector profiles (I vs Q). A Li||SPAN quartz cell was employed for the in-situ UV-vis spectroscopy measurements (Agilent Technologies, Cary 60).
Electrochemical measurement
The 2032-type coin cells were used for the electrochemical characterization under ambient conditions. All coin cells were assembled in the Ar-filled glovebox (O2 and H2O < 0.1 ppm). The reduction potential testing was based on Li||Cu cells using a Solartron 1470E CellTest workstation at a scan rate of 1 mV s-1. EIS of cycled Li||SPAN cells were tested in different electrolytes using a Solartron 1470E CellTest workstation under ambient conditions. The in situ EIS was conducted using a Solartron 1470E CellTest workstation with a repetitive frequency range of 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz and an amplitude of 10 mV. A regular interval of 0.5 h during discharging/charging processes was used in the repetitive in situ EIS. Finally, the distribution of relaxation times (DRT) analyses was derived using DRT tools (the MATLAB GUI toolbox for computing DRT).
Li||SPAN half cells were prepared with 12 mm diameter Li foil (450 μm), 40 μL electrolyte, 16 mm diameter separator, and 10 mm cathode (1.3 mg cm-2). The long-term stability of Li||Cu and Li||SPAN cells was tested by the LAND instruments. The Li||SPAN full cells were composed of 50 μm thick Li foil (12 mm), 16 mm diameter separator, and 10 mm diameter SPAN cathode (6.1 mg cm-2). The areal loading of SPAN cathode was calculated based on the first discharge cycle. The full cells were performed between 1.0 V and 3.0 V with 0.1 C current density (1 C = 650 mA g-1). Pouch cells were assembled with a multilayered stacking configuration in a dry room at a dew point of -60 °C.
Theoretical calculation
DFT calculations were carried out with ORCA 5.0.3.[7-8] The structure optimization and frequency analysis were conducted at the level of B3LYP-D3 def2-TZVP(-f).[9-12] The binding energies were calculated under the level of B3LYP-D3 def2-TZVP.[9-11, 13] Energy calculations used for ESP analysis were performed using WB97M-V def2-TZVP/J.[14-15]

The free energy of various species was calculated with the assistance of the Shermo program.[18] Energy calculations used for reduction potential were performed using WB97X-2 D3 aug-cc-pVTZ.[9-10, 19-21]
The MD simulations were conducted by using the GROMACS (2020.6) package with the AMBER99SB-ILDN force field.[22] The MD parameters for Li+ were in the built-in force field parameters. The MD parameters for FSI-, NO3-, DME, THF, and DEE were generated by Sobtop[23], and the corresponding atom charges were based on restrained electrostatic potential (RESP2)[24], which were generated by the Multiwfn[25-26] program based on electrostatic potential charges. Partial charges for charged ions were scaled by 0.7 to adjust for electronic screening to enhance the accuracy of interionic interaction predictions when a non-polarizable force field is used. The PACKMOL[27] was utilized to construct a simulated electrolyte box. First, the energy minimization was conducted by using the steepest descent algorithm. Then, the simulations were equilibrated for 20 ns using the NPT ensemble (isobaric-isothermal ensemble). The specific simulation steps are as follows: the electrolyte boxes were heated up from 0 to 100, 200, and 298.15 K within 6 ns, and equilibrated at 298.15 K for another 14 ns. Finally, a 10 ns production run was performed at 298.15 K and 1 bar to observe the evolution of the system. The RDFs and CN results were extracted from the final 10 ns of the production run. The VMD software was used to visualize the solvation structures.[28]
