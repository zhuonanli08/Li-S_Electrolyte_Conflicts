Experimental Section/Methods
Materials: The electrolyte solvents and lithium salt, such as lithium bisfluorosulfonimide (LiFSI), lithium bis((trifluoromethyl)sulfonyl)azanide (LiTFSI), lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF6), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) and carboxymethylcellulose sodium (CMC) were obtained from Suzhuo Dodochem Ltd., China, without any additional pretreatment. The micron silicon (~1 µm) and polyacrylic acid (PAA) were purchased from Aladdin. The Super P came from TIMCAL. The polyacrylonitrile (PAN) (Mw = 150 k) and sulfur were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and Sinopharm Group Co., Ltd., respectively. In the figure legends, the LiFSI-based electrolyte is abbreviated as LiFSI, the LiTFSI-based electrolyte as LiTFSI, and the LiPF6-based electrolyte as LiPF6.
SPAN preparation: The sulfur (S) powder and polyacrylonitrile (PAN) powder were mixed uniformly at a mass ratio of 1.6:1 by thorough grinding, followed by loading into a quartz tube. After evacuation and sealing, the mixture was heated in a muffle furnace at 350 °C for 12 h to obtain the SPAN composite, with a sulfur content of 49.4 wt.%.
Preparation of electrodes: The SPAN cathodes were prepared by mixing SPAN powder, Super P, and CMC with a mass ratio of 7:2:1 to form a slurry. Then the slurry was coated on the aluminum foil to dry at 55 ℃ for 12 h under vacuum. Unless otherwise noted, all SPAN cathodes used for cycling measurements had a sulfur areal loading of 1.5 mgS cm⁻². For the micron silicon electrode, mixing micron silicon powder, PAA, and Super P with a mass ratio of 70:15:15 to form a slurry, which was coated on the copper foil to dry at 100 ℃ for 12 h under vacuum. The micron silicon loading was about 1 mg cm-2.
Electrochemical measurements: The electrochemical performance was tested by CR2016-type and CR2032-type coin cells on the Neware battery testing systems. The electrolyte volume of each coin cell was about 60 µL or 100 μL, and all cells were assembled in an argon-filled glove box. In the Li/SPAN coin cells assembled in this work, lithium metal with a thickness of 100 μm was used as the anode, corresponding to an N/P ratio of 8.2. The N/P ratios and related parameters of the pouch cells are summarized in Tables S3-S4. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was evaluated on a VMP3 potentiostat/galvanostat station (Bio-logic Science Instruments) in the frequency range of 100 kHz to 10 mHz with a voltage of 5 mV amplitude. The Bruce-Vincent-Evans (BVE) technique was used to calculate the Li+ number with an applied voltage of 10 mV. The Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was also carried out on the VMP3.
Characterizations: X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected using a Rigaku SmartLab with filtered Cu Kα radiation (Rigaku D/max-2500, λ = 1.504 Å) and a high power of 9 kW. The elemental contents of C, H, N, and S in the materials were determined using an elemental analyzer based on Elementar UNICUBE. Element analyzer (EA) was carried out on UNICUBE, it was used for quantitative analysis of C, H, N and S elements in SPAN. The pair distribution function (PDF) measurements were carried out at the Rigaku SmartLab with filtered Ag Kα radiation and a high power of 6 kW. For PDF characterizations, powder samples were loaded into glass capillaries and sealed with clay. Raman spectra of the electrolytes and solvents were recorded with an excitation laser wavelength of 532 nm using the DXR 532 nm HP LASER (Thermo Fisher, USA) at room tem perature. 7Li NMR of the electrolytes were performed on a Bruker Avance III HD 600 MHz NMR to identify the ion binding environment. The chemical shifts of 7Li NMR are referenced to 0.1 M LiCl in D2O. Each electrolyte was injected into a capillary tube, which was sealed and inserted into an NMR tube containing an external standard solution inside an argon glovebox. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analyses were performed with the ECSALAB 250Xi high-performance electron spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Company), referenced to the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was carried out using the FEI Zeiss 460 scanning electron microscope. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was applied to reveal the CEI layers on a JEM-2100 plus. Atomic-resolution Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS) mapping was performed using the Super-X system integrated into the Talos F200X G2. The Thermo Scientific Velox software was used to correct for sample drift in real-time by cross-correlating consecutive frames, resulting in high signal-to-noise ratio elemental maps with atomic precision.
Theoretical calculation: In this work, the microscopic solvation structure of electrolytes was simulated by the MD simulations via the GROMACS simulation package (version 2024.2)[1-3] with OPLS-AA force field[4]. The parameters and atomic charges of Li+ as reported by Soetens et al were used[5], while the parameters of anions obtained from research of Doherty et al[6]. Moreover, LigParGen program was employed to construct the parameters of solvents based on the wave function derived from quantum chemical calculations at B3LYP/ma-TZVP level. 150 Li+, 150 anions, 360 DME and 518 FEC were randomly inserted into 6×6×6 nm box as the initial model. All simulations used a MD timestep of 2 fs. Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) algorithm was used to compute the short-range electrostatics in 2.0 nm, while the cut-off for van der Waals interactions was set as 2.0 nm. In a typical simulation, the system was initially equilibrated in the NPT ensemble at 298.15 K and 1.01325 bar for 5ns, after the energy minimization step. And then simulation in the NVT ensemble at 298.15 K was carried out for 5ns. Finally, the production runs at 298.15 K for 20ns was performed as the end. Subsequent statistics and analysis were all completed in GROMACS.
HOMO and LUMO energy levels and Li+-anion binding energies (Ebinding) were calculated by the Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculation. It was conducted in the DMol3 module in Material Studio software within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) formulation. Binding energies between Li+ and the anion molecules were calculated from the formula:
Ebinding = Ecomplex - Eanion - ELi+
Ecomplex, Eanion, and ELi+ are the total energy of the Li+-anion complex, the anion, and Li+ ion, respectively.
