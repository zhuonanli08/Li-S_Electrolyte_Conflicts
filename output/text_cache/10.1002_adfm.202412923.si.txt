Experimental Section 
Electrode and battery preparation: 
The cathode material slurry was prepared by mixing sulfur/carbon nanotube(CNT-S) composite, acetylene black and polyvinylende fluoride (PVDF) in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) with mass ratio of 8:1:1. Subsequently, the slurry was coated onto aluminum foil and placed in an oven to dry at 60 ℃ for 12h. After the aluminum foil coated with CNT-S was punched into 14 mm diameter electrode sheets, the CNT-S electrodes with an average area sulfur loadings of 1.2~1.4 mg cm-2 were obtained. The CR-2025 coin cell was assembled in an Ar-filled glove box with CNT-S aluminum foil as the cathode, lithium metal foil as the anode, and Celgard 2400 as the separator. The LMA with 70 μm was processed by physically suppression of the commercial LMA with 500 μm, and the thicknedd was determined using micrometer. 
Preparation of electrolytes:  
The baseline electrolyte consists of 1 M lithium bis(trifluorome-thanesulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI) in 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME)/1,3-dioxolane (DOL) (v/v=1:1), 2.0 wt% LiNO3. Different dosage of potassium-3-thiophene trifluoroborate (KPTB) (Aladdin, 98%) were added to the baseline electrolyte and stirred for 24 h to obtain 0.01 M, 0.02 M and 0.03 M KPTB electrolyte. The above operations are done in a glove box filled with sperm, oxygen and water content is controlled below 0.01 ppm.
The Li2S deposition experiment: 
The experiment uses carbon paper as the cathode and aluminum foil as the anode to assemble the cell. In the cell assembly process, 20 𝜇L electrolyte with a concentration of 0.2 M Li2S6 was added on the carbon paper side, and 20 𝜇L electrolyte with/without KPTB was dropped on the Li foil side. The cells were first galvanostatically discharged at 0.05 mA to 2.06 V and then discharged with the constant voltage at 2.05 V until the current was below 10−5 A for the nucleation and deposition of Li2S. Then, the current distribution is evaluated by Faraday's law to estimate the precipitation amount of Li2S.
Structure characterizations:
The microstructures were detected by field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7001F) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-F200 microscope with an accelerating voltage of 200 kV). Raman spectra of the samples were measured on a HR Evolution LabRAM using 532 nm laser source. Fourier-transformed infrared (FT-IR) transmission spectroscopy was carried out in a Bruker Equinox 55 spectrometer between 4000 and 400 cm−1. XPS measurements were performed on an AXIS ULTRA spectrometer (Kratos), using nonmonochromatic Al Kα X-ray as the excitation source and choosing C 1s (284.8 eV) as the reference line.
Electrochemical measurements: 
Galvanostatic discharge/charge measurements were performed using a LAND CT3001A battery tester and neware-BTS-8.0.0-20220606Ch within the potential window of 1.7 and 2.8 V. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) test was measured on the CHI 660E electrochemical workstation at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s-1 between 1.7 and 2.8 V (vs. Li/Li+). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were measued on the CHI 660E electrochemical workstation with a frequency range of 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz and a voltage amplitude of 5 mV. 
Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was performed on symmetric Li||Li cells to measure the exchange current density. The symmetric Li||Li cells are required to be tested after 24h or more. A fixed sweep rate of 1.0 mV s-1 was employed within a voltage range from -200 mV to 200 mV. 
For the Li2S6 symmetric cells, the preparation process of the cathode material is the same as that of the Li-S battery, except that the CNT-S composite material is replaced by CNT. The Li2S6 symmetrical cells was assembled by adding 40 𝜇L 0.2M Li2S6 solution as electrolyte with sulfur-free CNT as cathode. Another 40 𝜇L electrolytic with/without KPTB were added to the separator. CV curves of symmetric cells were tested at a scan rate of 10 mV s-1 in the range of -1.0 to 1.0 V.

Theoretical calculation details
Analyses of free energies, binding energies and reaction energies were carried out with DFT by using Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP).[2] The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional of generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was used to describe electron-electron exchange correlations.[3-4] The projector augmented wave (PAW) potential was used to describe the electron-ion interactions. 550 eV was chosen as the cutoff energy of the plane-wave basis set. The convergence criterion of energy was set to be 10−5 eV, and that of force was set to be 0.01 eV Å-1. A hexagonal 6 × 6 × 1 supercell with a vacuum slab of 20 Å in the vertical (z) direction was constructed to model the graphene substrate. A Gamma-centered 3 × 3 × 1 k-point was sampled for the Brillouin zone integration. Van der Waals corrections were considered in this work.
