Figure S6: Raman spectra of 2 M LiTFSI in HME, 2 M LiTFSI in HME/DOL (1:1, by volume) and 2 M LiTFSI in HME/DME (1:1, by volume).
The signals in the Raman spectra are assigned to the specific TFSI- anion interaction with Li+.  At frequencies between 739 – 742 cm-1 the Raman band is assigned to uncoordinated TFSI-, thus, reflecting a solvent separated Li+-TFSI- ion pair (SSIP). At higher frequencies (745 cm-1 and 755 cm-1) an interaction of the TFSI- anion with Li+ is correlated due to the formation of contact ion pairs (CIP). The pristine electrolyte with 2 M LiTFSI in HME and 2 M LiTFSI in HME/DOL (1:1, by volume) show Raman bands in the higher frequency region. Thus, the formation of CIPs is confirmed for both electrolytes. In contrast, by adding DME (2 M LiTFSI in HME/DME), the Raman band is shifted to slightly lower frequencies. The low frequency shift is attributed to the stronger solvation ability of DME driving the formation of SSIP.


Figure S7: Calculation of the cut-off voltage in the symmetrical S-Li2S cell in HME based electrolyte. The cut-off voltage is calculated based on the intial states of the electrodes. The pristine sulfur cathode has a potential of 2.6 V vs. Li/Li+ whereas the pre-lihtiated electrode has a potential of 1.5 V vs. Li/Li+ in the initial state. The initial sulfur and the pre-lithiated electrode are combined for the symmetrical S-Li2S cell, hence, the new voltage range is calculated as the difference between the electrode potentials of the pristine sulfur and the pre-lithiated electrode to give a cut-off voltage of 1.1 V. After one discharge (lihtiation of pristine sulfur electrode and silmutaneous delithiation of the pre-lithiated electrode) the electrode potentials are reversed, thus, the new cut-off is -1.1 V. The acutal cycling of the symmetrical S-Li2S cell is accomplished between 1 V and -1 V to limit side reactions. The resulting calculated voltage profile for the symmetrical S-Li2S cell in 2 M LiTFSI in HME/DOL electrolyte is shown in black between 1 V and -1 V.

Figure S8: Example for the electrode potential change during discharge of the symmetrical S-Li2S cell in 2 M LiTFSI in HME/DOL when the symmetrical cell is limited to the pre-lithiated capacity. 

The pristine sulfur electrode provides a sulfur reservoir, hence, when pre-lithiated electrode is oxidized (red graph) the sulfur reduction is nonetheless limited to a capacity of 1.7 mAh/cm². Thus, reduction stops in the range of the second plateau corresponding to intermediate polysulfide species. If one of the electrode potentials shows a steep voltage increase or decrease, the resulting voltage in the symmetrical S-Li2S cell shows a descending or ascending voltage, respectively. However, the voltage monitored in the symmetrical S-Li2S cell can stay on a certain level, e. g., at ca. ±0.2 V without reaching the cut-off voltage. Hence, neither of the electrode reactions proceeds to the final state and the S-Li2S cell reaction stops at an intermediate state, as indicated by the arrow. Two reasons may cause the voltage levelling at ca. ±0.2 V. First, as already mentioned, neither of the electrode reactions proceeds to the final state which is most probably caused by unbalanced electrode capacities and, thus, unadapted charge/discharge limitation factors, e. g., time or capacity. Second, levelling of the symmetrical charge/discharge voltage can be a result of polysulfide shuttle. The existence of diffusing and shuttling polysulfides cause the monitoring of corresponding constant electrode potentials of 2.3 V. This eventually leads to a voltage plateau close to 0 V in the symmetrical cell.
