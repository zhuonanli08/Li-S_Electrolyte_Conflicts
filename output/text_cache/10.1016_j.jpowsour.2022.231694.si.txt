Experimental section
Preparation of THE-based electrolytes. The LiTFSI was dried under vacuum at 60 °C for 12 h. A mixed solvent of DME/DOL (v/v=1:1) was prepared. THE-based electrolytes were composed of 1 M LiTFSI dissolved in THE/DOL/DME. More specifically, 1 M LiTFSI was dissolved in a mixture of DOL/DME (v/v=1:1) and THE with different volume ratios of 20%, 40%, 50%, 60%, and 80% (THE, vt.%).
Preparation of S@C composite cathode. Sulfur and Ketjenblack powders were mixed at a mass ratio of 8:2, and heat-treated at 155 °C for 12 h. Using deionized water as a dispersion medium, Ketjenblack-sulfur powders were obtained. The SEM and Raman tests of Ketjenblack-sulfur composite are shown in Figure S13.
Preparation of Li2S8. In an Ar-fulled glovebox, 800 mg sulfur and 230 mg Li2S powders were dissolved in 10 mL DME, and stirred for 24 h at 60 °C until the solid was completely dissolved to form a brownish-red color.
Materials characterizations. The morphology of Li anodes after cycling in various electrolytes was characterized with SEM measurements (Zeiss Supra, Germany) and 3D-AFM (Dimension Fastscan, Germany). The contact angle of the electrolytes on cathode and Li2S was tested by an automatic contact angle measuring instrument (ZhongChen, JC2000D3M, China). The electrodes after cycling were studied by XPS (Thermo-Fisher, ESCALAB 250Xi, U.S.A.). The solubility of Li2S8 in different electrolytes was tested by ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectroscopy measurements (HITACHI, U-3900H, Japan). Raman spectra were taken with Raman spectroscopy (Renishaw inVia, Renishaw, England). The concentration of sulfur ion was tested with ICP (ICPMS, PerkinElmer, U.S.A.).
Electrochemical measurements. For the fabrication of electrodes, S@C composite, conductive carbon, and binder (PVDF in N-methyl pyrrolidone (NMP)) were mixed with a weight ratio of 8:1:1. The cathode slurry was coated on aluminum foil and dried at 60 ºC for 12 h. The resultant electrodes were cut into a circular sheet of 14 mm in diameter. The loading mass of the regular sulfur electrodes was about 1.33-1.57 mg cm−2. The loading mass of the high-loading sulfur electrodes was 4.86 mg cm-2. The Li-S cells were tested in 2032 coin-type cells. The amount of electrolyte used was 45 μL. The separator was Celgard 2500 with the diameter of 16 mm. Galvanostatic measurements were performed on the battery testing system (Neware, CT-3008), within the voltage range of 1.0-3.0 V (vs Li+/Li) for Li-S cells. CV and EIS measurements were carried out with a Princeton VersaSTAT electrochemical workstation. The CV analysis of Li-S batteries was conducted in a voltage range of 1.5-3.0 V at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s-1. EIS experiments were studied at a frequency of 10 mV in a frequency range from 1000 kHz to 10 mHz. The self-discharge efficiency was represented by ∆R, ∆R=C10th-C11th/C10th.

Shuttle currents tests. For the measurement of shuttle currents, the cells were firstly cycled three times with a small current density of 1 mA cm−2 to activate sulfur electrodes. Then, the voltage of the cells was charged to 2.7 V and remained at an open-circuit state for 5 min. The cells were switched to a potentiostatic mode to offset the drop voltage at different stages (2.7, 2.6, 2.5, 2.4, 2.3, and 2.2 V) for 1 hour before reaching a steady-state value. The steady-state current was the shuttle current as the voltage would continue to drop if there was no external current to compromise the shuttle effect.
In-Situ Raman spectroscopy analysis. In-situ micro-Raman analysis was worked out by a Renishaw InVia Raman microscope with a 532 nm laser. The laser passed through a 5 mm hole on the negative case and Li metal to irradiate the electrolyte to obtain the Raman signal. The battery was assembled in a DELLIX glove box and all operations were performed with water and oxygen content below 0.1 ppm. After the ageing, the Li/NMC811 battery was performed for galvanostatic measurements at 0.2 C in the voltage range of 2.6-1.2 V.
