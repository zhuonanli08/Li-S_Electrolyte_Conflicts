Methods
Working principle of SFG spectroscopy. SFG is a second-order nonlinear process that is used to probe molecules at the interface between two media. The technique involves the overlap of two pulsed laser beams at the surface, generating a third beam with a frequency that is the sum of the two input frequencies. The SFG technique possesses inherent interface selectivity owing to the fact that even order susceptibilities become zero in centrosymmetric media under electric dipole approximation. The SFG intensity from an interface region is proportional to the incident laser intensities and the square of the absolute value of the effective sum frequency susceptibility .

In the present work, SFG spectra are recorded with ssp polarization combination. The experimental SFG spectra are fitted to Equation S3, where B is normalized to unity for simplification, and C is limited to a small value to account for the drift of baseline. The integrated intensity of each peak is proportional to the square of the corresponding oscillator strength Aq. To calculate the value of (IE/IS)0.5 defined in the main text, the oscillator strength of the electrolyte peak ~1402 cm-1 and that of the solvent peak ~1440 cm-1 are derived from the fitting results, respectively.
Our picosecond sum frequency generation spectrometer laser system was built by EKSPLA, operating at 50 Hz with a copropagating configuration. The pulse width is ~25 ps. The visible wavelength is fixed at 532 nm and the infrared (IR) pulse is tunable in the range of 1000-4300 cm-1. The incident angle is 60° for the visible beam, and 55° for the IR beam. The SFG signal is collected around 59.5° at the reflection geometry, within a small range depending on the IR wavenumber (2800-3000 cm-1 in the experiment). The spectrum is recorded with 2 cm-1 increment per scan, averaged over 200 laser pulses per point. The energy of the visible beam is typically less than 100 mJ and that of IR beam less than 200 mJ and no obvious photodamage or heat effect is observed throughout the study. The polarization combination is typically ssp during the experiment, which represents s-polarized sum-frequency signal generated by the incidence of s-polarized visible pulse and p-polarized IR pulse. For each spectrum, the SFG intensity is calibrated by the surface resonance signal of z-cut quartz. On the other hand, we rely on the internal standard (i.e. the solvent) for comparing the SFG spectral intensity between different samples.
To allow a long working distance and less-disturbed polarization, the lens used for focusing the incident laser beams are of low Numerical Aperture (NA), and the resulting beam diameter at the focusing points is several mm for the IR beam, and even larger for the visible beam. During the measurement process, the probing lights are always fixed at the relatively smooth position without any holes, thus the holes do not influence our results.
Density functional theory calculation methods
First-principles calculations are performed using density functional theory (DFT), as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP). Core-valence interactions are characterized by projector-augmented wave approach and Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional respectively.1 To simulate a nitrogen-doped graphene, a 9 × 9 × 1 supercell of graphene is used, and a 20 \AA vacuum layer is inserted between slabs. A 400 eV plane wave cutoff was selected, and a gamma centered 1 × 1 × 1 k mesh in Brillouin zone sampling in relaxation. For static calculation, a denser k mesh of 5 × 5 × 1 is used. The binding energy of molecules on this substrate is defined as , where Es, Em, and Et are total energies of the substrate, molecule and the whole adsorption system, respectively. For a positive , the adsorption configuration is energetically favorable. 
To qualitatively explain the blue shift of S=O SFG peak, we stretch the S=O bond by 0.1 \AA and calculate the energy difference between the stretched structure and stable structure. It is observed that adsorbed on nitrogen-doped graphene, this energy difference increased by 0.068 eV, which is consistent with the blue shift.
DFT was adopted for all calculations to perform a detailed analysis of the solvation behavior in electrolytes. Unless mentioned, the quantum chemistry program package ORCA was utilized for the calculations. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional was used for the geometrical optimization of clusters,2 with Grimme’s dispersion correction with Becke-Johnson damping (namely, DFT-D3(BJ)).3, 4 The ma-def2-TZVPP basis set was chosen for expanding the electronic wavefunctions. In addition, the resolution of identity (RI) technique was activated for higher computational efficiency. Considering the electrolyte was a mixture of DOL and DME, a conductor-like polarizable continuum model (CPCM) was applied for assessing the solvent effect, with the DME parameter (the dielectric constant was set as 7.55) since DME was more easily coordinated with the lithium cation than DOL. The keywords “tightscf” and “tightopt” were activated to guarantee a tight convergence for both the electronic self-consistent calculation and the geometrical optimization. The harmonic frequency calculation was also performed for optimized structures. For a few structures, very small imaginary frequencies were identified and were deemed as the numerical imperfection caused by the implicit solvation model.5
To obtain the solvation free energy of the lithium cation accurately, a mixed cluster/continuum model was adopted. In this model, a few solvent molecules around the lithium cation were considered explicitly and the implicit solvation model was considered to simulate the solution phase. In order to obtain a more accurate description of the Gibbs free energy, the double-hybrid functional B2PLYP with D3(BJ) correction was used to calculate the single point energy of the optimized structure in vacuum, with the ma-def2-TZVPP basis set. To analyze the non-covalent interaction with an intuitionistic expression, the reduced density gradient (RDG),8 with the definition as  , was calculated for the optimized structure. The MultiWFN 3.8 (dev)9 and VMD 1.9.3 program10 packages were used for analyzing and visualizing the RDG, respectively.
To obtain a more quantitative understanding of the nature of interaction in these complexes, the energy decomposition analysis (EDA) was performed for these optimized structures including the lithium cation, with the ADF 2016 package. In this scheme, the interaction energy between the lithium cation and its surrounding molecules, denoted as ΔEint, could be divided into four components, i.e., the electrostatic interaction (ΔVelst), the orbital interaction energy (ΔEoi), the Pauli repulsion (ΔEPauli), and the dispersion energy (ΔEdisp). The B3LYP hybrid functional with the D3(BJ) correction and the slater-type TZ2P basis set were adopted, and the solvation environment was not included.
