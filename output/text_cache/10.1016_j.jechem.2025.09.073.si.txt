Supporting Information for
Heteroatom-Tuned Weakly Solvating Electrolyte with Good Wettability Enriches Solid Electrolyte Interphase Chemistry toward Stable Lithium Metal Batteries
1. Experimental section
Materials: Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), ethylene carbonate (EC), and ethyl methylene carbonate (EMC) were purchased from Suzhou Dodo Chemical Technology Co., Ltd. 1,3-Dioxolane (DOL), 1,4-Dioxane (1,4-DX), and 1,4-Thioxane (OTA) were purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (Shanghai) Development Co., Ltd. Lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) cathode materials were provided by Narada Power Source Co., Ltd. Li foils (> 99.9%) with different thicknesses (300, 50 and 20 μm) were purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. These chemicals were kept and electrolyte preparation was carried out in an Ar-filled glove box with the concentrations of moisture and oxygen below 0.01 ppm.
Preparation for electrolytes and electrodes: All solvents were dried and purified with a 4Å molecular sieve (Sigma-Aldrich) before use. Molarity (M, moles of salt in liters of solution, mol L-1) was used to denote the salt concentration in electrolytes. To verify the dissolution of LiFSI in the different solvents, different concentrations of 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 M LiFSI were dissolved in the DOL, 1,4-DX and OTA solvents, respectively. Thereinto, the electrolyte with 2.0 M LiFSI dissolving in the OTA solvent was used as the test sample, labeled as “2.0 M LiFSI-OTA”. Further, the carbonate electrolyte was selected as the baseline, which indexed 2.0 M LiFSI dissolving in the mixture of EC and EMC with a volume ratio of 3:7 and was remarked as “2.0 M LiFSI-EC-EMC”. 
For electrode preparation in the CR2025 coin half-cell, the slurry was prepared by mixing LiFePO4, acetylene black and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) with a mass ratio of 8:1:1. The slurry was casted on Al foil and dried at 120 ℃, then cut into the disks with a diameter of 12 mm. The disk would serve as the cathode for battery, and its mass loading was ~5.18 mg cm-2. In CR2025-type coin full cells, the LiFePO4 electrodes contained 96.32% active materials and the cathode loadings were controlled at approximately 11.43 mg cm−2 and 18.35 mg cm−2, respectively, to regulate the different negative-to-positive ratios. In the pouch battery, the commercially available LiFePO4 electrodes with a length of 5.5 cm and a width of 4.8 cm contained 97.1% active materials, and the mass loading was 19.73 mg cm-2.
Physical characterizations: Before characterization, all the cycled electrodes were rinsed three times with dimethyl carbonate (DMC) solvent to remove residual lithium salts, dried in the high-purity Ar-filled glove box and sealed using a sealing device until testing. The Li+ solvation structure was characterized by confocal Raman spectrometer (WITec Alpha300 R, Germany). 7Li-NMR spectra were performed on a nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometer (AVANCE III HD 500 MHz NMR, Bruker) at 25 ℃. The morphology of Li deposition under different electrochemical testing conditions were monitored by scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI Nova-Nano 450, America). The chemical bonds of the electrodes before and after cycling were verified by in-depth X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB Xi+, America) using Al Kα (hυ = 1486.6 eV). All XPS spectra were aligned to the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV. The ion distribution of samples was conducted by time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS, PHI nano ToF II, Japan). 
Electrochemical measurements: CR2025 coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glovebox with Celgard 2400 (25 μm, PP/PE/PP) as a separator. And each cell was injected into 60 μL electrolyte. In the Li||Li and Li||Cu cells, Li foils with 300 μm thickness were applied as the reference and counter electrode, while Li disk with 12 mm diameter or Cu foil with 16 mm diameter was as a working electrolyte, respectively. For full battery, Li foil with a thickness of 50 μm used as anode and then paired with different capacities of LiFePO4 cathode to control the N/P ratio. Both the charge/discharge cycling and Li plating/stripping coulombic efficiency (CE) were carried out on the Neware battery tester (BTS-5V10mA, China) at room temperature. The Li||Li symmetric were cycled at a current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 with 1.0 mAh cm-2 dynamic Li capacity. And Li||Cu cells were assembled to evaluate CE for Li deposition/stripping, with a desirable Li capacity for plating and stripping back by a cut-off voltage of 1.0 V (vs. Li/Li+) after 10 cycles for formation between 0 and 1 V (vs. Li/Li+). The Li||LiFePO4 half cells were operated at 0.1 C for 5 cycles before long-term cycling at 1 C within the voltage range of 2.6 ~3.8 V (vs. Li/Li+) (1 C = 170 mA g-1). The Li||LiFePO4 full cells with N/P = 5.3 and N/P = 3.3were operated at 0.1 C for 5 cycles before long-term cycling at 0.7 C and 0.5 C within 2.6-3.8 V (vs. Li/Li+), respectively. The long-term cycling of Li||LiFePO4 pouch cells with N/P=1.15 were operated at 0.2 C for charging and 0.5C for discharging in the voltage range from 2.8 to 3.65 V. (vs. Li/Li+).
Both linear sweep voltammetry (LSV), cyclic voltammetry (CV), and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed on a multi-channel electrochemical workstation (Metrohm PGSTAT-30, Switzerland).

Theoretical calculations: All the classic molecular dynamic simulations conducted in this work were performed using the Large–scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS, http://lammps.sandia.gov). The 2.0 M LiFSI-EC-EMC electrolyte model contains 115 LiFSI, 506 EC and 379 EMC. The 2.0 M LiFSI-OTA electrolyte model contains 115 LiFSI and 844 OTA. OPLS–AA parameters with 1.14*CMIA partial atomic charges were generated by LigParGen for the Organic solvent molecules. The charges and force fields for FSI anions in the electrolytes were taken from previous publications The OPLSAA force field developed by Jorgensen was used for Li+ ions. To get the electrolyte structure, isothermalisobaric (NPT) ensemble runs were first performed at 330 K for 2 ns and then 298 K for 2 ns to ensure the equilibrium salt dissociation. Then, 5 ns canonical (NVT) ensemble simulations were performed, and the last 10 ns (sampled every 0.5 ns) were analyzed using Python codes to count the number of anions and solvent molecules surrounding each Li+ and to calculate the percentages of all solvation structures.
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations for geometry optimization were conducted by Gaussian 16 software package[8]. Geometry optimizations and energy calculations were performed using B3LYP/6–311G(d, p), and the convergence tolerance quality was the default. The solvation effect was considered with the universal solvation model of SMD. Based on the optimized structures, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO), the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level, and corresponding atomic orbital components were analyzed using the Natural Bond Orbitals (NBO) theory.
