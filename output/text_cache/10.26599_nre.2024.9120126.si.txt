Experimental Section
Preparation
of
electrolyte.
The
conventional
electrolyte
was
prepared
by
dissolving
0.8
M
lithium
bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and 0.2 M lithium nitrate (LiNO3) into a mixed binary solvent containing the DOL
and DME with a volume ratio of 1:1, which was denoted as DOL/DME. The DOL/NMP electrolyte was prepared via replacing the
DME with equivalent NMP. As for the DOL/NMP-NH4I electrolyte, the concentration of NH4I additive was controlled to 100
mM.
Solubility test of Li2S8 and Li2S. For the test of Li2S8 solubility, a mixture of S and Li2S with a molar ratio of 7:1 was placed into a
bottle. Note that the mass of Li2S8 was controlled to 20 mmol. 0.20 mL DOL/DME, DOL/NMP or DOL/NMP-NH4I was added
into the bottle each time, which was stirred for 30 mins to enable the full dissolution. Such a procedure was repeated until no
solid residue could be observed. Hence, the solubility of Li2S8 could be calculated based on the initial Li2S8 mass and whole solvent
volume. With regard to the test of Li2S solubility, it followed a similar procedure except that the initial Li2S mass was 0.1 mmol
and solvent volume added each time was 0.1 mL.
Preparation of S/CNT electrode. Firstly, sulfur powder and multiwalled carbon nanotube (CNT) were mixed and ground with a
mass ratio of 3:1. The thus-derived mixture was subject to a thermal treatment with a temperature of 155 ℃for 6 h, which allows
for the complete sulfur infusion. Then, as-obtained S/CNT composite (80 wt%) was mixed with SuperP (10 wt%) and LA133
binder (10 wt%) to form a homogeneous slurry, which was casted onto a piece of aluminum foil and dried at 60 °C for 12 h in a
vacuum oven. Finally, the S/CNT electrodes were achieved by cutting the above materials into circular disks with a diameter of 10
mm prior to the assembly of Li–S batteries. Note that the typical sulfur loading in an electrode was controlled to be ~1.0 mg cm−2.
The high sulfur loading electrode is ~4.0 mg cm−2.
Symmetric cell test. 0.5 M Li2S6 was prepared by dissolving Li2S and S with a mole ratio of 1:5 into the pure DOL/DME,
DOL/NMP, and DOL/NMP-NH4I solvents. Two identical CPs and as-prepared Li2S6 solution were employed as the electrode and
electrolyte, respectively. CV test was operated at scan rates of 3 and 50 mV s–1 with the applied voltage ranging from –0.8 to 0.8 V.
PITT test. 0.5 M Li2S8 was prepared by dissolving Li2S and S with a mole ratio of 1:7 into the three solvents aforementioned. A
piece of CP and Li metal foil were used as the cathode and anode, respectively. 20 µL as-prepared Li2S8 solution was dropped onto

the cathode side. 5 µL routine electrolytes with corresponding solvent were dropped onto the anode side to ameliorate the
polysulfide crossover. The assembled Li | Li2S8 cells were potentiostatically discharged from 2.18 V to 2.0 V with 20.0 mV per
interval to study the Li2S deposition process. The assembled cells were fully discharged to 1.7 V, which were then
potentiostatically charged from 2.2 V to 2.38 V with 20.0 mV per interval to study the Li2S dissociation process.
Shuttle current measurement. Li−S batteries in three different electrolytes were galvanostatically discharged to 2.38 V at 0.2 C,
which were then subject to a potentiostatic test. Note that the potentiostatic current was almost unchangeable at a constant value
after about 105 seconds, which was regarded as the shuttle current.
Characterizations. SEM images were collected by the Quattro. Raman spectra were recorded by the Witec Apyron spectrometer.
Liquid NMR data were collected on the Bruker 600 MHz AVANACIII NMR spectrometer. Solid-state NMR spectra were
recorded on a Bruker Advance III 500 MHz. UV−Vis spectra were collected on the Lambda 950 NIR. FTIR spectra were recorded
on the Nicolet iS spectrometer. EPR spectra were collected on the x-band Bruker EMX PLUS spectrometer. XPS spectra were
recorded on the Kratos Axis Supra photoelectron spectrometer.
Electrochemical tests. CR2032 coin-type cells were employed to evaluate the electrochemical performance of Li–S batteries,
which were assembled using S/CNT composite as the cathode, Li metal as the anode, and Celgard 2500 as the separator in the
three types of aforementioned electrolytes. EIS, CV, and PITT profiles were recorded on the Bio-logic VMP3 electrochemical
workstation. Note that the frequency in the EIS measurement was set in the region of 300,000−0.01 Hz with an amplitude of 10
mV. GCD, GITT and rate/cycling curves were recorded on the Shenzhen Neware battery testing system in the voltage range of
1.7–2.7 V.
In-situ EIS test. Five voltage points (2.4, 2.25, 2.1, 1.95, 1.7 V) during the discharge process and five voltage points (1.8, 1.95, 2.1,
2.25, 2.4 V) during the charge process were selected to carry out the EIS test. Both discharging and charging currents were
controlled to 0.1 mA.
In-situ Raman test. The as-assembled in-situ cells were measured by a GCD test at 0.05 C. In the meanwhile, Raman spectra with
a wavenumber range from 150 to 600 cm–1 were recorded at multiple voltage points (2.3–1.7 V, 0.1 V for each interval during the
discharge process and 2.1–2.4 V, 0.1 V for each interval during the charge process).
DFT calculation.
The Density functional theory (DFT) calculations are carried out using Gaussian 16 suite of programs1 at the M06-2X/6-31+G(d)
level of theory. Frequency calculations were carried out to confirm that the geometries correspond to local minima (no imaginary
frequency) or transition states (one imaginary frequency). The influence of solvent was modeled by using SMD solvation model.2
