Experimental section 
Materials.  
1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3-hexafluoropropane-1, 3-disulfonimide potassium salt (KHFDF), sulfur 
(S), acetylene black (AB), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) were supplied by Aladdin 
and used as received. The Li-S electrolyte containing 1M lithium bis(trifluoromethane 
sulfonimide) 
(LiTFSI) 
in 
a 
mixture 
of 
1,3-dioxolane 
(DOL) 
and 
1, 
2 dimethoxyethane (DME) (1:1 v/v) with 2 wt % lithium nitrate (LiNO3) as additive) 
were commercially purchased from DoDoChem Co., Ltd. Lithium (Li) metal anode (~ 
450 μm) applied for coin cells and ultra-thin Li anode (signal side with 50 μm) 
applied for Li-S pouch cells were commercially purchased from China energy lithium 
Co., Ltd.  
Preparation of DCL electrolyte and assembly of Li-S coin cell and pouch cell. 
In order to compatible with the existing commercial manufacturing techniques, 
for the preparation of DCL electrode, typically, 0.3~0.4 M KHFDF salt was involved 
into the commercially purchased Li-S electrolyte. The DCL electrolyte was uniformly 
stirred before use.  
For preparation of the S cathode, commercial AB and S powder with mass ratio 
of 8:2 were firstly mixed via ball process to form uniform mixture (AB@S), and then 
the AB@S mixture was heated under Ar atmosphere at 155 ℃ for 12 h to allow the S 
uniformly coating on the surface of AB. The slurry of S cathode was prepared via 
uniformly stirred the AB@S mixture, AB and PVDF with mass ratio of 8:1:1 in NMP 
solvent for 24 h under Ar atmosphere protection. The S cathode then was uniformly 

<<PAGE 4>>
casted on the surface of aluminum foil. For pouch cell tests, the slurry was 
double-coated. The average loading of S for coin cell tests is ~ 1 mg cm−2, and for 
pouch cell tests is ~ 4 mg cm−2 (single side).  
For cell assembly, the coin-type Li-S cells were assembled under an argon filled 
glove (H2O and O2<0.1 ppm) with same methods as discussed with our previously 
reports.[1][2] The pouch cells were assembled under dry room with relative humidity of 
~1.6 %. For the pouch cell, typically, the two pieces of cathode electrodes with four 
sides surrounded by lithium metal is adopted via battery winding technology. The 
sulfur loading for pouch cell is ~500 mg/cell and PP separator is employed to separate 
the positive and negative electrodes. The ratio of electrolyte volume to sulfur loading 
is 6 for the long cycle of Li-S pouch cells. Before packaged, the Li-S pouch cell is 
placed in a vacuum of -50 kPa for 2h after injecting the electrolyte, and then the 
battery is packaged by external pressure ~-80 kPa.   
Electrochemical measurement.  
 
The symmetrical Li||Li cells were assembled in the argon filled glove box. The 
coin cells (CR-2032) were used to test the electrochemical performance. The coin 
cells were monitored using a battery cycler on CT2001A cell test instrument (Wuhan 
LAND Electronic Co., Ltd). The Li-S coin or pouch cells was monitored using a 
battery cycler (CT200 A, Wuhan LAND Electronic Co., Ltd). Electrochemical 
impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) were performed on 
CHI660E or CHI604E electrochemical workstation (Shanghai Chenhua instrument 
Co., Ltd).  

<<PAGE 5>>
Characterization.  
 
The morphologies of Li metal and Li deposits were observed via scanning 
electron microscope (SEM) (FEI, NANOSEI 450). High-resolution X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) assembled with an Ar+ sputtering depth profiling 
(ThermoFisher, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha+) was used to detect the information of 
SEI. The vacuum degree of analyzer room is ~ 5×10−9 mbar and source gun type is Al 
K Alpha with energy 1486.6 eV. For the observation of KHFDF molecular altered 
solvation structures of LiTFSI, micro-Raman system (532 nm) was employed with 
focusing the laser spot on the DOL solution.

Figure S1. Schematic illustration of cell architecture. a) Laboratory-scale coin cells 
that ~5 MPa external pressure was applied to ensure the compactness between 
cathode and anode. The applied high pressure is benefit to force the Li nuclei growth 
laterally, thus promoting the uniform Li deposition.[3] b) Commercial pouch cells that 
~80 KPa external pressure was applied between cathode and anode. Lower external 
pressures generally make lithium ions inclined to deposit freely, resulting in more 
severe growth of Li dendrites than coin cells.

Figure S2. Schematic illustration of the formation of solid electrolyte interface (SEI) 
using DCL and blank electrolyte. a) DCL electrolyte that the salt of KHFDF 
decomposes prior to other salts or solvents due to the lowest LOMO level energy. The 
formed KF/LiF inorganics are not only benefiting to uniform Li deposit but also can 
suppress the solvent gaining electrons from Li metal anode. b) Blank electrolyte that 
the formed SEI cannot inhibit the formation of Li dendrites, resulting in the SEI 
repeatedly forms so as to consume the electrolyte.

Figure S7. XPS characterization of O 1s spectrum. a) DCL electrolyte. b) Blank 
electrolyte.  
Obviously, for the DCL electrolyte, the peak representing organic oxygen 
gradually decreases with sputtering depth. In contrast, the characteristic peak of 
inorganic oxygen such as Li2O is strengthened. For blank electrolyte, organic oxygen 
reaches its maximum value at 400 s and remains strong after 600 s, indicating that 
organic components are always mixed in the SEI structure, which is not conducive to 
the inhibition of dendrites.
