Experimental Section
Materials
All reagents and solvents were commercial products and used without further purification. VO (≥ 98%) was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Perylene-3,4,9,10-tetracarboxylic dianhydride (≥ 98%), β-alanine (≥ 98%), imidazole (≥ 99%), and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, 99.5%) were purchased from Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Ethanol (AR), hydrochloric acid (37 wt.%), N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP, 99%), sulfur (99.5%), and lithium sulfide (Li2S, 99.98%) were purchased from Chengdu Colon Chemical Co., Ltd. Electrolyte 1 mol L–1 LiTFSI into a DME/DOL solution with a volume ratio of 1:1 containing 2.0 wt.% LiNO3, tetraglyme, polypropylene separator (PP, Celgard 2500), poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) binder, Super P, carbon paper (CP, TGP-H-060), aluminum foil, and lithium foil were supplied by Guangdong Canrd New Energy Co., Ltd.
Materials characterizations
The morphologies and compositions were probed by atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker Dimension Icon), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS Sigma 300) with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was employed to detect the detailed morphologies and elemental mapping. The Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were obtained by the FTIR spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Nicolet 380) employing a KBr tablet. The ultraviolet-visible (UV–Vis, Shimadzu UV1900) spectra were acquired using a UV–Vis spectrometer (Shimadzu UV1900). Sulfur (641.3 mg, 20.0 mmol) and Li2S (183.8 mg, 4.0 mmol) were dissolved in lithium-sulfur battery electrolyte (20.0 mL) to prepare a 0.2 M Li2S6 solution for UV–Vis spectra tests. The liquid chromatographymass spectrometry (LCMS, Shimadzu LCMS 8040) was used to analyze the VO electrolytes.
Preparation of materials
Preparation of VO homogeneous catalysts 
The VO homogeneous catalysts were prepared in an argon-filled glovebox. The different weights of VO (1.0, 2.0, 4.0, or 10.0 mg) were added into the electrolyte (2.0 g) and stirred to achieve VO mass fractions of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.5 wt.%, respectively. 
Synthesis of PDI supermolecule nanofibers 
The perylene-3,4,9,10-tetracarboxylic dianhydride (3.5 mmol, 1.37 g), β-alanine (28.0 mmol, 2.49 g), and imidazole (264.7 mmol, 18.0 g) were heated at 100.0 ℃ under argon flow for 4 h. The mixture was cooled to room temperature, dispersed in ethanol (100.0 mL), and then treated with hydrochloric acid (2.0 mol, 300.0 mL). After stirring overnight, the resulting red solid was collected by filtration, and washed with distilled water until the pH value of filtrate reached around 7.0. The collected solid was dried in a vacuum oven to obtain bulk PDI. The bulk PDI (1.0 mmol, 0.53 g) was ultrasonically dispersed in water (200.0 mL), and triethylamine (0.85 mL) was added to form a red clarified stock solution. Subsequently, hydrochloric acid (4.0 mol, 27.3 mL) was added to the PDI stock solution to induce the formation of PDI supermolecule nanofibers. The nanofibers were washed with distilled water until a neutral pH was achieved and dried in a vacuum oven. 
Preparation of sulfur/Super P cathodes 
The sulfur and Super P were mixed in a 3:1 mass ratio and heated at 155 °C for 6 h to facilitate sulfur diffusion into Super P. The resulting sulfur/Super P composite (0.9 g) and PVDF binder (0.1 g) were added in NMP and kept in agitation to generate a homogenous slurry. The slurry was uniformly cast onto carbon-coated aluminum foil by a doctor blade method and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 ℃ for 12 h to obtain the sulfur/Super P cathode.
Preparation of PDI-modified PP (PDI@PP) separator 
The PDI and Super P were mixed in an 8:1 mass ratio and thoroughly ground. The composite was then mixed with PVDF binder in a 9:1 mass ratio, uniformly cast onto the PP, and then dried in a vacuum oven to form the PDI@PP separator. The PDI@PP separator was further punched into small disks with a diameter of 19 mm.
Electrochemical measurements
The batteries were assembled in an argon-filled glovebox using sulfur/Super P as the cathode, lithium foil as the anode, and PP or PDI@PP as the separator. The electrolyte petains to 1 mol L–1 LiTFSI into a DME/DOL solution with a volume ratio of 1:1 containing 2.0 wt.% LiNO3. The electrochemical evaluations including charge-discharge curves, cycling, and rate performance were carried out using a NEWARE Battery testing system. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were conducted on a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation in the sweep rate range of 0.10.4 mV s–1 and the voltage window of 1.72.8 V. The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were performed using a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation with a frequency range from 100,000 Hz to 0.01 Hz and an amplitude of 5 mV.
Li2S deposition and decomposition tests
Sulfur (903.4 mg, 28.0 mmol) and Li2S (183.8 mg, 4.0 mmol) were dissolved in tetraglyme solvent to form a solution of Li2S8 (0.2 M). The cells were assembled by using CP as the cathode, lithium foil as the anode, Li2S8 solution (20.0 μL, 0.2 M), and Li2S8 solution (10.0 μL, 0.2 M) with VO solution (10.0 μL, 0.1 wt.%) as the anolyte and catholyte, and PDI@PP or PP served as the separator. The cell was first discharged at a constant voltage of 2.06 V, followed by potentionstatical discharge at 2.05 V until the current dropped below 0.01 mA for the nucleation and deposition of Li2S. For the Li2S decomposition, the cells were initially subjected to galvanostatic discharge at a current of 0.112 mA until the voltage reached 1.7 V. Subsequently, the cells were charged at 2.35 V until the current decreased to 10–5 A.
Theoretical calculations
All calculations were based on the density functional theory (DFT) and performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) code [1,2]. The electron exchange-correlation interaction was parameterized by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [3,4]. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 450 eV was used, and electron spin polarization was included in all calculations. A vacuum space of 20 Å was applied to avoid periodic interactions. The geometric relaxations were performed with convergence criteria of 10⁻⁵ eV for energy and 0.02 eV Å–1 for forces. The van der Waals (vdW) correction with the Grimme approach (DFTD3) was included in the interaction between single molecules/atoms and substrates [5]. The energy barrier of Li2S decomposition and Li atom diffusion was calculated using the climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method [6].
