Experimental Section 
Materials and Methods 
Preparation of electrodes. S/KB cathodes were prepared as described previously[1] with 
different areal sulfur loading of 1.5 mg/cm2 for one-sided coating, 3.0 and 5.0 mg/cm2 for 
double-sided coating. The 1.5 mg/cm2 cathode was cut into a small disc with diameter of 12 
mm to assemble coin cell. The 3.0 and 5.0 mg/cm2 cathodes were cut into 31×54 and 47×102 
mm2 rectangular patch to assemble Li-S pouch cells with targeted capacities.   
Similarly, copper-lithium composite slices (80-μm-thick lithium foils conglutinated on 
two sides of 6-μm-thick Cu foil) as anodes were also prepared as described previously[1]. Two 
copper-lithium composite anodes with different sizes of 33.5 × 57.5 and 49 × 104 mm2 were 
used to assemble pouch cells with 3.0 and 5.0 mg/cm2 cathodes, respectively. 
 
Preparation of electrolytes/solutions. The control electrolyte (Shenzhen Capchem 
Technology Co., Ltd, China) was obtained by dissolving 1 M LiTFSI (Lithium 
bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) 
imide), 
2

LiNO3 
in

working electrolyte for Li-S pouch cells was prepared by adding LiPO2F2 (98%, MACKLIN) 
to the control electrolyte in different proportions, and was denoted as LiPO2F2-electrolyte. In 
addition of LiPO2F2, different lithium salts including lithium bis(oxalate)borate 
(LiBOB, >98%, Adamas), lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF6, >97%, Aladdin), lithium 
perchlorate (LiClO4, 99.99%, Aladdin) and lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI, >98%, 
Aladdin) were also used as electrolyte additives to compare their catalytic effects. 
In radical detection test, 0.05 M Li2S4 and Li2S6 were prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide 
(DMSO, 99%, J&K) (named DMSO-Li2S4 and DMSO-Li2S6) and DOL/DME (named 
DOL/DME-Li2S4 and DOL/DME-Li2S6). Different content of LiPO2F2 was added to the 
above solutions and their denotation was added -X%LiPO2F2 (DMSO-Li2S6-1%LiPO2F2, for 
example, means adding 1% LiPO2F2 in DMSO-Li2S6 solution).

Electrochemical measurements and characterizations. The charge/discharge tests were 
conducted using LAND system for Li-S coin cells and multichannel battery cycler 
(CT-3008W, Neware) for Li-S pouch cells. Li-S coin cells were performed at an active rate of 
C/10 and a cycling rate of C/2, Li-S pouch cells were evaluated at an active rate of C/100 and 
a cycling rate of C/20 or a cycling rate of C/5 without activation cycling in a 28 ℃ 
constant-temperature chamber. The charge/discharge voltage range was controlled between 

<<PAGE 5>>
1.6-2.6 V.  
The sulfur and lithium metal electrodes were harvested from the disassembled Li-S 
pouch cells at the charging state and rinsed with DME and dried at room temperature in an 
antechamber of the high purity argon-filled glovebox overnight before test. The morphologies 
of the stored and cycled electrodes of the pouch cell with Control- and LiPO2F2- electrolytes 
were studied using scanning electron microscope (SEM, ZEISS ULTRA 55, Germany), and 
the corresponding element analysis of the electrode surface were conducted using an energy 
dispersive X-ray (EDX) analyzer attached to the SEM. The UV-Vis spectroscope (Lambda950, 
England) with a wavelength accuracy of 0.08 nm was used to obtain the adsorption spectra of 
electrolytes after switching the instrument on for at least 30 min before stabilizing the test 
light source. Control-electrolyte was used as a reference and measured every time before and 
after each test. The test electrolyte was diluted five hundred times with Control-electrolyte 
before testing. The X-ray photoelectron spectrum (XPS) profiles were obtained on PHI 1800 
using a focused monochromatized Al Kα radiation (h = 1486.6 eV) under ultra-high vacuum. 
XPS analysis of each elemental spectrum were performed using the XPSPEAK software 
assuming Gaussian/Lorentzian (20% Lorentzian) line shapes and utilizing Shirley background 
correction. 7Li magic angle spinning (MAS) NMR of sulfur electrodes was tested using the 
apparatus described in ref.[2]. 19F and 31P NMR spectra of Li2Sn-containing electrolyte were 
taken using Bruker DRX-400 NMR spectrometer.  
The Raman spectra were recorded at room temperature using an inVia Qontor Raman 
spectrophotometer (RENISHAW Co., Ltd.), which was equipped with a single 
monochromator as a laser filter. Raman spectra were excited by a 633 (DOL/DME) or 532 
(DMSO) nm laser. Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopic analysis was 
conducted using Bruker A300 EPR spectrometer with a X-band microwave frequency of 9.87 
GHz at room temperature and spectra were recorded under the following instrumental 
conditions: sweep width of 4000 G, attenuator of 10 dB, power of 1.86 mW, modulation 
frequency of 100 kHz, modulation amplitude of 1 G, sweep time of 40.96 s, time constant of 
40.96 ms, conversion time of 20 ms, static field of 1500.000 G, center field of 3500.000 G, 
receiver gain of 1.0 × 103.

Computational method. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using 
the Gaussian 09 package and Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP 5.4.4)[3]. The 
equilibrium structures of isolated neutral molecules and free radicals are calculated by 
Gaussian 09 under the level of B3LYP/6-311G*. As the structures of polysulfides are flexible, 
several possible configurations of each polysulfide are tried. Configurations with the lowest 
energy are used for the following discussions. The solvation effect of electrolyte was 
described by Polarizable Continuum Model (PCM) with dielectric Tetrahydrofuran. The 
calculations on energy differences between Li2S2/S8 molecules and their crystals were 
calculated by VASP 5.4.4 by implementing the projector augmented wave approach and 
Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized-gradient approximation (GGA). All the crystal 
and the molecule models were relaxed until the Hellmann-Feynman force on each atom is 
smaller than 0.001 eV/ Å. Energy were calculated with an energy cutoff of 750 eV and a 
k-point mesh with density of 50 × 50 × 50 per Å-1.

Figure S12. Operando visual test of V-type Li-S cells for 5 min lightning and following open 
circuit standing: V-control-cell (left) and V-LiPO2F2-cell (right). 
 
As seen in Movie S1 and Figure S12, the electrolyte color at the cathode side 
changed into yellow gradually during the lighting time of 5 minutes for both cells 
because of the dissolved LiPS. Meanwhile, V-LiPO2F2-cell shows the lighter color in 
cathode side than the V-control-cell due to the slower dissolution of LiPS. 
Interestingly, after removed LED lamps, the yellow color at the sulfur cathode side 
disappeared gradually during the open-circuit state for V-LiPO2F2-cell, indicating the 
dissolved LiPS are gradually carried to the sulfur cathode instead of diffusing to the 
anode side. But for V-control-cell, the yellow color of the dissolved LiPS spread out 
and diffused to the anode side, finally the electrolyte showed deep yellow color in 
total, indicating that large amounts of LiPS flowed out from cathode and shuttled 
severely to the anode side.

Figure S18. Polysulfide solubility test in LiPO2F2-electrolyte: Photo of LiPO2F2-electrolyte 
adding S8 and Li2S (a), stirring for 6 h (b), resting for 240 h (c) and the corresponding bottom of

As above discussion, polysulfides disproportionation reaction can be accelerated 
by the addition of LiPO2F2 catalyst. To exclude the possibility of reaching polysulfide 
saturation solubility affected by adding LiPO2F2, a polysulfide solubility test in 
LiPO2F2-electrolyte was operated. As shown in Figure S18a, 0.1 M elemental sulfur 
and 0.02 M Li2S were added into LiPO2F2-electrolyte, and then stirred. If the addition 
of LiPO2F2 leads to the saturation solubility of polysulfides, the reaction between S8 
and Li2S is unable to proceed. As a result, after stirring for 6 h, the color of electrolyte 
turned into brownish red due to the generation of LiPSs (Figure S18b), indicting the 
faster kinetics of S8 and Li2S under stirring than that of the inverse process of LiPS 
catalytic disproportionation by LiPO2F2. When stopping stirring and keeping resting, 
the catalytic disproportionation of LiPSs was dominant reaction reflected by the fade 

<<PAGE 20>>
in color and precipitation production (Figure S18c and d). The LiPS dissolution and 
precipitation processes demonstrate the electrolyte discoloration and precipitation 
production originates from LiPO2F2 catalyzed LiPS disproportionation instead of 
reaching polysulfide saturation solubility.

Figure S30. Possible reaction paths of biradicals during a cycle period in Stage I (Li2S8-rich, a), II (Li2S6-rich, b) and III (Li2S4-rich, c). 

<<PAGE 33>>
Radical initiation and transformation have different paths for disproportionation reaction. In this energy ladder diagram, the feasible paths in Stage I 
(Li2S8-rich), II (Li2S6-rich) and III (Li2S4-rich) are displayed, the reactants in initiation and transfer stage are Li2S8, Li2S6 and Li2S4, respectively. This three stages 
experience a highly spontaneous conbination bewteen LiPO2F2 and Li2Sn, and tend to transform into biradical with a homolysis way subsequently. As shown in the 
figure, compared to orange path, the green one has higher dynamical feasibility due to its lower radical initiation energy (rate determining). In Stage I, the negative 
energy (-0.324 eV) is captured in the loop of radical transfer, indicating this two paths could be spontaneously operated. While this energy rise to 1.83 and 3.084 
eV in Stage II and III, respectively. The average energy of each step is increasingly high from Stage I to III, representing the more difficult dynamics are operated 
in later period of disproportionation reaction for forming more Li2S2 reduction production.

this optimized structure. The biradical is about 1 unit, which suggests they are 
biradical[21]. It can be seen that the biradical state of LiPO2F2-Li2S8 is 0.63 eV higher 
than its ground state. However, alone Li2S8 and LiTFSI-Li2S8 requires about 5.99 and 
1.25 eV to reach their biradical states.
