Experimental Methods 
 
Electrochemical Measurements 
The Li2S cathode used in this work was prepared by ball-milling multiwalled carbon 
nanotubes (MWCNT, NanoAmor) and lithium sulfide (Li2S, Alfa Aesar) in a 1 : 4 weight 
ratio with a large excess of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) co-
solvents and yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) grinding balls to create a smooth, homogenous  
slurry. The slurry was then drop-cast between two pieces of Avcarb P50 gas diffusion 
membranes (7/16-inch diameter) to assemble Li2S/CNT composite cathodes with a final Li2S 
loading of 4 mg cm-2. Avcarb P50 carbon papers are used as a conductive substrate to increase 
the active surface area of the cathode. Li || Li2S half cells and anode-free Ni || Li2S full cells 
were assembled with the Li2S/CNT composite cathode, Celgard 2325 separator, and nickel 
foil (25 µm) or lithium foil (0.75 mm) as the anode in CR2032 coin cell format. Ni foil is used 
as the current collector instead of the more commonly used Cu foil due to the relative 
inertness of nickel to polysulfides. Ni || Li half cells were similarly assembled with lithium 
foil as the cathode and nickel foil as the anode. The various electrolyte formulations used for 
this study have been outlined throughout the paper. The electrolyte amount in the cells was 
not controlled to avoid convoluting the results with the effects of low E/S ratio. The cells were

cycled galvanostatically using an Arbin multi-channel battery cycler at C/10 or C/5 rates 
between voltage cutoffs of 1.8 V and 2.8 V and after resting for 6 h. The first charge step was

Materials Characterization 
The deposited lithium on the nickel foils in the anode-free Ni || Li2S full cells was 
characterized with scanning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray photoelectron microscopy 
(XPS) and time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS). After disassembly, 
the Ni foils with the deposited lithium were harvested and washed with blank DOL/DME 
solvent. SEM images were obtained with a FEI Quanta 650 FE-SEM. XPS measurements 
were conducted on the samples transferred using an air-sensitive capsule with a Kratos Axis 
Ultra DLD spectrometer. The spectra were collected with the charge neutralizer turned off to 
avoid artifacts with respect to peak positions or line shapes. An Al kα source at 1.47 keV 
tuned to 12 kV and 10 mA was used with a pass energy of 20 eV and 0.1 eV step size. ToF-
SIMS data were collected with an ION.TOF TOF.SIMS 5 spectrometer. A 2,000 eV Cs+ ion 
beam was used for sputtering the sample and generating secondary ions, while a 30 keV Bi+ 
ion beam was used for depth profiling. The area of the sample sputtered by the beam was 0.3 
mm x 0.3 mm, and secondary ions were read out over an area of 0.1 mm x 0.1 mm.

Figure S3. Coulombic efficiencies (CE) of the anode-free Ni || Li2S full cells with the 
different electrolytes at C/10 rate. It can be seen that the electrolyte with LiTFSI salt and with 
no LiNO3 additive (E4) shows low CE values at ~ 60 – 80% for the 100 cycles. In contrast to 
the electrolytes with LiTFSI and LiCF3SO3 salts that show stable CE values close to 100% for 
the 100 cycles, the electrolyte with LiFSI salt shows unstable CE values that fall rapidly, 
keeping with the poor lithium cycling efficiencies and the cell failing completely within 50 
cycles. The electrolyte with LiTFSI and LiCF3SO3 salts shows CE values exceeding 100%, 
which can be attributed to the low first-cycle Coulombic efficiency (~ 60%) and some of the 
capacity being recovered with further cycling. The excess lithium formed at the end of the 
first charge step leads to greater discharge capacity than charge capacity in subsequent cycles 
as some of the unused S8 at the cathode is converted to Li2S. This effect is further reinforced 
by the polysulfide shuttle effect that acts as an internal short in the Li-S system.
