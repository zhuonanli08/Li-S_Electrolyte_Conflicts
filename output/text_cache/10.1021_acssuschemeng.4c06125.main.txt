ABSTRACT: The efficiency and stability of lithium−sulfur (Li−S)
batteries are mainly limited by slow reaction kinetics and lithium
dendrite growth. To deal with these issues, in this study,
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 was employed as a dual-functional additive to
improve the performance of Li−S batteries. In the case of sulfur
cathodes, as evidenced by electrochemical measurements and
density functional theory calculations, Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibits a
strong affinity toward lithium polysulfides (LiPSs), accelerating the
LiPS conversion process. Conversely, in the case of Li anodes, due
to LiF-rich solid electrolyte interphases formed by PF6
−anions,
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 can react with the Li metal anode to reduce Li
dendrite growth. Batteries incorporating Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 were
prepared and subjected to charging and discharging cycles. These
batteries achieved a first-cycle discharge capacity of 1228 mAh g−1 at 0.2 C and a capacity of 914.4 mAh g−1 at 2 C. They retained
99.7% of their initial capacity when returning to 0.2 C. Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 was commonly used in dye-sensitized solar cell electrolytes
and responsible for charge transport. This study demonstrates that Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 can be used as a dual-functional additive, and it
offers avenues for advancing practical Li−S batteries and fresh perspectives for integrated optical energy storage devices.
KEYWORDS: lithium−sulfur battery, additive, dual function, cobalt complex, lithium regulation
■INTRODUCTION
With the widespread use of electric cars and digital devices in
current times, the need for high-energy-density batteries is
increasing worldwide. Lithium−sulfur (Li−S) batteries have
received considerable attention as promising candidates for the
next generation of ideal energy storage batteries owing to their
high energy density.1−3
However, several challenging issues associated with S
cathodes and Li anodes significantly impede the practical
applications of Li−S batteries. In the case of the cathode, the
shuttle effect of lithium polysulfides (LiPSs), along with the
insulating nature of S and lithium sulfide (Li2S), results in
decreased S utilization, sluggish conversion kinetics, and rapid
capacity decay in batteries.3,4 In the Li anode, the inherently
active nature of Li metal instigates an ongoing interaction with
the corrosive electrolyte, leading to an unstable solid
electrolyte interface (SEI).5,6 Numerous approaches have
been reported to address these issues, including the use of
advanced electrode materials,7−9 electrolyte regulation,10−14
and electrode surface modification.15−17 Recently, with the
advancements in electrode materials, considerable endeavors
have been made using electrolyte additives to deal with the
issues associated with Li−S batteries. For example, additives
such as NiDME,18 6-azauracil,19 DMTS,20 and dithiothreitol21
have been found to be effective in improving their performance
characteristics. These additives can alleviate the shuttle effect
of LiPSs, lower the activation energy of Li2S oxidation, and
accelerate the conversion process of LiPS reactions. However,
these additives can only act on the sulfide cathode, but not on
the anode, to inhibit the growth of Li dendrites. Generally, Li−
S battery additives are inorganic or organic additives with a
sole function.22−25 Organic additives offer several advantages
owing to the diversity of molecules that can be used as
additives. Therefore, it is important to find dual-functional
additives for the construction of stable and efficient Li−S
batteries.
Herein, the dual-functional material Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 is
proposed as an electrolyte additive for Li−S batteries to
achieve an efficient catalytic effect and mitigate lithium
dendrite formation with good solubility in the electrolyte.
For sulfur cathodes, Co(bpy)3
2+ shows a powerful affinity for
LiPSs during the LiPS conversion process, as demonstrated by
theoretical calculations. In addition, the electron transfer rate

practical significance in pioneering integrated optical energy
storage devices, reducing energy losses between solar energy
conversion and electrical energy.28 This study thus proposes
simple and practical methods to boost the performance of Li−
S batteries, demonstrating their significant potential for
practical applications.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Preparation of Electrolyte and Cathode. Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 was
purchased from a commercial source and directly used as the additive
in a commercially available lithium−sulfur battery electrolyte (1 mol
L−1 LiTFSI in DME/DOL (1:1 in volume) with 1% LiNO3 as an
additive) to form a uniform bright yellow solution. The cathode was
prepared by adding drops of S-solution in CS2 solution onto Bucky
paper.29
Characterizations and Measurements. The mass spectrum was
performed on an LTQ Orbitrap XL. Scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) images were obtained on a NOVA NanoSEM 450. The UV−
vis spectra were measured through an Agilent Cary 60. Galvanostatic
charge−discharge curves and rate capacity measurements of batteries
were carried out by a Neware battery tester in the voltage range from
1.7 to 2.8 V (versus Li/Li+). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was measured
using a Chenhua electrochemical workstation CHI660E in the
potential range from 2.8 to 1.7 V. The symmetric cells were tested
in the voltage range from 1 to −1 V at a scan rate of 50 mV s−1. Li2S
nucleation measurement was carried out on a Shenzhen Neware
battery tester at 0.1 mA cm−2, followed by a potentiostatic
measurement at 2.07 V until the current was below 10−5 A on a
Shanghai Chenhua electrochemical workstation CHI660E. Li/Cu
half-cells were tested at 1 mA cm−2 to 0.5 mAh cm−2 on a Shenzhen
Neware battery tester. The conductivity of the electrolyte was
measured on a Shanghai Leici DDS-11A, and the concentration was
selected from 0 to 4%.

Interaction between Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 and LiPSs. Co-

pyridine N atoms are expected to have a certain adsorption

the LiPSs and precipitate. To elucidate their interactions, mass

Further DFT calculations were performed to investigate the

First-principles calculations were performed to reveal its

adsorption model of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 with LiPSs was
constructed, as shown in Figure 2a. After one of the

the LiPSs. Furthermore, we evaluated the interactions between
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 and S8, Li2S8, Li2S6, Li2S4, Li2S2, and Li2S. The

the complex Co(bpy)3 on LiPSs were calculated to verify their

adsorption capacity of the complex Co(bpy)3

LiPSs. Furthermore, we calculated the adsorption energies of
the Co(bpy)3

Co(bpy)3
2+, the adsorption capacity of the substrate for
polysulfides was enhanced by about 1−2 eV.
The reduction process of S8 first involves a double reduction
process of S8 with two Li ions.32 The formed Li2S8 is further
reduced to three intermediate LiPS states, namely, Li2S6, Li2S4,
and Li2S2, and finally reduced to Li2S.33 We calculated the
Gibbs free energies with and without Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 during
the transformation of the LiPSs. The adsorption configurations
and Gibbs free energy distributions of the intermediate LiPSs
are shown in Figure 2d. Except for the spontaneous exothermic
conversion of S8 to Li2S8, all reaction steps are endothermic
reactions that require energy to drive the reaction.32 In the
presence of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2, the energy barriers for the
evolution from Li2S8 to Li2S decrease to 0.16, 0.09, 0.14, and
0.58 eV, compared to 0.23, 0.41, 0.54, and 0.93 eV without
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2. The lower Gibbs free energy indicates that
the presence of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 can significantly reduce the
reaction energy barrier in the S8 reduction process, thereby
accelerating the kinetics of the LiPS reaction conversion. The
computational results indicate that Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 not only
exhibits a strong anchoring capability toward LiPSs but also
reduces the conversion energy barrier of LiPSs. This
demonstrates the excellent catalytic ability of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2,
which could accelerate the reaction kinetics of the LiPS
conversion.

Electrochemical Performance of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 in Li−
S Batteries. To directly verify the catalytic performance of
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 in Li−S batteries and to improve battery
performance, galvanostatic charge−discharge measurement
was conducted. The optimized dosage of the electrocatalyst
was maintained at 2 wt % of the electrolyte (Figure S8). As
shown in Figure 6c, the battery with Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibited
a higher initial capacity of 1228 mAh g−1 at a rate of 0.2 C (1 C
= 1675 mAh g−1), compared to the battery without
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2. To eliminate the contribution of Co-
(bpy)3(PF6)2 to the capacity of the battery, we prepared a
battery without an S cathode. As shown in Figure S9, the
capacity provided by Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 during the charge−
discharge cycles, except for the first cycle, was below 5 mAh
g−1, which can be considered negligible. In addition, to
compare the individual effects of Co(bpy)3
2+ and PF6
−on Li−
S batteries, we tested the cycling performance of Li−S batteries
containing Co(bpy)3(TFSI)2 and LiPF6 (Figure S10).
Compared to Co(bpy)3(PF6)2, both Co(bpy)3(TFSI)2 and
LiPF6 exhibited poorer cycling performance at 0.2 C. After 100
charge−discharge cycles at a rate of 0.2 C, the battery
containing Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 still retained a capacity of 990
mAh g−1, with a decay rate of only 0.19% per cycle, compared
to the battery without Co(bpy)3(PF6)2. This demonstrates that
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 can facilitate the construction of stable Li−S
batteries.
To evaluate the electrochemical performance of Co-
(bpy)3(PF6)2 in high-rate charge−discharge cycles, galvano-
static tests were performed at rates of 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 C
(Figure 6a). As expected, the battery containing Co-
(bpy)3(PF6)2 demonstrated high capacities of 1168, 1055,
1001, and 914 mAh g−1 at rates of 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 C,
respectively. As the current density returned to 0.2 C, the
discharge capacity recovered to 1165 mAh g−1, corresponding
to 99.7% of the initial capacity. This result suggests that the
battery with Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibits a good rate performance
and reversibility. From the charge−discharge curves of the
battery (Figure 6b), it can be observed that the battery
containing Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibited distinct dual charge−
discharge plateaus. Compared with the battery without
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 (Figure S11), it also exhibited lower polar-
ization at various current densities. This indicates that
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 enhances the utilization of S and accelerates
the kinetics of LiPS conversion. Besides, considering the
contribution of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 to the conductivity of the
battery, the conductivity was also tested and verified to verify
its contribution (Figure S12).
To verify the electrocatalytic conversion effect of Co-
(bpy)3(PF6)2 on LiPSs, we conducted CV tests (Figure 6d)
and calculated the Tafel slopes.43,44 The reduction peak C1 can
be attributed to the conversion of long-chain LiPSs to short-
chain LiPSs, whereas the reduction peak C2 can be associated
with the conversion of short-chain LiPSs to Li2S. The battery
containing Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibits higher and sharper
reduction peak currents (3.2 mA) and a decreased potential
difference between the oxidation and reduction peaks. This
indicates that the presence of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 can accelerate
the conversion of LiPSs, improve S utilization, and reduce
battery potential differences. Tafel slope diagrams were
obtained based on the calculations of peaks C1 and C2
(Figure 6e,f). At peak C1, the Tafel slopes of batteries with and
without Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 were −0.065 and −0.085 mV dec−1,
respectively. At peak C2, the Tafel slopes of batteries with and
without Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 were −0.044 and −0.081 mV dec−1,
respectively. The reduced Tafel slopes indicate that in the
presence of Co(bpy)3(PF6)2, the conversion process from
long-chain to short-chain LiPSs and the deposition kinetics of
Li2S are accelerated. This acceleration contributes to the
improvement of S utilization of enhancement of catalytic
activity in Li−S batteries under homogeneous conditions.
Based on the mass spectroscopy and UV−Vis analyses,
together with the electrocatalytic analysis from the theoretical
and experimental tests described above, we can readily
conclude that Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 is responsible for durable and
efficient Li−S electrochemistry. With the help of Co(bpy)3
2+
cations, LiPSs were strongly anchored and their reaction
kinetics accelerated. Conversely, PF6
−anions helped to form a
LiF-rich SEI, thereby suppressing the Li dendrite growth and
protecting the Li anode. Consequently, Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 aids in
the construction of efficient and stable Li−S batteries, as
schematically demonstrated in Figure 6g.
■CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we employed a dual-functional additive, Co-
(bpy)3(PF6)2, to accelerate the dynamic conversion of LiPSs
and suppress the growth of Li dendrites in Li−S batteries.
Through the interaction between Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 and the
LiPSs, Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 accelerates the conversion process
from long-chain to short-chain LiPSs, as well as the deposition
process of Li2S, thereby improving the utilization of S.
Additionally, the abundant PF6
−in Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 contrib-
utes to the formation of a compact SEI with Li anodes, thereby
suppressing the growth of Li dendrites. The battery with
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibited an initial capacity of 1228 mAh g−1
and can still maintain a capacity of 990 mAh g−1 after 100
cycles with a decay rate of only 0.19% per cycle. Under
different charge−discharge rates, the battery containing
Co(bpy)3(PF6)2 exhibits superior rate performance. After the
battery returns from a high rate to 0.2 C, the battery capacity is
