Experimental Section
Synthesis of PG-DAAQ: 2,6-diaminoanthraquinone (DAAQ, 1.429 g, 6 mmol, Ambeed) was dispersed in 20 mL of deionized water and 2.8 mL of concentrated HCl (Fisher Chemical) before cooled down to 0 °C in an ice bath. A precooled NaNO2 solution (0.856 g, 12.2 mmol, in 10 mL deionized water, Alfa Aesar) was then added dropwise to the mixture and stirred for 30 mins at 0 – 5 °C to produce an orange to brown solution of diazonium salt. After that, the solution was neutralized to pH ~ 4 with precooled Na2CO3 solution. Phloroglucinol (PG, 0.5044 g, 4 mmol, Acros Organics) and Na2CO3 (2 g) were dissolved in 20 mL of water. The mixture was cooled and dripped into the diazonium salt solution at 0 – 5 °C. After 12 h of stirring, the product was suction filtered and washed successively with water, methanol, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and water. Finally, the residue was dried in a vacuum oven at 80 °C for 12 h to get the PG-DAAQ polymer.

Electrodes Preparation: Sulfur cathodes for coin cells were prepared by a slurry drop-cast method. Sulfur (75 wt%, ~100 mesh, Thermo Scientific), PG-DAAQ (6 wt%), and Ketjen Black (KB, 19 wt%, EC-600JD) were ground and melt diffused in an autoclave at 155 °C for 12 h to obtain the S/PG-DAAQ/KB composite. A 4 wt% binder solution of polyethylene oxide (PEO, average MW ≈ 4,000,000, Sigma Aldrich) and polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP, average MW ≈ 1,300,000) (w/w = 4:1) was prepared in deionized water. The S/PG-DAAQ/KB composite (80 wt%), super C65 (5 wt%), carbon nanofibers (CNF, 5 wt%), and binder solution (10 wt% of solid) were mixed in a Thinky mixer. The slurry was then dropped onto bucky paper discs and then dried at 50 °C under vacuum overnight. Sulfur cathodes for the control cells were prepared following the same procedure, except with a S/KB composite (75 wt% S). For pouch-cell cathodes, the slurry was doctor-blade cast onto a carbon-coated Al-foil and dried. To make PG-DAAQ/C cathodes for the symmetric cell, PG-DAAQ (60 wt%) was mixed with super C65 (30 wt%) and PEO/PVP binder (10 wt%) in water/isopropanol in a Thinky mixer. The slurry was then blade-casted onto an aluminum foil and punched into discs. The PG-DAAQ loading in these electrodes are 0.9 – 1 mg cm-2. To make PG-DAAQ/C cathodes for the nucleation test, PG-DAAQ (70 wt%) was mixed with super C65 (20 wt%) and PEO/PVP binder (10 wt%) in water/isopropanol before drop-casting onto bucky paper discs.

Electrochemical Measurements: All coin cells were assembled with CR2032 cell cases inside an Ar-filled glovebox. One layer of Celgard 2325 separator was utilized in each cell. The electrolyte consisted of 1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, Solvionic) and 0.3 M LiNO3 in 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) (v/v = 1:1). The cells were rested for 5 h before being cycled. For Li || S cells, the galvanostatic cycling tests were performed at a C/10 rate for one formation cycle (C/20 rate for high-loading cells) and then at specified rates between 1.7 and 2.8 V at 21 °C. Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests were performed at a C/10 rate between 1.7 and 2.8 V after a formation cycle. A constant discharge/charge current was applied for 15 min, followed by a 30 min relaxation. The internal resistance was calculated by dividing the overpotential by the pulse current. For Li || PG-DAAQ cells, they were cycled at a C/10 rate between 1.7 and 2.8 V. All the cycling and GITT data were collected on a Landt or an Arbin cycler. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were conducted in the potential range of 1.7 to 2.8 V with different scan rates. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed in the frequency range of 1 MHz to 0.01 Hz. After one formation cycle, the cells were discharged to various voltages and measured at open-circuit voltage (OCV). CV and EIS data were collected on a Biologic VMP-3 system.

Pouch Cell Assembly and Testing: Li-S pouch cells were fabricated with Li foil anode and blade-cast sulfur cathode. The Li anode was pressed on a Ni current collector. The cathode was punched into 5.6 × 4.3 cm (≈24 cm2) sheet, while the anode had a slightly larger dimension (5.8 × 4.5 cm). Ni and Al tabs were welded on the anode and cathode current collector, respectively. The E/S ratio was controlled to be 5 µL mg−1. The pouch cells were put in pressure holders to ensure uniform pressure. The cells were cycled between 1.7 and 2.8 V at a C/20 rate for 2 formation cycles, followed by cycling at a C/10 rate.

Li2S Nucleation Study: Li || Li2S8 cells were assembled with PG-DAAQ/C or carbon paper (without PG-DAAQ) at the cathode. 0.2 M Li2S8, A solution of 1 M LiTFSI, and 0.3 M LiNO3 in tetraglyme was utilized as a catholyte, while electrolyte without Li2S8 as an anolyte. 20 μL of the catholyte and anolyte were added to the cathode and anode side, respectively. The cells were galvanostatically discharged to 2.08 V at 89 μA and then potentiostatically discharged at 2.05 V for 30,000 s.

Li2S6 Symmetric Cell Assembly: Two identical PG-DAAQ/C or carbon control (without PG-DAAQ) electrodes were used as the working and counter electrodes. A solution of 0.2 M Li2S6, 1 M LiTFSI, and 0.3 M LiNO3 in DOL/DME (v/v = 1:1) was utilized as the electrolyte. 20 μL of the electrolyte was added on each side.

Materials Characterization: X-ray diffraction (XRD) was performed with a Rigaku Miniflex 600 diffractometer to study the crystalline phases of the samples. Operando XRD analysis was carried out on a Rigaku Ultima IV X-ray diffractometer with a specially designed cell. Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra were collected with a Thermo Scientific-Nicolet iS5 spectrometer. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed on a Netzch STA 449 system with a temperature range of 40 – 500 °C and a heating rate of 5 °C min-1. The N2 adsorption/desorption isotherm and the corresponding Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface area was measured with a Micromeritics TriStar II Plus analyzer. The surface morphology of the materials was imaged with an Apreo 2C LoVac SEM. A Cary 5000 spectrophotometer was used to collect UV–vis spectra. A Kratos X-ray photoelectron spectrometer with monochromatic Al Kα radiation was used for XPS analysis. The cycled Li samples were sputtered for various amounts of time with Ar+ ions (in a rate of ~ 5 nm min-1).

Computational methods: interactions between PG-DAAQ and polysulfides were simulated with the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP). Generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional was used in conjunction with the projector augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotentials.[1-3] An energy cutoff of 550 eV and a 1 × 1 × 1 Γ-centered k-point mesh was applied. The structure of PG-DAAQ was simplified by using one PG and DAAQ unit. PG-DAAQ and Li2S6 molecules were relaxed separately before putting together and relaxing. Supplementary notes: The actual electrochemical process in the Li-S cells may be a mixture of diffusion-controlled and surface-controlled processes. Therefore, we tested a few different n values when fitting ip and vn. For each redox peak of cells with and without PG-DAAQ, the best fitting (R-square closest to 1) is with a different n value, indicating slightly different kinetics processes. Consequently, it may be difficult to directly calculate the diffusion coefficients from the slopes here due to the complexity of the reaction kinetics. Nevertheless, as the fittings when n = 0.5 are acceptable for all the redox peaks, we can still use the Randles–Sevcik equation to compare the diffusion coefficients for Li+ in two samples.
