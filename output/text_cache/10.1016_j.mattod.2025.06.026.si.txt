Materials and methods
Materials of electrolyte. All solvents and salt used in this work are purchased from Suzhou DoDochem without further treatment. DOL (DoDoChem, 99.9% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), DME (DoDoChem, 99.9% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), DEGDME (DoDoChem, 99.5% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), TEGDME (DoDoChem, 99.5% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), EGDEE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), DEGDEE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), DEGDBE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), OTE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), TTE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), LiNO3 (DoDoChem, 99.9 % purity, 100 ppm H2O max), LiTFSI (DoDoChem, 99.9 % purity, 100 ppm H2O max), and LiFSI (DoDoChem, 99.9 % purity, 100 ppm H2O max).
Preparation of electrolyte. TE was prepared via dissolving 1 M LiTFSI into a mixed DOL/DME solvent (volume ratio of 1:1) with the addition of 2 wt% LiNO3. For the preparation of BSSE, the average LiTFSI concentration is fixed to 1 M and the volume ratio of DEGDEE/OTE is 1:4. In the meanwhile, the DEGDEE was also substituted by DEGDME, TEGDME, EGDEE, EGDBE for studying the effect of various solvents. TTE was also used as an out-layer solvent to replace OTE and LiFSI was considered as another Li salt choice to LiTFSI. Besides, volume ratio of solvent/diluent was adjusted to 1:2 for the comparison of electrochemical performance. Note that the electrolytes without the addition of OTE are donated as MSSE, wherein the LiTFSI salt concentration is 5 M.
Preparation of S/G electrode. Firstly, nanosulfur powder and graphene (G) were mixed and ground with a mass ratio of 3:1. The obtained composite was then heated at a temperature of 155 °C for 12 h under a vacuum environment. For the slurry preparation, S/G mixture (80 wt%), SuperP (10 wt%) and LA133 binder (10 wt%) were combined and ball-milled with the appropariate addition of water as the solvent, which was casted onto a piece of Al foil. Finally, the homogenous slurry was dried at 60 °C for 12 h under vacuum environment to remove water and oxygen residue, which was cut into small disks with a dismeter of 12 mm prior to the battery assembly. Note that routine sulfur loading in an electrode was controlled to be ca. 1.2 mg cm−2 and the high loading sulfur cathode has an elevated loading of ca. 4.0 mg cm−2.
Shuttle current measurement. Li–S batteries equipped with BSSE and TE were firstly placed into an oven with a constant temperature of 60 °C for 6 h, which were then galvanostatically charged to 2.90 V at 0.1 mA. The batteries were further discharged to 2.39, 2.38, 2.37, 2.36, 2.35, 2.34, 2.32, 2.30, and 2.12 V step by step. Note that at each voltage step, the batteries were kept potentiostatically for 1.0 h while the time was adjusted to 6 h only at 2.12 V to obtain the current–time curves. 
In-situ EIS measurement. As-assembled Li–S batteries were cycled for 3 cycles at 0.1 C under 60 °C to stabilize the SEI. Then the EIS test was conducted at multiple voltage stages (2.4–2.0 V, 0.5 V for each interval) during the discharge and (2.0–2.4 V, 0.5 V for each interval) the charge process.
Characterizations. SEM images were obtained on a FEI Magellan 400 XHR. Liquid-state NMR data were collected on a Bruker 600 MHz AVANCE III NMR spectrometer equipped with a BBFO probe. 
XPS etching test. Li||Cu half cells were assembled with BSSE and TE to carry out the Li metal deposition with a capacity of 1 mAh cm–2 at 0.5 mA cm–2 under a temperature of 60 oC. The batteries were disassembled at glove box and the samples were washed with DME, which were sealed into a dedicated transfer module that was connected afterwards to the XPS instrument without any air exposure prior to performing the XPS measurement. High-resolution XPS spectra were acquired using a Kratos Axis Ultra DLD instrument, which is equipped with a monochromatic Al Kα X-ray source (hν = 1486.6 eV) and operated at 75 W. Depth profiling was carried out using Ar+ beam operated at different sputtering times. The binding energies scale were calibrated using the adventitious carbon (C–C bond) referenced at 284.8 eV.
Cryo-TEM test. 0.1 mAh cm–2 Li metal was plated on the Cu TEM grid with a current density of 1 mA cm–2, which was then washed with DME solvent and sealed into a small centrifuge tube to avoid exposure to air. The sample was transported to a Gatan cryo-TEM holder in a pre-prepared liquid nitrogen-filled environment. The imaging and EELS were carried out using a Titan 300 TEM. 
NMR test. Note that customized NMR tubes with a coaxial internal insert were employed to eliminate the possible influence of deuterated solutions on the pristine microstructure of samples, wherein 10 mM LiPF6 was dissolved into 120 μL DMSO-d6 as the standard reference for 1H, 7Li, 17O, and 19F nuclei. For the qNMR test, 2 mM DCBF was dissolved into 120 μL DMSO-d6 was prepared as the internal reference for 1H and 19F nuclei. The testing temperature in this work was controlled to 298, 313, 328, and 343 K to study temperature-dependent electrolyte ion dynamics. 
qNMR test was obtained via combined 1H-NMR and 19F-NMR electrolyte analysis. The assembled Li–S batteries with TE and BSSE were cycled at 0.1 C for 50 cycles, which were then disassembled in a glove box. Subsequently, cathode, separator and Li metal were quickly transferred into a centrifugal tube containing 1 mL of DMSO-d6 together, which were shaken for 10 min. Finally, 500 μL electrolyte/DMSO-d6 mixture was extracted and analyzed. Note that the remanent electrolyte number after the battery cycling can be obtained by multiplying the integrated area by 2. In the meanwhile, the molar ratio of remaining LiTFSI and DME can be determined based on the integral of the corresponding peaks in 19F and 1H NMR spectra.

T1 (spin–lattice) time is measured using an inversion recovery (IR). T1 relaxation is the process by which the longitudinal net magnetization (MZ) grows to its initial maximum value (M0). T1 is defined as the time required for the MZ value to reach 63% of the M0. T2 (spin–spin) time is obtained using Carr–Purcell Meiboom–Gill (CPMG). T2 relaxation is the process by which the transverse magnetization (Mxy) experiences a decay. T2 is the time that is requested for the Mxy value to fall to ~ 37% of M0. 

The NMR relaxation time values of Li+ and TFSI– can be obtained via integrating the corresponding peak region in the 1D 7Li and 19F NMR spectra, respectively, followed by the T1/T2 analysis module of the Bruker software.
Li+ transfer number (t+) can be obtained according to the following equation:
t+=D+/D++D–
where t+ is the Li+ transference number, D+ is the Li+ diffusion coefficient, and D– is the anion diffusion coefficient.
Electrochemical tests. CR2032 coin-type cells were assembled to evaluate the ion transportation and electrochemical performance of Li–S batteries with S/G, Li metal, and Celgard 2400 serving as the cathode, anode, and separator, respectively. EIS, CV, and PITT profiles were recorded on the Bio-logic VMP3 electrochemical workstation at room and high temperature. The frequency in the EIS measurement was kept within the region of 200,000−0.01 Hz with an amplitude of 10 mV. GCD, GITT and rate/cycling curves were achieved on the NEWARE Battery Test System (CT-4008T-5V50mA-164, Shenzhen, China) within a voltage range of 1.7–2.8 V.
