I. Supporting Text 
1. Experimental Section 
1.1. Fabrication and electrochemical test of Li–S pouch cells 
The sulfur/carbon nanotube (S/CNT) composite cathodes were fabricated by a convenient ball-milling 
process. S and CNT (7:3 by mass) were firstly homogenously mixed in a batch, then the mixture was ball milled 
with polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder (9:1 by mass) in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP) solvent to obtain a 
slurry which was further coated on the two sides of aluminum (Al) current collector. After that, the coated pieces 
were dried at 60oC for 12 h and cut into suitable size (4.0 × 7.0 cm2) for pouch cells. The S content of the total 
electrode is 63 wt.% and the S loading of cathode is 5.25 mg cm−2 (Table S1). Lithium (Li) metal with 50 μm 
thick was rolled into copper (Cu) current collector and trimmed into the same size as cathodes. Then the pouch 
cells were fabricated layer by layer with the alternation of 4 pieces of S/C cathodes, 5 pieces of Li anodes, and

polypropylene-polyethylene-polypropylene (PP-PE-PP) separator in a dry room with a dew-point temperature 
less than −40oC, followed by the injection of 3.45 g LiNO3 electrolyte (1.0 M Li bis(trifluoromethane 
sulfonimide) (LiTFSI)-1,3-dioxolane (DOL)/methoxymethane (DME) (1:3 by vol.)-5 wt.% lithium nitrate 
(LiNO3) electrolyte). The capacity and energy density of pouch cells are 1.0 Ah and 248.52 Wh kg−1 respectively. 
The pouch cells were activated at 0.34 mA cm−2 (0.076C) for the first two cycles and further cycled at 0.68 mA 
cm−2 (0.152C) between 1.8 V-2.5 V. As a result, the 100% state of charge (SOC) Li–S pouch cells with different 
cycle numbers were obtained for tests.  
1.2. Preparation of different electrolytes 
The LiNO3 electrolyte (1.0 M LiTFSI-DOL/DME (1:3 by vol.)-5 wt.% LiNO3) was prepared by dissolving 
1.0 M LiTFSI in the mixture of DOL and DME with the LiNO3 as electrolyte additive. The ether electrolyte (1.0 
M LiTFSI-DOL/DME (1:3 by vol.)) was the mixture of LiTFSI, DOL and DME without LiNO3 compared to 
LiNO3 electrolyte. Different LiPS species were synthesized by the various stoichiometric reactions between 
lithium sulfide (Li2S) and sulfide (S) and the Li2Sx indicated the average composition of LiPSs (Fig. S1 and 
Table S2). The Li2S4, Li2S6, and Li2S8 electrolyte were obtained by dissolving 1.2 g Li2S and 2.4 g/ 4.0 g/ 5.6 g 
S in 1000 mL ether electrolyte and the mixtures were stirred at 50oC for 240 h. Compared to Li2S4, Li2S6, and 
Li2S8 electrolyte, 5 wt.% LiNO3 was added during the stirring process for Li2S4 + LiNO3, Li2S6 + LiNO3, and 
Li2S8 + LiNO3 electrolyte while the contents of other components were consistent. 
1.3. Li residual of anodes for cycled Li–S pouch cells 
The Li–S pouch cells with different cycle numbers were dissembled in glove box (oxygen (O2) < 0.01 ppm, 
water (H2O) < 0.01 ppm) to obtain the cycled Li anodes. Then the cycled Li anodes were washed by DME until 
the soluble lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) were completely washed off. The cycled Li anodes were tailored into 
disks with the diameter of 7 mm. Li || Li symmetric coin-type CR2025 cells were assembled with the cycled Li 
anode, 600 μm Li plate, PP-PE-PP separator and 50 μL LiNO3 electrolyte. The symmetric cells were charged at 
0.26 mA cm−2 until 2.8 V. The Li residual of cycled anode can be calculated based on the charge capacity of the 
coin cells (Fig. S6). Considering the pulverization of Li anode during cycling process, cycled Li and cycled S/C 
were obtained from the 45-cycle pouch cells for the DSC tests.

Differential scanning calorimeter (DSC, Q2000 TA) was used to compare the thermal stability of different 
battery components with a heating rate of 10oC min−1 in argon (Ar) atmosphere between 30 and 300oC. The 
addition of the mixing components during DSC test is based on their proportions in fresh Li–S batteries. Apart 
from the materials thermal stability, the thermal safety of pouch cells was analyzed by accelerating rate 
calorimeter (ARC). The test procedure of ARC was heat-wait-seek method. In detail, a heating increment of 
10oC with a waiting time of 30 min is carried out. Then the temperature increase rate of pouch cells is sought for 
10 min and compared with 0.02oC min−1. If it exceeds the value, the temperature at that time is defined as the 
self-heating temperature (T1) and ARC switched into adiabatic mode without external heat exchange. The onset 
temperature of the thermal runaway (T2) is recorded when the battery’s temperature rise rate reaches 1oC s−1. To 
be noted, T2 is an essential indicator of battery safety which means the cells enter an uncontrollable state. T3 is 
the highest temperature of pouch cells during the ARC test. In addition, the real-time open-circuit voltage (OCV) 
of cells was also tested and the temperature of internal short circuit for cells can be acquired.
