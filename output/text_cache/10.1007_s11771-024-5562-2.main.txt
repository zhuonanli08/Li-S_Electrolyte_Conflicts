Abstract: High-energy-density lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries are among the most promising energy storage devices, but 
their potential has been constrained by the shuttle effect and poor active material utilization. Here, 3,3'-dithiodipropionic 
acid (DTPA), an efficient electrolyte additive, is applied to Li-S batteries while its underlying mechanism on the 
electrochemical performance is examined. The results show that the DTPA additive can quickly interact with the 
polysulfides scattered in the electrolyte, facilitating the conversion of long-chain polysulfides to Li2S2 and Li2S. In this 
way, by lessening the shuttle effect of long-chain polysulfides towards the anode, the redox kinetics and the usage of 
active materials can both be improved. The electrochemical performance of Li-S batteries is thus enhanced by the 
addition of DTPA, with capacities of 1093.4 mA∙h/g for the initial cycle and 714.8 mA·h/g after 250 cycles at 0.5C 
(DTPA concentration is 1.5 wt.% ). Also, the DTPA-containing batteries have higher electrochemical stability, with a 
capacity retention rate of 57.5% relative to 0.1C at 2C. The improved performance demonstrates the importance of 
electrolyte additives, and the DTPA introduced in this work offers a potential solution for reducing the shuttle effect of 
polysulfide to increase the capacity of Li-S batteries.
Key words: lithium-sulfur battery; 3,3'-dithiodipropionic acid; polysulfide conversion; shuttle effect; electrolyte additive

1 Introduction
There is a pressing need to develop next-
generation energy storage systems with higher 
energy density and lighter weight because of the 
rapidly expanding market demand for advanced 
energy storage devices. Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) 
batteries, a promising alternative system to lithium-
ion batteries (250 −300 W·h/kg) [1], have gained 
great attention due to their high theoretical capacity 
and high energy density, which can theoretically 
reach 1675 mA·h/g and 2600 W·h/kg, respectively. 
Furthermore, because elemental sulfur is abundant 
and non-toxic, its use as a cathode material makes 
Li-S batteries inexpensive and environmentally 
friendly [2 −3]. However, practical Li-S batteries 
have challenges that the insoluble insulating 
compounds such as S8, Li2S2, and Li2S are frequently 
generated during the charge/discharge reactions,

obstructing the transfer of electrons and ions in the 
battery system. Even worse, the shuttle effect and 
self-discharging phenomenon are frequently caused 
by the fact that the long-chain polysulfides Li2Sx 
(4£x£8) can dissolve in organic solvents and readily 
react with lithium anode to form insoluble short-
chain polysulfides [4−5], and these will result in the 
reduced coulomb efficiency, severe corrosion of 
lithium anode and poor cycling stability. Therefore, 
it is crucial to alleviate polysulfide dissolution and 
the shuttle effect if Li-S batteries are to be 
commercialized.
Electrolyte design has so far been found to be 
effective in inhibiting the shuttle effect, self-
discharging, and electrode passivation because 
electrolytes can not only serve as the ionic 
conductor but also participate in the conversion 
reactions of both positive and negative electrodes 
[5−8]. There have been many excellent works when 
it comes to manipulating the electrolyte to enhance 
the performance of Li-S batteries. For example, it 
has been discovered that the additive hexadecyl 
trioctyl ammonium iodide (HTOA-I) has effects on 
prolonging the cycling life of Li-S batteries by 
inhibiting 
the 
polysulfide 
shuttle, 
facilitating 
uniform lithium deposition, and favoring effective 
solid electrolyte interface (SEI) formation on the 
lithium anode [9]. Moreover, KIM et al [10] 
investigated the effect of various metal iodides as 
electrolyte additives, and it was found that the 
presence of metal iodides like LiI and MgI2 in ether-
based electrolytes can increase the viscosity of the 
electrolyte, promote SEI formation, and prevent the 
shuttle effect of polysulfides. Thioacetamide (TAA) 
electrolyte additive was reported to significantly 
reduce 
battery 
passivation 
and 
boost 
the 
electrochemical performance of Li-S batteries by 
improving the solubility of Li2S and protecting the 
lithium anode [11]. LIU et al [12] found that the 
addition of toluene and phosphorus pentasulfide in 
the electrolyte can help to remove the sediments that 
are generated on the electrode surface during the 
charging/discharging 
process, 
thereby 
greatly 
reducing the passivation degree of the sulfur 
cathode. YE et al [13] introduced a biological 
reagent called dithiothreitol (DTT) to Li-S battery 
electrolyte, which can significantly speed up the 
reaction kinetics and prevent the shuttle effect of 
polysulfides. According to the studies mentioned 
above, electrolytes have a variety of roles in Li-S 
batteries, and changing the electrolyte compositions 
can considerably increase the cycling stability and 
coulomb efficiency of Li-S batteries. Despite this, 
we also found that the current capacities achieved 
by Li-S batteries are far below their theoretical 
capacity, and additional work on efficient electrolyte 
additives is still required and crucial for the 
commercialization of Li-S batteries.
Herein, 
we 
propose 
to 
employ 
3,3'-dithiodipropionic acid (DTPA) as an additive in 
Li-S batteries. Based on various experimental 
techniques, it is found that the added DTPA 
molecule can encourage the conversion of long-
chain polysulfide to short-chain polysulfide and 
inhibit the shuttling of polysulfide during the 
charging/discharging process. As a result, the 
electrochemical 
performance 
is 
effectively 
improved in the presence of DTPA additive, with 
the capacity being able to reach 1093.4 mA·h/g for 
the initial cycle and to stay at 714.8 mA·h/g after 
250 cycles at 0.5C.
2 Material and methods
2.1 Preparation of cathode material
Preparation of sulfur-carbon composite: As 
mentioned in our earlier research [14], carbon 
disulfide was used as a dispersant to prepare porous 
carbon material. The porous carbon and sulfur blend 
(mass ratio of 35: 65) were thoroughly ground and 
mixed and then sintered in a muffle furnace at 
160 ℃ for 2 h in an argon atmosphere. The created 
sulfur carbon composite, acetylene black, and 
polyvinylidene 
fluoride 
(PVDF) 
were 
then 
thoroughly stirred with n-methyl-2-pyridinone-one 
(NMP) solvent before being ground in a mortar with 
a mass ratio of 7:2:1. After that, the aluminum foil 
was covered with the prepared composite material, 
which was then dried in an oven for 24 h at 60 ℃. 
To use it as the cathode, the obtained foil with active 
material was finally cut into a circle with a 13 mm 
diameter. 
The 
cathodes 
made 
using 
the 
aforementioned technique have sulfur mass loadings 
ranging from 1.3 to 1.6 mg/cm
2.
2.2 Preparation of electrolyte
The basic electrolyte was a 1.0 mol/L LiTFSI 
in DOL/DME. The volume ratio of DOL to DME

was 1:1, and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 was an additive.
Electrolyte 
containing 
DTPA: 
Different 
amounts of DTPA additive (1.0 wt.%, 1.5 wt.% and 
2.0 wt.% ) were added into the basic electrolyte, 
which were named 1.0 wt.% DTPA, 1.5 wt.% DTPA 
and 2.0 wt.% DTPA. The electrolyte was stirred at 
room temperature for 24 h after adding additives.
The above operations were completed in the 
glove box with a pure argon atmosphere.
2.3 Battery assembly and electrochemical test
The coin cells were assembled using the 
prepared material. The anode and diaphragm in the 
battery were made of lithium metal sheet and 
polypropylene, respectively, and the amount of 
electrolyte was optimized to 30 μL. The whole 
assembly process was completed in the glove box in 
pure argon atmosphere. Charge-discharge cycle was 
implemented on the assembled Li-S batteries 
through the LAND CT2001A test system at a rate of 
0.5C within the voltage range of 1.7−2.8 V. Cyclic 
voltammetry testing was performed at a scan speed 
of 0.1 mV/s with the voltage of 1.5−3.0 V.
2.4 Characterization of samples
The basic electrolyte was supplemented with 
polysulfide, and DTPA was added as an additive. 
The molecular interactions in the electrolyte were 
confirmed using laser confocal Raman spectroscopy 
and ultraviolet (UV) -visible absorption light. After 
several cycles of charging and discharging, the Li-S 
batteries were disassembled in the glove box with 
an argon atmosphere before the cathode and anode 
were removed.
Scanning electron microscope (SEM) and 
X-ray photoelectron spectroscope (XPS) were used 
to characterize the electrolyte, and electrode plates. 
The surface morphology and composition of the 
cathode and anode of Li-S batteries with different 
electrolytes before and after charge-discharge were 
analyzed.
3 Results and discussion
To start, the characteristics of the electrolyte 
with and without the additive are compared. The 
optical pictures of two different electrolytes, the 
basic electrolyte (1 mol/L LiTFSI in DOL/DME= 
1:1 (vol% with 2.0 wt.% LiNO3) and the electrolyte 
with DTPA additives (1 mol/L LiTFSI in DOL/ 
DME=1:1 vol% with 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 and 1.5 wt.% 
DTPA), are shown in Figure 1(a). To examine the 
interaction between DTPA molecular and Li2S8 
compounds, we first dissolved 20 mmol/L Li2S8 into 
the basic electrolyte (left picture in Figure 1(b)) and 
then added 1.5 wt.% DTPA to the above solution 
(right picture in Figure 1(b)). As seen, the dark 
brown solution changed to a yellow color within 
1 min while the white precipitates appeared at the 
bottom simultaneously, indicating that DTPA can 
rapidly react with polysulfides [15 −17]. Then, the 
chemical properties of the upper solution and 
precipitates were analyzed. After the original 
electrolyte had been let to remain for 2 h, the 
supernatant solution was filtered out and examined 
using 
the 
ultraviolet-visible 
(UV-vis) 
spectrophotometer (Figure 1(c)). For a solution 
containing Li2S8, a significant peak at around 
310 nm is related to species of S6
2− or S4
2− [18], while 
the shoulder peak at 420 nm is caused by S4
2− [19−
20]. In contrast, the peak at 310 nm and the shoulder 
at 420 nm disappear after Li2S8 reacted with DTPA, 
but the peak at 265 nm appears, which is attributed 
to the S2
2− signal. These findings imply that DTPA 
can promote the disproportionation reaction of long-
chain polysulfides and generate short-chain sulfides. 
Additionally, these phenomena may also account for 
the quick reaction between DTPA and polysulfide 
solution as shown in Figure 1(b) [21−22], which is 
conducive to lessening the shuttle effect of 
polysulfides [23 −24]. After the Li2S8 solution and 
DTPA molecular had sufficiently reacted, the 
precipitates were collected and dried for the laser 
micro-confocal Raman spectroscopy analysis. As 
seen in Figure 1(d), the precipitation presents a 
characteristic that is identical to the sulfur powder 
near 480 nm, demonstrating that sulfur was 
generated during the DTPA-polysulfide reaction. As 
a result of the characterizations above, we may 
conclude that the introduction of DTPA can 
effectively convert long-chain polysulfides into 
short-chain sulfides and precipitate sulfur, thus 
reducing the shuttle effect of polysulfide. Both of 
these effects are expected to improve the utilization 
of active materials and battery performance.

The electrochemical performance of batteries 
with basic electrolyte and 1.5 wt.% DTPA-
containing 
was

Figures 2(c) and (d) show the charge −discharge

capacities of batteries with basic electrolyte and 
1.5 wt.% DTPA are shown in Figure 2(d). It can be 
seen that the discharge capacity of the basic 
electrolyte battery in the first cycle is 899.2 mA·h/g, 
which is lower than that of 1.5 wt.% DTPA-
containing electrolyte battery (1093.4 mA·h/g). In 
addition, after 100 cycles, the discharge capacity of 
the 1.5 wt.% DTPA-containing battery can remain at 
815.2 mA·h/g with a coulombic efficiency of 
97.92%, while that of basic electrolyte battery is 
only 625 mA·h/g with a lower coulombic efficiency 
of 69.51%. Furthermore, the 1.5 wt.% DTPA-
containing 
battery 
offers 
better 
long-cycle 
performance, presenting a capacity of 714.8 mA·h/g 
after 250 cycles, with the coulombic efficiency of 
95.50%. The discharge capacity of the basic 
electrolyte battery is only 608.9 mA ∙h/g, and the 
coulomb efficiency decreases to 67.72% after the 
same cycles.
Figure 2(e) shows the rate performances of 
batteries with basic electrolyte and 1.5 wt.% DTPA. 
It is found that the discharge capacities of basic 
battery at 0.1C, 0.2C, 0.5C, 1.0C and 2.0C rates are 
1174.3, 782.7, 722.1, 677.8 and 609.2 mA·h/g, 
respectively. While the discharge capacities of 
battery contained 1.5 wt.% DTPA at 0.1C, 0.2C, 
0.5C, 1.0C, and 2.0C are 1158.2, 837.2, 776.6, 
736.3 and 665.6 mA·h/g, respectively. Figure 2(f) 
shows the capacity retention rate of both batteries at 
different rates relative to 0.1C. When the rate finally 
recovers to 0.2C, the battery with 1.5 wt.% DTPA 
additive can recover to 837.6 mA·h/g, while the 
capacity of the basic battery is only 788.3 mA·h/g, 
with a capacity retention rate of 67.1% relative to 
that at 0.1C. The capacity retention rates of 
1.5 wt.% DTPA-containing battery are all higher 
than those of basic battery at different rates, as 
shown in Figure 2(f). For instance, 1.5 wt.% DTPA-
containing battery at 2C has a capacity retention rate 
of 57.5% relative to 0.1C, whereas basic battery 
only has a capacity retention rate of 51.9% relative 
to 0.1C at 2C. The increase in electrochemical 
performance with the addition of DTPA should be 
attributed to the fact that DTPA can offer more 
sulfur elements, facilitate the mutual conversion of 
long-chain polysulfides to Li2S2 and Li2S, and 
reduce the shuttle effect of polysulfide, thus 
improving the reaction kinetics.

In order to further explore the interaction 
between 
DTPA 
and 
polysulfides 
during 
electrochemical reactions, the cathode and anode 
were removed after 150 charging-discharging 
cycles, and the sulfur element on the cathodes was 
analyzed by XPS. Figures 3(c) and (d) present S 2p 
XPS spectra of cathodes disassembled from 
batteries with and without 1.5 wt.% DTPA after 150 
cycles. For the basic battery, thiosulfate at 167.3 eV 
and 168.6 eV [39] and the S—O bond at 169.2 eV 
and 170.4 eV have the highest levels of sulfur-
containing compositions on the cathode surface 
[19]. Moreover, the S elemental peaks at 164.2 eV 
and 165.5 eV are attributed to the incomplete 
transformation of sulfur, resulting in the loss of 
active components in the battery system [40−41]. In 
addition, the peaks of Li2S2 located at 162.3 eV and 
163.5 eV are also presented [40−42]. The 1.5 wt.% 
DTPA-containing battery, on the other hand, 
exhibits additional species of Li2S at 160.0 eV and 
161.1 eV [43], and the peak intensity of Li2S2 
increases when compared to the basic battery, 
indicating that the addition of DTPA promotes the 
conversion of long-chain polysulfides to Li2S2 and 
Li2S, thereby reducing capacity loss.
Figures 3(e) and (f) present S 2p XPS spectra 
of anodes in the basic electrolyte battery and 
1.5 wt.% DTPA-containing electrolyte battery after 
150 cycles. As seen, most sulfur elements in the 
anode come from S—O and thiosulfate species [19], 
and the content of Li2S2 on the anode surface in the 
1.5 wt.% DTPA-containing electrolyte battery is 
significantly lower than that of basic battery. In 
general, Li2S2 is produced by the side reaction 
between polysulfide and lithium anode; however, 
this side reaction is inhibited after the addition of 
DTPA, as seen from the lower content of Li2S2. It 
has been observed that DTPA may efficiently 
accelerate the conversion of polysulfide, allowing 
the lithium anode to avoid polysulfide erosion 
[44−45], which is beneficial to the stability of Li-S 
batteries.
Finally, to examine the morphology of the 
cathode and anode in the presence of DTPA 
additives, Figure 4 compares the SEM images of the 
lithium anode and sulfur cathode after 150 cycles in 
the presence of 1.5 wt.% DTPA or not. It has been 
discovered that the surface of the lithium anode 
before cycling is smooth and flat, while after a 
certain number of Li-S battery cycles in the basic 
electrolyte, the anode's surface hardens considerably 
and develops a significant number of gullies, 
making it simple for the electrolyte to enter and 
react with the lithium through the gullies, and this 
will cause the loss of Li and the corrosion of the 
SEI. However, after 150 cycles of a battery 
containing 1.5 wt% DTPA, the surface of the 
lithium anode is uniform and dense with fewer 
gullies, suggesting that the addition of DTPA may 
have some protective effects on the lithium anode. 
As for the sulfur cathode, as compared to the basic 
battery, it should be carefully noted that more 
materials are deposited on the cathode surface of 
1.5 wt.% DTPA-containing battery. According to the 
XPS results, the material should be lithium sulfide, 
which further indicates that the addition of DTPA 
can promote the transformation of polysulfides and 
alleviate the shuttle effect of polysulfides in the 
battery system.

3，3'-二硫代二丙酸作为锂硫电池功能电解质添加剂的研究
摘要
摘要：锂硫电池由于其高理论容量、高能量密度以及较低的原料价格，被认为是目前最具潜力的储能
电池之一。但是在实际应用中，锂硫电池的发展受到充放电过程中的“穿梭效应”和低活性材料利用
率等的限制。本文探究了一种新型电解质添加剂3，3′-二硫代二丙酸(DTPA)对锂硫电池电化学性能提高
的影响机制。结果表明，DTPA添加剂能与分散在电解液中的多硫化物相互作用，促进长链多硫化物
转化为Li2S2和Li2S，从而缓解长链多硫化物向阳极的穿梭行为，提高氧化还原动力学和活性物质的利
用率。添加DTPA 后，锂硫电池的电化学性能得到了提高，在0.5C 下，使用了1.5 wt.% DTPA 作为电
解液添加剂的锂硫电池初始循环容量为1093.4 mA·h/g，经过250次循环后容量为714.8 mA·h/g。此外，
含DTPA的电池具有更高的电化学稳定性，其在2C下相对于0.1C的容量保持率为57.5%。本文的研究
结果证明，DTPA作为电解质添加剂对锂硫电池的性能提高具有显著效果，为减少多硫化物的穿梭效
应以增加锂硫电池的容量提供了一种可行的解决方案。
