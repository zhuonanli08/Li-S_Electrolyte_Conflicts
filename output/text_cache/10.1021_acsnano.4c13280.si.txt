METHODS
Characterizations
The morphologies of all samples were characterized using a field-emission scanning electron 
microscope (SEM) (HITACH 8010). To analyze the morphology of Li metal deposited in 
specific electrolytes, Li||Cu coin cells were disassembled after plating 2 mAh cm−2 of Li at 0.5 
mA cm−2. The samples were gently rinsed with DME inside the glove box and transferred to an 
SEM for morphology analysis. The electrode-electrolyte interphases were analyzed using an 
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectrometer (Thermo Escalab 250xi) and a TEM 
(JEOL, JEM-F200). Raman spectroscopy (Thermo Scientific DXR3xi) was performed to 
investigate the solvation structure of different electrolytes. The sulfur content of SPAN was 
determined using an elemental analyzer (Vario Micro cube). Thermogravimetric analysis of 
electrolytes was conducted using a TGA 2 System (Mettler Toledo) under N2 gas flow (20 
mL/min) at a ramp rate of 10 oC/min from 25 oC to 500 oC using a 100 μL aluminum crucible 
with a predrilled lid.
Electrochemical Measurements
The electrochemical performance was evaluated using CR2025-type coin cells on battery 
testing systems (LANHE CT3001A, Wuhan, China). All coin cells were assembled within an 
argon gas-filled glove box (H2O and O2 < 0.1 ppm). The reversibility of Li plating/stripping in 
different electrolytes was analyzed by assembling Li||Cu cells, with copper foil as the working 
electrode and Li metal foil as the reference/counter electrode. These cells were precycled 
between 0 and 1 V versus Li/Li+ at 50 μA cm−2 for five cycles to eliminate surface impurities. 
Subsequently, 1.0 mAh cm−2 of Li was deposited on the collectors at various areal currents and 
recharged to 1 V to strip the predeposited Li. Coulombic efficiency (CE) is defined as the ratio 
of stripped capacity to plated capacity. Symmetric Li|Li cells were assembled to investigate the 
Li plating/stripping behavior under various current densities and capacities. The 
electrochemical stability of the electrolytes was investigated via linear sweep voltammetry 
(LSV) in the voltage range of 2 to 5.5 V versus Li/Li+ at a scanning rate of 1 mV s−1. A stainless-

3 V vs. Li/Li+. Capacity values were calculated based on the sulfur content in the SPAN powde. 
Regarding the electrochemical testing at various temperatures, all the cells were equilibrated 
for at least 1 hour to reach the targeted temperature. 
Theoretical Calculations
All the calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) were carried out in the 
Gaussian 09 software package. Geometric optimization and frequency calculations were 
performed using B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p).1 The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) 
and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) maps were rendered using the visual 
molecular dynamics (VMD) software based on the files exported from Multiwfn.2,3 The binding 
energy (Eb) between two components was defined as follows: 
      
Eb = Etotal – EA – EB
where Etotal, EA, and EB are the energy of the A–B complexes, the isolate A component, and the 
isolate B component, respectively.
Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using GROMACS 2023 to 
study the designed electrolytes.4 The OPLS-AA force field,5,6 with restrained electrostatic 
potential (RESP) charges generated by Multiwfn,3,7 were used. Bonds involving hydrogen 
atoms were constrained using the linear constraint solver (LINCS) algorithm.8 For the DE 
system (1 M LiFSI in DME), the initial configuration was generated using Packmol,9 containing 
40 LiFSI molecules and 385 DME molecules in a 43.5×43.5×43.5 Å3 box. For the LHCE 
system (1 M LiFSI in DME/TTE (1:1, v/v)), the simulation box contained 40 LiFSI molecules, 
193 DME molecules, and 132 TTE molecules. The DPIE system (1 M LiFSI in dibutyl ether

(DBE)/TTE (1:1, v/v)) consisted of 40 LiFSI molecules, 132 TTE molecules, and 117 DBE 
molecules. Each system was expanded to approximately 5000 atoms for better statistical 
accuracy. The charge of LiFSI was scaled by a factor of 0.7, as per a previous study.10 All the 
electrolyte systems were heated from 10 K to the target temperature (either 298.15 K or 263.15 
K) over 100 ps, followed by a 5 ns equilibration under the isothermal-isobaric (NPT) ensemble 
at 1 bar. A 50 ns production run was then conducted. All the MD simulations were carried out 
with a time step of 1 fs. The Nosé-Hoover thermostat was used to maintain the temperature,11 
with a time constant of 2 ps. The pressure was controlled using the C-rescale approach (as 
implemented in GROMACS) with a coupling constant of 5 ps. Electrostatic interactions were 
treated using the Particle-Mesh-Ewald (PME) method,12,13 with a cutoff distance of 1.3 nm. 
Radial distribution functions (RDFs) and solvation snapshots were analyzed using VMD 
software.

solvated Li+ approaches the Li anode due to the negative electric field, we neglected the 
desolvation energy between Li+ and anions. Consequently, the desolvation energy was 
calculated from the remaining Li+(solvent)n complexes: 64.5% Li+(DME)3 and 35.5% 
Li+(DME)2 for LHCE, and 54.7% Li+(DBE)2, 28.4% Li+(DBE)1, and 16.9% Li+(DBE)3 for 
DPIE. The corresponding binding energies (Figure S20) were used in the following 
calculations:
For LHCE: Edsv = (5.07 eV × 64.5% + 4.59 eV × 35.5%)× 96.5 kJ mol−1 = 472.8 kJ mol−1 
For DPIE: Edsv = (4.23 eV × 54.7% + 2.10 eV × 28.4% + 3.59 eV × 16.9%)× 96.5 kJ mol−1 
= 339.4 kJ mol−1
