Experimental Section  
Preparation of cysteamine-functionalized rGO: Hummers’ method was used to synthesize 
Graphene oxide.1 The GSH cathode material was prepared in a sealed glass vessel by heating 
the mixture of Cysteamine hydrochloride (1 g), ammonium hydroxide (1 ml) and GO (90 ml, 
1 mg/ml) at 90 ºC for 6 h. After several filtration and rinse cycles, the final product was 
freeze-dried to obtain GSH powder with high porosity.  
Preparation of S-GSH composites: For the synthesis of S-GSH composite, the predetermined 
amounts of sulfur (at 1:3 weight ratio of GSH:sulfur) were embedded into the GSH at 185 ºC 
for 10 h under an argon atmosphere and further heated at 245 ºC for 2h to stimulate the ring-
opening polymerization of elemental sulfur (S8) into a linear polysulfane. This product was 
further heated at 155 ºC for 3 h under an Ar stream in tubular furnace using a heating rate of 
10 ºC/min to remove the inert sulfur. The resultant S-GSH showed a dark gray color. The 
targeted high sulfur loading of 60.6 wt % was confirmed by thermogravimetric (TGA analysis 
of S-GSH, Figure S14) and elemental analysis (Table S1). 
Structure Characterization: X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS) was used to explore 
elemental valence (Kratos Axis Ultra Dld, Japan). Field emission scanning electron 
microscope (FESEM) and field emission transmission electron microscope (FETEM) were 
used to observe the morphologies. The FESEM was carried on SU8010 Japan, and FETEM

Electrochemical Performance Evaluation: The slurry material was obtained by dispersing 
acetylene black, S-GSH and polyvinylidene fluoride binder (PVDF) in NMP solvent in the 
weight ratio of 2:7:1 and then uniformly coated on aluminum foil, which was used as current 
collector. The whole electrode was dried under vacuum at 60 °C for 10 h prior to battery 
assembly, and the loading density of sulfur was about 1.0 mg cm-2. 1 M lithium bis 
(trifluoromethanesulfony) imide was dispersed in 1, 2-dimethoxyethane and 1, 3-dioxolane 
with a volume ratio of 1:1 to be used as electrolyte. The additives of 1 wt % LiNO3 and 0.02 
M L2S8 were used to evaluate the battery performance. The Li2S8 solution can be obtained by 
stirring the mixture of sulfur and lithium sulphide at 90 °C.2 Cells were assembled in a 
glovebox filled with Ar gas and tested after aging for 6 h. CHI 660E electrochemical 
workstation (Chenhua, China) was used to perform Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy 
(EIS) test with a frequency range between 0.001 Hz and 100 kHz as well as cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) measurement. The LAND battery testing device (CT 2001A, China) was 
used to test batteries’ rate capability and cycle life at room temperature.  
In-situ UV/Vis spectra tests: The UV Lambda 750 UV/Vis/NIR spectrometer was used to 
operate In-situ UV/Vis spectroscopy. The sulfur loading of cathode electrode was 1 mg/ cm2. 
The electrolyte used 1M LiTFSI in DME:DOL was used as the electrolytes and the counter 
electrode used lithium metal foil, in the middle of which was a hole that was one third the size 
of the cathode. 2025 coin cells were covered with a sealed glass during the assembly. The 
punched cathode should be sealed by a glass cover to form a window, which was no effect on 
the transmitting of light. The separator was wetted with 90 µL. Six different samples with 
different ratios of lithium sulphides and sulfur were prepared, which were denoted according 
to the proportion of the added amount (i.e., Li2Sx, in which x=2–8). For example, to prepare

Computational method: All the computations were conducted using the rb3lyp density 
functional method. All the atoms were simulated by using the 6-31G (d+p) basis set. The 
stationary points as local minima or transition states were characterized through analyzing 
vibrational frequency within the same theoretical level. Except where noted, all the present 
discussions used the gas-phase Gibbs free energy. The whole process adopted Gaussian 09 3 
suite of programs. 
 
CR2025 coin batteries were used to assess the electrochemical performances of S-GSH 
cathodes in Li–S batteries. Cyclic voltammetry method was first employed to explore 
electrochemical kinetic process of the S-GSH cathodes (Fig. S3). Two obvious cathodic peaks 
were observed with each onset potential located at approximately 1.83 and 2.15 V, 
respectively. During the subsequent anodic scan, an obviously strong oxidation peak can be 
seen at 2.54 V, which can be ascribed to the oxidation of lithium sulphide to sulfur. Since the 
second cycle, the multistep reduction mechanism of covalent binding sulfur came into play 
and two reduction peaks around 2.23 and 1.91 V were observed. As showed in Fig. S4A, CV 
tests at various sweeping speeds were conducted to further explore the Li+ diffusion 
coefficient of S-GSH, and with the increasing of sweeping speeds, the cathode-anode peak 
differences increased, indicating an enlarged irreversibility when the current density was 
higher. The Randles–Sevcik equation (eqn (1)) 4 was used to figure the Li+ ion diffusion 
coefficient DLi+.
