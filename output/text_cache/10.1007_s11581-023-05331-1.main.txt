Ammonium silicofluoride as bifunctional electrolyte additive 
to regulate the electrode interface in lithium‑sulfur battery
Chuanxin Zong1 · Jiahao Gu1 · Peng Wang2 · Chenyang Shi1 · Shiyu Shao1 · Mengran Wang1 · Bo Hong1,3 · 
Jianhong Yang4
Received: 26 May 2023 / Revised: 10 November 2023 / Accepted: 24 November 2023 / Published online: 27 December 2023 
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2023
Abstract
The passivation and corrosion of active interface of cathode and lithium anode caused by the uncontrollable deposition of 
discharged product ­(Li2S2/Li2S) in lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries lead to deterioration cycling stability and increase voltage 
polarization. Herein, ammonium silicofluoride ­(NH4)2SiF6 is demonstrated as an effective soluble ammonium salt to enhance 
the solvation of ­Li2S, resulting from the inductive effect of ­NH4
+ cation. In particular, the addition of ­(NH4)2SiF6 stabilizes 
the lithium metal interface through the formation of LiF-rich robust SEI. Due to these advantages, excellent cyclic stabil-
ity is shown in the Li-S cells with ­(NH4)2SiF6-based electrolyte and the long cycle tests of 0.5 C and 2 C. The proposal of 
­(NH4)2SiF6 provides a new avenue to solve the interface problem of Li-S cells and stimulates the research enthusiasm for 
developing soluble ammonium salt.
Keywords  Ammonium silicofluoride · Electrolyte additive · Electrode interface · Lithium-sulfur battery
Introduction
Nowadays, LIBs are considered the most alternative energy 
sources for mid- to large-scale energy storage systems. 
Recently, the lithium-sulfur (Li-S) battery has attracted 
interest for energy storage because of its inherently high 
theoretical specific energy density of 2600 Wh ­g−1 [1, 2]. 
Despite their great potential, the practical application of 
Li-S batteries is still impeded by multiple issues: the poor 
electrical conductivity of sulfur and the large volumetric 
change of sulfur during the cycling process [3, 4]. Typi-
cally, ­S8 is reduced to dissolvable long-chain LiPSs which 
causes shorts in the further reduction process. Worse still, 
the insoluble discharge products ­(Li2S2/Li2S) deposit uncon-
trollably on the surface of cathode, leading to the passivation 
of electrode surface, significantly shortening the lifetime of 
batteries [5, 6]. Besides the electrochemical process above, 
long-chain polysulfide intermediates with high solubility 
permeate into the Li anode caused by concentration gradient 
where they can be chemically reduced to irreversible ­Li2S2/
Li2S deposition, which would hinder the ion/electron trans-
fer for Li redox [6–11]. The so-called LiPS shuttle effect 
would also cause reduced Coulombic efficiency, severe Li 
anode corrosion, low and irreversible sulfur utilization, and 
poor cycle life. From this perspective, regulating the behav-
ior of LiPSs in the Li-S batteries is of great significance for 
the Li-S battery’s performance.
Many strategies, such as cathode design, electrolyte for-
mula, and lithium anode modification, have been utilized to 
solve the problems mentioned above. Such modifications, 
however, on the electrolyte-electrode interfaces are unable to 
completely drive the dissolved LiPSs away from the cathode

provide alternative chemical routes for preventing the shut-

passivation of electrodes by ­Li2S, which decrease the sulfur 
utilization, increase polarization, and decrease cycling sta-

electrolyte may be a feasible settlement. Therefore, ­NH4NO3

In the present work, ammonium silicofluoride ­(NH4)2SiF6 
is proposed as an electrolyte additive, which can be dis-
solved in the basic electrolyte without any other additives. 
The ­(NH4)2SiF6 can direct a promising pathway toward high 
sulfur utilization through the inductive effect of NH+
4 on Li-S 
bond of ­Li2S, resulting in accelerating the sluggish conver-
sion kinetics and alleviating the passivation of cathode sur-
face. In addition, the solubility of ­Li2S can help stabilize the 
Li metal since ­(NH4)2SiF6 enriches LiF SEI compositions. 
Consequently, both capacity and stability are enhanced for 
Li-S cells with ­(NH4)2SiF6 additive.
Results and discussion
Most of ­NH4-based additives are reported to be insoluble in 
basic lean electrolyte, which requires high-ε solvents, such 
as dimethyl acetamide (DMA) [15] and dimethyl sulfoxide 
(DMSO) [16] to improve dissolution. However, ­(NH4)2SiF6 
can significantly be dissolved in the base electrolyte with-
out any other additives and additional heating procedure. 
Mayer bond order of S-H bonds between ­(NH4)2SiF6 and 
­Li2S is calculated to detect the interaction between them [17, 
18]. The Mayer bond order is the most important parameter 
which determines the dissolution behavior of substance due 
to the energy-driven complexation process. As displayed in 
Fig. 1a, the Mayer bond order values (0.562) between each 
S and H are not able to form stable bonds, but the values are 
greater than zero, demonstrating strong interaction between 
NH+
4 and S2−(N-H…S2−) via hydrogen binding [17, 19]. 
Furthermore, the visual experiment (Fig. 1b) confirms the 
theoretical calculation results [20]. According to the opti-
cal picture of the blank electrolyte and ­(NH4)2SiF6 -added 
electrolyte after ­Li2S dissolved, it is confirmed that the elec-
trolyte with ­(NH4)2SiF6 has good solubility for ­Li2S with 
no precipitation, while ­Li2S is insoluble and the solution is 
turbid in the base electrolyte [21]. Nuclear magnetic reso-
nance (NMR) spectroscopy is applied to probe the molecu-
lar interaction between ­Li2S in the blank electrolyte with 
the addition of ­(NH4)2SiF6 in the dissolution process. As 
displayed in Fig. 1c, when ­(NH4)2SiF6 coexists with ­Li2S, 
the 1H resonance of NH+
4 cation shifts to the low-frequency 
region (from 4.4 to −1.63 ppm). The shifting of 1H reso-
nance confirms the strong interaction between electronega-
tive ­S2− (from ­Li2S) and closely aggregated positive ­H+ 
(from NH+
4 ) produces electron shielding to H atom [21].

degree of passivation of electrodes. The diffusion coefficient 
of Li ions can be characterized by the cyclic voltammetry 
(CV) tests at various sweep rates to estimate the diffusion 
coefficient of Li-ions in the second discharge plateau. And

To a certain extent, the rate capability reflects the redox 
reaction kinetics of the Li-S battery. The rate capability 
of Li-S cells with different concentrations of ­(NH4)2SiF6 
was performed at 0.2 to 2.0 C (Fig. 2d). As demonstrated 
in Fig. 2d, the best performance is shown with 0.5 wt% of 
­(NH4)2SiF6. With current rates of 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 C, the 
discharge capacities of the battery with 0.5 wt% ­(NH4)2SiF6 
convey a capacity of 1018.4, 868.7, 801.4, and 762.5 mAh/g, 
respectively, which were much higher than those of the bat-
teries with other contents of ­(NH4)2SiF6 at the same cur-
rent densities. Once the current density is turned back to 
0.5 C, the specific capacity is recovered to 917.1 mAh ­g−1, 
as well as the best cyclic stability in subsequent cycles. In a 
word, the batteries with 0.5 wt% of ­(NH4)2SiF6 demonstrate 
excellent electrochemical performances. The poor electro-
chemical performance of batteries with higher content of 
additive may originate from high viscosity [30]. Therefore, 
the following discussion will focus on a comparison of the 
electrochemical performances of batteries without and with 
0.5% ­(NH4)2SiF6. Benefiting from the positive effects of the 
additive ­(NH4)2SiF6 on activation of ­Li2S, the cyclic perfor-
mance of the batteries without and with 0.5% of ­(NH4)2SiF6 
was further tested as shown in Fig. 2e. The 0.5 wt% of 
­(NH4)2SiF6-containing batteries delivers a superior initial 
capacity of 1133.3 mAh ­g−1, corresponding to a desirable 
areal capacity of 1.5 mAh ­cm−2. After 350 cycles, a capac-
ity of 733.1 ­mAhg−1 was still maintained with a Coulom-
bic efficiency of over 99%, which is much higher than the 
battery without ­(NH4)2SiF6. For practical applications, low 
electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio and high sulfur loading must 
be considered. The batteries with an areal loading of 5.2 
mg ­cm−2 and E/S ratios of 10:1 are assembled to evalu-
ate the practical application of ­(NH4)2SiF6. Based on the 
consideration above, the cycle performance of the battery 
with high sulfur loading and low E/S ratio electrolyte is 
assembled and evaluated under 0.2 C (Fig. 2f). Under lean 
electrolyte (E/S = 10:1), 0.5wt% of ­(NH4)2SiF6-containing 
batteries delivered 1261.7 ­mAhg−1 and retained 703.5 mAh 
­g−1 after 120 cycles. In contrast, the batteries without any 
additive displayed a faster deterioration and worse Coulom-
bic efficiency.

Generally, the lithium anode is unstable in the high-ε 
electrolyte due to the high reactivity of electrolyte [34, 35]. 
Based on frontier molecular orbital theory, the LUMO level 
of solvent is a dominant factor that determines the property 
of an electrolyte. Therefore, as demonstrated in Fig. 4a–c, 
the LUMO values of ­(NH4)2SiF6 are more negative than 
those of solvent in the electrolyte. During the initial dis-
charging process, ­(NH4)2SiF6 may trigger the spontaneous 
reduction at the anode interface and produce a stable and 
thin interfacial protective film (SEI) on the metal surface, 
which is beneficial for protecting the lithium anode and 
decreasing the polarization of the battery. From compari-
son with surface status of ­F1s (Fig. 4d), the patterns suggest 
that ­(NH4)2SiF6 additive in situ generated a stable interfacial 
protective layer with more LiF on lithium anode during the 
cycling process. The formation of a robust SEI layer with 
rich LiF component on Li metal anode from the reduction 
of ­SiF6
− can effectively enhance the stability of Li metal 
and promote Li plating/stripping reversibility. To explore the 
potential of ­(NH4)2SiF6 for stabilization of the Li interfacial 
layer, Li|Li symmetric batteries were assembled with and 
without ­(NH4)2SiF6 additive (Fig. 4e). At a current density 
of 0.5 mA ­cm−2, the symmetric battery with ­(NH4)2SiF6 
additive achieved very stable reversible lithium stripping/
plating for over 600 h with less overpotential, which is much 
longer than that of the batteries without ­(NH4)2SiF6 addi-
tive and the overpotential of the latter surpassed 1.0V after 
300 cycles. The reversibility and interfacial stability of the

presses the shuttling of LiPSs.
Conclusion
In summary, this study has discovered and demonstrated the 
bifunctional effect of ­(NH4)2SiF6 as an additive for stable 
Li-S batteries. The additive is found to dissolve the final 
discharge product ­Li2S and effectively retard surface pas-
sivation of both the cathode and anode, thereby improv-
ing battery stability. During the charge/discharge process, 
­(NH4)2SiF6 is beneficial for producing a protective and sta-
ble SEI layer with reduced impedance, resulting in stable 
cycling of lithium metal anode. Based on the advantages 
above, the batteries with 0.5 wt% of ­(NH4)2SiF6 additive 
exhibit 733.1 ­mAhg−1 after 300 cycles at 0.5 C, exhibiting 
competitive electrochemical performance. This study offers 
valuable insights for the future development of practical Li-S 
batteries and highlights the potential for innovation in the 
Li-S battery field.
