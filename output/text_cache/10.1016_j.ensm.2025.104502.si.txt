1. Experimental section 
1.1. Raw materials 
All the raw materials including sulfur (S), lithium sulfide (Li2S), lithium 
bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), 
multi-walled carbon nanotubes (CNTs), poly(vinylidenefluoride) (PVDF), N-
methylpyrrolidone (NMP), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), 1,2-
dimethoxyethane (DME), di-isopropyl sulfide (DIPS), carbon papers, aluminum (Al) 
foils, copper (Cu) foils, polypropylene (PP) membranes (Celgard 2500), lithium (Li) 
metal foils, 2025 coin cell shells, and 2032 coin cell shells were purchased from 
commercial sources and directly used without further purification. 
 
1.2. Evaluation of thermodynamics and kinetics under different E/S ratios 
1.2.1. Sulfur cathode preparation 
Sulfur and CNT were first mixed in a batch with a mass ratio of 7:3. The mixture 
was then ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a slurry. 
The obtained slurry was then coated on Al foils and fully dried at 60°C for 24 h. The 
sulfur cathode was cut into small disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm. The sulfur content 
was 63 wt.% and the areal sulfur loading was 4.0 or 7.0 mgS cm−2. 
 
1.2.2. Weakly-solvating electrolyte preparation  
The weakly-solvating electrolyte was mixed solvents of DME/DOL/DIPS 
(1:0.75:0.25, molar ratio) with 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 5.0 wt.% LiNO3. To prepare the 
Li2S8 electrolyte with a sulfur concentration of 1, 2, 4, or 6 M[S], 0.625, 1.25, 2.5, or 
3.75 mmol Li2S and 4.375, 8.75, 17.5, or 26.25 mmol S were mixed in 5 mL weakly-
solvating electrolyte at a molar ratio of 1:7 respectively, and then stirred at 60°C. All 

<<PAGE 2>>
 
 
SI2 
the above procedures were conducted in an Ar-filled glove box with water and oxygen 
contents below 0.1 ppm. 
 
1.2.3. Weakly-solvating Li‒S cell assembly and electrochemical measurement 
Weakly-solvating Li–S cells under a series of different E/S ratios were assembled 
and used for galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests. Concretely, the 
sulfur cathodes with a diameter of 13.0 mm were used as the cathode, lithium metal 
foils with a diameter of 15.0 mm and a thickness of 50 μm were adopted as the anode, 
PP membranes with a diameter of 19.0 mm were used as the separator, and the 
electrolyte was the weakly-solvating electrolyte. The E/S ratio was 12, 8, 6, 5, or 4.5 
μL mg−1. All the components were sealed with standard 2032 coin cell shells and all 
the above procedures were conducted in an argon-filled glove box with water and 
oxygen contents below 0.1 ppm. 
GITT tests were performed using the weakly-solvating Li–S cells to locate the 
thermodynamic and kinetic voltages. The GITT curves were obtained by a series of 
galvanostatic discharge or charge processes of 6.0 min at 0.1 C (1 C = 1672 mA g−1) 
followed by 60.0 min rest at each step. All the electrochemical tests were performed on 
a Neware multichannel battery cycler. 
 
1.3. Polarization decoupling 
1.3.1. Evaluation of the influence of lithium anode 
Li | Li symmetric cells were assembled and used to evaluate the polarization of the 
lithium anode. Concretely, two pieces of lithium metal foil with a diameter of 15.0 mm 
and thickness of 50 μm were used as the electrodes, and PP membranes with a diameter 
of 19.0 mm were used as the separator. 40 μL of 1 or 4 M[S] Li2S8 electrolyte was added 

<<PAGE 3>>
 
 
SI3 
to the symmetric cells, respectively. All the components were sealed with standard 2025 
coin-type cells. The Li | Li symmetric cells were galvanostatically cycled at 0.72 mA 
cm−2 and 3.60 mAh cm−2 which corresponded to the full-cell conditions. 
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was carried out on a Solartron 1470E 
electrochemical workstation. EIS was performed using the potentiostatic mode at the 
open circuit voltage. A sinusoidal voltage with an amplitude of 10 mV and a frequency 
range from 0.1 Hz to 10 kHz was applied. The intercept at the Z’ axis was regarded as 
the ohmic resistance. The voltage at the 3rd cycle of the Li | Li symmetric cells was 
regarded as the total polarization, while the ohmic polarization was obtained through 
the ohmic resistance multiplying the current. The kinetic polarization of the anode was 
obtained through the total polarization minus the ohmic polarization. 
 
1.3.2. Decoupling of activation, concentration, and ohmic polarization. 
A GITT‒EIS method was developed to decouple the total polarization (ηtotal) into 
activation (ηac), concentration (ηcon), and ohmic polarizations (ηohm) in weakly-solvating 
Li–S batteries under a series of E/S ratios. The weakly-solvating Li–S cells were 
assembled following the same procedure as in section 1.2.3. The parameters of GITT 
were also the same as above involving a series of galvanostatic discharge processes of 
6.0 min at a current density of 0.1 C followed by 60.0 min rest at each step. An 
additional EIS measurement was conducted before each galvanostatic step to obtain the 
ohmic resistance (Rohm). The EIS measurements were conducted in a frequency range 
from 0.1 Hz to 10 kHz. The ohmic resistance was obtained from the intercept at the Z’ 
axis. 
The ηtotal was determined as the voltage difference between the thermodynamic 
voltage and the kinetic voltage of the weakly-solvating Li–S batteries. The voltage 

<<PAGE 4>>
 
 
SI4 
recovery during the rest step in GITT was resulted from the concentration gradient built 
in the discharge step and was adopted as the ηcon. ηohm was determined by multiplying 
the ohmic resistance obtained through the EIS measurements with the applied current 
density. ηac was obtained though ηac = ηtotal ‒ ηohm ‒ ηcon. The three kinetic polarizations 
of the sulfur cathode during the discharge process under different E/S ratios were 
further evaluated at three stages regarding sulfur dissolution, Li2S nucleation, and Li2S 
growth, respectively. 
 
1.4. Measurements of interfacial charge transfer activation energy 
 
Three-electrode Li | Li2S8 cells were assembled using 2, 4, or 6 M[S] Li2S8 
electrolyte. Commercial carbon papers were used as the working electrode and lithium 
metal foils with a diameter of 16.0 mm and thickness of 600 μm were adopted as the 
counter electrode. Two pieces of PP membranes with a diameter of 19.0 mm were used 
as the separator and a Cu@Li reference electrode was placed between the two 
separators to avoid the contact with the other two electrodes. 70 μL of 2, 4, or 6 M[S] 
Li2S8 electrolyte was added to the Li | Li2S8 cells to guarantee sufficient electrolyte 
infiltration. 
The three-electrode Li | Li2S8 cells were discharged to the Li2S nucleation stage 
which is at the beginning of the second voltage plateau at the current of 0.5 mA. EIS 
measurements under 303, 308, 313, 318, or 323 K were carried out on the Solartron 
1470E electrochemical workstation. Distribution of relaxation time (DRT) analysis was 
conducted by applying the MATLAB GUI based DRT tools which are developed by 
Ciucci’s groups.[1] The parameters for DRT were based on the Gaussian discretization 
method with a regularization parameter of 10−4 and FWHM control of 0.5, where the 
inductive data was neglected during fitting.

1.5. Evaluation of Li2S nucleation and deposition behaviors 
1.5.1 Morphology evaluation of Li2S nucleation and deposition 
Li | Li2S8 cells with a sulfur concentration of 2, 4, or 6 M[S] Li2S8 electrolyte were 
assembled. Commercial carbon papers were used as the cathodic current collector and 
lithium metal foils with a diameter of 16.0 mm and thickness of 600 μm were adopted 
as the anode. PP membranes with a diameter of 19.0 mm were used as the separator. 75 
μL 2 M[S], 37.5 μL 4 M[S], or 25 μL 6 M[S] Li2S8 electrolyte were added into the Li | 
Li2S8 cells to control the active materials to be the same.  
The Li | Li2S8 cells were discharged to the Li2S nucleation stage or the end of the 
Li2S deposition stage, respectively. The discharged Li | Li2S8 cells were disassembled 
and the carbon papers were taken out and washed with pure DME until the color of the 
DME was not changed. The morphology of Li2S was observed on a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM, FEI-Apreo C, FEI Ltd., USA) operated at 5.0 kV. The Li2S 
nucleation size and deposition thickness were quantitatively measured by the Digital 
Micrograph software via the difference of the graph intensities.  
 
1.5.2 Evaluation of Li2S deposition capacity 
Potentiostatic intermittent titration technique (PITT) was performed using the Li | 
Li2S8 cells to locate the voltage of Li2S deposition. The cells were potentiostatically 
discharged at 2.30, 2.25, 2.20, and 2.15 V for 10.0 h and then potentiostatically 
discharged at 2.10 and 2.05 V for 50.0 h. The Li2S deposition occurs at 2.10 V and the 
corresponding deposition capacity was obtained by integration and compared under 
different sulfur concentrations.

1.6. Performance of lean-electrolyte Li‒S batteries using the LiFSI electrolyte 
The LiFSI electrolyte was prepared using mixed solvents of DME/DOL/DIPS 
(1:0.75:0.25, molar ratio) with 0.8 mol L−1 LiFSI, 0.2 mol L−1 LiTFSI, and 5.0 wt.% 
LiNO3. The LiTFSI electrolyte was prepared using mixed solvents of DME/DOL/DIPS 
(1:0.75:0.25, molar ratio) with 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 5.0 wt.% LiNO3. 4.0 mgS cm−2 
Li‒S cells with the LiFSI electrolyte or the LiTFSI electrolyte were assembled at the 
E/S ratio of 4.5 μL mg−1 and the rest configurations were kept the same with the above 
weakly-solvating electrolyte Li‒S cells. To evaluate the rate performances, the Li‒S 
cells were activated at 0.05 C for two cycles and then cycled at 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.2, and 
0.1 C for 3 cycles in order. The Li‒S cells were cycled between 1.70–2.60 V on the 
Neware multichannel battery cycler. 
To evaluate the long cycling performance, 4.0 mgS cm−2 Li‒S cells at the E/S ratio 
of 5 μL mg−1 were assembled. The LiFSI electrolyte or the LiTFSI electrolyte was used 
as the electrolyte, respectively. The Li‒S cells were activated at 0.05 and 0.1 C for two 
cycles and then galvanostatically cycled at 0.2 C between 1.70–2.60 V on the Neware 
multichannel battery cycler. 
The lean-electrolyte kinetics under practical high sulfur loading conditions was 
further evaluated by assembling Li‒S cells with the sulfur loading of 7.0 mgS cm−2. The 
LiFSI electrolyte or the LiTFSI electrolyte was used as the electrolyte, respectively. 
The E/S ratio was 5 μL mg−1. The 7.0 mgS cm−2 Li‒S cells were activated at 0.05 C for 
two cycles and then galvanostatically cycled at 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, and 0.25 C for 3 cycles. 
The 7.0 mgS cm−2 Li‒S cells were then cycled at 0.1 C after the rate test. The 7.0 mgS 
cm−2 Li‒S cells were cycled between 1.70–2.60 V on the Neware multichannel battery 
cycler.

1.7. Li–S pouch cell assembly and evaluation 
Sulfur cathodes were cut into 4.0 cm × 7.0 cm with a sulfur loading of 7.5–7.9 mgS 
cm−2 on a single side. Lithium anodes were fabricated by rolling lithium metal foils on 
both sides of Cu foils and then cut into electrodes of the same size as the sulfur cathodes. 
The thickness of the lithium foil was 75 μm on a single side. The separator used in 
pouch cells was PP membranes or PE membranes. The pouch cell electrolyte was the 
LiFSI electrolyte or the LiTFSI electrolyte. 
To prove the kinetic promotion effect of LiFSI, Li‒S pouch cells were assembled 
for performances evaluation. Five sulfur cathodes (7.8 mgS cm−2 on a single side) and 
six lithium anodes were stacked layer-by-layer and separated by PP membranes. All the 
electrodes were set in parallel connection and then put in a package. Then, the LiFSI 
electrolyte or the LiTFSI electrolyte was injected into the pouch cells and the E/S ratio 
was 2.8 gele gS−1. After that, the 3 Ah Li‒S pouch cells were vacuumed (‒65 kPa) and 
sealed for further electrochemical tests. The 3 Ah Li‒S pouch cells were discharged at 
0.025 C at the 1st cycle for activation after resting for 12.0 h. Then the Li‒S pouch cells 
were discharged at 0.05, 0.08, 0.1, and 0.15 C for two cycles and the charge current 
density was kept at 0.025 C (1 C = 1000 mA g−1). 
Furthermore, 5 Ah Li‒S pouch cells were assembled with nine sulfur cathodes (7.9 
mgS cm−2 on a single side) and ten lithium anodes using PE separators and the E/S ratio 
was 2.5 gele gS−1. The 5 Ah pouch cells were discharged at 0.05 C after resting for 12.0 
h. The 8 Ah Li‒S pouch cell was assembled with fifteen sulfur cathodes (7.5 mgS cm−2 
on a single side) and sixteen lithium anodes using PE separators and the E/S ratio was 
2.8 gele gS−1. The 8 Ah Li‒S pouch cell was cycled at 0.025 C after resting for 12.0 h.
