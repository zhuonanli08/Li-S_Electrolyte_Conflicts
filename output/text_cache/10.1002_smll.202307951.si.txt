Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) in the frequency range of 10 mHz to 1.0 MHz with an amplitude of 10 mV was used to evaluate the electrolyte stability and resistance of the cell systems. The Li||Li symmetric cells were measured by EIS after 10, 24, 48, and 72 h. Transference number, LSV, and EIS were performed using a Bio-logic VMP3 instrument at room temperature.
Galvanostatic experiments were carried out using CR2032-type coin cells on a WBCS3000L battery cycler system (Wonatech, Korea) at a constant temperature of 25 °C or 50 °C. The CE of the lithium electrode was examined using Li||Cu cells with polypropylene film (PP, Wellcos Corporation) as a separator, and Cu served as the substrate for Li metal deposition. The current density for the Li metal plating/stripping was set at 0.25mA cm−2. During each cycle, 0.25 mAh cm−2 of Li metal was deposited on the Cu substrate at various current densities and then stripped until the potential reached 1.0 V versus Li/Li+. The Li||SPAN coin cells were monitored in galvanostatic mode within a voltage range of 1.0–3.0 V versus Li/Li+. The Li||SPAN cells were first cycled at 0.1 C for two cycles and then cycled at 0.3 C (1.0 C ≈ 1672 mA gS−1 based on a percentage of sulfur in SPAN cathode materials). Each coin cell was filled with 70 µL of the electrolyte. The tested coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box.
Characterization of electrodes
The morphology and surface components were investigated by SEM and XPS. Before analysis, Cu substrates with lithium deposited were immersed in pure DME or DMM solvent for 12 h, followed by washing with pure DME or DMM several times to remove the salt residue, and then dried in the vacuum chamber of an Ar-filled glove box for 24 h. SEM images were taken using a Hitachi S4800 instrument at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV and an absorbed current of 10 µA. XPS measurements were conducted to characterize the chemical species using a K-alpha X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (Thermo Fisher) with a monochromatic Al Kα source (1486.7 eV) for excitation. All spectra were referenced using the C 1s line at 285.0 V for comparison. All samples were prepared in a glove box and transported to SEM and XPS instruments in a glass container filled with Ar gas.

Quantum mechanical calculations
Quantum mechanical calculations were performed using DFT (B3LYP) methods in the Gaussian 16 package, with the 6–311++g(d,p) basis set [2]. Among the various optimized conformations, the structure with the lowest electronic energy was used.

Classical molecular dynamics	
The solvation structures present in the DOL:DME and DX:DMM based electrolytes were further investigated by classical molecular dynamics (CMD) simulations. For this, CMD simulations of the 1.0 м LiTFSI in DOL:DME (1:1, by vol), 0.4 м LiTFSI in DX:DMM (1:1, by vol), and 0.1 м Li2S8 in DOL:DME (1:1, by vol) or DX:DMM (1:1, by vol) systems was carried out. The number of salt and solvent molecules for each system is summarized in Table S4. The force field parameters for Li+ and S82– were taken from Hu et al.,[4] where the employed force fields have been shown to reasonably reproduce experimental densities for different types of lithium polysulfides. For the case of TFSI−, the force field parameters from Lopes et al. were employed.[5] The force fields for the solvent molecules were obtained from OPLS.[6] The partial charges of the ions were scaled to 0.8 times their original values to mimic atomic polarizability.[7] 
The starting structure was created by the Packmol program.[8] The simulation box was first subjected to a 1 ns heating period from 0 to 800 K. This was followed by a 1 ns cooling stage from 800 to 298 K. Both the heating and cooling were performed at the NVT ensemble. This was followed by NPT simulations at 298 K and 1 atm for 10 ns to obtain an equilibrated box size. Further production runs were carried with the NPT ensemble for 40 ns, where the coordinates were saved every 0.1 ps. All CMD simulations were carried out using the AMBER 22 software package.[9]  The temperature was controlled with a Langevin thermostat and the parameter for collision frequency was set to 1.0 ps–1. All hydrogen bonds were kept fixed with the SHAKE algorithm and a time step of 1.0 fs was used. A cutoff distance of Å 12 for non-bonded interactions and the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) method was used to take into account long-range electrostatic interactions.
