1. Experimental section 
1.1. Materials 
Polyacrylonitrile 
(PAN, 
Mw=150,000), 
sulfur 
(S), 
acetylene 
black, 
N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP), poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, HSV-300), 
ethylene carbonate (EC), diethyl carbonate (DEC), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DOL), 
1,2-diamethoxyethane (DME), diisopropyl ether (DIPE), lithium hexafluorophosphate 
(LiPF6), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)imide (LiTFSI), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), 
polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard 2400), and aluminum (Al) foil were purchased 
from commercial sources and used without further purification in this contribution. 
Lithium (Li) metal foils (50 or 600 μm in thickness) were purchased from China 
Energy Lithium Co., Ltd and were used as received. Li metal foil (50 μm) was rolled 
on the copper foil of 9 μm before use. 
 
1.2. Synthesis of SPAN 
PAN powder and sulfur powder were mixed with a weight ratio of 1:4 and ball 
milled under 300 rpm for 4.0 h. The mixture was then annealed at 350oC for 6.0 h 
with a ramping rate of 5oC min−1 under argon protection to obtain SPAN materials. As 
a control, PAN powder without sulfur was treated using the same method and denoted 
as PANC. 
 
1.3. Fabrication of SPAN cathodes 
The SPAN cathodes were prepared by casting SPAN slurry on Al foil. The

SPAN slurry was prepared by mixing SPAN powder, acetylene black, and PVDF 
(mass ratio 8:2:1) in NMP and stirring for 24.0 h. The electrodes were dried at 60oC 
overnight and then cut into discs with 13.0 mm in diameter. The areal sulfur loading 
of the working electrode is 1.0 mg cm‒2. 
 
1.4. Materials characterization 
The sulfur content of the samples was analyzed using an elemental analyzer 
(Vario EL Ш, Elementar, Germany) with the mode of CHNS. The precision of the 
elemental analysis measurements is ≤ 0.1%. Raman spectra were obtained using a 
LabRAM HR800 Raman spectrophotometer (Horiba Jobin Yvon, Japan) with a He–
Ne laser excitation at 532 nm. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the samples were 
recorded on an X-ray powder diffractometer (D8 Advance, Germany) at 40.0 kV and 
120 mA with Cu-Kα radiation. The surface composition was confirmed by X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., 
USA). The morphologies were characterized by a JSM 7401F (JEOL Ltd., Japan) 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) operated at 3.0 kV. 
 
1.5. Electrochemical measurements 
Electrochemical performance was evaluated using standard 2025 coin cells with 
40.0 μL electrolyte in an argon-filled glove box with oxygen and water contents 
below 0.1 ppm. The diameter of Li metal anode was 16.0 mm for thick Li (600 μm in 
thickness) and 15.0 mm for thin Li (50 μm in thickness), respectively. PP membranes

served as the separators. 
Three kinds of electrolyte were employed in this contribution. The EC/DEC 
electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiPF6 dissolved in EC/DEC (1:1, by volume) 
mixed solvents. The DOL/DME electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI and 2.0 
wt.% LiNO3 dissolved in DOL/DME (1:1, by volume) mixed solvents. The 
mixed-DIPE electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI dissolved in 
DIPE/DOL/DME (2:1:1, by volume) mixed solvents with 5.0% (in volume) EC added 
as additive. 
Li | SPAN full cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.0–3.0 V under 
different C-rates (1.0 C = 1672 mA g[S]–1). Li | Cu half cells were assembled with Li 
foil as the anode and Cu foil (with a diameter of 17.0 mm) as the cathode. 600 μm Li 
and 50 μm Li were used in Li | Cu half cells for cycling and SEM characterization, 
respectively. The current density was 1.0 mA cm−2 with a capacity of 1.0 mAh cm−2. 
The battery cycling tests were all conducted using a LAND multichannel battery 
cycler (Wuhan LAND Electronics Co., Ltd.). The electrochemical impedance 
spectroscopy (EIS) tests were conducted on a Solartron 1470E electrochemical 
workstation with the potentiostatic mode over a frequency range from 0.1 Hz to 10.0 
kHz.
