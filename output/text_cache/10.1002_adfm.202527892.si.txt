Preparation of cathodes: The cathode materials were prepared by mixing CNT/S, acetylene black, and polyvinylende fluoride (PVDF) binder in a weight ratio of 8:1:1 in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The slurry was then coated onto the aluminum foil and dried at 50 °C for 12 h. Afterwards, the dried coated aluminum foil was punched into disks with a diameter of 14 mm for Li-S coin cell use, and the loading of the active-material was 1.1 and 4.2 mg cm-2.

Preparation of electrolytes: CE, i.e., 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.2 M LiNO3 in DOL/DME (1:1, by vol.), LWSE, i.e., 0.5 M LiTFSI and 0.2 M LiNO3 in MTBE (Methyl tert-Butyl Ether)/DME (1:1, by vol.), and MBE, i.e., 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.2 M LiNO3 in MBE/DME (1:1, by vol.). 
Li2S and S (1:7, by mol.) were added into the DME or DME/MTBE (1:1, by vol.) electrolyte to prepare the Li2S8 electrolyte. Li2S and S (1:5, by mol.) were added into the DME or DME/MTBE (1:1, by vol.) electrolyte to prepare the Li2S6 electrolyte. All were prepared in an argon-filled glove box.

Electrochemical measurements: All the cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box below 1.0 ppm. CR-2025 type coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box using the CNT/S-coated aluminum foil as the cathode, lithium foil as anode, and Celgard 2400 as the separator. The linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) cells are assembled using stainless steel as the electrodes. All the cells are assembled in an Ar-filled glove box with oxygen concentration below 1.0 ppm.
Both the coin cells and pouch cells were tested in a galvanostatic mode within the voltage window of 1.7-2.8 V using a Neware battery test system. Cyclic voltammogram curves of various pure solvents and EIS measurements of the coin cells were performed on CHI604E electrochemical workstation. EIS measurements of the coin cells were performed in the frequency range of 100 kHz-0.01 Hz. Cyclic voltammogram curves of various pure solvents using a carbon-coated current collector as the working electrode and Li as the counter and reference electrode with a scan rate of 0.5 mV s−1.
The Li2S deposition experiment was measured with assembled cells using carbon paper as the cathode and lithium foil as the anode. In the cell assembly process, 50 𝜇L electrolyte containing 0.2 M Li2S8 was added on the carbon paper side and another 50 𝜇L electrolyte was dropped on the Li foil side. The cell was first discharged at constant current to the 2.06 V and then discharged with the constant voltage at 2.05 V until the current was below 10−5 A for the nucleation and deposition of Li2S.

Characterizations: The morphologies of Li depositions and Li2S depositions were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7001F). The SEI components of Li-S cells were further characterized through X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, AXIS, SUPRA+). X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded in the range of 2θ = 5-60° on a desktop X-ray diffractometer (RIGAKU-Miniflex II) with Cu Kα radiation (λ= 1.5406 Å). Ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry (UV, PE Lambda 950) was employed to detect the signal change of Li2S6 in solution. The Raman spectra were recorded by a confocal microprobe Raman spectrometer (Renishaw Raman spectroscopy). Fourier-transformed infrared (FT-IR) transmission spectroscopy was carried out in a Bruker Equinox 55 spectrometer between 4000 and 400 cm-1. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were obtained on aBRUKER 600 Nuclear Magnetic Resonance spectrometer. WAXS measurements were performed on Xeuss 2.0 (Xenocs, France) with a Cu X-ray source of 30 W (wave length of 0.1542 nm) and a detector of Pilatus 300 K (Dectris). For the in situ XRD and in situ Raman test, customized mold cells equipped with Be or quartz as the testing window were employed respectively to monitor the sulfur cathode during in-situ charge/discharge processes at a current density of 0.2 C, which was conducted via XRD (Rigaku Miniflex 600) and Renishaw Raman microscope (532 nm laser) respectively.

Figure S18. XRD patterns of (a) sulfur powder, (b) CNT and (c) CNT/S composite. The standard XRD patterns of α-sulfur and β-sulfur phases are provided as reference. It can be found that, the commercially received sulfur powder is of α-sulfur (Orthorhombic phase). During cooling process of CNT/S composite, β-sulfur crystals formed first. As the temperature continues to drop below the transition point of 95.6°C, β-sulfur could all transform into α-sulfur thermodynamically. While, due to the energy barrier for nucleation of α-sulfur and relatively sluggish transition from solid β-sulfur to solid α-sulfur, the transformaton of pre-formed β-sulfur into α-sulfur was dynamically delayed during cooling, resulting in the co-existence of α-sulfur and β-sulfur phases in CNT/S composite, where the β-sulfur would transform into α-sulfur at room temperature.

Figure S23. Comparison between the first and last Raman spectra (21th) of sulfur cathodes during in-situ Raman collected in (a) LWSE and (b) CE. The Raman spectra during in-situ Raman were collected for 21 times at fixed time intervals, and the first and last Raman spectra corresponding the starting and terminal points of discharge and charging processes, respectively, where the three dominant Raman peaks for the Raman spectra of sulfur cathodes are all attributed to the S8 element species. It can be found that, the recovery degree of the peak intensity corresponding S8 element species in LWSE is observably higher than the case in CE, which indicated a higher retention rate of S8 element species after discharge/charge processes in LWSE, than the case in CE.
