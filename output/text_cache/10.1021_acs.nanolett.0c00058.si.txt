S1. Experimental procedures
2
Preparations of S nanoparticles and liquid electrolytes
3
The S nanoparticles for both in situ TEM and electrochemical tests were synthesized as 
4
below. Briefly, 0.580 g of sodium sulphide (Na2S) powder was added into 25.0 mL of 
5
ultrapure water and stirred until Na2S solution became clear. Then, 0.720 g of S powder was 
6
added to the prepared Na2S solution and stirred until the solution became a transparent with 
7
dark orange color. The prepared Na2Sx solution was slowly added into 500 mL of 0.4 M 
8
formic acid solution dropwise and stirred for 2 hours. Finally, light yellowish S powder was 
9
collected by filtration method and rinsed using ultrapure water. The collected S powder was 
10
dried in a vacuum oven chamber at 50 °C for 10 h. As shown in Figure S18a, the size of 
11
spherical sulfur nanoparticles ranges from 50 nm to 1μm. The crystal structure of the sulfur 
12
nanoparticle (α-phase, Fddd (70), JCPDS Card No. 00-024-0733) was confirmed by X-ray 
13
diffraction (XRD) (Figure S18b). Two types of electrolyte solution were prepared for 
14
comparative TEM observation; one consists of 1.0 M bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide 
15
lithium salt (LITFSI) in dioxolane and dimethoxyethane (DOL:DME=1:1, v/v, referred as IL-
16
free electrolyte), and the other contains 1.0 M LITFSI in the ionic liquid (IL) containing 
17
mixture (DOL:DME:1-butyl 1-methyl pyrrolidinium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide 
18
(PYR14TFSI)=1:1:2, v/v/v).
19

20

21
Briefly, 0.58 g of sodium sulfide (Alfa Aesar, Na2S) was added in 25 mL of ultrapure water 
22
and stirred with a magnetic stirrer until the solution became clear. Then, 0.72 g of sulfur

14
and freeze dried. The lyophilized powder sample was heat-treated in a tube furnace at 155 °C 
15
for 18 h under an Ar atmosphere.

19

21
electrolyte were drop-casted onto overlapped TEM grids with the graphene coated sides 
22
facing each other. After removing extra electrolyte solution with a vacuum pump, the 
23
overlapped TEM grids were carefully separated and dried for 24 hours. All these GLC

In-situ and ex-situ TEM observations
7
A conventional microscope (JEM-3010, 300kV, JEOL, Japan) was used to observe real-
8
time lithiation process of S nanoparticles. For the comparative real-time observations, we set 
9
the electron beam density to 5.0×10-14 A/nm2 (spot size:1). However, low beam dose 
10
condition (spot size:5, exposure time: 0.5s) and 20 µm-diameter selected area aperture were 
11
employed to obtain electron diffraction patterns shown in Figure 3b. These efforts help us to 
12
avoid not only the electron beam irradiation damage but also the strong diffraction from the 
13
sulfur particle that makes the diffracted beam from other tiny crystalline phases invisible. The 
14
lithiation process was monitored under a live viewing mode at 3 frames/s and recorded with a 
15
movie capturing function using Gatan Digital Micrograph software. For triggering the 
16
chemical lithiation, the electron beam dose was maintained at 5.0×10-14A/cm2. Ex situ EELS 
17
analyses were conducted with a Titan 80-300 FP 5701/30 microscope (300kV, FEI, USA) 
18
with GIF Quantum (965 model, Gatan) to demonstrate the Li-PS dissolution by detecting the 
19
faded S L-edge around the lithiated S particle due to the loss of long-range order crystalline 
20
symmetry in lithiated S.

Shuttle current measurement
9
The sulfur electrode was prepared as follows: the slurry was prepared by adding 
10
synthesized sulfur powder into aqueous poly acrylic acid (PAA) binder solution with weight 
11
ratio of 95:5 between sulfur and PAA. Then the slurry was drop-casted onto a carbon fiber 
12
paper current collector and dried at room temperature for 6 hours and then dried at 50.0 °C in 
13
a vacuum oven overnight. Coin cells (2032-type) were fabricated with a Li metal foil as 
14
counter/reference electrode and a porous polypropylene separator (2400, Celgard) in a 
15
glovebox filled with Ar gas. The conventional electrolyte and the IL-electrolyte (50 % IL by 
16
volume) were used. To measure the shuttle current, the cells were first galvanostatically 
17
discharged to 1.7 V (vs. Li/Li+) and charged until the charge capacity reached 80 % relative 
18
to the obtained discharge capacity, where the soluble high-order polysulfides preferentially 
19
formed. Then the cell was kept at open circuit for 1.5 h in order for the cell to reach the 
20
equilibrium potential. The Li-PS shuttle current was measured while charging the cell at the 
21
measured equilibrium voltage for 40 hours. For SEM observation of the tested Li electrodes, 
22
the Li electrodes were collected from the disassembled cells and rinsed using DOL followed 
23
by drying in the Ar filled glovebox.

Electrochemical test of the S-GO-CTA electrodes
2
The S-GO-CTA electrode (70% S) was prepared by mixing the S-GO-CTA composite, the 
3
mixture of CNT and Ketjenblack (KB, AkzoNobel, 1:1 w/w) as carbon additives and the 
4
styrene−butadiene rubber/carboxymethylcellulose (SBR/CMC, 1:1 w/w) composite binder at 
5
a weight ratio of 82.3:9.7:8 in water. The slurry was stirred using a magnetic stirrer and drop-
6
cast onto the Al foam (Sumitomo Electric). Two different electrolytes composed of 1) 1 M 
7
LiTFSI lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide and 0.21 M LiNO3 in 
8
PYR14TFSI/DOL/DME (2:1:1, v/v/v) and 2) 1 M LiTFSI and 0.21 M LiNO3 in DOL/DME 
9
(1:1, v/v) were used as a IL and IL-free electrolytes, respectively. Coin cells (2032-type) 
10
were fabricated with a lithium metal foil as counter/reference electrode and a porous 
11
polypropylene separator (2400, Celgard) in a glovebox filled with Ar gas. 30 µl of electrolyte 
12
was added in the cells. Galvanostatic cycling tests were conducted using a battery cycler 
13
(Maccor).
14
Morphology tracking of sulfur and Li-PS during lithiation
15
From the captured images of the movies at 1-second intervals, we measured and noted the 
16
two boundaries of S/Li-PS and outer Li-PS on the basis of contrast steps between them. 
17
Using ImageJ software, we counted the number of pixels, which can be calculated into the 
18
area of each interest region. The dimensions of one pixel in serially captured images are 
19
calibrated with the scale bar. The measured areas as a function of time were plotted in a graph 
20
as shown in Figure S8.

S3. Captions for Supporting Movies 1 to 3

Movie S1
3
A movie shows the lithiation dynamics of a sulfur nanoparticle in IL-free electrolyte. As the 
4
lithiation progresses, originally spherical sulfur particles shrinks to irregular shaped one, due 
5
to rapid formation and diffusion of Li-PS in the liquid electrolyte. The movie was recorded at 
6
3 frames per a second and the fast forward rate was edited to 4 times faster than real-time.
7
Movie S2
8
A movie shows the lithiation dynamics of a sulfur nanoparticle in IL-containing electrolyte. 
9
Compared to the sulfur particle in IL-free electrolyte, the particle in the IL-containing 
10
electrolyte maintains its original morphology with slow formation and diffusion of Li-PS 
11
during lithiation. The movie was recorded at 3 frames per a second and playing rate was set to 
12
20 times faster than real-time.
13
Movie S3
14
A movie shows the nucleation process of tiny precipitates in the Li-PS region during further 
15
lithiation in the IL-free electrolyte. The movie was recorded at 3 frames per a second and its 
16
playing rate was edited to 64 times faster than real-time.
