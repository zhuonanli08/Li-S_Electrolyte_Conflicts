Covalent Organic Framework Quantum Dots for Near-frictionless 
Ion Transport Battery Electrolyte

Abstract: Optimization of electrolyte with enhanced energy density and durability in battery system is highly demanded yet challenging 
owing to the high ion transport resistance and complex by-reactions in electrolyte. In this work, we tailored the solid-liquid phase 
transport interface by adding covalent organic frameworks quantum dots (COFs QDs) into electrolyte. COFs QDs with abundant 
lipophilic ether-oxygen groups and ~12 nm nano-size can serve as myriad ion-transport nano-engines for constantly replenishing the 
interface with Li+ to achieve near-frictionless ion transport (tLi+ = 0.98) and exceptional stability (>400 days). Lithium-sulfur battery based 
on COFs QDs shows high discharge specific capacity of 1063.7 mAh g-1 after 100 cycles at 0.2 C and maintains a low overpotential 
(~16 mV) over 4000 h at 1 mA cm-2 with 1 mAh cm-2 in Li//Li symmetric battery. A Li-S pouch battery has also been successfully 
assembled to validate the practicality. Our results may promote the development of COFs QDs in energy storage field.

Characterization 
Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns were tested by a Rigaku SmartLab diffractometer (Rigaku SmartLab, Japan) under the Cu 
Kα radiation at 45 kV, 200 mA. Morphological and structural characterizations were carried out through high-resolution transmission 
electron microscopy (HRTEM FEI TEC-NAI G220, 200 kV) and a field-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi SU-8010) 
system analysis instrument. Fourier transform infrared spectrometry (FT-IR) spectra were investigatedby a Bruker Optics Tensor 27 
ranged from 4000 to 400 cm-1 with KBr pellets. The ESCALAB 250Xi system from Thermo Scientific wae used to record on X-ray

isotherm of the samples was identified using a Quantachrome Autosorb-IQ2 at 77 K. Specific surface area was verified based on 
Brunauer-Emmett-Teller analysis. 
Experimental Procedures 
Synthesis of COF-TAP-4EO 
A schlenk tube with dimensions of 19 × 65 mm (o.d × length) was charged with TAP (28.35 mg, 0.08 mM), 4EO-PA (55 mg, 0.12 mM), 
n-BuOH (0.5 mL), DCB (0.5 mL) and aqueous acetic acid (6 M, 0.1 mL). After sonication for about 15 min, the tube was flash-frozen at 
77 K followed by three freeze-pump-thaw cycles and sealed. After the temperature returned to room temperature, the reaction mixture 
was heated at 120 °C for 72 h. The resulting brick red precipitate was isolated by filtration in Buchner funnel, followed by washing with 
EtOH until the filtrate changed to colorless. After that, the wet sample was transferred into a Soxhlet extractor and washed with THF. 
Finally, the product was evacuated at 80 °C under vacuum overnight to yield the activated sample. 
 
Synthesis of COF-TAP-3EO 
The synthesis of COF-TAP-3EO followed similar procedures as that of COF-TAP-4EO except that 4EO-PA (55.0 mg) was replaced 
with 3EO-PA (44.4 mg). 
 
Synthesis of COF-TAP-OMe 
The synthesis of COF-TAP-OMe followed similar procedures as that of COF-TAP-4EO except that 4EO-PA (55.0 mg) was replaced 
with OMe-PA (23.3 mg). 
 
Synthesis of COF-TAP-PA 
The synthesis of COF-TAP-PA followed similar procedures as that of COF-TAP-4EO except that 4EO-PA (55.0 mg), n-BuOH (0.5 
mL) and DCB (0.5 mL) were replaced with PA (23.3 mg), MES (0.5 mL) and DOA (0.5 mL), respectively. 
 
Polysulfide adsorption test 
Sulfur power and Li2S with a molar ratio of 3 : 1 were added to an appropriate amount of DME and DOL under stirring overnight at 
70 °C in a glovebox to prepare 0.5 M Li2S6 solution and was then diluted to 10 mM for the test of polysulfide adsorption. After complete 
dissolution, an equivalent amount (4 mg) of COF-TAP-4EO was immersed into 2 mL as-prepared Li2S6 solution. Photo images were 
taken to show the difference in adsorption capability at different time. 
 
Fabrication of sulfur cathode 
Sulfur powers (70 wt%) and carbon nanotubes (30%) were mixed and heated at 155 °C in a sealed tube for 12 h to prepare the 
carbon-sulfur composite. Then, the carbon-sulfur composite (70 wt%) was mixed with Super P (20 wt%) and PVDF (10 wt%) in NMP 
and ground to generate the uniform slurry. After that, the slurry was coated on Al foil to prepare sulfur cathodes and then dried at 60 °C 
for 12 h. 
 
Fabrication of electrolytes 
To test the properties of Li-S batteries, COF-TAP-4EO as the additive of electrolyte was added into the blank electrolyte (0.5 M 
LiTFSI in DME/DOL (1 : 1 vol) with 0.5 M LiNO3) and stirred to generate uniform mixture to achieve the COF-TAP-4EO based electrolyte.

Electrochemical measurements 
The Li-S batteries, Li//Li and Li//Cu symmetric batteries were assembled by CR2032 coin in a high-purity Ar-filled glovebox by 
stacking the Li metal anode, Celgard 2500 separator, and electrolyte system (blank, COF-TAP-4EO, COF-TAP-3EO, COF-TAP-OMe 
or COF-TAP-PA based electrolyte). The charge-discharge tests were conducted on a LAND battery-testing system (Li-S battery, a 
potential range of 1.7 - 2.6 V) at room temperature. The corresponding electrochemical performances were measured on CHI660E 
electrochemical workstation in terms of electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS, from 10 to 100 kHz), linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) 
and Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurement. 
 
Computational methods. 
Density functional theory calculations 
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed with periodic super-cells under the generalized gradient approximation 
using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof functional1 for exchange-correlation. The Kohn-Sham orbitals were expanded in a plane-wave basis 
set with a kinetic energy cutoff of 30 Ry and the charge-density cutoff of 300 Ry, for beyond which the adsorption energy changes little 
(within 0.02 eV). The Fermi-surface effects have been treated by the smearing technique of Methfessel and Paxton2, using a smearing 
parameter of 0.02 Ry. The convergence criteria are set as 10-3 Ry/Bohr of cartesian force components acting on each atom and 10-5 
Ry of total energy. The Brillouin-zones were uniformly sampled with a gamma k-point mesh, for molecules do not have periodicity. The 
dispersion correction is applied in all calculations with the semiempirical zero damping D3 method of Grimme. All the DFT calculation 
is carried out by Quantum Espresso3. 
 
Molecular dynamics simulations 
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of the desolvation, deposition of Li in COF structure that are deal with the force field 
construction of machine learning potentials (MLPs). MLP is a machine learning based force field construction method, the basic neural 
network structure of which was proposed by Behlor and Parrinello in 20074. This method record and extrapolate the force and energy 
information from DFT calculation, so that when DFT data is abundant enough, forces for every new structures can be well predicted. 
Driven by this force field, the molecular mechanics can possess the precision from first-principle method. In this paper, we employ the 
DeepMD-kit package5 to construct MLP. And we use the solution model that only includes the electrolyte and the interface model that 
contains the electrolyte, COF-TAP-4EO and Li were used as initial models to initialize the force field. 
Subsequently, we optimized the force field iteratively using a DPGEN approach6. In each iteration, a set of force fields is generated 
by different random seeds, and the accuracy of the force field is assessed by the error in force calculations across the same structures. 
During each iteration, the force error range for structure selection is set between 0.2 and 0.4, and by the 15th iteration, 97% of the 
structures have force errors reduced to within 0.2, at which point we consider the force field to be reasonably accurate.  
During the learning phase, a cut-off radius of 5 Å is employed, encompassing a maximum of 80 neighboring atoms treated via full 
relative coordinates for all atoms. Smoothing procedures are initiated from a fractional interval of 0.2 and extend to 2. The embedding 
nets consist of hidden layers with neuron counts of 10, 20 and 40, while the fitting net is composed of hidden layers with neuron counts 
of 240, 240 and 240. The training process begins with energy and force loss parameters set at 0.02 and 1000, respectively, and these 
are adjusted to 2 and 1 at the training limit. The initial learning rate is set at 0.001, with a decay step of 1000 and a decay rate of 0.95. 
The choice of the loss function depends on the specific context, whether it pertains to atomic energy or a combination of force and 
atomic energy components. Considering that the system contains as many as eight different elements, we used a DPA descriptor 
combined with an attention mechanism for training. 
In each iteration of DPGEN, the training steps amounted to 150 000, and after the final force field was developed, the training steps 
increased to 500,000. Testing revealed an average training force error of 373 meV/Å and an average testing error of 423 meV/Å. 
We employed the force field to conduct MD simulations of the interface and solvent. All simulations were executed using the Large-
scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator7. For the models, the pure Li structure consisted of electrolyte phases and COF-
TAP-4EO solid phases. The molar ratios of DOL, DME, LiTFSI and Li were referenced from commercial electrolytes and set at 
7:20:1:3.5, with a total density of 1 g cm-³. The electrolyte solution was constructed using the Packmol package8. The model used for 
the desolvation simulation was a slab model. An NVT ensemble with an Andersen thermostat was employed for temperature control, 
adjusting the temperature every 100 fs. The simulation time was set to 200 ps. In the interface desolvation simulation, we simulated 
the effect of the electric field on Li by applying an additional force. 
 
Finite element method simulation 
Finite element method (FEM) simulations were carried out using COMSOL Multiphysics. To analyze the inhibitory effect of the COF-
TAP-4EO layer on Li (111), 2D electric field distribution model at the interface was established. In the model, the size of the simulation 
box is 25 × 25 × 30 mm³. The array (3 × 3) of nano cones was uniformly arranged at the bottom, representing Li metal. The top and 
bottom surface radii of each cone were 1 mm and 2 mm, respectively, with a height of 3.23 mm. The region from 5 mm to 8.25 mm 
above the bottom was designated as the COF-TAP-4EO region. We modeled the effect of the COF by adjusting the electrical 
conductivity within this region. The ionic mobility outside the COF region was assumed to be 1e-12 smol kg-1, while inside the COF 
region it was set to 1e-13 smol kg-1. The diffusion coefficient was uniformly set to 1e-11 m² s-1. The total interfacial electric potential 
difference was set to 0.03 V. The Nernst-Planck equation was used to describe the physical field. 
 
Computational details of lithium polysulfides 
As DFT can meet difficulties in calculating the charged species, the usual trick used is to use the equilibrium potentials of the 
corresponding redox pairs, along with the chemical potential of the corresponding uncharged counterpart. One famous example is the 
computational hydrogen electrode (CHE)9 method that uses the chemical potential of H2 and the equilibrium potential of H+/H2 (φ0 
(H+/H2)) at 0 V vs SHE, to calculate the chemical potential an H+. Here, we should also use this strategy. We calculate the Gibbs energy 
of Li+, via the φ0(Li+/Li) of -3.045 V vs SHE and the chemical potential of Li in a Li solid [uLi (s)]: 
uLi+ = φ0 (Li+/Li) + uLi (s) 
Then, we can focus on the reaction of the cathode electrode. The full  discharging reaction is

S8 (s) + Li++16e → 8Li2S (s)   R1 
The chemical potential of S8 (s) and Li2S (s) are calculated by the associated solid bulk. Notice for S8, we choose the orthorhombic 
S8 that is considered to be most stable at room temperature. Notice the experimental equilibrium potential for this reaction is -0.758 V, 
but our calculations indicate that is -0.81 V. Considering this kind of error occurs often in DFT calculations, and the error here is very 
small, in this paper, we artificially add a modification energy of Li2S (s), which can force the equilibrium potential to be -0.758 V. 
We need to assume/claim a mechanism to calculate the activity of R1: before the reaction takes place, as S8 can be easily volatilized, 
in a meanwhile, a large amount of Li ions are transported to the anode, there can be a large number of free S 8 and Li+; they join at the 
reaction center and catch the electron to form LiSx, which initialize the reaction; with the increasing of the number of Li, x value 
decreases, and finally, we get Li2S fragments; those Li2S fragments then depart the reaction center, connecting with each other to form 
the Li2S solid. Following this mechanism, the elementary reactions are written as: 
(1) S8 (s) + 2(Li+ + e-) → *Li2S8 (R2) 
(2) *Li2S8 + 4(Li+ + e-) → *Li2S6 + 2Li2S (R3) 
(3) *Li2S6 + 4(Li+ + e-) → *Li2S4 + 2Li2S (R4) 
(4) *Li2S4 + 4(Li+ +e-) → *Li2S2 + 2Li2S (R5) 
(5) *Li2S2 + 2(Li+ +e-) → *Li2S + Li2S (R6) 
(6) *Li2S → Li2S (s) (R7) 
Where * is the reactive site of a catalyst. With this mechanism, we can calculate the Gibbs energy of each step to form the Free 
energy diagram (FED) of the R1, as we did in the CHE method. The chemical potential of each adsorbate is calculated by  
uX* =ΔEX* + ZPEX* - TSX*        (Equation 1) 
where ΔEX* is defined by the ΔEX* = EDFTX* - EDFT*, where EDFTX* and EDFT* represent DFT energies of the model with and without the 
adsorbate X*. The ZPEX* and TSX* are Zero-point energy and entropy contributions to the adsorbate X*. The former is calculated by 
the PHONON module in Quantum ESPRESSO distribution, which gives 0.185, 0.289, 0.272, 0.323, 0.064 and 0.239 eV for Li 2S2*, 
Li2S4*, Li2S6*, Li2S8*, Li2S* and S8*; the entropy term of each adsorbed state is set to be zero since the vibrational excitation energy are 
usually zero point a few eV (which means a very little occupation fraction in excitation state for room temperature).  
The binding energy ∆Eb is calculated by the following equation (1): 
∆Eb = ΔEX* - EX         (Equation 2) 
where EX is the DFT energy of X in a vacuum..
