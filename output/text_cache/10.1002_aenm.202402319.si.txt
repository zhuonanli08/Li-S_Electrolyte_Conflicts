Experimental procedures
1.1 Materials
All raw materials were purchased through commercial channels without further processing. 4-Mercaptopyridine (4Mpy, Aladdin), Carbon nanotubes (CNT, Nanjing XFNANO Materials Tech Co., Ltd), acetylene black (AB), polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF, HSV-900, Shenzhen Kejing Eectronic Co., Ltd.), N-Methylpyrrolidone (NMP, Aladdin), Li2S (Aladdin), Sulfur powder (Aladdin), Lithium-sulfur electrolyte (1M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (vol 1:1) with 1wt% LiNO3, DoDoChem.), carbon paper (CP, TORAY, Japan), Lithium (Li, China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd.)
1.2 Preparation of the sulfur cathode 
Firstly, sulfur powder and CNT were mixed in a ratio of 7:3 and heated at 155°C for 12 hours to obtain CNT/S. Then, CNT/S was mixed with AB and PVDF in an 8:1:1 weight ratio, and NMP was added as a solvent to obtain a black cathode slurry. Coating the cathode slurry with a scraper on the carbon-coated aluminum (Al) foil, then dry overnight under vacuum at 60°C and punch the cathode electrode plate into a diameter of 12 mm.
1.3 Preparation of the electrolytes and LiPSs
The modified electrolyte involves adding different gradients of 4Mpy (0.02-0.2 M) to the traditional base electrolyte (1M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (vol 1:1) with 1wt% LiNO3). The prepared electrolyte is fully dissolved for 24 hours before use.
1.3.1Preparation of 0.5 M Li2S6 for the LiPSs catalytic experiment. 
Sulfur powder and Li2S in a 5:1 weight ratio in the glove box were mixed and added to 1 M LiTFSI with 1wt% of LiNO3 in DOL/DME (vol1:1), and continuously stirred at 60°C for 48 hours to obtain brownish red Li2S6.
1.3.2Preparation of 0.2 M Li2S8 for the Li2S nucleation and decomposition.
Sulfur powder was mixed with Li2S in a 7:1 ratio in the glove box, added to tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether, and stirred at 60°C for 24 hours to obtain brownish red Li2S8.
1.4 Electrochemical measurements.
The CR2025 (Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd) Li-S coin battery was assembled in the Ar-filled glove box(O2<0.01 ppm, H2O<0.01 ppm), with CNT/S as the cathode, lithium as the anode, and coating CNT on separator-coated polypropylene (PP) 2500 separator to physically limit the shuttle of LiPSs. The loading of CNT was about 0.3 mg cm-2. The sulfur load was 1.3 mg cm-2 and 50 μL electrolyte for every coin battery. For batteries with higher sulfur loading (3.29 mg cm-2, 4.58 mg cm-2, and 5.59 mg cm-2), the Electrolyte/Sulfur (E/S) is 10 μL mg-1 and 12 μL mg-1for 10.88 mg cm-2. The constant current charging and discharging experiments were conducted between 1.7-2.8 V at 30°C (Wuhan Land Electronics Co., Ltd.). Other electrochemical tests were conducted on the Chenhua CH660E electrochemical workstation. The CV test measurements were conducted in the same potential range of (1.7-2.8 V,) at a scanning speed rate of 0.1 mV s-1, and. The EIS test frequency was conducted in the range of 0.01-100000 Hz.
The Li-S single-layer pouch cell was assembled in a dry room with a dew point below −40°C. The sulfur load of loading was adjusted at about 1.6 mg cm-2, and the cathode electrode size was 4.5*6.8 cm2. The anode was lithium-plated copper foil (50 μm), and 1.5 mL modified electrolyte was added. Li-S pouch cell were cycled for 10 charges and discharges at 0.05 C before cycling test increasing the rate to 0.1 C.
1.5 Li2S6 symmetrical battery testing and Li2S deposition decomposition testing
The Li2S6 symmetrical battery used carbon paper as the counter electrode, with 10μL Li2S6and 10 μL modified electrolyte added to both sides of the separator. The CV test range was chosen between -1 to 1 V, and the scanning speed ranges from 1 mV s-1 to 9 mV s-1. The frequency range for EIS testing was 0.01 to 100000 Hz.
For the Li2S deposition experiments, the Li2S8 battery were assembled using carbon paper as the cathode, and lithium as the anode. The electrolytes were 20 μL of 0.2 M Li2S8 and 20 μL modified electrolyte was added. The batteries were first discharged to 2.05 V at a constant current of 0.112 mA, and then at a constant voltage of 2.02 V for 10 hours.
The decomposition of Li2S was evaluated using a cell similar to that used for the Li2S deposition. First, the batteries were discharged at 0.113 mA constant current to 1.7 V and then discharged to a voltage less than 1.7 V at a constant current of 0.011 mA to ensure complete Li2S deposition. Subsequently, they were charged at a constant voltage of 2.4 V for 10 hours, and the decomposition curve of Li2S was measured.
1.6 Characterizations
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to analyze the phases in the cathode materials (Bruker D8 Advance, scanning rate of 12° min-1). A Field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, Gemini SEM 500, Zeiss) determined the morphology of the Li2S deposited/decomposition on carbon paper, Li anode, and S cathode after cycling. The morphology of the sulfur cathode after cycling was characterized by the yield-emission transmission electron microscope (FE-TEM, JEOL JEM-F200 (HR)). The Raman spectroscopic measurements of the cathode after cycles were conducted through Thermo Fisher DXR2xi with an argon ion laser (532 nm). Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) (WATERS I-Class VION IMS QTof) were run after disassembling Li-S batteries after three discharge cycles at 0.1 C and reaching a charging or discharging state in the Ar glove box. The electrode was immersed in 2 mL of chromatographic acetonitrile and left stationary for half an hour. The supernatant was taken for LC-MS testing, with a mobile phase of 80% acetonitrile and 20% water. The test was conducted using EIS positive ion mode.
1.7 Density functional theory (DFT) calculations
Theoretical calculations on the geometrical and electronic structures of the molecules entity discussed in this paper were conducted using the Gaussian 16 program[1]. All the structures were fully optimized at the B3LYP[2] level with the 6-311G (D, P)[3] basis set. Calculations on the relative energies of structures and the lithium polysulfides adsorption energies were based on the electronic energies of the related species modified by their zero-point energy corrections. The adsorption energy of lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) is expressed by the following equation:
Eb=EAB−(EA+EB)
in which Eb is the binding energy and E(AB), E(A), and E(B) are the energy values of A, B, and A binding with B, respectively.
