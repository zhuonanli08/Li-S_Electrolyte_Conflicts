Experimental Section 
Chemicals: Sodium saccharin (99%), lithium nitrate (99%) and octaphenyl polyoxyethylene (99%) were purchased from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Li foil (>99.99%) with a thickness of 450 µm and a diameter of 16 mm was purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. The unmodified electrolyte was sourced from Duoduo Chem.
Preparation of Electrolyte: The unmodified electrolyte was 1 M LiTFSI dissolved in a mixture solution of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethyl ether (DME) in a volume ratio of 1:1. For the dual-additive electrolyte, 0.2 M LiNO3 and 2mM SAC additives were added into the unmodified electrolyte. For the tri-element additive electrolyte, 1 vol% OP-10 was added into the dual-additive electrolyte. 
Throwing Power Test: The throwing power of various electrolytes was measured in a Haring-Blum cell. The cathodes were two copper films with a diameter of 8 mm coated with varnish on the back. The anode was a Li film of the same size as the cathode and with small holes in it. The current density was 2 mA cm-2, and the ratio of the distance between the farther cathode and the nearer cathode relative to the central anode was 2, plating 30 minutes.
Electrochemical Measurements: The electrochemical performance of batteries were conducted on NEWARE CT/CTE-400 system. The symmetrical Li|Li cells were constructed using identical Li foils on both electrodes. For Li|S full cells, the carbon black@S (70 wt.%), CNT, and PVDF are coated on aluminum foil in a mass ratio of 7:2:1 to prepare the S cathode sheets. The mass of S used in this work was controlled to 1.0-4.12 mg cm-2. All cells were assembled in CR2032 coin cells using Celgard 2400 separators at room temperature in an argon-filled glovebox. For high S mass loading cells, the amount of electrolyte used in the coin cells is 5 µL/mgS. EIS, Tafel plot, and LSV measurements were carried out on a CHI760E workstation. Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were measured with an amplitude of 5 mV under a frequency range from 10 mHz to 100 kHz. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was carried out at scan rate of 5 mV s-1.
Materials Characterizations: The morphology of lithium anode in symmetric cells after cycling in different electrolytes was examined by Hitachi SU8010 scanning electron microscope (SEM). XPS was conducted on a K-Alpha photoelectron spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) equipped with a monochromatic Al Kα X-ray source (1486.6 eV) operating at 100 W. An automatic contact angle measuring instrument (JC2000D3M) was used to characterize the wettability of various electrolytes on the surface of Celgard 2400 separators. The ionic conductivity of electrolytes with different additives was performed with a FiveEasy Plus FE38 conductivity meter.
Computational Methods: For the HOMO and LUMO analyses, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed by using B3LYP [1] hybrid functions as implemented in Gaussian09[2] package with D3 empirical dispersion correction development by Grimme.[3] Orbital properties of the first three periodic elements were described by 6-31G* allelectron basis, while the other heavier elements were described by Stuttgart Dresden pseudopotential (SDD) defined in Gaussian. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) analyses are finished by Mutiwfn3.8 software. For the binding energy, DFT calculations were conducted using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [4]. Systems were modeled with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) and the projector augmented wave (PAW) method [5, 6]. The Brillouin zone was sampled using a 1 × 1 × 1 k-point grid4. The cut-off energy was set to 500 eV, while the convergence criteria for energy and force in the self-consistent iterations were defined as 10−5 eV and 0.02 eV Å−1, respectively. The binding energy (Eb) was determined by the following equation: Eb = EAB–EA–EB, where the Et, EA and EB mean the single point energy of complex AB, component A and component B.
