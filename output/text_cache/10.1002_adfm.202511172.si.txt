Figure S12. Scheme of (a) hard short circuit and (b) soft short circuit. (c) Critical current density of different electrolytes. In 1 M LiFSI DME electrolyte, Li+ deposition is often uneven, which promotes the growth of lithium dendrites in the out-of-plane direction. This issue is further exacerbated under high current densities, and when lithium dendrites come into direct contact with another electrode, a hard short circuit occurs. In contrast, DEM solvent molecules form a weaker solvation structure with Li+. This leads to more uniform Li+ deposition, predominantly in the in-plane direction, thereby reducing the likelihood of lithium dendrite formation. However, lithium dendrite formation cannot be completely avoided under high current densities. DEM solvent has lower LUMO levels, making it more susceptible to participating in redox reactions. When lithium dendrites do form, their interaction with the electrolyte generates ionic and electronic currents. This triggers a chemical reaction within the battery, but it does not immediately cause battery failure, resulting in a soft short circuit instead.
