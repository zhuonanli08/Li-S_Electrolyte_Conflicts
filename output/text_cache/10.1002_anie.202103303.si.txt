1. Experimental procedures 
Materials. Graphene (8% GN slurry) and carbon nanotubes (4% CNT slurry) were purchased from 
Sixth Element Materials Technology Co., Ltd. Methyl propyl ether (MPE) was purchased from 
Sigma-Aldrich, impurities were removed with lithium metal several times, and the upper solvent 
was then stored over molecular sieves. LiTFSI, LiFSI, DME, and the conventional electrolyte (1 M 
LiTFSI in DME/DOL (1:1) with 2% LiNO3) were purchased from DodoChem. All other reagents 
were purchased from Aladdin or Innochem. All solvents (Table S2) used in this work were dried 
with molecular sieves before use. The preparation process of the ultralight electrolyte was as follows: 
LiTFSI and LiNO3 were fully dissolved in DME, and then MPE was mixed with the above solution. 
The “m” in this work represents salts-amount/solvent-volume. Mo6S8 was fabricated according to 
the method described in our previous work. Briefly, MoS2, Cu, and Mo with a mole ratio of 2:1:1 
were mixed by high-energy ball milling in Ar, and then an appropriate amount of iodine was added 
as the catalyst. After that, the mixture was pressed into a pellet in a stainless steel vessel, heated at 
800°C for 24 h under an Ar atmosphere, and then cooled slowly at room temperature. The obtained 
powder was composed of Cu2Mo6S8. Mo6S8 powder was obtained from Cu2Mo6S8 by the acid (6 M 
HCl) leaching method. Subsequently, the Mo6S8 powder was washed several times with water and 
then dried at 60 °C under vacuum. 
Preparation of sulfur cathode. The S/C electrode with a sulfur loading of 1.0–1.2 mg cm−2 was 
prepared by mixing sulfur, Super P, and PVDF in a mass ratio of 6:3:1 in the NMP. The slurry was 
cast onto a carbon-coated Al foil using a doctor blade and dried at 60 °C for 24 h. The sulfur cathode 
(S8+Mo6S8+CNTs+GNs+CMC+SBR with the mass ratio of 52:28:7:7:3:3) with high sulfur loading 
was prepared as follows: Mo6S8, CNTs (slurry), and GNs (slurry) were mixed thoroughly by 
ball milling, and then Na2S2O3 was dissolved in the slurry. Next, 0.2 M H2SO4 solution was slowly 
added into the mixture by a peristaltic pump and vigorously stirred for more than 6 h. The mixture 
was centrifuged and washed several times with ultrapure water, followed by drying at 60 °C 
overnight. A slurry was fabricated by mixing a 94 wt.% mixture (S8+Mo6S8+CNTs+GNs) with 3.0 
wt.% carboxy methyl cellulose (CMC) and 3.0 wt.% styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), coated onto 
a carbon-coated Al foil and then dried at 60 °C for 24 h. Saturated polysulfide solutions were 
obtained by mixing Li2S and S in a stoichiometric ratio and stirred for 48 h at 50 °C. 
Material characterization. The morphology of the samples was characterized by scanning electron 

<<PAGE 3>>
microscopy (SEM) using a Hitachi S-4700 operating at 10 kV. XPS analysis was conducted using a 
Kratos AXIS 165 X-ray photo electron spectrometer with monochromatic Al Kα radiation. The 
conductivity of the electrolytes was measured using an impedance analyzer (Zahner Zennium, 
Germany) in the range of 0–50 °C in a thermostated container. The Raman spectra of the electrolytes 
were measured with an NRS-5100 spectrometer (JASCO) between 200 and 2000 cm−1. NMR 
analyses were conducted on Varian VNMRS 500 MHz spectrometers using D2O as an external 
reference. The viscosity of the electrolytes was measured on an MCR 502 Anton Paar with a 
controlled shear rate and shear stress at 0–50°C. The sulfur content was measured by 
thermogravimetric analyses (STA 449 F5 Jupiter) from 50 to 500 ℃ at a heating rate of 10 ℃/min 
under argon atmosphere.  
Electrochemical measurements. Electrochemical performance studies were performed using 
CR2032 coin cells and pouch cells with a 3 × 4 cm electrode. Li-S coin cells were assembled using 
the S/C or MS+S/C cathode and 450 μm Li metal in an argon glovebox，thin PP with 19 μm (0.88 
mg/cm2) was used as a separator, and 30 μL/mg and 15 μL/mg electrolytes were used for S/C or 
MS+S/C, respectively. Before assembling, the lithium foils that were used in high loading Li-S cells 
were polished and then immersed in 0.5 m LiFSI+0.5 mLiNO3 +DME/DOL (50 ℃ for 24 h). This 
step is to reduce the side reactions between Li metal and electrolytes, especially the limited salt in 
ultralight electrolyte. Single-layer pouch cells were assembled with a 3 × 4 cm (12 cm2) electrode, 
and 50 μm Lithium foil was used as the anode. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy 
measurements were measured by an electrochemical workstation (ZAHNER IM6). All cells were 
assembled in an argon-filled glovebox, in which oxygen and water concentrations were less than 1 
ppm.
