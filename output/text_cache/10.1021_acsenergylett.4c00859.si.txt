Experimental Section 
Materials. Li chips (99.9%) with the thickness of 500 µm was purchased from MTI. Li foils 
(99.9%)  with the thickness of 50 µm was purchased from Tianjin China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. 
Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) (99.9% (water : 20 ppm max.)) was purchased from 
Solvionic, diethyl ether (DEE) (≥99.9%, inhibitor-free), acrylonitrile (AN) (≥99%), dimethyl 
sulfoxide (DMSO) (anhydrous, ≥99.9%), azodiisobutyronitrile (AIBN) (≥99%) and acetone 
(suitable for HPLC, ≥99.9%) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich, ethyl 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 
ether (ETFE) ( ≥98%) was purchased from TCI America, 1-methyl-1-propylpyrrolidinium 
bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (Py13TFSI) (99%)  was purchased from Alfa Aesar. All solvents 
for electrolyte preparation were dried over molecular sieve in the glove box for 24 h before use. 
Synthesis of SPAN composite. The PAN was obtained by radical polymerization in an N2 
atmosphere at 60 oC. DMSO and H2O were used as the solvents with the mass ratio of 5:5. The 
AN and AIBN were employed as the monomer and initiator, respectively, with the wight ratio of 
100:1.5. The mass of AN monomer was controlled at 30% of DMSO/H2O solvents. In the 
polymerization process, the solvents, monomer and initiator were added to a three-necked flask 
which was heated in 60 oC oil bath and magnetically stirred in N2 atmosphere. After 4h, the white 
solid powder was filtered out and washed with acetone and hot water, respectively. The PAN 
powder was obtained after vacuum drying and grinding. Afterwards, the prepared PAN was mixed 
with element sulfur uniformly by ball milling at a mass ratio of 1:8 for 1h with 750 RPM. Then, 
the SPAN was achieved after heating the mixture at 300 oC for 10h with a heating rate of 1 oC min-
1 in Ar atmosphere.

Electrolyte preparation. To prepare LDEFE electrolyte, 1.5 M LiFSI (based on the total volume 
of DEE and ETFE solvents (1:3 with volume ratio)) was added in a plastic vial only containing 
corresponding volume of DEE solvent and stirred for 0.5 h to form a homogenous solution. After 
that, the corresponding volume of ETFE solvent was added in the obtained homogenous solution 
and stirred for 1h to get LDEFE electrolyte. For the LDEFE-IL electrolyte, 1.5 M LiFSI (based on 
the total volume of DEE and ETFE solvents (1:3 with volume ratio)) was added in a plastic vial 
only containing corresponding volume of DEE solvent and stirred for 0.5 h to form a homogenous 
solution. After that, the corresponding volume of ETFE solvent and 10 v% Py13TFSI ionic liquid 
(based on the total volume of DEE and ETFE) were added in the obtained homogenous solution 
and stirred for 1h to obtain the LDEFE-IL electrolyte. The density of the LDEFE-IL electrolyte is 
1.25 mg μL-1. The obtained electrolytes were stored in glove box with H2O content < 1ppm and 
O2 content < 1 ppm before use.  
Electrochemical test. Coulombic efficiency (CE) of Li/Cu asymmetric cells and cyclic stability of 
Li/Li symmetric cells were investigated in CR2032-type coin cells. For the average CE test, the 
Li/Cu asymmetric cells were running at the voltage range of 0.01-2V for 5 cycles to clean the 
surface of Cu foil and form a uniform SEI layer. Afterwards, Li metal was plated on Cu foil under 
current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 for 2h and then fully striped to 1V. The CE is calculated by the 
ratio between stripping time and plating time. To accurate CE test follows the PNNL protocol, 5 
mAh cm-2 Li metal was deposited on Cu foil at current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 and then fully 
stripped to 1V. After that, another 5 mAh cm-2 Li metal was deposited on Cu foil at current density 
of 0.5 mA cm-2, and the cell was then cycled at 0.5 mA cm-2 with a capacity of 1 mAh cm-2 for 10 
cycles, followed by stropping to 1V at 0.5 mA cm-2. The CE is calculated by the ratio between the 
total stripping time and total plating time except the first cycle. To fabricate low mass loading

SPAN cathode, the SPAN powder was mixed with ketjen black and sodium alginate binder to form 
a slurry at the weight ratio of 80:10:10. The electrode was prepared by casting the slurry onto 
carbon coated Al foil using a doctor blade and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 oC overnight to prepare 
the SPAN cathode. The obtained SPAN cathode was punched into circular electrodes with a mass 
loading of ~2 mg cm-2 (based on SPAN). For the preparation of high mass loading SPAN cathode 
(12 mg cm-2, based on SPAN), the SPAN powder was mixed with ketjen black and 
polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) binder at the weight ratio of 80:10:10, a thick film was obtained 
directly after grinding for 20 min. The thick film was cut into small pieces, and each piece was 
pressed together with a stainless-steel mesh. The cathode with high mass loading was obtained 
after drying in a vacuum over at 90 oC overnight. To prepare high mass loading SPAN cathode (6 
mg cm-2, based on SPAN) for the assembly of pouch cells, 54 mg SPAN powder was mixed with 
6.75 mg ketjen black and 6.75 mg PTFE. A thick film with a size of 3*3 cm-2 was obtained directly 
after grinding for 20 min. The prepared film was pressed together with a stainless-steel mesh and 
dried at 90 oC for 12h in a vacuum oven. The Li-SPAN pouch cells under lean electrolyte 
conditions were assembled using SPAN (3*3 cm-2) as cathodes, Li foil (3.5*3.5 cm-2, 50 µm in 
thickness) as the anode, Celgard 2025 membrane as the separator, LDEE, LDEFE or LDEFE-IL 
as the electrolyte, and Al-plastic film as packing. The ratio of electrolyte to SPAN is 5 mL g-1. 
Galvanostatic cycling tests of Li-SPAN pouch cells were conducted within a voltage window of 
1-3 V using battery tester (Landt) at room temperature. The pouch cells were charged to 3 V at 
0.25 mA cm-2 and then discharged to 1 V at 0.25 mA cm-2 for the first formation cycle. After that, 
the pouch cells were charged to 3 Vat 0.5 mA cm-2 and held at 3 V until the anodic current dropped 
below 0.25 mA cm-2 before discharged to 1 V at 0.5 mA cm-2 for the rest cycles.

Characterizations. The morphologies of Li metal anode and SPAN cathode were observed by 
SEM (JEOL JSM-IT500HR). Raman spectroscopy was recorded by Horiba XploRA PLUS Raman 
microscope with a 532 nm laser. The SPAN powder was directly loaded on glass slide for the 
Raman testing. The electrolytes and solvents will be sealed in glass vials in the glovebox first to 
avoid atmospheric contamination. After transferring out from the glovebox, they will be laid down 
and loaded on glass slide which has barriers to fix vials for Raman testing. Fourier transform 
infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was recorded by Agilent Cary 630 FTIR Spectrometer. The SPAN 
powder was directly loaded on the platform of instrument for FTIR testing. XPS measurements 
were carried out at a PHI 5000 VersaProbe II system (Physical Electronics) spectrometer, which 
is equipped with a hemispherical analyzer. The spectrometer is attached to the Ar glovebox and 
sample transfer was directly through it to avoid any contact with air and moisture. Monochromatic 
Al-Kα excitation (hν = 1486.6 eV) was used at power of 25W, additionally applying a low-energy 
electron charge neutralizer. The high-resolution spectrum of each element was collected with a 
pass energy of 23.25 eV in an analysis area of 100×100 μm. The binding energy scale was 
corrected based on the C1s peak from contaminations (C-C at 284.7 eV) as internal binding energy 
standard. EIS test of Li-SPAN cells in LDEE. LDEFE and LDEFE-IL electrolytes were carried 
out on Gamry Reference 1010E Potentiostat/Galvanostat/ZRA with a 10 mV perturbation in the 
frequency range of 1 M Hz to 100 mHz. The soft XAS measurements at F K-edge were performed 
in total electron yield (TEY) at IOS beamline in NSLS II. The collected soft XAS data were 
analyzed using the ATHENA software package 
MD simulations. MD simulations were performed in GROMACS version 20221 using the OPLS-
AA forcefield2 with the molecules description for FSI- and TFSI- molecules by Gouveia et al3 and 
the lithium ions by Dang.4 We included the electronic polarizability contribution using the

Molecular Dynamics Electronic Continuum (MDEC) model5-7 and replaced the partial charges of 
all ions in the simulation by rescaled charges as suggested by Park et al.8 The partial atomic charges 
on the FSI, TFSI anion and Li ions were scaled by 0.739 and 0.725 for pure DEE and the mixture 
of DEE /ETFE, respectively.  
Three systems were built to represent each electrolyte solutions. Simulation boxes containing 
57 LiFSI and 314 DEE, or 195 LiFSI, 267 DEE and 651 ETFE, or 14 Py13TFSI, 58 LiFSI, 90 DEE 
and 220 ETFE molecules correspond to the 1.5 M LiFSI in DEE solution, 1.5 M LiFSI in 
DEE/ETFE (1:3 v:v) solution and 1.5 M LiFSI in DEE/ETFE(1:3 v:v) + 10% Py13TFSI electrolytes, 
respectively. For each system, we applied an initial energy minimization at 0 K and followed by a 
quench-annealing dynamics simulation as described by Holoubek et al.9 After the final annealing 
process, the systems were further equilibrated in the constant temperature (298 K) and constant 
pressure (1 bar) (NPT) for another 5 ns. Radial distribution functions (RDF) were calculated using 
the gmx rdf tool. The solvation structures and compositions of solvent-separated ion pair (SSIP), 
contact-ion pair (CIP) and aggregates (AGG) in each system were analyzed using an in-house 
python script. Each simulation protocol was repeated 5 times for statistics.  
In validating our MD models, we utilized the OPLS-AA force field for the solvent molecules, 
supplemented by parameters from Gouveia and Dang for FSI and TFIS molecules, as well as 
Lithium ions. We specifically focused on replicating the experimental densities of DEE and 
ETFTE solvents. The calculated densities are 720.45 ± 6.58 kg/m³ for DEE and 1157.56 ± 12.36 
kg/m³ for ETFTE, compared to the experimental values of 706 kg/m³ and 1198 kg/m³, respectively. 
The variations between our computational model and experimental measurements are within 3% 
(Figure S2b). Additionally, we analyzed the solvation structure of LiFSI in the DEE/ETFTE 
solution (Figure S2c). Our calculated radial distribution functions and coordination numbers for

Figure S15. (a) Charge capacities and Coulombic efficiency of SPAN cathode measured at 500

The SPAN cathode delivers superior cyclic stability in the LDEFE-IL electrolyte. Figure S15a 
shows the long-term cycling performance of the SPAN cathode at the current density of 500 mA 
g-1. As expected, the SPAN cathode presents an obvious capacity decay from 621.7 mAh g-1 to 
221.8 mAh g-1 after 300 cycles in the LDEE electrolyte, which is ascribed to the high organic 
content in the CEI. Improved cyclic stability was observed in the LDEFE electrolyte due to the 
increased inorganic components in the CEI. The SPAN cathode delivers an initial specific capacity 
of 638.6 mAh g-1 with higher capacity retention of 61% after 500 cycles. Under the same 
conditions, the SPAN cathode exhibits a further enhanced cyclic stability in the LDEFE-IL 
electrolyte, delivering an initial capacity of 651.7 mAh g-1 and retaining a reversible capacity of 
507.5 mAh g-1 after 500 cycles. The exceptional cycling stability was also observed at low and 
high current densities from 100 to 2000 mA g-1 (Figure S15b-d), demonstrating high stability of 
the LiF-rich CEI layer on the SPAN cathode in the coordinated-anion-enriched electrolyte.

def merge_common(lists): 
     neigh = defaultdict(set) 
     visited = set() 
     for each in lists: 
         for item in each: 
             neigh[item].update(each) 
     def comp(node, neigh = neigh, visited = visited, vis = visited.add): 
         nodes = set([node]) 
         next_node = nodes.pop 
         while nodes: 
             node = next_node() 
             vis(node) 
             nodes |= neigh[node] - visited 
             yield node 
     for node in neigh: 
         if node not in visited: 
             yield sorted(comp(node))
