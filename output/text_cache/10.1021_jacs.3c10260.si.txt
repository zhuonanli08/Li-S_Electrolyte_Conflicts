Experimental Section 
Raw Materials: Ketjen black (KB), 1, 2-dimethoxyethane (DME) and 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL) 
were purchased from DoDochem. Methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) and lithium sulfide (Li2S) 
were purchased from Innochem. Methyl butyl ether (MBE) and methyl propyl ether (MPE) 
were purchased form TCI. Methyl sec-butyl ether (MSBE), lithium nitrate (LiNO3) and 
precipitated sulfur powder (S) were purchased from Alfa Aesar. Methyl tert-amyl ether (MTAE) 
was purchased from Aladdin. Fluorinated graphite (CFx) was purchased from XFNANO. 
Carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and polymerized styrene butadiene rubber (SBR) were 
purchased from Daicel and Zeon, respectively. Graphene (G) and carbon nanotube (CNT) were 
purchased from Cnano. Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) was purchased 
from Sdhirong. Lithium metal was purchased from Cellithium. All solvents were dried with 
molecular sieves before use.  
Preparation of electrolytes: The ultralight electrolyte (UE) was prepared by dissolving 0.4 m 
LiTFSI and 0.2 m LiNO3 into the mixture of DME and MTBE (1/1 vol%) and stirring until 
completely dissolved. The conventional electrolyte (CE) was prepared by the same process 
while the amount of LiTFSI is changed to 1 m and MTBE was replaced by DOL. The 
conventional-like electrolyte (AE) was prepared by the same process with a composition of 0.4 
m LiTFSI + 0.2 m LiNO3 in DME/DOL = 1/1 vol%. 
Preparation of cathodes: The S-KB-CFx mixture was prepared by ball milling S, KB and CFx 
at a weight ratio of 60/15/25 for 3 hours, and then heat the obtained powder at 155 °C for 15 
hours in autoclaves. The hybrid cathode (HC) was prepared by mixing S-KB-CFx, G, CNT, 
CMC and SBR at a weight ratio of 90/2.5/2.5/2.5/2.5 in deionized water, and then casting the 
slurry onto carbon-coated aluminum foil, which was later dried at 65 °C for 12 h under vacuum. 
The conventional cathode (CC) was prepared by the same process while the composition was 
changed to (S/KB = 70/30 wt%) / (CMC/SBR = 1/1 wt%) = 90/10 wt%. The conventional-like 
cathode (AC) was prepared by the same process while the composition was changed to (S/KB 
= 80/20 wt%) / (G/CNT = 1/1) / (CMC/SBR = 1/1 wt%) = 90/5/5 wt%. 
Preparation of polysulfide solutions: For the conductivity test (Fig. 2e), 1.5 m Li2S8 and 1.5 
m Li2S6 solutions were obtained by mixing Li2S and S at the stoichiometric ratio in 4 mL

solvents and stirring for 48 h at 50 °C. For the viscosity test (Fig. 2e), 0.5 m Li2S8 and 0.5 m 
Li2S6 solutions were prepared by the same process. For the solubility test (Fig. 2f), 0.1 m Li2S8 
solutions were prepared by the same process. For the NMR test (Fig. 3c), 0.5 m Li2S8 solutions 
were prepared by the same process. 
Material Characterizations: The electrolyte conductivities were collected using a 
conductivity meter (INESA DDBJ-350). The electrolyte densities were measured by weighing 
4 mL electrolyte for 3 times and take the average for calculation. The electrolyte viscosities 
were measured on a MCR 502 Anton Paar with a controlled shear rate and shear stress at 10-
40 °C. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) data were collected on a Varian VNMRS 500 MHz 
at 50 °C with pure DME used as the reference and set to -25 ppm. Scanning electron microscope 
(SEM) data were collected on a Regulus 8100, operated at 10 kV. X-ray diffraction (XRD) data 
were collected on a Rigaku Smart-Lab 9Kw. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) and linear sweep 
voltammetry (LSV) data were all collected on a CHI660E and an Autolab PGSTAT302N at a 
scan rate of 0.5 mV/s. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) data were collected on 
the same equipment. All samples from dissembled cells were washed carefully with DME 
before characterization. 
Battery Assembly and Testing: All cells were fabricated in an argon-filled glovebox and tested 
on battery testers (Wuhan LAND) under room temperature. For all the coin cells, PP 
membranes were used as the separator. The size of cathode, separator and anode are Φ12, 19 
and 15 mm, respectively. The discharge current was set as 0.05 C unless otherwise specified. 
For all the pouch cells, ceramic membranes coated with KB were used as the separator. The 
size of cathode, separator and anode are 3 × 4, 3.2 × 4.3 and 3.2 × 4.1 cm2, respectively. The 
discharge current was set as 0.2 mA/cm2.

In this equation, E represents the total energy directly given by LAND battery testers. MCathode 
and MSeparator represent the mass of cathode (including aluminum current collector) and 
separator, respectively, and they were all directly given by weighing the corresponding parts. 
MEletrolyte represents the mass of electrolyte obtained by multiplying its volume and density. MLi 
represents the mass of lithium anode obtained by multiplying its thickness and areal density. It 
should be noted that no copper current collector was used in our pouch cells, because the N/P 
ratio is high enough that lithium metal itself can serve the corresponding function (Fig. S21).

Molecular dynamics simulation: 
All the molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were carried out using MD package 
GROMACS1. Two models including 200 Li+, 100 S82- ions, 962 DME, 1430 DOL molecules 
and 200 Li+, 100 S82- ions, 962 DME, 840 MTBE molecules were constructed. The force field 
parameters of DME, DOL and MTBE molecules were generated by LigParGen web server2, 
except for their restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) atomic partial charges. These charges 
were assigned to each atom based on the electrostatic potential (ESP) charges obtained from 
the Multiwfn program3. The force field parameters for Li+ and S82- were obtained from Jensen 
et al.4 and Rajput et al.5, respectively. These molecules were initially packed randomly in a 
cubic box of size 10×10×10 nm3 using Packing Optimization for Molecular Dynamics 
Simulations (PACKMOL)6. 
All the initial configurations were minimized using steepest descent employing a 
convergence criterion of 500 kJ mol-1 nm-1. All the systems were equilibrated using Berendsen 
barostat7 to keep the pressure of 1 bar with a time constant of 3 ps. Subsequently, these models 
were heated from 298 K to 400 K for 2 ns and maintained at 400 K for 2 ns, and subsequently 
annealed from 400 K to 298 K for 2 ns. Finally, the production runs of 120 ns in NPT ensemble 
under Nóse-Hoover thermostat8, 9 and Parrinello-Rahman barostat10 were conducted with a time 
constant of 1 ps at 298 K and with a time constant of 3 ps at 1 bar, respectively. The last 10 ns 
simulations were used for the radial distribution function (RDF) analysis. The van-der-Waals 
interactions was calculated with a cutoff distance of 1.2 nm. The particle-mesh Ewald (PME)11 
method with a 1.2 nm real space cut-off was used to calculate the electrostatic interactions.
