Experimental
Synthesis of Cu-2-B
The binuclear copper-based precursor (Cu-2) was synthesized via heating in an oil bath. This copper complex was prepared using 1,10-phenanthroline, copper (II) chloride dihydrate, and sodium phenolate as reactants. Deionized water and ethanol with a volumetric ratio of 1:2 were used as the solvent system, with a trace amount of formic acid added to the system. The mixture was sealed in a flask and maintained at 50 ℃ for 2 h. Upon completion, the mixture was allowed to cool at room temperature. A blue precipitate, identified as Cu-2 powder, was collected through filtration and subsequently washed. Cu-2 powder was dissolved in 1 mL of an electrolyte composed of DME/DOL solvent, 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI, and 2.0 wt% LiNO3, forming the final product of Cu-2-B.
Preparation of carbon layer
Super P powder and PVDF were mixed at a mass ratio of 9:1 using NMP as the solvent to generate a uniform slurry. The slurry was subsequently coated onto a Celgard 2500 PP film and dried at 60 ℃ under vacuum for 12 h. The resulting film was punched into 19 mm-diameter disks to form the carbon layer.
Material characterizations
The blue single crystal of Cu-2-B was obtained by electrolyte evaporation. The structure was analyzed using an X-ray single crystal diffractometer with a Bruker SMART APEX-II CCD detector at 150 K with Mo Kα radiation (λ = 0.71073 Å). The structure was verified by SHELXT and refined by full-matrix least-squares on F² using SHELXL. The chemical compositions of the Cu-2-B were analyzed via XPS on a Thermo Scientific K-Alpha system. The FTIR spectra of samples were collected on a Nicolet iS50 FTIR spectrometer. The UV-vis spectra of the samples were recorded on a Shimadzu UV-vis spectrometer. The morphologies, and elemental distributions of samples were determined with the ZEISS Sigma 360 instrument. The surface roughness of lithium electrodes after cycling was examined using a Bruker Dimension Icon AFM.
Shuttle current measurements
The cells were utilizing Celgard 2500 PP film as the separator, lithium foil as the anode. The electrolytes employed are two DME/DOL mixtures (v/v=1:1) with 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI, differing by the presence and absence of 0.3 wt% Cu-2-B. The electrochemical process began with potentiostatically charging at 0.5 C until the voltage reached 2.8 V. This voltage was held for 1000 s to pledge full stability. Then, potentiostatically discharging was conducted at the same rate till the voltage dropped to 2.38 V. After that, a galvanostatic charge-discharge cycling was performed at 2.38 V for 10000 s until system stabilization was ensured. Finally, chronoamperometric shuttle current measurements were executed.
Li2S nucleation and dissociation tests
A 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution was prepared by mixing Li2S and sulfur at a mole ratio of 1:7 using DME as the solvent. A commercial carbon paper was cut into small disks with a diameter of 13 mm and then loaded with 0.2 mg of Super P (carbon), leading to the cathode. Lithium metal was employed as the anode. To assemble the cells, 20 μL of the Li2S8 solution containing Cu-2-B was added to the cathode side, and 20 μL of the blank electrolyte was added to the anode side. The batteries underwent a galvanostatic discharge process at 0.112 mA until the voltage dropped to 2.06 V. Following this, a potentiostatic discharge was carried out at 2.05 V until the current was below 0.01 mA. For the Li2S decomposition tests, the battery was first discharged at 0.112 mA until the voltage decreased to 1.7 V, followed by a charge at 2.35 V till the current reached 0.01 mA.
Li2S6 symmetric cell tests
The symmetrical batteries utilized commercial carbon paper as the electrodes. 20 μL of 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S6 electrolyte solution containing Cu-2-B was added to both the cathode and anode sides. CV tests were performed on symmetric cells at scan rates of 3 and 50 mV s–1, within a voltage range of –1.0 to 1.0 V. Li2S6 solution without Cu-2-B was added to a cell for acting as a reference sample.
Li plating/stripping tests
Cu||Li cells were fabricated using copper foil as the cathode and lithium metal as the anode. The electrolyte consisted of DME/DOL solvent with 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI, 2.0 wt% LiNO3, and 0.3 wt% Cu-2-B. Lithium stripping/plating tests were conducted at a current density of 0.5 mA cm–2, with a stripping/plating capacity of 0.5 mA h cm–2. The Li||Li cells were constructed using two pieces of lithium metal as the working electrodes, with a control group consisting of a blank electrolyte.
SR X-ray 3D nano-CT tests
SR X-ray 3D nano-CT was performed at the BL07W soft X-ray imaging beamline of the National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory (NSRL) in Hefei, China. The pristine cathode material was carefully removed from the current collector in an argon-filled glovebox after nucleation tests. The resulting sample was loaded onto a piece of carbon-free formvar film (100 mesh) and transferred to the vacuum chamber for imaging tests. The tomographic projection images were attained over a tilt range of ±60°, with an exposure time of 2 s per projection. The acquired 2D projection data were reconstructed into cross-sectional slices using Xradia XMReconstructor software at the beamline facility, followed by 3D visualization and quantitative data analysis via Avizo Fire software (Visualization Sciences Group, VSG).
SANS tests
SANS data were collected on the small angle scattering spectrometer at the China Mianyang Research Reactor (CMRR) in Mianyang, China. The lithium electrodes were collected from Li||Li symmetric cells after working at 1 mA cm–2 and 1 mA h cm–2 over 50 cycles. The lithium surface was first cleaned with a DME solvent, and sealed in an Al-plastic film for detection. The neutron wavelength used in this experiment was 0.53 nm. The range of the scattering vector q values was 0.005 to 0.2 Å (q=(4π/λ)sin(θ/2)). 
Neutron imaging tests
The neutron imaging experiments were performed at the China Spallation Neutron Source (CSNS) in Dongguan, China, using a field of view of 23 cm × 23 cm and a spatial resolution ranging from 100 to 200 μm, with an exposure duration of 300 s, to characterize the morphologies of lithium layers before and after electrochemical cycling in a Li||Li symmetric cells at 1 mA cm–2 and 1 mA h cm–2 over 30 and 100 cycles.
Electrochemical evaluations
The sulfur/carbon (S/C) composite was achieved by mixing sulfur powder with Super P at a mass ratio of 3:1. It was then sealed and heated in a glass bottle at a temperature of 155 ℃ for 6 h in an argon-protected oven. The cathode slurry was obtained by mixing the composite, LA133 binder, Super P at a mass ratio of 8:1:1. The slurry was applied onto the aluminum foil, dried in a vacuum environment at 60 ℃ for 12 h. Then, it was cut into tablet-shaped cathodes with a diameter of 13 mm. The conventional coin-type cells were assembled using a sulfur cathode and a lithium anode. A Celgard 2500 PP separator coated with carbon on one side was placed between them. The catalytic electrolytes were added to the cathode side, and blank electrolyte was added to the anode side. The sulfur content for the entire cathode was 60 wt%. The electrolyte-to-sulfur ratios for the conventional cells (sulfur loading: 1.4–1.6 mg cm–2) and high-loaded cells (sulfur loading: 6.5 and 7.2 mg cm–2) were 12.0 and 4.5 μL mg–1, respectively. Measurements for galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD), rate capability, and cycling stability were performed using a Neware Battery Testing System within a voltage range of 1.7 to 2.8 V. Additionally, CV, LSV, and EIS profiles were obtained using a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation.
Theoretical calculations
All calculations were performed using the vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) based on density functional theory (DFT) [1,2]. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional was adopted to accurately describe the electron exchange-correlation interaction [3,4]. The DFT-D3 method with Grimme correction accounts for weak van der Waals interactions between molecules/atoms and substrates [5]. The cut-off energy was set to 450 eV. The spacing along the z-axis was set to 20 Å to eliminate interactions between periodic images. Energy and force were converged to thresholds of 10–5 eV and 0.02 eV Å–1, respectively. The climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method was employed to determine the energy barriers for Li2S decomposition and Li diffusion [6].
