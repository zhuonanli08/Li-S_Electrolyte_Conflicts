1. Experimental Section
1.1 Electrolytes and electrodes
Bis[2-(trimethylsilyloxy)ethyl] Ether (≥98%) (BTEE), 1,2-Difluorobenzene (DFBn) (≥98%) were purchased from Aladdin. Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), Diethylene glycol dimethyl ether (Diglyme), 1,1,2,2-Tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropylether (TTE) were purchased from Dodo Chem. All solvents were dried by 4 Å molecular sieve before use. Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) (Mw=150,000) and sulfur were purchased from Aladdin. To synthesize sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN), polyacrylonitrile and sulfur were mixed in an agate mortar at a mass ratio of 1:3. The mixture was heated in an argon-filled furnace at 350 °C for 6 h with a ramping rate of 3 °C min-1. The 0.5M Li2S6 solution was prepared by mixing Li2S and S in a 1: 5 molar ratio.The SPAN cathode was prepared by uniformly mixing SPAN, carbon black (Super P) and Poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) with a weight ratio of 8:1:1 and it was casted onto Carbon coated Al collector, then drying with 80 ℃ at vacuum environment for 12 h. 
1.2 Characterization
The as prepared electrolytes were dissolved in CDCl3 to conduct Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) analysis (Bruker 400M). Raman test of different electrolytes was conducted using 532 nm laser source (Invia Reβex, Renishaw). The morphology of cycled electrodes was observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7610F, Japan), The components of electrolyte-electrode-interface (EEI) were measured through X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha). The structure of S, PAN, SPAN were tested by powder X-ray diffraction (MiniFlex 600, Japan) with Cu K-α radiation (λ=0.154 nm).

2. Electrochemical measurements 
All cells were assembled in a glove box (O2＜0.01 ppm, H2O＜0.01 ppm) using 2023-type coin cells with Celgard 2400 as the separator. The E/SPAN ratio was controlled to be 20 μLmg−1 for low-loading evaluations and 4.5–5.5 μLmg−1 for high-loading assessments. The charge and discharge processes were manipulated in the CT-4008 Neware system. The voltage window of Li-SPAN cell is 1-3 V. Two precycles with the current density of 0.2C, 0.1C, 0.05C then two precycles with the current density of 0.5C, 0.2C, 0.1C were applied in three types cells with SPAN loading of ≈1 mg cm-2, ≈2 mg cm-2, ≈5 mg cm-2, and then cycled at 1 C, 0.4C, 0.2C respectively. High (60 °C) temperature cycling was conducted in an environmental test chamber (1C=700 mA h gSPAN−1). For the Li-Cu half cell, the diameter of Cu foil is 12 mm and Li foil is 10 mm, and need five cycles from 0 V-1 V under the current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 to form the SEI and remove the contaminants on Cu foil. For the LSV test, the Li||SS cell was assembled and the sweep rate is 5 mV s-1. The Li||Li symmetric cells were assembled with 10 mm Li foils, the testing method for Li symmetric cells is as follows: (1) charge the Li symmetric cell with a constant current density of 0.5 mA cm-2/1 mA cm-2 for 1 h; (2) then, discharge the Li symmetric cell with a constant current density of 0.5 mA cm-2/1 mA cm-2 for 1 h. The Li+ transference number () of the electrolyte was measured using the method from Abraham et al.[1]. The procedure was as follows: a Li symmetric cell was assembled and a constant voltage of 20mV was applied. The polarization current including the initial current (I0) and steady-state current (Is) was recorded. Simultaneously, the initial and steady-state values of the bulk resistance (Rb0 and Rbs) and electrode-electrolyte interface resistance (Ri0 and Ris) were measured by the EIS test before and after constant potential polarization. 3. Theoretical calculations
The simulations presented here were carried out using the commercial software package Materials Studio, developed by Accelrys lnc. This software includes the modules CASTEP[2] (Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package) and Dmol3[3], which we have used to perform the DFT (density functional theory) calculations. Materials Studio Dmol3 achieves its speed and accuracy by using numerical functions on an atom-centered grid as its atomic basis[2,3]. The atomic basis functions are obtained from solving the DFT equations for individual atoms and are hence quite accurate. The high quality of these basis sets minimizes basis set superposition effects and allows an improved description of molecular polarizabilities[4]. 
The theoretical calculations were performed via the Gaussian 16 suite of programs. The structures of the studied systems were fully optimized at the B3LYP-D3BJ/6-311+G(d) level of theory. The molecular orbital levels of studied compounds were investigated via theoretical calculations, including the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO). The Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) program was used to plot the color-filled iso-surface graphs to visualize the molecular orbitals.The binding energy and the minimum molecular electrostatic potential (MEP) of various species were evaluated at the B3LYP level. For CASTEP, the convergence tolerance chosen was set to “fine (quality of calculation),” which corresponds to the energy value of 1.0 × 10− 6 eV/Å, the maximum number of electrons interactions being 500 and the energy cutoff (used to filter all the atomic basis functions, the pseudo-core densities for partial core corrections, and the neutral-atom potentials) 570 eV. For Dmol3, we use to also adopt the fine or custom options for the energy calculations. It also included the DFT-D correction method. As for the integration accuracy, it was also set as “fine” and the SCF tolerance was chosen to be 1.0 × 10−6. We included 500 SFC cycles. The core treatment chosen was DFT semi-core pseudopotentials, the chosen basis set was DNP+ and the orbital cutoff quality was set as “fine”. Binding energy (𝐸𝑏𝑖𝑛) between two components is defined as: 
𝐸𝑏𝑖𝑛 = 𝐸𝑎/𝑏 − 𝐸𝑎 − 𝐸𝑏
In which 𝐸𝑎/𝑏, 𝐸𝑎and 𝐸𝑏 were the total energy of the a and b complexes, component a and component b, respectively. 
In this system, the x, y and Z dimensions were periodic boundary conditions. The OPLS force field, which is suitable for electrolyte solutions,was used to optimize sample structures for preliminary structural optimization. Atomic charges of ions were multiplied by scale factor 0.73 to correct the polarization effect of anion and cation. First, the conjugate gradient algorithm and energy minimization were performed to obtain a stable structure before using dynamic simulations. Each sample was then equilibrated under the NPT ensemble at a constant temperature of 400 K to achieve an equilibrium state with zero pressure for 5 ns. Subsequently, the system temperature was reduced from 400 K to 298 K for annealing 5 ns under the NPT ensemble (under 1 atmosphere). The Andersen feedback thermostat and Berendsen barostat algorithm were applied in the system with temperature and pressure conversion. Next, MD simulations were further carried out for 50 ns with a time step of 1 fs per integration step under the ensemble conditions of NVT (298 K). System energy can be obtained through structural optimization using the energy minimization.
