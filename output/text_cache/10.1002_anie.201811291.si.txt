EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES 
Preparation and Characterization of the Proposed Electrolyte 
Battery-grade lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF6), lithium bis (fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), 
ethylene carbonate (EC), dimethyl carbonate (DMC), fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) 
were purchased from Shenzhen CAPCHEM Technology Co. Ltd. The triethyl phosphate 
(TEP) solvent was commercially purchased from Aladdin Shanghai China. TEP was 
purified with a re-distillation step under vacuum after its dehydration with calcium hydride 
(CaH2, Aladdin, AR 95%) under the atmosphere of N2 for 6 hr at 100 °C. After collected 
the 
distilled 
TEP,

1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl (TTE, purity >99.9%) was kindly provided 
by Braunway Technology Co., Ltd. The TEP and TTE were dried using 4 Å molecular 
sieves prior to use. The electrolytes were prepared by dissolving the designed amount of 
salt into the solvent mixtures (decided by the volume ratio) in the aluminum bottles. All 
chemicals were stored in and experiments were conducted in an Ar-filled glove box 
(MB-10 compact, MBRAUN) with less than 1ppm O2 and H2O. The viscosity of electrolyte 
was measured on programmable rheometer (DV-Ⅲ Ultra, Brookfield, USA). The ionic 
conductivity of electrolyte was measured by a conductivity meter (DDS-301A, Shanghai 
INESA Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd. ,China) at room temperature.        
Sulfur cathode preparation  
Poly(acrylonitrile) (PAN, weight-averaged molecular mass, Mw 150000, Aldrich) and 
element sulfur (Aladdin, AR 99.5%) were mixed homogeneously by ball milling at weight 
ratio 1:8 for 6 hr with isopropyl alcohol (Aladdin, ACS 99.5%) as a dispersant. The mixed 
powder was dried to remove the solvent and then heated at 300°C for 450 minutes or 300 
minutes in N2 atmosphere to collect S@pPAN composite material with sulfur content 44.5 
wt% and 52.6 wt%, respectively. The sulfur content was determined by elemental analysis 
(Perkin Elmer PE 2400Ⅱ)The cathode was fabricated by mixing 80 wt% S@pPAN 
composite, 10 wt% conductive carbon (Super P), and 10 wt% sodium alginate binder in 
water. Then the slurry was coated onto a piece of carbon-coated aluminum foil and cut 
into small disks with a diameter of 12 mm after dying. The weight load of all the cathodes 

<<PAGE 3>>
was ca. 2.5-3.0 mg cm-2. 
Electrochemical measurements 
All testing CR2016 coin-type cells used in this work were assembled in an Ar-filled glove 
box and tested under constant temperature (25 or 60°C). Lithium foils (thickness: 300 μm), 
ENTEK ET20-26 (PE, thickness: 20 μm) and Cu foils (thickness: 12 μm) were used in this 
work. The galvanostatic discharge/charge tests of Li-S cells were carried out on a 
LAND-CT 2001A Cell Test System (Wuhan, China) in the voltage range of 1.0~3.0 V (vs. 
Li/Li+) at controlled temperatures (25 or 60°C). The A.C. impedance spectroscopy was 
measured using AUTOLAB PGSTAT302N (Metrohm) in the frequency ranging from 100 
kHz to 0.01 Hz with an amplitude of 5 mV. Cyclic voltammograms (CVs) were performed 
on a CHI760E Electrochemical Workstation (Shanghai, China) between 1.0 V~3.0 V. 
Electrode Characterizations 
The morphology Li anodes were observed using SEM (FEI Nova Nano-scanning electron 
microscope). Before examination, the coin cells were disassembled in an argon-filled 
glove box and then obtained electrodes were rinsed with DMC thoroughly. It should be 
noted that the dried Li anode must be sealed in Ar-filled container and transferred quickly 
into the SEM equipment to avoid oxidation. Surface composition of the Li anode and sulfur 
electrode were analyzed by the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), using a Kratos 
Axis UltraDLD spectrometer (Kratos Analytical-A Shimadzu Group Company) with 
monochromatic Al Ka source (1486.6 eV) under ultra-high vacuum.

Figure S1 Schematic illustration of drawbacks of common Li-S batteries. Common 
Li-S batteries usually exploit sulfur/carbon composite cathode and ether-based electrolyte. 
In this case, the issues of long-chain lithium polysulfides (LiPS) dissolution into liquid 
electrolyte still remains whatever the modification work on cathode design or separator 
coating. The parasitic reaction between LiPS with Li anode results in low CE and capacity 
fading. Moreover, dendritic Li would be rapidly formed due to incessant side-reaction and 
uniform deposition.[1] When taking the flammability of ether solvent and heat release in 
practical pack cell, the Li-S batteries would become less meaningful though the advantage 
in energy density.

Figure S17 Raman spectra of solvents and electrolytes with different LiFSI 
concentrations in the region 500 to 1400 cm-1. 
 
Raman spectroscopy was conducted to explain the coordination of Li+ with TEP 
molecules in series of electrolytes. In the results, the free TEP molecules display 
absorption peaks at 733 cm-1, which is assigned to the symmetric stretching vibration of 
P-O-(C).[7] After saturated Li salt concentration (3.8 M) addition, the symmetric stretching 
vibration shifts to 741 cm-1 due to enhanced Li+-TEP solvation. Similarly, in the 
TEP/TTE-based electrolyte with different Li salts concentration, this special solvation 
structure maintains, which is indicated by the same stretching vibration at 741 cm-1. 
However, the consistent presence of the 603 cm−1 vibration band declares that TTE does 
not engage in the solvation of Li+. In addition, the distinct signal at 1225 cm-1 (the 
stretching vibration of S=O of dissolved LiFSI) shows no changes in high concentrated 
electrolyte and the dilute IFR electrolyte.[8] This result shows that TTE addition has 
minimal effect on the solvation structures and the property of high concentrated electrolyte 
maintained. It indicates that benign Li metal anode property can be preserved even in the 
dilute electrolyte system. Moreover, the low Li concentration and low viscosity exhibits 
advantages in decreasing cost and over-potential of batteries.
