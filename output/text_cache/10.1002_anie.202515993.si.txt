EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Materials of electrolyte. All solvents and salt used in this work are purchased from Suzhou DoDochem Co., Ltd. All the solvents were dried with 4 Å molecular sieves for at least 24 h before the electrolyte preparation. DME (DoDoChem, 99.9% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), DEGDBE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), OTE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), TTE (DoDoChem, 99.8% purity, 20 ppm H2O max), LiTFSI (DoDoChem, 99.9 % purity, 100 ppm H2O max), and LiFSI (DoDoChem, 99.9 % purity, 100 ppm H2O max). 
Preparation of S/mwCNT electrode. Firstly, nanosulfur powder and multiwalled carbon nanotube (mwCNT) were mixed and ground with a mass ratio of 3:1. The obtained S/mwCNT composite was further heated at a temperature of 155 °C for 6 h under a vacuum atmosphere. For the slurry preparation, S/mwCNT mixture (80 wt%), Ketjenblack (10 wt%) and LA133 binder (10 wt%) were mixed and ball-milled with the appropariate addition of water as the solvent, which was then casted onto a piece of clean Al foil. Finally, the homogenous slurry was dried at 60 °C for 12 h under vacuum environment to remove water and oxygen residue. The as-prepared electrode was cut into small disks with a diameter of 12 mm prior to the battery assembly. Note that routine sulfur loading in an electrode was controlled to be ca. 1.2 mg cm−2 and the high loading cathode has an elevated sulfur loading of ca. 5.0 mg cm−2.
Shuttle current measurement. Li–S batteries was assembled with r-LHCE were firstly placed into an oven with a constant temperature of 25 oC and 60 oC for 6 h, which were then galvanostatically charged to 3.0 V at 0.1 mA. The batteries were further discharged to 2.3, 2.25, 2.2, 2.15, 2.1, 2.0, 1.85, and 1.7 V step by step. Note that at each voltage step, the batteries were kept potentiostatically for 2.0 h while the time was adjusted to 4 h at both 2.0 and 1.85 V to observe the current–time curves. For the test of B-LMCE, M-LMCE, and DOL/DME the discharge voltages are set to 2.4, 2.35, 2.3, 2.25, 2.2, 2.15, 2.1, and 2.05 V. 
GITT measurement. The GITT curves were obtained by a series of galvanostatic discharge processes of 7.5 min at 0.05 C followed by a 75.0 min rest at each step. 
In-situ EIS measurement. As-assembled Li–S batteries with different electrolytes were placed into a homothermal oven of 60 oC to stabilize the SEI. Then the in-situ EIS test was conducted at multiple cycling stages during the discharge (open-circuit voltage, 2.3, 2.2, 2.0, 1.7, and 1.2 V) and charge (1.7, 2.0, 2.2, 2.3, 2.8, 3.5 V) process.
LSV measurement. The oxidation stability of the electrolytes was investigated by LSV, and a clean Al foil was used as the working electrode, and lithium metal was used as both the reference and counter electrodes. The scan rate was set to 0.2 mV s–1.
Characterizations. Raman spectra were collected by the Oxford Witec Alpha300 R spectrometer. XRD patterns were collected by the Bruker D2 phaser. 2D 19F-7Li solution-state HOESY experiments were obtained on a 900 MHz Bruker AVANCE III wide-bore NMR spectrometer equipped with a 3.2mm H/F-X CPMAS probe. XPS spectra were acquired using a Kratos Axis Ultra DLD spectrometer, which is equipped with a monochromatic Al Kα X-ray source (hν = 1486.6 eV) and operates at 75 W. The imaging and EELS of TEM were carried out using a Titan 300 TEM. SEM images were obtained on a FEI Magellan 400 XHR. UV−vis spectra were collected on the Lambda 950 NIR. 
NMR test. Note that customized NMR tubes with a coaxial internal insert were employed to eliminate the possible influence of deuterated solutions on the pristine solvation microstructure of samples, wherein 10 mM LiPF6 was dissolved into the DMSO-d6 as the standard reference for 1H, 7Li, 17O, and 19F nuclei. Liquid-state NMR data were recorded on the Bruker 600 MHz AVANACIII NMR spectrometer with varied test temperatures.
qNMR test was obtained via combined integral analysis of 1H-NMR and 19F-NMR spectra, in which 2 mM DCBF dissolved into the DMSO-d6 was prepared as the internal reference for both 1H and 19F nuclei with the 1H chemical shifts of 7.95–7.67 ppm and 19F chemical shift of –61.20 ppm. The as-assembled Li–S batteries with r-LHCE were cycled at 0.1 C for 50 cycles at 20 °C and 60 °C, which were then disassembled in the glove box without the exposure to air. Subsequently, cathode, separator and anode were quickly transferred into a small centrifugal tube containing 1 mL of DMSO-d6, which were shaken for 10 mins. Finally, 500 μL electrolyte/DMSO-d6 mixture was extracted and tested. Note that the final molar mass of various remaining electrolyte components after the cycling process can be obtained by multiplying the original qNMR results by 2. In the meanwhile, the molar ratio of FSI– to DME and TTE to DME can be calculated based on the integral results of the corresponding NMR peaks while the DCBF serves as a reference. Concretely, the 19F NMR peak at around 53 ppm is assigned to two F atoms of FSI–, the 1H NMR peaks at around 3.23 ppm and 6.58 ppm are ascribed to terminal 2 CH3 groups in DME and terminal 2 CF2-H groups in TTE, respectively. The molar ratio is equal to the integral area divided by the corresponding F/H atom numbers.

The NMR relaxation time values of Li+ and TFSI– can be obtained via manually integrating the corresponding peak region in the 1D 7Li and 19F NMR spectra, respectively, followed by the T1/T2 dynamic analysis module of the Bruker software.

Electrochemical tests. CR2032 coin-type cells were used to test the electrochemical performance of Li–S batteries, which were assembled with S/mwCNT, Li metal, and Celgard 2500 serving as the cathode, anode, and separator, respectively. EIS and current–time profiles were recorded on the Bio-logic VMP3 electrochemical workstation. The frequency in the EIS measurement was kept within the region of 300,000−0.01 Hz with an amplitude of 10 mV. GCD, GITT and rate/cycling curves were achieved on the NEWARE Battery Test System (CT-4008T-5V50mA-164, Shenzhen, China) within a certain electrochemical voltage range. 
Theoretical calculation. DFT calculations were performed at the ωB97XD/6-31+G* level using Gaussian 16 (Revision C.02)[1] to determine molecular electrostatic potentials, binding energies, and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energies. 
  Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using GROMACS 2023.1[2] to investigate Li⁺ coordination behavior. Force field parameters were generated using the Generalized Amber Force Field (GAFF) via Sobtop[3], with Restrained Electrostatic Potential (RESP) charges derived from DFT-optimized geometries, computed with Multiwfn[4]. Initial configurations were prepared using Packmol[5], followed by energy minimization (10,000-step steepest descent algorithm), NVT ensemble equilibration (1 ns at 298 K and 333 K), and production runs (20 ns at both temperatures) to examine Li⁺ coordination behavior and solvent dynamics. 
  The ab initio MD (AIMD) calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)[6]. Exchange-correlation effects were treated with the PBE functional under the generalized gradient approximation (GGA). Long-range dispersion interactions were incorporated using the Grimme D3 method with Becke-Johnson damping[7,8]. A plane-wave energy cutoff of 400 eV and a Γ-centered k-point mesh were used for Brillouin zone sampling. AIMD simulations were performed at 333 K using a Nosé–Hoover thermostat. A 1 fs time step was used with the mass of hydrogen set to 2 atomic mass units. The initial models of the OTE and DEGDBE solvation structures were obtained from MD simulations. The Li metal (001) surface was modeled as a 5×5×1 supercell with a 5-layer slab.
