S2 Experiment 
Two types of low-porosity electrodes were prepared, and the corresponding discharge curves 
were measured with those electrodes to validate the proposed multiscale model. To synthesize 
the electrode material, the Nitrogen-doped integrated Ketjen Black/Sulfur (NKB/S) composite 
was prepared using a modified previously reported approach [1,2]Typically, KB powder 
(AkzoNobel) and poly(melamine-co-formaldehyde) methylated solution (Sigma-Aldrich) were 
blended thoroughly at a weight ratio of 1:1, and the mixture then was dried and carbonized at 
900 °C under Ar atmosphere for 10 hours. The collected NKB was heat treated with sulfur 
(weight ratio NKB:S = 1:4) at 155°C for 12 hours resulted in the formation of secondary NKB/S, 
which then were sieved and separated into different size ranges. 
To prepare the cathode, carbon nanofiber (Sigma-Aldrich) first was dispersed in a 
polyacrylic acid (Sigma-Aldrich) dimethylformamide (DMF) solution (2 wt%) to form a uniform 
slurry. Then, the NKB/S particles were added and thoroughly mixed in a Thinky mixer for 15 
minutes (ARE-310, Thinky). The weight ratio of NKB/S, carbon nanofiber, and polyacrylic acid 
was controlled at 8:1:1, respectively, and the solid content in the slurry was 20%. The obtained 
slurry was cast onto the aluminum foil with a sulfur loading of 4 mg cm-2
 and dried in a vacuum 
oven at 60 °C for 12 hours. 

<<PAGE 4>>
3 
The resulting low-porosity electrodes come with two different particle size. The large-
particle cathode (LPC) was prepared using secondary particles with a size of around 90   . The 
small-particle cathode (SPC) was prepared using secondary particles with a size of around 20 
  . Porosities of the electrodes were controlled by manipulating electrode thickness by 
calendering. For a 4 mg cm-2 cathode with 64% sulfur content, the electrode porosity was 
estimated to be 45%, with an electrode thickness of 60 µm for both the LPC and SPC. To 
qualitatively understand the wetting of the LPC and SPC, energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) 
was used to track the electrolyte (lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, Gotion), 
1,3-dioxolane (DOL, Gotion) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, Gotion) (1:1, v/v)) distribution 
inside the electrode. Fluorine only comes from LiTFSI, which can be a good indicator of 
electrolyte wetting conditions. As can be seen in Fig. S 1, the LPC provides a more uniform 
wetting pattern across the whole electrode, while the electrolyte inside the SPC only partially 
wets the electrode, indicating worse wetting. 
To measure the discharge curve, 2032-type coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled 
glovebox. The electrode area was 1.26 cm2 with a 250-µm lithium anode. A Celgar-2400 
separator with a 1.9 cm diameter and 25 µm thickness was used in all coin cells. The electrolyte 
was 1 M LiTFSI/DOL/DME and 0.3 M LiNO3. The electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio was controlled at a 
lean-electrolyte condition of E/S = 4 µL/mgs. The coin cells were rested for 3 hours and then 
tested on a LANHE battery tester CT3001A at 0.1 C (1 C = 1,000 mA/g) at 30 °C in a voltage 
range of 1.8–2.8 V.

S3 Multiscale Li-S battery modeling 
The governing equations and physics of the 3-D pore-scale electrode model and the 1-D 
device-scale model is provided in this section. The strategy of integrating results of those models 
into the multiscale Li-S battery model framework is discussed by explicitly considering the low-
porosity electrode wetting influences simulated with first-principal.  
 
S3.1 Three-dimensional (3-D) pore-scale electrode wetting model  
The 3-D pore-scale electrode model aims to simulate electrolyte infiltration based on the first 
principles. The acquired wetting distribution is used as the input profiles for the 1-D device-scale 
electrochemical Li-S battery model for discharge predictions. The cathodes were generated using 
the discrete element method (DEM). The secondary NKB/S particles (Fig. S 2e-f) were 
represented with spherical particles of similar size (20 µm or 90 µm) to generate the SPC and 
LPC, respectively. After settling down the locations of the spherical particles in the DEM, the 
electrode geometry can be extracted for the electrolyte infiltration simulations. The CFD-
generated cathodes have a porosity of 42%, which is close to the experimental cases after 
calendaring.

model is shown in Fig. S 2(a-c). The volume of fluid (VOF) [4] is adopted to simulate the 
gas/liquid, two-phase flow during the electrolyte infiltration. In the VOF simulation, the mass 
and momentum conservation equations are solved iteratively using the segregated solver together

S4 Influence of the low-porosity electrode wettability 
The proposed 1-D device-scale Li-S model takes inputs from the electrolyte distribution 
profile provided by the 3-D pore-scale model to model the heterogeneous electrochemical 
reactions and precipitations inside the low-porosity electrode. This provides an integrated 
multiscale model capability to predict the Li-S battery discharge curve when electrode wetting 
becomes a limiting factor.  
In the electrode or separator, the porosity,  , generally stands for the pore volume fraction, 
  , which is defined as the fraction of pore volumes over the total electrode volume. In a fully 
saturated electrode, the filled electrolyte volume fraction    equals to the pore volume fraction 
  . However, for the partially wetted electrode, the pore volume would consist of the electrolyte 
volume,      , and the air volume,        , as a function of the location x in the electrode. Air

access in those local regions. This unsaturated wetting behavior is expected to affect several 
aspects of the electrochemical reactions and precipitation processes. First, use of the loaded
