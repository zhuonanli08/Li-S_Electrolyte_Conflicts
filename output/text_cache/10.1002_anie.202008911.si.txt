1. Experimental details 
1.1. PESn fabrication  
The reactants, including sulfur powder (325 mesh) and dithiol poly(ethyl oxide) 
(Mr=1.0×103, Huaweiruike Co.) were purchased without other refinement. The 
fabrication of PESn underwent a thermal substitution process. Firstly, 250 mg S8 was 
compounded with 250 mg dithiol poly(ethyl oxide), followed by heating at 160oC for 
2.0 h under vacuum and continues stirring in a 25 mL flask. Secondly, 2.25 g ordinary 
electrolyte was poured into the flask, which is composed of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL):1,2-
dimethoxyethane (DME) v/v=1:1 and 1.0 M lithium bis-trifluoromethanesulfonimide 
(LiTFSI) with or without 2 wt.% LiNO3. The exceed sulfur powder unreacted was 
insoluble, which is filtered out, while the PESn electrolyte solution was the product. 
 
1.2. Fabrication of reduced state PES2Li2 
The reduced state PES2Li2 was fabricated by electrochemical reduction of PESn. 
Lithium plate electrodes and carbon paper collectors were stacked together and merged 
in PESn electrolyte solution (no LiNO3) to form a primary battery. After 24 h, the 
PES2Li2 solution was synthesized after filter out insoluble lithium and Li2S. 
 
1.3. Synthesis of fully storage state PESn+xLi2 
The PESn+xLi2 was synthesize by the reaction between PES2Li2 and sufficient sulfur 
powder at room temperature for 2.0 h. After removing bulk sulfur particles, PESn+xLi2 
solution was remained. The molar concentration should be equal to the PES2Li2 solution.

1.4. Cell assemblage for cycling test 
Li–S cells for cycling test were assembled by stacking anode plate, electrolyte, 
separator, electrolyte, and cathode in CR2025 shells. The cathode was carbon/sulfur 
composite on Al foil prepared by slurry coating method. Typically, multi-walled carbon 
nanotubes (CNTs) and sulfur powder (Aladin, 325 mesh) with a mass ratio of 3:7 were 
grinded completely, followed by melting in a flask at 155oC under vacuum for 2.0 h. 
Then, the carbon/sulfur composite were blended with PVDF binder, of which the mass 
ratio was 9:1, then dispersed in NMP and stirred for 10 h to form the cathode slurry. 
The cathode was produced by coating the slurry on Al foil and heated at 80oC for 2 h 
to remove the solvent. The sulfur content was calculated 63% in cathode layer, and the 
sulfur area loading was 1.2 mg cm–2 and 3.6 mg cm–2 for ordinary and high loading 
batteries. For cell assemblage, the cathode foil was stamped into small round plate with 
a diameter of 1.3 cm. 
The anode was metal lithium plate of 16 mm in diameter and 0.6 mm in thickness. The 
separator was commercial Celgard 2400 PP membrane, and the electrolyte was 
DOL/DME 1:1 in volume ratio, with 1.0 M LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3. The amount of 
electrolyte applied in cathode and anode was 20 and 5 μL respectively for ordinary 
battery. For high loading Li–S battery, electrolyte of 30/10 μL was applied on 
cathode/anode.  
 
1.5. Cell assemblage and in situ Raman measurement 

<<PAGE 4>>
The Li–S cell for in situ Raman test was assembled in a special custom type shell. The 
cathode is carbon paper of 8.0 mm in diameter with coaxial hole of 3.0 mm in diameter 
bladed with C/S slurry (synthesized in section 1.4.) with a sulfur loading of 8.5 mg cm–
2. The anode was metal lithium plate of 16 mm in diameter and 0.6 mm in thickness. 
The separator was commercial Celgard 2400 PP membrane, and the electrolyte was 50 
μL DOL/DME 1:1 in volume ratio, 1.0 M LiTFSI, and 2 wt.% LiNO3 with or without 
10 wt.% PESn. Raman spectra of sulfur cathode was measured by a Horiba Jobin Yvon 
LabRAM HR800 Raman spectrometer under a Raman laser wavenumber of 532 or 633 
nm. Meanwhile, the voltammetric sweeping was imposed by CH660 electrochemical 
workstation. During the measurement, Li–S cells were voltammetrically discharged 
from 2.7 to 1.8 V at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1. Raman laser are focused on the hole of 
S/carbon cathode behind a quartz glass window. 
 
1.6. Nucleation test and in situ X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement 
The batteries for nucleation test are composed of carbon paper electrode (diameter=1.3 
cm) and Li2S8 catholyte (DOL:DME=1:1, 1 M LiTFSI, and 2% LiNO3 with 1.0 M [S], 
50 μL) with or without 10 wt.% PESn, Celgard 2400 separator, anolyte (DOL:DME=1:1, 
1 M LiTFSI, and 2% LiNO3, 10 μL), and lithium anode (diameter=1.6 cm). The battery 
was galvanostatically discharged to 2.1 V at 0.1 C, followed by a long potentiostatic 
measurement at 2.0 V. The batteries with beryllium window are directly measured by a 
D8 advanced X-ray diffractometer (Bruker, Germany) after the nucleation test.

1.7. Material characterization 
The morphology of cathode material in Li–S battery was detected by a JSM 7401F 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JEOL Ltd., Japan) working at 3.0 kV. Matrix-
assisted laser desorption/ionization time of flight mass spectrum (MALDI-TOF-MS) 
was detected by MALDI-TOF-MS Performance system (Shimadzu, Japan). UV–vis 
adsorption spectroscopy was characterized by UV-3600 spectrometer (Shimadzu, 
Japan). To prevent the oxidization of samples in aerobic condition, solution for UV–vis 
test are sealed in Ar-filled cuvette.
