1. Supplementary Experimental Section
1.1 Materials and Electrolytes Preparation
Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl) imide (LiFSI, battery grade), 1, 2-dimethoxyethane (DME, G1), diethylene glycol dimethyl ether (G2), triethylene glycol dimethyl ether (G3), tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether (G4), tetrahydrofuran (THF), dimethyl carbonate (DMC), fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC), tetramethylene sulfone (SUL), 1,1,2,2-Tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropylether (HFE), traditional carbonate-based electrolyte of 1 M LiPF6 in ethylene carbonate (EC)/diethyl carbonate (DEC) (1:1 by vol.) and ether-based electrolyte of 1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl) imide (LiTFSI) in 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL)/DME (1:1 by vol.) were purchased from Suzhou Duoduo Chemical Technology Co., Ltd. The electrolytes were obtained through dissolving a certain amount of salt into different solvents. For 0.3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE (1:15.7 by vol.) electrolyte, 0.56 g LiFSI was dissolved into 0.6 ml DME via fully stirring, then 9.4 ml HFE was added into the above transparent solution. For 1 M LiFSI in DME/HFE (1:4 by vol.) electrolyte, the mass of LiFSI was 1.12 g and the volume of DME and HFE were 1.2 ml and 4.8 ml, respectively. X (X=0.3, 1, 5) M LiFSI in DME electrolytes were prepared by adding 0.336 g, 1.12 g and 5.6 g LiFSI in 6 ml DME, respectively. M here represents the mole of salt dissolved in a liter of solvent.
1.2 Material Characterizations
Raman spectra were tested by LabRAM HR800 (HORIBA Jobin Yvon Ltd.) with an exciting laser of 532 nm. Viscosity of different electrolytes was probed via Thermo HAAKE MARS60 with a rotator of 60 mm in diameter. The ion conductivity of electrolyte was detected using DDS-307 conductivity measuring meter (Leici, China). Flammability tests of the electrolytes were conducted by immersing glass fiber separators into different electrolytes and then ignited to observe. The contact angles of electrolytes on Celgard separators were collected by DSA100 contact angle meter. Lithium deposition morphology was tested by scanning electron microscope (SEM, JEOL JSM-7610FPlus). The surface compositions of cycled lithium metal anodes were detected by XPS (Thermo Scientific Nexsa system). The solid electrolyte interface (SEI) characterizations were detected by cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (cryo-TEM, Talos F200C 200 kV).
1.3 Electrochemical Measurement
Ar-filled glovebox (Mikrouna) was utilized to assemble CR2016 coin cells. The amount of electrolyte in each coin cell was 80 μL. All electrochemical cycling were carried out on CT-4008T Neware test system at 30 °C. The Coulombic efficiency (CE) tests were conducted utilizing Li||Cu cells adopting definite methods as follows:
Method-Ⅰ [1]: Firstly, quantitative lithium (Qd, 5 mAh cm−2) was deposited on Cu foil under a current density of 1 mA cm−2 and then stripped with a cut-off voltage of 1 V to stabilize the surface of substrate. Secondly, 5 mAh cm−2 Qd was deposited on Cu foil again under 1 mA cm−2 to construct the working electrode. Thirdly, lithium metal was repeatedly stripped and deposited on Cu foil with 1 mAh cm−2 (Qc) at the same current density with n cycles. Finally, lithium metal was completely stripped from Cu substrate under a current density of 1 mA cm−2 and cut-off voltage of 1 V. The final stripping capacity was indicated as Qs. Based on the above testing protocol, the average CE of electrolyte in Li||Cu cells can be calculated based on Equation (1).
                       (1)
Method-Ⅱ: Quantitative lithium (Qd, 1 mAh cm−2) was deposited on Cu foil under a current density of 2 mA cm−2 and then stripped with a cut-off voltage of 1 V with capacity of Qs. This protocol was repeatedly for n cycles. The CE of each cycle is simply calculated as the ratio of Qs to Qd.
The Li||Li4Ti5O12 (LTO) full cells were assembled using Li metal foils as anodes and LTO cathodes with recognized stability to examine the effect of modified electrolytes on lithium metal anode. The LTO cathode was produced by blending LTO (BTR New Material Group Co., Ltd.), super P and polyvinylidene fluoride (8:1:1 by wt.) in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP) solvent. Al foil was used as current collector and average mass loading of active material was controlled about 3 mg cm−2. Li||LTO full cells were tested at voltage range of 1−2.5 V (1C = 175 mA g−1). LiFePO4 cathode was prepared using the same process with LTO cathode and mass loading of active material was about 3 mg cm−2. The cell was cycled at 2.5−3.6 V (1C = 170 mA g−1). The Li||sulfur full cells were also packaged by mixing sulfur/Ketjen black (KB) composite (70 wt.% sulfur), super P and LA133 binder with a mass ratio of 8:1:1 in deionized water/isopropanol mixture (4:1 by vol.). The slurry was coated onto carbon-coated Al foils using doctor blade. The average mass loading of sulfur is about 1 mg cm−2. Li||sulfur full cells were cycled at 0.5−3 V (1C = 1675 mA g−1). The specific capacities are active material capacities in full cells.
1.4 Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulation
MD simulations were performed utilizing the Forcite module of Materials Studio of Accelrys Inc. Five electrolyte models were constructed to simulate the different LiFSI-based electrolytes. The details of molecule numbers are listed in Table S4. A condensed−phase optimized molecular potentials for atomistic simulation studies (COMPASS Ⅱ) force field was selected for MD simulations. Time step was fixed to be 1 fs. Electrolyte systems were equilibrated in the isothermal-isobaric ensemble (constant NPT) using the Berendsen barostat to maintain the pressure of 0.1 GPa with a decay constant of 0.1 ps for 2 ns. The temperature was set to be 298 K with Nose thermostat. Then, the simulation models were equilibrated in canonical ensemble (NVT) for 2 ns in the production stage. The simulation time was long enough to ensure reaching the equilibrium states of electrolyte systems.
1.5 Density Functional Theory (DFT) Calculation
The binding energy of Li+ absorbed on different substrates and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of molecules and Li+ coordination structures were calculated using DMol3 module of Materials Studio. The exchange correlation energy employed Becke threeparameter hybrid functional incorporated with the Lee−Yang−Parr correlation (B3LYP) functional. [2, 3] Tkatchenko−Scheffler (TS) method was adopted to fulfill the van der Waals correction. [4] Convergence tolerance was set to 5.0×10−3 Å for maximum displacement, 1.0×10−5 Ha for energy and 2.0×10−3 Ha Å−1 for maximum force. Figures S13-16 show that the ULCEs adopting G2, G3, G4 and THF also have the feasibility for use in LMBs. These ULCEs all display uniform transparent solutions with the same as DME-based ULCE due to the strong solvent powers of these active ethers (Figure S13), which are originated from their chelating effect from ethereal O and high DN values (Table S3). Figure S14 exhibits the optimized geometrical structures and binding energies between Li+ and different anion/solvents, revealing that these active ethers deliver strong interactions with Li+. Therefore, the metallic Li in these electrolytes also tend to uniform deposit and grow to micron-sized blocky Li attributed to the FSI−-leading solvent sheaths in localized high-concentration active region (Figure S16).
