1. Experimental Section 
1.1 Raw materials 
All the raw materials, including carbon nanotubes (CNTs), sulfur (S8), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), lithium 
nitrate (LiNO3), lithium sulfide (Li2S), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), hexyl methyl ether (HME), benzo[1,2-b:6,5-
b’]dithiophene-4,5-dione (BDTD), poly(vinylidenefluoride) (PVDF), N-methyl pyrrolidone (NMP), polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard 
2500), aluminum (Al) foil, copper (Cu) foil, and lithium (Li) foil were purchased from commercial sources and directly used without 
further purification. 
 
1.2 Preparation of cathode 
1.2.1 Preparation of sulfur cathode 
Sulfur and CNTs were mixed in a batch with a mass ratio of 7:3. The mixture was then ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight 
ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a slurry. The obtained slurry was then coated on one or both sides of Al foils and fully dried at 60°C for 24 
h to prepare sulfur/carbon (S/C) cathodes for assembly of coin or pouch cells. The sulfur content in the S/C cathode was 63%. For the 
coin cells, the areal sulfur loading was controlled as 4.0 mgS cm−2, and the cathode was cut into disks with a diameter of 15.0 mm. For 
the pouch cells, the areal sulfur loading was 7.4 mgS cm−2 for the 3 Ah-level pouch cells and 8.2 mgS cm−2 for the 5 Ah-level pouch 
cells on a single side and the S/C cathodes were cut into 7.0 × 4.0 cm2 square pieces. 
 
1.2.2 Preparation of carbon-coated Al foil 
100 mg CNTs was stirred with 15 mg PVDF in NMP solvent overnight to form a homogeneous slurry with a CNTs content of 1.5 
wt.%. The CNTs electrode was obtained by coating the slurry on Al foils with doctor blades and dried at 60°C for 24 h. The areal loading 
of CNTs was around 0.1 mg cm−2. This electrode was called carbon-coated Al foil. 
 
1.3 Preparation of electrolyte 
For Li–S cells, the control electrolyte was composed of 0.8 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3 in DOL/DME (1:3, by vol.) and was 
denoted as the strongly solvating electrolyte (SSE). The electrolyte composed of 0.8 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% LiNO3 in DOL/DME/HME 
(1:3:4/3, by vol.) was denoted as the weakly solvating electrolyte (WSE). Therein, HME was selected to reduce the solvation power of 
the electrolyte due to its weak coordination ability and reductive stability arising from the significant steric hindrance. 
For the rotating disk electrode (RDE)-based three-electrode model system measurements, DOL/DME (1:3, by vol.) mixed solvents 
containing 0.8 mol L−1 LiTFSI was adopted as the SSE. DOL/DME/HME (1:3:4/3, by vol.) mixed solvents containing 0.8 mol L−1 LiTFSI 
was adopted as the WSE. Additionally, 100 mmolS L−1 Li2S8 was added to the SSE and WSE as the cathodic reactant, prepared by 
mixing Li2S and S8 in corresponding electrolyte with a molar ratio of 8:7 stirring at 55°C until all the solids were dissolved. 
To improve the WSE-based cells, BDTD was introduced in WSE as a redox mediator (RM) to promote the charging process. The 
BDTD concentration was set to be 10 mmol L−1. 
To get the value of the oxidation peak potential of Li2S8, 1.0 molS L−1 Li2S8 was added to the WSE as the cathodic reactant, prepared 
by mixing Li2S and S8 in corresponding electrolyte with a molar ratio of 8:7 stirring at 55°C until all the solids were dissolved. To get the 
value of the oxidation peak potential of BDTD, 10 mmol L−1 BDTD was added to the WSE as the cathodic reactant. The above-
mentioned WSE is a mixture of DOL/DME/HME (1:3:4/3, by vol.), with 0.8 mol L−1 of LiTFSI added. 
 
1.4 Electrochemical measurements 
1.4.1 Electrochemical tests on Li‒S coin cells 
Li‒S coin cells were assembled using standard 2032-coin cell configurations comprising a 50 μm-thick Li anode (15.0 mm in 
diameter), a PP separator (19.0 mm in diameter), and the S/C cathode (15.0 mm in diameter) with areal sulfur loading of 4.0 mgS cm−2. 
Each cell was injected with 32 μL electrolyte, corresponding to the electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio of 4.5 μL mgS−1. 
For the galvanostatic charge–discharge tests, the cells were galvanostatically tested at 0.1, 0.2, 0.25, and 0.3 C (1 C = 1672 mA 
g−1) between 1.70–2.60 V on a Neware multichannel battery cycler. For the galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests,

the cells were initially activated at 0.05 C for 2 cycles, followed by charge or discharge at 0.05 or 0.25 C for 20 or 5 min, respectively, 
followed by a 60 min relaxation period after each step until reaching the termination voltage. To avoid missing any tiny fluctuations, 
relative data were recorded every 0.2 s. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were performed using the potentiostatic 
mode at the open-circuit potential. A sinusoidal voltage with an amplitude of 10 mV and a frequency range from 0.1 to 105 Hz was 
applied. 
Li | Li2S8 coin cell was assembled using standard 2025-coin cell configurations comprising a 600 μm-thick Li anode (16.0 mm in 
diameter), a PP separator (19.0 mm in diameter), and carbon-coated Al foil (13.0 mm in diameter) as the cathode. Each cell was 
injected with 25 μL electrolyte. Additionally, 1.0 molS L−1 Li2S8 was added to the WSE as the cathodic reactant. Similarly, the Li | BDTD 
coin cell was assembled with 10 mmol L−1 BDTD added to the WSE as the cathodic reactant. 
For the cyclic voltammetry (CV) test of the Li | Li2S8 cells, the scan rate was set at 0.2 mV s−1, sweeping between 3.2–1.8 V. For 
the CV test of the Li | BDTD cell, the scan rate was set at 0.2 mV s−1, sweeping between 3.0–2.0 V. 
To test the own capacity contribution of BDTD, the Li | BDTD coin cell mentioned above was galvanostatically tested at 0.01 mA 
between 1.70–2.60 V on a Neware multichannel battery cycler. 
 
1.4.2 Electrochemical tests on Li‒S pouch cells 
Li–S pouch cells were fabricated in a dry room (dew point < −40°C). The anode of the Li–S pouch cells was double-sided Li metal 
rolled on Cu current collector with a size of 4.0 × 7.0 cm2 (75 μm thickness on a single side). For the 3 Ah-level Li–S pouch cells, the 
areal sulfur loading was 7.4 mgS cm−2 on a single side, and the E/S ratio was 3.5 gele gS−1. For the 5 Ah-level Li–S pouch cells, the areal 
sulfur loading was 8.2 mgS cm−2 on a single side, and the E/S ratio was 2.8 gele gS−1. For the assembly of the Li–S pouch cells, the 
cathodes, separators, and anodes were stacked layer by layer. The electrodes were controlled in parallel connection by using an 
electrode lug terminal. 
For the cycling tests, the WSE- and SSE-based Li–S pouch cells were cycled galvanostatically at a given rate of 0.05 or 0.25 C (1 
C = 1000 mA g−1) between 1.70–2.55 V on a LAND multichannel battery cycler. For the rate tests, the WSE- and SSE-based Li–S 
pouch cells were galvanostatically cycled at 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.25, and 0.3 C between 1.70–2.55 V on the LAND multichannel 
battery cycler. 
To verify the effectiveness of the charging RM strategy, 3 Ah-level WSE-based Li–S pouch cells with or without the RM were 
assembled and subjected to cycle tests. For the cycling tests, the pouch cells were cycled galvanostatically at 0.2 C between 1.70–
2.55 V on the LAND multichannel battery cycler. To realize high-specific-energy devices, a 5 Ah-level Li–S pouch cell with the RM was 
cycled galvanostatically at 0.025 C between 1.70–2.55 V on the LAND multichannel battery cycler. 
 
1.5 RDE test 
The electrolytes used in the test were WSE or SSE with 100 mmolS L−1 Li2S8. The reference electrode employed was an Ag/AgCl 
electrode, and the counter electrode was a Li foil (16.0 mm in diameter and 600 μm in thickness). The working electrode was a RDE, 
featuring a glassy carbon electrode with a diameter of 5.0 mm (Pine Research Instrument, USA). 
The electrochemical cell was filled with 12 mL of electrolyte with an open-circuit potential of −0.63 V vs. Ag/AgCl tested under room 
temperature (25oC). For linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) with variable rotational speeds, the scan rate was fixed at 10 mV s−1, ranging 
from the open-circuit potential to 0.2 V (vs. Ag/AgCl), at rotating speeds of 400, 625, 900, 1225, or 1600 rpm, respectively. For CV tests 
with varied scan rates, the electrode was stationary (0 rpm), and the scan rates were set at 10, 20, 50, 100, or 200 mV s−1, sweeping 
from the open-circuit potential to 0.2 V (vs. Ag/AgCl).

S13). The slope, 1/B1, can be obtained by linearly fitting the reciprocal of JL, ox at different rotation speeds with the inverse square root
