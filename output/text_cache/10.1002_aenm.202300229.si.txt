Figure S22. Schematic illustration of the coordinated state of Li+ in a) dilute electrolyte and b) 
high-concentrated electrolyte respectively.  
For the reason why the higher concentration leads to thinner CEI, on the one hand, the low DN 
of VC suppresses the dissolution and diffusion of lithium polysulfides, confining them to the 
pores or an area very close to the cathode. On the other hand, we have proved that LiTFSI 
participates in the reaction, in other words, the lithium polysulfides should collide with the 
LiTFSI molecules, while the electrolyte of high concentration offers more solvated Li+ and 
TFSI-. In addition, the high concentration electrolyte with low-DN solvent can effectively 
change the coordinated state of Li+, from solvent-separated-ion-pair to contacted-ion-pair or 
aggregate, in other words, anions enter the solvated sheath of Li+. The special solvated structure 
is more beneficial to the fast collision of coordinated VC molecules and lithium polysulfides 
molecules. Therefore, the restricted LiTFSI can react with the coordinated VC within a very 
short time at the pores or on the surface of cathode by consuming a rarely small amount of 
active sulfur species in a high concentrated VC-based electrolyte. In this way, we can obtain a 
thin and uniform CEI to resist the further dissolution and diffusion of the intermediates and the 
decomposition of VC solvent by wasting negligible active materials, thoroughly inhibiting the 
shuttle effect.
