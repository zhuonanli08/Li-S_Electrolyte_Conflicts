1. Experimental
1.1 Reagents and materials
Molybdenum pentachloride (MoCl5, 99.6%), bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)imide (LiTFSI, 99.9%), lithium sulfide (Li2S, 99.5%) and lithium nitrate (LiNO3, 99.9%) were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin Bio-Chem Technology Co., Ltd. 1,3-Dioxetane (DOL, 99.95%), 1,2-diamethoxyethane (DME, 99.95%), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, 99.9%) and polypropylene separator (PP, Celgard 2500) were purchased from DoDo Chem. Sulfur power (S, 99.5%), Ketjen Black (KB) carbon, acetylene black, LA133 binder, aluminum (Al) foil, and battery shell accessories were obtained from Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. Lithium (Li) foil was purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. Carbon paper (CP, TGP-H-060) was purchased from TORAY (Japan).
1.2 Material characterizations
The morphology observations of cycled electrodes were observed by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, TESCANMIRA) at accelerating voltages of 0.3, 2, 10, and 20 keV, equipped with energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, Oxford). The crystal structure of the sample was identified with X-ray diffraction (XRD, PANalytical, Cu K radiation). Raman spectra were measured by an inVia (Renishaw) at the wavelength of 400-4000 cm-1. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB250Xi) using a Thermo Fisher-VG Scientific XPS detector was conducted to verify the surface compositions and chemical valence of materials and electrodes. The coordination state of lithium was detected by nuclear magnetic resonance methods (NMR, AVANCE III 500M).
1.3 Li-S cell assembly and electrochemical characterizations
For preparing the sulfur cathode, the carbon-sulfur composite was prepared by a melt-diffusion method. Firstly, Ketjen Black (KB) carbon and sulfur powder at the weight ratio of 1:10 were mixed and heated in an oil bath at 155 °C for 12 h, then the mixture was evaporated at 170 °C to remove excess sulfur. The final proportion of sulfur was 75 wt% in the carbon-sulfur composite (named KB/S). According to the weight ratio of 8:1.5:0.5, the KB/S, acetylene black, and LA133 binder were mixed and stirred with deionized water and ethanol to form a slurry. The slurry was coated on circular aluminum foils (12 mm diameter) and heated in a vacuum oven at 40 °C for 12 h. The general areal sulfur loading of the cathode was controlled at 1.0-1.8 mg/cm2 and increased to 3.2-4.6 mg/cm2 for high-sulfur-loading cycling tests.
For preparing the modified separator, Super P (80 wt%) and LA133 (20 wt%) were dispersed in the mixed solution with alcohol and deionized water (1:1, v/v). The slurry was ultrasonicated for 12 h and evenly coated on a polypropylene separator (Celgard 2500) by vacuum filtration machine, then dried at 40 ℃ for 12 h. Finally, the area density of the modified separator (19 mm diameter) was 0.28 mg/cm2. The conventional electrolyte of Li-S cells was 0.5 mol/L LiTFSI and 0.5 mol/L LiNO3 dissolving into DOL/DME solutions (1:1, v/v), denoted as blank electrolyte. The MoCl5-based electrolyte was prepared by adding MoCl5 powder (5 mmol/L) into the blank electrolyte and stirring for 24 h until completely dissolved.
The Li-S cell was equipped with the KB/S cathode, lithium metal anode, modified separator, and different electrolytes using CR2032 coin cells, in which the ratio of electrolyte to sulfur (E/S) was about 20 μL/mgS for general sulfur loading, and the amount of electrolyte injected is kept at 30 μL for high sulfur loading. The cells were cycled at the voltage range of 1.7-2.7 V (vs. Li/Li+) on the LAND battery test system (Wuhan, China). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves were measured on the electrochemical workstation (Reference 600+, Gamry Instruments) at different scanning rates between 1.7 V and 2.7 V (vs. Li/Li+). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test was obtained with a frequency range of 100 kHz-0.01 Hz.
1.4 Experiments on the interaction of polysulfides with MoCl5
Li2S6 and Li2S4 are used as examples of polysulfides to analyze their spontaneous chemical interaction with MoCl5.The Li2S6 solutions (0.02 and 0.2 mol/L) were obtained by dissolving S and Li2S powders with a molar ratio of 5:1 into DOL/DME solvents (1:1, v/v) and stirring at 60 °C for 24 h. The MoCl5 solutions (0.02 and 0.2 mol/L) were prepared by dissolving MoCl5 powder into DOL/DME solvents (1:1, v/v). The 0.02 mol/L Li2S6 solution and 0.02 mol/L MoCl5 solution were mixed (1:1, v/v) for visual color observation and Raman test analysis. Then, the mixed solution was diluted to 0.001 mol/L and sealed for UV-vis detection with a wavelength ranging from 200 nm to 800 nm. Besides, the mixed solution of Li2S6 and MoCl5 (0.2 mol/L, 1:1, v/v) and 0.2 mol/L Li2S6 solution were dried into solid powders in the glove box for XPS detection. 
The Li2S4 solution (0.1 mol/L) was prepared by adding S and Li2S powders with a molar ratio of 3:1 into a blank electrolyte. And MoCl5 powder (0.01 mol/L) was added to the Li2S4 solution, denoted as the Li2S4+MoCl5 solution. The above two solutions were mixed with deuterated DMSO (1:4, v/v) for the 7Li NMR analysis, respectively.
1.5 Symmetrical cell catalysis experiment
To explore the chemical catalytic effect on polysulfides conversion, the CV and EIS tests were conducted by assembling the Li2S6 symmetrical cells. The electrolyte used was prepared by dissolving 1 mol/L LiTFSI into the 0.2 mol/L Li2S6 solution, denoted as Li2S6 electrolyte. And 5 mmol/L MoCl5 power was added to the Li2S6 electrolyte, denoted as the Li2S6+MoCl5 electrolyte. The cathode and anode were carbon paper (12 mm diameter), and the electrolytes (20 μL) were added to both sides of the electrodes to assemble symmetrical cells. For the symmetrical cells, the CV tests were performed in a voltage range of -1 V to 1 V with a scanning rate of 10 mV/s, and the EIS measurements were performed in a frequency range of 100 kHz-0.01 Hz.
1.6 Lithium sulfide deposition experiment
To study the deposition process of Li2S, the Li||Li2S8 cells were assembled with a polypropylene separator by applying the carbon paper and Li foil as the cathode and anode. 0.2 mol/L Li2S8 solution was prepared by dissolving S and Li2S powders (with a molar ratio of 7:1) into the conventional Li-S electrolyte mentioned above. The cathode electrolyte can be obtained by adding 5 mmol/L MoCl5 into the Li2S8 solution, while conventional Li-S electrolyte was used as the anode electrolyte. During the assembly process, 20 μL of the cathode electrolyte was added to the cathode side and 5 μL of anode electrolyte was also injected to the anode side. Electrochemical deposition of Li2S was carried out by Gamry instrument by discharging the Li||Li2S8 cells to 2.06 V at a constant current of 0.174 mA, then continuously discharging at a constant voltage of 2.05 V to the current below 10-5 A. The morphology of deposited Li2S was characterized by SEM. The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests were conducted via the LAND battery test system. Li||Li2S8 cells with different electrolytes were discharged at an electrostatic current of 0.174 mA to 1.7 V with a step time of 10 min. Subsequently, the cells were also charged at a constant current at 0.174 mA to 2.7 V with the same step time. And the relaxation time always was 20 min.
1.7 Oxidation experiment of lithium sulfide
To study the chemical reaction between MoCl5 and Li2S, 2 mmol/L MoCl5 solution and 2 mmol/L Li2S solution were first prepared in the solvent of absolute ethanol, respectively. Then the above two solutions were mixed (1:1, v/v) and diluted to 1 mmol/L for UV-vis detection.
Li||Li2S cells were assembled to investigate the oxidation process of Li2S with a polypropylene separator by using the Li2S cathode and Li anode. The Li2S cathode was obtained by mixing Li2S power, acetylene black, and PVDF binder (weight ratio of 8:1:1) in the NMP solvent and coating on an aluminum foil (12 mm diameter), then drying at 40 °C overnight in a blast oven. The electrolyte was the conventional electrolyte of Li-S cells with the MoCl5 additive (10 mmol/L), and the ratio of electrolyte to Li2S was about 20 μL mg (Li2S)-1. The Li||Li2S cells were charged with a cut-off voltage of 3.4 V (vs. Li/Li+) at 0.1 C via the LAND battery test system.
1.8 Visualization experiment of polysulfides shuttling
To visually explore the inhibiting effect of MoCl5 for polysulfide shuttling, the optically transparent Li-S cells were conducted. The KB/S electrode and Li foil were used as cathode and anode, respectively. The electrolyte was the conventional electrolyte of Li-S electrolyte with 5 mmol/L MoCl5 and the injection volume of the electrolyte was 30 mL. The discharge cut-off voltage was 1.7 V (vs. Li/Li+) by the LAND battery test system at 0.1 C. After the discharge was completed, SEM and XPS tests were used to detect the surface topography and analyze the chemical composition of the cathode surface, respectively. The contrast experiment was conducted by the same method, except using the electrolyte without MoCl5.
1.9 Quantum chemistry calculations
All structures of stationary points on the reaction potential energy surfaces including reactants, intermediates, transition states, and products, were fully optimized using the Gaussian 16 package[1] at B3LYP/6-311+G(d,p) level of theory. The harmonic vibrational frequencies were calculated to characterize the nature of the stationary points and to obtain the zero-point energies which were used to correct the relative energies. Each transition state was identified as they have unique imaginary frequencies. The relative energies in the potential energy profiles were obtained based on optimized molecular structures during optimization.
