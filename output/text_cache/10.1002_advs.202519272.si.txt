We performed a long-term plating/stripping experiment to estimate the sustainability of the post-generated Li3PS4/Li7P3S11 in a PS electrolyte cell. Noted that the Li/Li symmetric cell were adopted in this section to minimize the difference of physical surface roughness and electrode conductivity by coating error, and the platting steps were performed at a current density of 1 mA cm-2 and areal capacity of 1 mA h cm-2. Figure S5(a) shows the Li-P-S cell delivered a constant square signal over a 1000-hour plating/stripping experimental period, and magnified views in Figure S5(b) and (c), the voltage hysteresis of the Li-P-S cell has a minor increase of 4 mV to 8 mV after 400 hours interval. It is inferred that this phenomenon originates from the dynamical assemble of lithium thiophosphate during the plating process, and the dense structure by Li3PS4/Li7P3S11 growth further reduces exposure to electrolyte, resulting in a slight increase in the polarization potential. Similar effect can be reflected to the step-up behavior of capacity growth shown in the galvanostatic cycling (Figure S5(a)). As a control experiment, the LiTFSI cells exhibit a sudden rise in overpotential during plating period, which is usually caused by the growth of spindle-shaped precipitants or uneven corroded surface [1]. And the local high electric field at SEI tip promotes the decomposition of electrode deposits, which makes the decomposition of LiTFSI unable to stably supply capacity and causes additional electrolyte consumption. Extended comparison of Nyquist plot for pristine and activated Li-P-S symmetry cell can be found in Figure S5(d) and (e). It shows the interface complexity and the significant change of cell impedance before and after activation step.

Figure S8 shows the comparison of GITT profiles for Li-S cells using Li-P-S (left) and LiTFSI-based (right) electrolytes after activation cycles. Both curves are divided into three characteristic regions (I-III) along the discharge path, representing distinct electrochemical stages. The solid black lines represent the calculated thermodynamic potential profiles. In the LiTFSI cell, the voltage profile exhibits a classic two-step discharge behavior with regions II and III corresponding to the reduction of long-chain polysulfides (Li2S6-Li2S4) and short-chain polysulfides to Li2S2/Li2S, respectively. Notably, region II accounts for only 31% of the overall capacity, while region III dominates with 69%, reflecting the sluggish conversion kinetics and parasitic losses during polysulfide shuttling. The large voltage deviation between the GITT and thermodynamic curves also indicates significant internal resistance and poor interfacial transport. In contrast, the Li-P-S cell demonstrates a more symmetric and stable voltage profile. The discharge process is nearly evenly split between region II (48%) and region III (52%), suggesting a more balanced and kinetically favorable conversion. The narrower gap between GITT and thermodynamic curves highlights enhanced ion transport and reduced interfacial polarization. Additionally, the sharper transitions between regions imply a solid-solid redox behavior, likely governed by the reversible transformation between Li3PS4/Li7P3S11 and Li3P/Li2S2, rather than conventional polysulfide dissolution/precipitation reactions.
