Experimental Procedures 
Material Synthesis 
The cathode used in this work was a mixture of Ketjen Black-sulfur (KB@S), Acetylene black and Acrylonitrile multi-copolymer binder 
(LA133, 5 wt.%). The KB@S particles were fabricated by a melt-diffusion method. Ketjen Black carbon (MTI, >99 %, China) was first 
mixed with excessive sulfur (ten times the calculated amount), and then heated at 155°C in a sealed glass bottle 10 h to allow the 
sulfur to melt and infiltrate the carbon. Afterwards, the redundant sulfur was evaporated on a hot-plate at 180°C to get the KB@S with 
an appropriate sulfur content of ~70% (wt.). Next, the KB@S, Acetylene black and LA133 were mixed according to the ratio of 
80:15:5 (wt.) and added with proper amount of deionized water to form a homogeneous slurry. The slurry was then cast on aluminum 
foil and dried at 40°C overnight before being cut into round cathode (12 mm in diameter). The sulfur mass loading was controlled at 
around 1.0-1.5 or 2.0-2.5 mg cm−2. The electrolytes were prepared by dissolving different amount of Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide 
(LiFSI) in mixed ether solvents of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME). 0.2 M (about 1 wt.%) LiNO3 (Aladdin, anhydrous, 
99.99%, China) additive was dissolved in the same time. Celgard 2400 (Celgard, USA) served as a separator in coin cells, while pure 
lithium foil (China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd) was chosen as the anode. The coin cells were assembled in a glovebox filled with inert 
argon gas (< 0.1 ppm of H2O, < 0.1 ppm of O2, Mikrouna, China). 
Electrochemical Measurements 
The cells were galvanostatically cycled at different rates in the voltage range 1.7-2.8 V (versus Li/Li+) at 0 or −20°C by LAND battery 
test system (Wuhan LANHE Instruments, China). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) spectrum in the frequency 
range of 1 MHz-0.01 Hz and the cyclic voltammetry studies in the voltage range of 1.7-2.8 V were recorded by Gamry 
electrochemical workstation (Reference 600+, Gamry Instruments, USA). 
Material characterizations 
SEM micrographs was conducted with a scanning electron microscope (SEM, TESCANMIRA) at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV. X-
ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) were obtained by using a Thermo Fisher-VG Scientific XPS detector (ESCALAB250Xi). A 
nearly airtight plastic bottle was selected as a transfer tool to convert the sensitive samples (cycled electrodes) between these test 
equipment and the glovebox. The ionic conductivities of various electrolyte samples at different temperatures (25°C, 0°C, and −20°C) 
were measured by a conductivity meter (Leici, DDSJ-319L, China). 
Statistical Analysis 
Data of the cycling test of cells at 0 and −20 °C were extracted by LANHE software. Data of EIS and CV test of cells were obtained 
by Echem Analyst (Gamry). Data used for the calculation of diffusion coefficient were normalized and plotted by Origin software with 
lineal fitting. 
Ionic Models and Computational Methodology 
Quantum chemistry calculations were first performed to optimize molecular geometries of S22−, S42−, S62−, and S82− anions, DME and 
DOL solvent molecules using the Gaussian 16 package [1] at B3LYP/6-311+G(d) level of theory. The atomic partial charges on these 
solvent molecules were computed by fitting to the molecular electrostatic potential at atomic centers with the Møller-Plesset second-
order perturbation method and the correlation-consistent polarized valence cc-pVTZ(-f) basis set. The atomistic force field 
parameters for all ions and molecules are described by the AMBER format and are taken from previous work[2]. The cross-interaction 
parameters between different atom types are obtained from the Lorentz-Berthelot combination rule. 
Two sets of modelling systems were constructed consisting of 0.25 M Li2Sn(n=2, 4, 6, and 8), 0.1M/1.0M LiFSI, 0.2M LiNO3 in a 
binary DME/DOL solvent mixture (v:v=1:1). More specifically, two set of modelling systems consist of 25 Li2Sn (n=2, 4, 6, and 8) ion 
pairs, 20 LiNO3 ion pairs in the binary DME/DOL solvent mixture consisting of 480 DME and 720 DOL solvent molecules. The 
numbers of LiFSI ion pairs in two sets of modelling systems are 10 and 100, corresponding to a concentration of 0.1M and 1.0M in 
binary mixtures, respectively, which are comparable to experimental concentrations. 
Atomistic simulations of this modelling system were performed using GROMACS package with cubic periodic boundary conditions[3]. 
The equations for the motion of all atoms were integrated using a classic Verlet leapfrog integration algorithm with a time step of 1.0

fs. A cutoff radius of 1.6 nm was set for short-range van der Waals interactions and real-space electrostatic interactions. The particle-
mesh Ewald (PME) summation method with an interpolation order of 5 and a Fourier grid spacing of 0.15 nm was employed to 
handle long range electrostatic interactions in reciprocal space. All simulation systems were first energetically minimized using a 
steepest descent algorithm, and thereafter annealed gradually from 700 K to room temperature (300 K) within 10 ns. All annealed 
simulation systems were equilibrated in an isothermal-isobaric (NPT) ensemble for 20 ns of physical time maintained using a Nosé-
Hoover thermostat and a Parrinello-Rahman barostat with time coupling constants of 0.4 and 0.2 ps, respectively, to control the 
temperature at 300 K and the pressure at 1 atm. Atomistic simulations were further performed in a canonical ensemble (NVT) for 60 
ns, and simulation trajectories were recorded at an interval of 100 fs for further structural and dynamical analysis. 
Representative (Li2S6)n clusters with n=1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 were extracted from extensive simulation trajectories, and these 
configurations were used as starting configurations for DFT calculations. Calculations were performed using the Gaussian 16 
software [gaussian] using B3LYP functional form, 6-311+G(d) basis set, and Grimme’s-D3 (gd3bj) dispersion correction to obtain the 
optimized coordination structures, and thereafter to calculate the correspondent formation energy of each cluster from constituent Li+ 
cation and S62− anions.
