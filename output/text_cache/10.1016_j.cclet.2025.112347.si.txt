1.2. Preparation of electrolyte 
The routine electrolyte was composed of 1.0 mol/L LiTFSI and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 
in DME/DOL (1:1, by volume). To prepare the LiFSI + TPFPB electrolyte, 0.20 mol/L 
LiFSI and 4.0 mmol/L TPFPB were mixed in the routine electrolyte. The LiFSI 
electrolyte was composed of 0.20 mol/L LiFSI in the routine electrolyte without TPFPB. 
To prepare the electrolyte with 1.0 molS/L Li2S8, Li2S and S8 were mixed with a molar 
ratio of 8:7 in the above electrolytes. The TPFPB electrolyte was composed of mixed 
4.0 mmol/L TPFPB in the routine electrolyte without LiFSI. The above electrolytes 
were prepared in an Ar-filled glove box with H2O and O2 contents below 0.01 ppm. 
 
1.3. Preparation of sulfur cathodes 

<<PAGE 2>>
SI2 
Sulfur and CNT (7:3, by weight) were mixed first. Then, the mixture was ball-
milled with PVDF binder at a weight of 9:1 in NMP to form a homogeneous slurry. The 
slurry was coated on Al foils and dried at 60°C for 24 h. The sulfur content was 63 wt.% 
and the areal sulfur loading was 1.0 or 4.0 mgS/cm2. The sulfur cathodes were punched 
into disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm for electrochemical measurements. 
 
1.4. Electrochemical Measurements 
To verify the inhibition of LiPS corrosion, Li–S coin cells were assembled with 
600 µm Li anodes (16.0 mm in diameter), 1.0 mgS/cm2 sulfur cathodes (13.0 mm in 
diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 25 µL routine electrolyte or LiFSI 
+ TPFPB electrolyte without LiNO3 additive. The Li–S coin cells were tested on a 
Neware battery cycler (CT-4008T, Shenzhen, China) at room temperature. The Li–S 
coin cells were firstly galvanostatically discharged to 1.70 V and charged to 2.20 V. 
Then the cells were kept potentiostatically for 5.0 h at 2.20, 2.25, 2.34, 2.35, 2.36, 2.37, 
2.38, 2.39, 2.40, 2.43, 2.45, 2.60, and 2.90 V, respectively. The shuttle current was 
determined by the steady-state current at different potentiostatic voltages. 
Li | Li symmetric coin cells were assembled in standard 2032-configured coin cells 
with ultrathin Li anodes of 50 μm in thickness (15.0 mm in diameter), PP separators 
(19.0 mm in diameter), and LiPS electrolytes with 1.0 molS/L Li2S8 under a current 
density of 1.0 mA/cm2 and an areal capacity of 1.0 or 4.0 mAh/cm2.  
 
1.5. Li metal characterization 

<<PAGE 3>>
SI3 
The cycled Li metal anodes were washed with DME to remove the residual 
electrolyte and then dried. The as-prepared Li metal anodes were transferred for 
scanning electron microscope (SEM) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) 
analysis. 
Morphologies of Li metal anodes were characterized by a JSM 7401F SEM 
operated at 5.0 kV (JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). The sample preparation and transfer 
process were carried out under Ar-filled protection conditions. 
XPS measurements were conducted using Axis Supra+ by Kratos Analytical with 
an Al Kα X-ray source (1486.8 eV). The sputter etching was performed using an Ar+ 
gun (5 kV) to obtain the desired depth profile. The Ar+ sputtering rate was ~130 nm/min 
calibrated on Ta2O5 surface. 
 
1.6. Battery performance evaluation 
For Li–S coin cell evaluation, ultrathin Li anodes of 50 μm in thickness, sulfur 
cathodes of 4.0 mgS/cm2, PP separators, and routine electrolyte or LiFSI + TPFPB 
electrolyte (electrolyte to sulfur (E/S) ratio of 6.6 μL/mgS) were used. The galvanostatic 
cycling voltage of the Li–S full cells was between 1.70–2.60 V at 0.1 C (1 C = 1672 
mA/gS) after two activation cycles at 0.05 C.  
The sulfur cathodes used in Li–S pouch cells were double-side coated on Al foils 
by the slurry prepared in section 1.3 and dried at 60°C for 24 h. The areal sulfur loading 
was 7.4 mgS/cm2 on a single side. 4.0 × 7.0 cm2 electrodes were cut and directly used 
as the sulfur cathodes in pouch cells. The thickness of Li metal anodes was 75 μm on a

single side and 4.0 × 7.0 cm2 in size. A low E/S ratio of 3.0 g/gS was employed. The 
electrolyte for the Li–S pouch cell was 0.10 mol/L LiFSI, 2.0 mmol/L TPFPB, 0.50 
mol/L LiTFSI, and 5.0 wt.% LiNO3 dissolved in DOL/DME (1:3, by volume) mixed 
solvents. The pouch cell was cycled at 0.05 C afterwards within the voltage range of 
1.70–2.55 V (1 C = 1000 mA/gS). 
 
1.7. Theoretical simulations 
The S–F bond energy and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy 
levels of LiFSI and LiFSI–TPFPB complex were calculated by density functional 
theory (DFT) calculations. The calculations were carried out in Gaussian (G16) 
program [1] with Becke’s three-parameter hybrid method using the Lee–Yang–Parr 
correlation functional (B3LYP) at 6-311+G (d,p) level with a D3 dispersion correction 
[2]. The solvation effect was considered with the continuum Solvation Model Based on 
Density (SMD) [3].
