The mass fraction of electrolytes is the crucial factor affecting the energy density of lithium-sulfur (Li-S)
batteries. Due to the high porosity within the C/S cathode, high concentration of polysulﬁdes, and side
reaction in lithiun metal anode under lean electrolyte, it is extremely challenging to improve perfor-
mance while reducing the electrolyte volume. Here, we report a novel electrolyte with relatively low den-
sity (1.16 g cm3), low viscosity (1.84 mPa s), and high ionic conductivity, which signiﬁcantly promotes
energy density and cyclability of Li-S batteries under practical conditions. Moreover, such electrolyte
enables a hybrid cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) and solid electrolyte interface (SEI) layer with plen-
tiful LiF, which leads to fast kinetics of ions transport and stable cyclability even under low temperatures.
Compared to Li-S batteries in electrolyte employing 1,1,2,2-tetraﬂuoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetraﬂuoropropyl
ether (TTE) diluent, the ultra-thick cathode (20 mg cm2) shows a high capacity of 9.48 mAh cm2 and
excellent capacity retention of 80.3% over 191 cycles at a low electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio (E/S = 2) and
negative-to-positive capacity ratio (N/P = 2.5), realizing a 19.2% improvement in energy density in coin
cells (from 370 to 441 Wh kg1) and a high energy density up to 467 Wh kg1 in pouch cells. This study
not only provides guidance for the electrolyte design but also paves the way for the development of high
performance Li-S batteries under practical conditions.

1. Introduction
As the energy density of lithium-ion batteries reaches a bottleneck,
lithium-sulfur batteries (Li-S) are regarded as one of the most potential
candidates due to their high theoretical energy density (2600 Wh kg1)
and abundant resource of raw materials [1–4]. So far, the accessible
development energy density (300–400 Wh kg1) and electrochemical
performance of Li-S batteries are currently far from meeting the
expected goals [5–8]. The calculation of energy density needs to ade-
quately take into account various component of Li-S batteries, espe-
cially the amount of electrolyte [9–11]. Generally, the mass fraction
of electrolyte accounts for more than half of Li-S batteries under
ﬂooded electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio (E/S > 5) conditions [12–15]. Such
a high mass fraction of the electrolyte leads to a sharp drop in energy
density. Therefore, it is essential to reduce the mass of electrolyte to
improve the energy density of Li-S batteries [16–18]. However, the
challenges of reducing the dosage of electrolytes to the lithium-ion
batteries’ (LIBs) level are signiﬁcantly magniﬁed under practical Li-S
battery conditions, including: (1) the high porosity within C/S cathodes
requires sufﬁcient electrolyte to inﬁltrate electrode [19–21]; (2) the
dissolution and shuttling become severe under the lean electrolyte
condition [22–24]; (3) the consumption of limited amount of elec-
trolyte by the side reaction with the lithium metal anode is unsustain-
able. Therefore, the development of high-performance and minimal-
weight electrolytes is of great signiﬁcance for developing practical
and high energy density Li-S batteries.
Previous studies paid more attention to the volume rather than
the density of electrolytes. However, even at extremely lean elec-
trolyte (E/S = 2), the mass fraction of electrolytes is twice of LIBs
[25]. Therefore, the density of electrolytes becomes a key factor
affecting the energy density. At present, the reported ether and car-

Impressively, the Suo’s group proposed an ultra-light electrolyte
(0.83 g cm3), which realized a 19.2% improvement in energy density
[29]. However, such electrolyte shows a relatively low coulombic efﬁ-
ciency about 98% over 90 cycles at a low current density and capacity
(0.4 mA cm2, 1 mAh cm2). In comparison, the diluted high-
concentration electrolytes (DHCEs) have a high density, but exhibit
better compatibility with lithium anode and high-loading S cathode.
Our group recently reported a diluted high-concentration electrolyte
by 1,1,2,2-tetraﬂuoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetraﬂuoropropyl ether (TTE) dilu-
ent (TTE-DHCE) that induced a hybrid interface with the characteris-
tics of excellent stability and fast Li+ transport, thus improving
coulombic efﬁciency of Li-Cu half cells and facilitating kinetics of
electrode [30]. However, the density of TTE-DHCE remains at a high
level (1.52 g cm3) due to the high density of TTE. Moreover, highly
ﬂuorinated solvents usually are expensive. As an aromatic hydrocar-
bon, ﬂuorobenzene (FB) contributes to the formation of a LiF-rich
solid electrolyte interface (SEI) layer and possesses the advantages
of low cost and low density, which has been proven effective to for-
mulate DHCEs with low-density (1.21 g cm3) and high coulombic
effciency of 99.3% over 500 cycles [31,32].
To solve the issue of cathode on the dosage of electrolytes, sub-
stantial progress has been reported and these results are mainly from
reducing porosity of electrode, introducing appropriate catalysts, and
regulating reaction mechanisms [33–36]. Among these efforts, sulfu-
rized polyacrylonitrile (S@pPAN) shows great promise due to the fea-
tures of short-chain sulfur structure and high compatibility with
carbonate electrolytes, yielding low cathode porosity and high sulfur
utilization under lean electrolytes (E/S = 2) [26,37–40]. Moreover, a
small amount of Se and Te can be introduced into the S@pPAN elec-
trode as the eutectic accelerator to enhance the kinetics of thick elec-
trodes with low E/S [41–43]. Therefore, S@pPAN cathode with Se/Te
doping is an efﬁcient way to reduce the amount of electrolyte while
keeping fast kinetics [30,44].
Herein, to realize high-energy density and high-performance Li-S
batteries, the electrolyte (0.66 LiFSI + 0.33 LiTFSI + 0.2 DOL + 1
DME + 3 FB (molar ratio, DME = 1,2-dimethoxyethane, DOL = 1,3-diox
olane)) with a density of 1.16 g mL1 was successfully designed by
introducing FB instead of TTE diluent (FB-DHCE-3). As expected, more
than 23% weight decline per volume of electrolyte was achieved.
Moreover, such low-density electrolyte with high ionic conductivity
enables a hybrid cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) and SEI layer
with plentiful LiF, leading to fast kinetics of ions transport and stable
cyclability in thick electrode even with lean electrolyte and limited Li
anode. As the result, the ultra-thick sulfur cathode (20 mg cm2)
exhibits excellent cycle stability (80.3% capacity retention over 191
cycles), maintains a high areal capacity of 9.48 mAh cm2 at a low
E/S of 2 and limited lithium (N/P = 2.5), and realizes a 19.2% improve-
ment in energy density in coin cells (from 370 to 441 Wh kg1) and a
high energy density up to 467 Wh kg1 in pouch cells. This work
paves the way for achieving practical lithium-sulfur batteries with
a long cycling lifespan and high energy density.

2.2. Characterization
The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were carried out with
PANalytical X’pert PRO-DY2198. The scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) images of treated Li metal employing various electrolytes
were carried out with electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA). The
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images of CEI composite
were observed by Tenia G200 FEI. The Raman spectra were
observed by LabRAM HR800 with 758 nm Laser wavelength.
Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) results of different electrolytes
were tested by Ascend 600 MHz. The Wettability of various elec-
trodes was tested by KRUSS DSA100 at room temperature. The
density and viscosity of TTE-DHCE electrolyte were tested at
25 C. The impedance measurements were measured for the Li-S
coin cells using an impedance analyzer (Solartron, 1260) in the fre-
quency range of 1 Hz to 0.1 MHz.
2.3. Composition of ﬂuorobenzene-electrolyte
The TTE-DHCE electrolyte was composed of 0.66 M LiFSI and
0.33 M LiTFSI in a mixture of DOL, DME and TTE (0.2:1:3, molar
ratio). The DHCEs by FB (FB-DHCEs) were prepared by 0.66 M LiFSI
and 0.33 M LiTFSI in a mixture of DOL, DME and TTE (0.2:1:x, molar
ratio; x = 1, 2, 3, and 4.5, respectively).
2.4 Experimental methods
For Li-Cu half cells, the electrochemical performance of the
assembled batteries was measured on Land battery test instru-
ment. The coulombic efﬁciency (CE) of Li-Cu half cells is tested
under current densities of 1 or 2 mA cm2 and 1 mAh cm2 Li.
For Li-S cells, the Se0.05S0.95@pPAN composite was synthesized by
referring to the previous work of our group. All the electrodes were
prepared by a slurry coating procedure. The electrode was pre-
pared by mixing Se0.05S0.95@pPAN, KB, CF, SBR and CMC in deion-
ized water to form a uniform mixture at weight ratio 90, 4, 1,
2.5, and 2.5, respectively. Thereafter, the resulting slurry was
coated onto a current collector at various thicknesses. The elec-
trodes were ﬁrst frozen at 84 C for 6 h and then vacuumed to
0.1 Pa for 12 h. The rolling pressure was applied on a dried elec-
trode to obtain the corresponding dense electrode. For a limited
electrolyte system (20 mg cm2), the electrodes were cut into a
disk with a 12 mm diameter and the E/S ratio was controlled at
2. For the low sulfur loading cathode (4 mg cm2), the electrode
was prepared with an 8 mm diameter and the ﬁxed dosage of elec-
trolyte (30 lL). All thick electrodes are further compressed by roll-
ing equipment, which is ﬁxed at around 1.1 g cm3. Coin cells were
assembled using various electrodes and Li plate (200 lm) as anode
and Celgard 2400 membrane as separator in the Ar-ﬁlled glove. In
the pouch cell, the E/S ratio was also controlled at 2. All cycled Li
metal and electrodes were cleaned with DME before characteriza-
tion. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves of the coin cells were tested

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Physical and chemical properties of various electrolytes
Compared with TTE, FB as a cosolvent endows electrolyte with a
battery of excellent electrochemical and physical properties,
including considerable stability under high potential and light-
weight. In addition, FB can be partial reduced by Li to form LiF dur-
ing the cycle, thereby forming a denser and stable SEI layer on the
surface of the lithium metal anode. Table S1 summarizes the typi-
cal highly ﬂuorinated cosolvents that may be applied for Li-S bat-
teries. Except for FB (1.024 g mL1), there is almost no solvent
with a density below 1.3 g mL1. The component of FB-DHCE-3
has the equivalent molar ratio as TTE-DHCE, which presents
observably
lower
density
(1.16
g
cm3)
and
viscosity
(1.84 mPa s) compared to TTE-DHCE (Fig. 1a and Table S2).
Meanwhile, a smaller contact angle of FB-DHCE-3 (24.2) is
observed than that of TTE-DHCE, suggesting better wettability to
the separator (Fig. S1). In this work, FB-DHCE-1, FB-DHCE-2, FB-
DHCE-4.5 electrolytes with different amounts of FB were studied
for comparison. As shown in Fig. S2, when the amount of FB is
increased to 4.5 moles, the electrolyte presents obvious phase sep-
aration. Therefore, FB-DHCE-1, FB-DHCE-2, and FB-DHCE-3 are
main experimental subjects.
The electrochemical properties and solvation structure of FB-
DHCE-3 compared with TTE-DHCE were studied by Raman vibra-
tional spectroscopy and hydrogen NMR. As shown in Fig. 1(b), free
DME shows two characteristic peaks near 820 and 850 cm1,
which are caused by the stretching vibration of C–O–C. After add-
ing LiFSI and LiTFSI, a new peak presents near 880 cm1, which
implies Li+ coordination DME. In addition, the peak of FSI moves
from 720 to 760 cm1, which conﬁrms the formation of contact
ion pairs (FSI coordinates with a Li+, two or more Li+) [31]. With
the addition of TTE and FB co-solvents, the vibration bands of FB-
DHCE-3 and TTE-DHCE at 760 and 880 cm1 are consistent, indi-
cating
the
same
solvation
structure.
The
diluted
high-
concentration electrolyte shows free FB (810 and 1010 cm1) and
TTE (840 and 860 cm1), which further conﬁrmed our results
(Fig. S3). The similar solvation structure of electrolytes using cosol-
vent (FB and TTE) could also be proved by the signal of H nuclei
(Fig. 1c). The results show that the addition of FB cosolvent does
not change the solvation structure of the electrolyte, and retains
the characteristics of TTE-DHCE electrolyte. Besides, combined
with the advantages of lightweight, a Li-S battery with the stable
interface and high energy density can be obtained. The ionic con-
ductivities of the FB-DHCE-3 and TTE-DHCE were further studied.
Fig. 1(d) shows ionic conductivities under various temperatures
(20–40 C). The FB-DHCE-3 displays a superior ionic conductivity
(1.85 mS cm1) at room temperature, which is almost double than
that of TTE-DHCE (1.07 mS cm1). Moreover, at 20 C, the ion
conductivity of FB-DHCE-3 (0.55 lS cm1) is 5 times higher than
that of TTE-DHCE (0.11 lS cm1). Therefore, the FB-DHCE-3 has
great potential to fabricate practical Li-S batteries with high energy
density and good electrochemical performance.

3.3. Compatibility with S cathode
Various electrochemical test methods were applied to study the
reaction kinetics of Se0.05S0.95@pPAN cathode (cathode mass:
4 mg cm2) in two diluted electrolytes. The Li-S battery with FB-
DHCE-3 displays lower polarization and higher initial speciﬁc
capacity than that of TTE-DHCE, implying high utilization of active
material (Fig. 3a).
As shown in Fig. S9, CV tests at 0.1 mV s1 demonstrate that the
Li-S battery employing FB-DHCE-3 displays a higher peak current
and smaller voltage difference than that of TTE-DHCE in both oxi-
dation and reduction processes, which is in accordance with the
previous result. These results show that FB-DHCE-3 can promote
the reaction kinetics of lithium-sulfur batteries during cycling.
EIS tests of cycling Li-S batteries employing various electrolytes
show that the Li-S battery with TTE-DHCE electrolyte shows higher
interfacial impedance (high frequency-R2) and the charge-transfer
resistance (low frequency-R3) compared with FB-DHCE-3, implying
the lower ion migration speed through CEI (Fig. 3b and Fig. S8). In
addition, the interfacial impedance of Se0.05S0.95@pPAN cathode in
FB-DHCE-3 decreases slightly and then remains constant over100
cycles, indicating a stable CEI layer. In contrast, the R2 in TTE-
DHCE keeps increasing over 100 cycles, as shown in Table S3. To
avoid the effect of lithium anode, the EIS was performed on various
electrolytes using Se0.05S0.95@pPAN symmetric cells after the dis-
charge and charge processes. The interfacial resistances of Li-S bat-
teries using FB-DHCE-3 and TTE-DHCE are 230 and 350 X,
respectively (Fig. 3c), which is in accordance with the previous
result. In addition, the lithium-ion diffusion coefﬁcients in TTE-
DHCE and FB-DHCE-3 were also measured to evaluate the ionic
migration kinetics via basic electrochemical methods. As shown
in Fig. S10(a–d), the Li ion diffusion coefﬁcient of Se0.05S0.95@pPAN
cathode using FB-DHCE-3 is higher than that of TTE-DHCE. In sum-
mary, the use of low-density electrolytes (FB-DHCE-3) promotes
Li+ transport kinetics in the S cathode and the utilization of active
materials, yielding a higher energy density.

FB-DHCE-3 can not only generate a stable hybrid CEI, but also pro-
mote Li+ ion transport.

4. Conclusions
In conclusion, we reported a lighter and better performed elec-
trolyte to reduce the weight fraction of electrolytes and improve
battery
performance
of
practical
Li-S
batteries.
FB-DHCE-3
achieves a 19% higher energy density than TTE-DHCE and shows
good compatibility towards Li metal anode and S cathodes. Under
practical conditions, the Li-S battery using FB-DHCE-3 delivers a
high energy density (441 Wh kg1) and excellent cycling stability
(80.3% capacity retention after 191 cycles) at a E/S of 2 and N/P
ratio of 2.5. The corresponding pouch cell delivers a high energy
density of 467 Wh kg1 with excellent cyclability. The design strat-
egy of FB-DHCE shows great promise to fabricate practical high
performance Li-S batteries.
