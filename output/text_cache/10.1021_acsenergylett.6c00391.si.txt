predict the charge-discharge behavior of Li-S batteries. The complex, non-linear electrochemical

cathode. These phenomena span multiple length scales, from the particle level to the porous 
electrode scale, and are governed by the following set of conservation and reaction equations, 
which form the basis of the electrochemical performance model developed in this study.8,9 All the 
Kinetic parameters used in this model are adopted from Kumaresan et al.10

S1.5. Dissolution and precipitation at cathode:
In Li-S cathodes, solid sulfur dissolves and Li2S precipitates dynamically during discharge, 
causing continuous evolution of pore volume and the electrochemically active interfacial area. 
Equations S37-S39 compute the local volume fractions of solid sulfur and Li2S within the cathode 
by enforcing phase balance during dissolution/precipitation. The resulting solid volume fraction 
modifies the available electrolyte-filled pore volume and the carbon-electrolyte interface that 
sustains electrochemical reactions.
To account for precipitation-induced microstructure evolution in a physically consistent and 
reproducible manner, the effective tortuosity and active area are updated as explicit functions of 
(1) the instantaneous solid volume fractions and (2) precipitation morphology, using the 
correlations summarized in Table S1 (implementation details are provided in Section S1.2). This 
treatment follows the mechanistic picture that precipitation introduces two distinct but coupled 
resistive modes, surface passivation and pore blockage. 
Both solid sulfur and Li2S are treated as electronically insulating phases, and therefore the 
electrochemically active interface is restricted to the carbon-electrolyte interphase. At the 
beginning of discharge, solid sulfur occupies a substantial fraction of the pore space and covers 
portions of the carbon surface, reducing the initially accessible carbon-electrolyte interface. As 
discharge starts, sulfur dissolution exposes additional carbon surface, which increases the available 
active area. At later stages, Li2S begins to precipitate and progressively deposits on carbon, 
reducing the carbon-electrolyte interface again and inducing surface passivation. This loss of 
active area increases the kinetic overpotential through reduced availability of reaction sites,

 S1.6. Chemical reactions at anode:
During the discharge process, successive reduction of S occurs at cathode contributing to gain of 
overall electrochemical energy. However, these soluble polysulfides can travel to the anode and 
react chemically with highly reactive Li metal (Figure S2). This out-of-circuit reaction causes

S2. Experimental methods and results
S2.1 Experimental methods
Preparation of the sulfur/IKB composite. All reagents were used as received without further 
purification. The integrated Ketjenblack (IKB) host was synthesized following a previously 
reported method.15 Briefly, Ketjenblack (KB, AkzoNobel) and citric acid (Sigma-Aldrich) were 
dispersed in deionized water at a mass ratio of 1:1 and stirred at 60 °C for 2 h. Ethylene glycol 
(Sigma-Aldrich) was subsequently added at a molar ratio of ethylene glycol/citric acid = 2:1, and 
the suspension was heated to 130 °C and stirred for 6 h. The resulting mixture was dried overnight 
and then carbonized in a tube furnace at 800 °C for 10 h under flowing argon. The carbonized 
product (IKB) was ground and sieved through a 100-mesh screen. The S/IKB composite was 
obtained via a melt-infiltration process. Elemental sulfur powder (Alfa Aesar) was mixed with 
IKB and heated at 155 °C for 12 h in a sealed vessel. The sulfur content in the resulting composite 
was 80 wt%.
Preparation of the sulfur electrode. Conventional sulfur cathodes were prepared using a slurry-
casting method. S/IKB composite, Super P (MTI Corp.), and polyacrylic acid (Sigma-Aldrich) 
were mixed in deionized water at a weight ratio of 88:7:5 to form a homogeneous slurry. The 
slurry was cast onto carbon-coated aluminum foil using a doctor blade. The electrodes were dried 
at 60 °C under vacuum for 12 h, followed by punching into disks (diameter: 12.7 mm). The 
Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​aelccp/​article-supplement/​5074333/​pdf/​nz6c00391_si_001/​ by TSINGHUA UNIV user on 24 September 2026

<<PAGE 20>>
S20
cathodes exhibited a sulfur loading of 5.5-6.5 mg cm-2 and a pristine porosity of approximately 
80%. The overall sulfur content in the electrode was 70.4 wt%.
Cell assembly. CR2032-type coin cells (Xiamen Tmax Battery Equipment Ltd.) were assembled 
in an argon-filled glovebox (O2 and H2O < 1 ppm). Lithium metal foil (250 µm thick, Xiamen 
Tmax) was used as the anode, Celgard 2400 (20 µm thickness) as the separator, and the sulfur 
cathode as described above. Cathode and anode diameters were 12.7 mm and 16 mm, 
respectively. Electrolytes containing 0.3, 1.0, and 3.0 M LiTFSI (Solvionic) were prepared in a 
1:1 (v/v) mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, Gotion) and dimethoxyethane (DME, Gotion). The E/S 
ratio was fixed at 10 µL mg-1. Assembled cells were rested for 12 h prior to testing to ensure 
complete electrolyte wetting.
Electrochemical Test. Galvanostatic cycling was conducted using a LANHE CT2001A battery 
tester at 30 °C and 0.1 C (1 C = 1000 mA g-1). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) 
was performed using a Bio-Logic VSP Classic potentiostat in the frequency range from 0.1 Hz 
to 1 MHz with a perturbation amplitude of 5 mV at 30 °C. 
Characterization. The viscosity and ionic conductivity of electrolytes (0.3, 1.0, and 3.0 M 
LiTFSI in DOL/DME) were measured as a function of temperature. Viscosity was measured 
using an Anton Paar rheometer (MCR-102e) equipped with a DG26.7 double-gap geometry and 
C-PTD200 temperature control system. Samples were cooled to 0 °C prior to testing. During 
measurement, the temperature was ramped from 0 to 60 °C over 45 min at a constant shear rate 
of 50 s-1, and viscosity values were recorded every 30 s. After each measurement, the geometry 
was disassembled, cleaned, dried, and reassembled. Nitrogen flow was applied to minimize air 
exposure during testing.
