Kinetic reduction reaction model. To calculate the Faradaic impedance of LiSB, we have assumed the sulfur reduction reactions as the follows:
We donate the rate constant of S1-S4 as K1-K4. According to the experimental impedance spectra, the Faradaic impedance exhibits the inductive loop at the first plateau voltage. That is, K2 should be larger than K1. S8, which is an insulator, does not easily accept charges, In contrast, Li2S8 with polarity may accept charges easily. At the second plateau voltage, the experimental impedance spectra exhibit the capacitive loop. K3 should be larger than K4 so that the Faradaic impedance exhibits the capacitive loop. The formation rate of Li2S may be slow. In addition, a single reaction rather than a multi-step reaction may occur at the end of the discharge since only the charge transfer resistance is observed. It seems that the impedance spectra exhibit only capacitive semicircle related to the charge transfer resistance because the main reaction at the end of the discharge is the Li2S formation.
