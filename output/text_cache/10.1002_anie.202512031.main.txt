Covalent Organic Framework Quantum Dots for Near-Frictionless Ion
Transport Battery Electrolyte
Can Guo, Pan Xue, Wenhai Feng, Yiwen Yang, Luanhua Zhou, Huifen Zhuang, Yuting Chen,
Yifa Chen,* and Ya-Qian Lan*
Abstract: Optimization of electrolyte with enhanced
energy density and durability in battery system is highly
demanded yet challenging owing to the high ion transport
resistance and complex by-reactions in electrolyte. In
this work, we tailored the solid–liquid phase transport
interface by adding covalent organic frameworks quan-
tum dots (COFs QDs) into electrolyte. COFs QDs with
abundant lipophilic ether-oxygen groups and ∼12 nm
nano-size can serve as myriad ion-transport nano-engines
for constantly replenishing the interface with Li+ to
achieve near-frictionless ion transport (tLi+ = 0.98) and
exceptional stability (>400 days). Lithium–sulfur battery
based on COFs QDs shows high discharge speciﬁc
capacity of 1063.7 mAh g−1 after 100 cycles at 0.2 C and
maintains a low overpotential (∼16 mV) over 4000 h
at 1 mA cm−2 with 1 mAh cm−2 in Li//Li symmetric
battery. A Li–S pouch battery has also been successfully
assembled to validate the practicality. Our results may
promote the development of COFs QDs in energy
storage ﬁeld.
Introduction
With the growing demand of battery techniques, the signif-
icant increase in electrolyte consumption has driven a giant
global electrolyte market that expects to reach $18.39 billion
by 2025.[1–3] The huge demand of electrolyte relies on its role
as crucial medium for ion and charge transfer that possesses
signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the electrochemical stability and
safety performance.[3,4] Lithium–sulfur (Li–S) battery offers a
high theoretical speciﬁc capacity (1675 mAh g−1), positioning

an effective manner to circumvent the challenging issues like
Li dendrites or electrolyte-electrode interfacial resistance.[9]
Unlike commonly investigated homogeneous additives, the
heterogeneous ones with more complex solid–liquid inter-
faces would be more beneﬁcial for the investigation of
advanced functions like Li+ ﬂux or catalytic conversion of
LiPSs owing to the possibly existed heterogeneous phase
induced interactions, yet they are still challenging in some
inevitable issues such as the dispersity, precipitation, or
unclear mechanisms of the heterogeneously hybrid systems.[9]
Therefore, it would be quite interesting to develop hybrid
electrolytes with heterogeneous additives to effectively reg-
ulate the electrolyte functions to construct high-performance
battery systems.
With these considerations, covalent organic frameworks
(COFs), a class of porous organic polymers with high porosity
and tunable functions that are composed of light-weight
elements, would be regard as a porotype of materials for
electrolyte regulators.[13–15] They might serve as potential
heterogeneous alternatives for electrolyte additives in long-
cycling Li–S battery owing to the following advantages: 1)
COFs with low density and high porosity will possess minor
effects on the overall quality of the battery;[14,15] 2) COFs
with ion-selective pore channels can serve as nano-engines
to shorten the ion diffusion pathways and manipulate Li+
ﬂux transport in electrolyte;[15] 3) the designable COFs with
abundant functional groups (e.g., ─C═O, ─C═N, ─COOH,
C≡N, and ─SO3H, etc.) can mitigate the shuttle effects
of PSs and create unique solid–liquid interfaces, further
homogenizing the electric ﬁeld around the Li anode surface
and improving rapid kinetics behavior of LiPSs.[16–18] Nev-
ertheless, the dispersity and stability of COFs in electrolyte
would still be challenging by the possibly existed aggregation

Compared with commonly investigated homogeneous

combine their advantages to create a uniform solid–liquid

electrolytes.
Herein, we have designed a kind of COFs QDs (i.e.,
COF-TAP-4EO) that can serve as additive into electrolyte
system to simultaneously manipulate the solid–liquid phase
transport interface and sulfur redox reactions of Li–S battery
(Scheme 1b). COF-TAP-4EO with abundant ether-oxygen
groups and ultra-small size can act as spontaneous nano-
engines to regulate ion ﬂux transport and accelerate reversible
sulfur redox reactions, resulting in nearly frictionless ion
transfer and remarkable stability. Speciﬁcally, the optimized
COF-TAP-4EO based electrolyte affords high reversible
discharge capacity of 1063.7 mAh·g−1 over 100 cycles in Li–S
battery and high stability over 4000 h (overpotential, ∼16 mV)
at 1 mA cm−2 in symmetric cell. This electrolyte regulation
strategy by using COFs QDs provides a new reference for
highly sustainable and long-cycling battery techniques.

To prepare the hybrid electrolyte, a certain amount of

To better understand the redox reaction kinetics dur-
ing the cycling process, the electrochemical impedance
spectroscopy (EIS) measurements of COF-TAP-4EO based
battery have been conducted before and after the cycles
(Figures 2g and S23). As detected, the charge transfer
resistance of COF-TAP-4EO based battery is lower than
that of blank battery, proving that COF-TAP-4EO can
enhance the charge/ion transfer efﬁciency (Figure S23).[27,28]
As expected, the cycling performance of COF-TAP-4EO
based battery has a higher discharge capacity at 505.0 mAh
g−1 after 500 cycles than that of blank battery (226.5 mAh
g−1 after 500 cycles) at 1 C (Figure S24). To our surprise,
COF-TAP-4EO based Li–S battery can also tolerate high
sulfur loading and lean electrolyte conditions to some extent
(Figure 2h and S25). When the sulfur mass loading is 5.2 mg
cm−2, an initial discharge capacity of 1148.1 mAh·g−1 can be
obtained and maintained to be 825.6 mAh g−1 after 50 cycles
(Figure 2h). Meanwhile, COF-TAP-4EO based Li–S battery
can be also operated with a relatively low electrolyte/sulfur
ratio of 8 µL mg−1 (Figure S25). The COF-TAP-4EO based
Li–S pouch battery shows the initial speciﬁc capacity of
820.5 mAh g−1 at 0.05 C and remains at a decent cycling
performance after 20 cycles (speciﬁc capacity, ∼767 mAh
g−1; CE, 99.2%) (Figure S26). Besides, the electrode mor-
phology of Li–S battery before and after cycles has been
characterized by SEM tests (Figure S27). In contrast to the
preserved bulk particle and serious cracks in blank battery, the
cathode surface in COF-TAP-4EO based electrolyte shows
uniform and ﬂat morphology with inconspicuous cracks,
which suggests that COF-TAP-4EO can effectively improve
the utilization of sulfur and promote sulfur redox kinetics
(Figure S27).[27,29,30]
To gain insight into the shuttling mechanism of LiPSs in
COF-TAP-4EO based Li–S battery, in situ Raman and PXRD
tests have been carried out. For in situ Raman analysis, S8
gradually reduces to shorter chain PSs (S42−and S62−) at the
initial stage of discharge process.[31] Notably, LiPSs have been
quickly reduced and disappeared at 2.16 V, corresponding
to the middle position of the ﬁrst slope in the voltage
curve, implying that the sulfur species have been rapidly
converted by COF-TAP-4EO (Figure 3a,b).[32] In addition,
the signals of LiPSs have been observed at the beginning
of charging process, indicating that Li2S is rapidly oxidized
to PSs (Figure 3a,b).[32] As the electrochemical reaction
proceeds, Li2S is ultimately oxidized to S8, illustrating that
the reaction is a reversible redox process.[33,34] Besides, the
in situ PXRD contour plots directly demonstrate that sulfur
can be converted into Li2S during the discharge process, and
ultimately transform back to sulfur in the subsequent charging
process, which veriﬁes that COF-TAP-4EO can facilitate the
catalytic conversion of LiPSs (Figure 3c).[26,33,35] Moreover,
the corresponding waterfall diagram more intuitively reﬂects
the conversion process of sulfur during the electrochemical
process (Figure S28).[36,37] Based on these results, we have also
evaluated the adsorption ability of COF-TAP-4EO for LiPSs.
The CV curves of COF-TAP-4EO based symmetric battery
show the higher current response than that of blank symmet-
ric battery, indicating its better catalytic activity to facilitate
PSs conversion (Figure S29).[5,38] Besides, the kinetics of Li2S
nucleation test exhibits that COF-TAP-4EO based electrolyte
has higher catalytic activity than blank electrolyte (Figure
S30).[31] To further elucidate the possible mechanism, density
functional theory (DFT) calculations have been conducted
to assess the adsorption and catalytic conversion effects of
COFs on LiPSs. DFT calculation results reveal that COF-
TAP-4EO exhibits a more favorable Gibbs energy difference
values for LiPSs adsorption than that of COF-TAP-3EO,
COF-TAP-OMe, and COF-TAP-PA, thereby promoting the
conversion reactions (Figures S31 and S32).[39] Besides, the
binding energy also exhibits that COF-TAP-4EO has better
adsorption ability for LiPSs (Figure S33).[39–41] Additionally,
the Gibbs free energy analysis delivers that COF-TAP-4EO
has a lower energy barrier compared to COF-TAP-3EO,
COF-TAP-OMe, and COF-TAP-PA, indicating COF-TAP-

charge density distribution shows that the ether-oxygen and
C═N groups of COF-TAP-4EO can complex with Li foil,
during which COF-TAP-4EO can not only improve the trans-
fer number of Li+ ions but also homogenize the distribution
of Li+ ions to reduce the space electric ﬁeld (Figures 5b
and S44).[48,50] The differential charge density distribution of
Li anode in COF-TAP-4EO based electrolyte demonstrates
strong interaction between the ether-oxygen chain of COF-
TAP-4EO and Li+ (Figures 5a,b and S43–S46).[50] Addition-
ally, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations have been utilized
to demonstrate the Li+ distribution on the Li anode surface
in blank and COF-TAP-4EO based electrolytes, assessing the
impact of COF-TAP-4EO on the Li+ transport/deposition
behavior at the electrolyte/electrode interface (Figures 5c–e
and S47).[51,52] The relative concentration of Li+ in COF-
TAP-4EO based electrolyte increases signiﬁcantly, while the
relative concentration of DOL, DME, and TFSI−reduce
dramatically (Figure 5c–e). Conversely, there is no discernible
desolvation effect of blank electrolyte (Figure S47).[52] It is
concluded that these ﬁndings conﬁrm that COF-TAP-4EO
can reduce the number of DOL, DME, and TFSI−around Li+
to signiﬁcantly reduce the resistance for Li+ transport and side
reactions, thereby optimizing the performance of Li metal
anode.[53,54] Besides, it has been found that COF-TAP-4EO
with ether-oxygen chain can offer abundant Li+ adsorption
sites and transport channels, which can improve the Li+
diffusion coefﬁcient of COF-TAP-4EO based electrolyte
(Figure S48).[55,56]

Conclusion
In conclusion, we tailored the solid–liquid phase transport
interface of Li–S battery by adding COFs QDs into the
electrolyte. Due to the abundant lipophilic ether-oxygen
groups and ∼12 nm nano-size, the optimal COF-TAP-
4EO can serve as myriad ion-transport nano-engines for
constantly replenishing the interface with Li+ to achieve
near-frictionless ion transport and remarkable stability. Due
to these superiorities, thus-assembled COF-TAP-4EO based
battery shows high discharge speciﬁc capacity and remarkable
cycling stability. A Li–S pouch battery has also been successful
assembled to validate the practicality. This study might
provide a new perspective on the application of COFs
QDs to concurrently tackle the inherent obstacles of Li–S
battery.
