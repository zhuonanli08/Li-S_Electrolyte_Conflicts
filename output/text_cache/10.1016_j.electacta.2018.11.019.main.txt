The effect of the lithium perchlorate concentration in sulfolane (in the range from 0.1M to 2.8М) on the
performance of lithium-sulfur cells is studied. It is shown that the concentration of lithium perchlorate in
sulfolane considerably affects the depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur, the reactivity of lithium
polysulﬁdes, and the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells. The maximum depth of
electrochemical reduction of sulfur is reached at the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane of
1.0M, while the minimum depth is reached at 2.8М. The depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur is
limited by the number of free solvent molecules. An increase in the concentration of the support salt
results in an increase in the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells. At concentrations of the
support salt above 2.4М, the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells is close to 100%.
We believe that the concentration of support salts affects the species of lithium polysulﬁdes, their
association-dissociation equilibria in electrolyte solutions and the processes of solvation of lithium ions
contained in lithium polysulﬁdes and support salts, and thus determines the performance of lithium-
sulfur cells.

1. Introduction
Lithium-sulfur batteries are batteries with a liquid cathode,
since the electroactive materials of the positive electrode (sulfur
and intermediate products of its electrochemical reduction, lithium
polysulﬁdes) are soluble in the electrolyte [1e7]. The presence of
active materials of the positive electrode in the liquid phase de-
termines the considerable effect of the physicochemical properties
of the electrolyte system on the regularities of chemical, physico-
chemical and electrochemical processes that occur in lithium-
sulfur batteries during their charge, discharge and storage. Ionic
conductivity and viscosity are the most important properties of
electrolyte systems as they affect the regularities of electrochemical
transformations of the dissolved active materials (sulfur and
lithium polysulﬁdes). The ionic conductivity and viscosity of the
electrolyte systems are determined by many factors, the main ones
being: the polarity and solvation properties of the solvents; the
size, geometry and donor properties of anions in the support salts;
and the concentration of the support salts in the electrolyte
solutions.
The regularities of electrochemical transformations of sulfur and
lithium polysulﬁdes are determined by their species in the elec-
trolyte solutions. By chemical nature, lithium polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn)
are salts of polysulfane acids (H2Sn) [8]. Lithium polysulﬁdes, dis-
solved in aprotic dipolar solvents, can undergo heterolytic (elec-
trolytic) [9e11] (Equations (1) and (2)) and homolytic dissociation
[12e22] (Equations (3) and (4)).

occur both by the ﬁrst and second stages, produces charged parti-

Solutions of lithium polysulﬁdes in aprotic dipolar solvents have
high ionic conductivity that is comparable to that of readily ioniz-
able lithium salts [24,25]. A speciﬁc feature of polysulﬁde solutions
is that they have high viscosity, which is considerably higher than
that of solutions of lithium salts with the same concentration. For
example, the 0.3m solution of Li2S6 in sulfolane has conductivity of
0.987 mS/cm and viscosity of 30 mPa s and 0.5m solution of LiClO4
in sulfolane has conductivity of 1.897 mS/cm and viscosity of
19 mPa s [26]. This indicates that lithium polysulﬁdes form inter-
molecular associates in solutions [24e26].
The presence of support lithium salts in the electrolytes of
lithium-sulfur cells affects the species of lithium polysulﬁdes in
electrolyte solutions and hence their reactivity. In fact, in the
presence of well ionizable salts (salts with high electrolytic disso-
ciation constants), the electrolytic dissociation of lithium poly-
sulﬁdes will be suppressed to a considerable extent. In support of
arguments on suppression of electrolytic dissociation of lithium
polysulﬁdes, it is also possible to bring the fact that there is no
synergetic effect in the speciﬁc conductivity of mixed solutions of
lithium polysulﬁdes and lithium perchlorate [26]. The degree of
electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulﬁdes will also decrease in
the presence of salts with low electrolytic dissociation constants
but to a much smaller extent.
Furthermore, the support lithium salts, present in electrolytes,
result in cationic linking of polysulﬁde anions [24], which impairs
the transport properties of electrolyte systems and the segmental
mobility of sulfur atoms in polysulﬁde chains. The effect of lithium
polysulﬁdes on the properties of electrolyte solutions has been
studied in a number of works [25e29]. It has been shown that the
presence of lithium polysulﬁdes affects the ionic conductivity of
electrolyte solutions and increases their viscosity.
Even a brief consideration of the structure and properties of
lithium polysulﬁde solutions in the electrolyte systems allows one
to assume that the properties of lithium-sulfur batteries will largely
depend on the nature and concentration of lithium salts in
electrolytes.
For example, the effect of the concentration of lithium salts on
the performance of lithium-sulfur cells was studied in a number of
publications [6,30e39]. However, the published results of studies
do not allow one to judge to the full extent about the mechanisms
of the effect of the concentration of support salts on the processes
into lithium-sulfur cells.
The purpose of this work is to clarify the mechanism by which
the concentration of support salts in electrolytes affects the per-
formance of lithium-sulfur cells, such as the depth of electro-
chemical reduction of sulfur, the rate of capacity depletion and the
coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells.
2. Experimental
2.1. Objects studied and justiﬁcation of their choice
Electrochemical studies were performed in airtight stainless
steel two-electrode disc cells by own design and production
(Swagelok® cells).
The sulfur electrodes (d ¼ 2.85 cm, A ¼ 6.38 cm2) used in this
study had the following composition: S (99.5%, Acros) - 70% wt.,
Ketjenblack EC-600JD (Akzo Nobel) - 10% wt., polyethylene oxide
(ММ ¼ 4  106, Aldrich) - 20% wt. The surface capacity of sulfur
electrodes was 2 mAh/cm2, which is equivalent to the sulfur load
about 1.2 mg/cm2. The sulfur content in the positive electrodes (in
mg of sulfur per cm2) was determined by thermogravimetry. The
theoretical capacity of the positive electrodes (in mAh per cm2) was
calculated from the sulfur content in the electrodes.
The negative electrodes (d ¼ 2.55 cm, A ¼ 5.10 cm2) were made
of lithium foil (99.9%, Russia) with a thickness of 120 mm. The
microporous polypropylene Celgard®3501 with a thickness of
25 mm (d ¼ 2.85 cm, A ¼ 6.38 cm2) was used as the separator.
Solutions of lithium perchlorate (LiClO4, 99.99% battery grade,
Aldrich) in sulfolane (99.8%, GC, Sigma-Aldrich) were studied as the
electrolyte solutions. Lithium perchlorate is an effective polysulﬁde
shuttle inhibitor [40]. Sulfolane is a solvent possessing high
ionizing capability and well dissolving various lithium salts [41],
including lithium polysulﬁdes. It should be noted that sulfolane has
a high FlashPoint (>166 C [42]). Gaseous products are not gener-
ated at the reaction of sulfolane with metallic lithium. The con-
centration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane was 0.1М, 0.5М, 1.0М,
1.5М, 2.0М, 2.4М and 2.8М. The content of electrolytes into lithium-
sulfur cells was 4 ml per 1 mAh, which is equivalent to 6.7 ml per
1 mg of sulfur. The electrolyte was introduced into lithium-sulfur
cells using a “MICROLITER™Syringe” (Hamilton Company, USA)
with a volume of 50 ml. The accuracy of electrolyte dosing into the
cells was ±5%.
The ionic conductivity and viscosity of highly concentrated so-
lutions of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane were measured using
techniques similar to those presented previously [41].
All operations for the preparation of electrolyte solutions and
assembling of lithium-sulfur cells were performed in a glove box in
a dry air atmosphere (the water content did not exceed 10 ppm).
Galvanostatic charge and discharge cycling of lithium-sulfur
cells were carried out using a BT-05PG battery tester in the
voltage range of 1.5e2.8 V at þ 30 C. The current density of the
charge and discharge were 0.3 mA/cm2 (0.15C, 250 mA/g(S)).
The results of the electrochemical studies were processed using
the Data Analyzer program, which is part of the specialized
ElChemLab V1© software package. The Data Analyzer program is

3.1. Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane on
the charge-discharge voltage proﬁles of lithium-sulfur cells

irrespective of the lithium perchlorate concentration. As an

high-concentrated electrolyte (2.8М LiClO4) are cycled, a low
voltage plateau appears on the charge and discharge voltage pro-

and c).
As the concentration of lithium perchlorate in the electrolyte
solution increases, the discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells
ﬁrst increases and then starts to decrease at salt concentrations in
the electrolyte close to 1M (Fig. 2). The decrease in the discharge
capacity occurs due to a predominant capacity decrease on the
second (low voltage) plateau. As the lithium perchlorate concen-
tration in the electrolyte increases, the voltages of the high voltage
plateaus decrease, whereas those of the low voltage plateaus pass
through a maximum (Fig. 3a). At the high voltage plateau, there are
two sections with different rates of capacity change. The voltage of
the high voltage stage was determined as the voltage in the middle
of the ﬁrst section of the high voltage plateau on the discharge
curve, which usually corresponded to 100 mAh/g(S). The voltage of
the low voltage stage of the discharge was assumed to be equal to
the value of the voltage at the maximum, which in most cases
corresponded to the middle of the low voltage discharge plateau
(Fig. 3b).
3.2. Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane
on capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling
The general regularities of charge and discharge capacity
depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling weakly depend on
the support salt concentration, except for lithium-sulfur cells con-
taining high-concentrated electrolytes (2.4М and 2.8М LiClO4)
(Fig. 4).
At moderate support salt concentrations in the electrolyte
(0.5e2.0М), the discharge capacity ﬁrst quickly decreases during
the cycling, then the capacity decrease slows down, and then the
capacity starts to decrease abruptly again.
The shapes of the capacity depletion dependences of lithium-
sulfur cells with high-concentration electrolytes on cycle number
differ from similar dependences observed for lithium-sulfur cells
with moderately concentrated electrolytes.
Of special interest is the dependence of capacity depletion of
lithium-sulfur cells with the electrolyte containing 2.4М LiClO4 on
cycle number. The cells with the electrolyte containing 2.4М LiClO4
feature the smallest rate of depletion in both the charge and
discharge capacities during cycling.
As lithium-sulfur cells with the high-concentration electrolyte
(2.8М LiClO4) are cycled, the charge and discharge capacities in-
crease and the maximum capacity values are reached by about the
40th cycle (Fig. 4). After that, both the charge and discharge ca-
pacities start to decrease.
It should be noted that the dependences of capacity depletion
on the number of charge-discharge cycles are functions of several
parameters, such as the rate of destruction of electrolyte system
components on the negative electrode, the passivation rate of the
surface of carbon particles, the sulfur redistribution in the volume
of the positive electrode in lithium-sulfur cells, etc. The concen-
tration of the support salt in the electrolyte affects the rates of all
these processes but to a different degree. Therefore, no strict and
unambiguous correlations are observed between the shapes of the
dependences of capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during

At different stages of cycling, the rate of capacity depletion is
determined by the action of various factors. In the ﬁrst-second
cycles, the redistribution of sulfur over the volume of the lithium-
sulfur cells has a signiﬁcant contribution to the depletion in the
capacity. At the initial stages of cycling (ﬁrst 10e30 cycles), the
main contribution to the reduction in capacity is made by the
displacement of sulfur onto the face of the sulfur electrode. The
resulting uneven distribution of sulfur over the volume of the sulfur
electrode leads to a rapid decrease in the charge and discharge
capacities of lithium-sulfur cells. With subsequent cycling, the ca-
pacity of lithium-sulfur cells depletes mainly due to the simulta-
neous action of two factors - the decomposition of the electrolyte
on the lithium electrode and the passivation of the surface of the
carbon component of the sulfur electrode with poor soluble prod-
ucts of electrochemical reactions. With a limited electrolyte content
in lithium-sulfur cells after a certain number of cycles, the cell
capacity decreases sharply to almost zero.
It should be mentioned that all of the factors listed above act
simultaneously, but at different stages of the cycling, their contri-
bution to the overall depletion in capacity is different.
3.3. Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane
on the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells
The coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells also
depends on the support salt concentration and changes with
cycling in a complex way (Fig. 5). The lowest coulomb efﬁciency of
cycling is observed for the cells with low concentrated electrolytes.
As the lithium perchlorate concentration increases, the coulomb
efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells increases. The largest
coulomb efﬁciency is observed for cells with high-concentration
electrolytes containing 2.0e2.8М of the lithium salt.

4. Discussion
Three aspects should be kept in mind in a consideration of the
mechanisms of the effect of the concentration of support salts on
the discharge capacity and cycling performance of lithium-sulfur
cells:
 species of lithium polysulﬁdes in electrolyte solutions;
 the association-dissociation equilibria in electrolyte solutions
containing lithium salts and lithium polysulﬁdes;
 the processes of solvation of lithium ions contained in lithium
polysulﬁdes and support salts.
4.1. Species of sulfur and lithium polysulﬁdes in electrolyte solutions
The electrochemical reduction of sulfur is a complex multistage
process [43,44] involving electrochemical reactions, reactions of
electrolytic and homolytic dissociation, and disproportionation of
intermediate products of sulfur reduction - lithium polysulﬁdes.
Species of sulfur are determined by the state of charge of
lithium-sulfur cells. Sulfur in the totally charged lithium-sulfur cell
exists mostly in molecular forms. Since elementary sulfur is prone
to form molecules with various lengths and can exist in various
crystallographic modiﬁcations, available experimental data hardly
provide enough grounds for judging about the particular species of
sulfur in charged lithium-sulfur cells after a number of charge-

The fraction of molecular species of sulfur in lithium-sulfur cells

increases. Ionic species of sulfur are lithium polysulﬁdes with

Solvate complexes of lithium polysulﬁdes can be isolated in
individual state. When solvate complexes of lithium polysulﬁdes
are formed, solvent molecules are bound in solvate shells of lithium
ions contained therein [45].
The species of lithium polysulﬁdes in electrolytes are deter-
mined by the support salt concentration. Due to the nature of the
anions, it should be expected that the electrolytic dissociation
constants of lithium polysulﬁdes are considerably smaller than that
of lithium perchlorate. If this assumption is correct, the fraction of
ionic species of lithium polysulﬁdes in the electrolyte solution
would decrease with the increase in the concentration of lithium
perchlorate whereas the fraction of neutral species would increase.

The species of lithium polysulﬁdes in electrolyte solutions

electrode to the polysulﬁde anion due to the decrease in the elec-
trostatic interaction of the electron and the polysulﬁde anion.
4.2. Association-dissociation equilibria of lithium polysulﬁdes in
electrolyte solutions
A speciﬁc feature of lithium polysulﬁdes is their liability to
disproportionation reactions that result in lithium polysulﬁdes
with both smaller and larger amount of sulfur atoms in the poly-
sulﬁde chain, as well as lithium sulﬁde. The liability of lithium
polysulﬁdes to undergo disproportionation, electrolytic and ho-
molytic dissociation results in the situation that the electrolyte
solutions in lithium-sulfur cells simultaneously contain lithium
polysulﬁdes in various species: molecular, ionic, ion-radical and
radical species.

ation, electrolytic and homolytic dissociation affects the mecha-
nisms of electrochemical processes that occur in lithium-sulfur
cells during charge and discharge. With consideration for the
disproportionation and solvation of the resulting lithium poly-
sulﬁdes with solvent molecules, the scheme of electrochemical
reduction of sulfur in lithium-sulfur cells can be presented as

4.3. Effect of solvation of lithium ions contained in lithium
polysulﬁdes and support salts on the discharge capacity of lithium-
sulfur cells
The electrochemical reduction of sulfur is accompanied by the
formation of lithium polysulﬁdes, compounds that are well soluble
in electrolytes. Dissolution of lithium polysulﬁdes in the electrolyte
solution results in binding of solvent molecules in solvate shells of
lithium ions contained in lithium polysulﬁdes (Equations (8), (12)
and (15)). As lithium-sulfur cells are discharged, the concentra-
tion of lithium polysulﬁdes in electrolytes and hence the number of
bound solvent molecules increase. The increase in concentration of
lithium polysulﬁdes in the electrolyte solutions occurs until
E.V. Karaseva et al. / Electrochimica Acta 296 (2019) 1102e1114
1108

<<PAGE 8>>
elementary sulfur is completely reduced to lithium tetrasulﬁde.
Electrochemical reduction of lithium tetrasulﬁde is not accompa-
nied by the increase in the concentration of lithium polysulﬁdes in
electrolyte solutions (Equations (16) and (18)). Therefore, its
reduction does not change the concentrations of free and bound
solvent molecules.
The increase in the concentration of lithium polysulﬁdes in
electrolyte solutions increases their viscosity and decreases the
ionic conductivity [25]. During reduction, the mean molecular mass
of lithium polysulﬁdes decreases. As a result, the viscosity of elec-
trolyte solutions somewhat decreases and the ionic conductivity
increases [49]. Furthermore, the mobility of lithium polysulﬁdes
increases due to two factors that act in the same direction, i.e., a
decrease in their molecular mass and a decrease in the viscosity of
electrolyte solutions.
Since solvent molecules are involved in the electrochemical
reduction of sulfur and high-ordered lithium polysulﬁdes, the
depth of sulfur reduction is determined by the number of free
solvent molecules, i.e., the solvent molecules not bound in solvate
shells of lithium ions in the support salts. To ensure complete sulfur
reduction, enough free solvent molecules should be available for
the solvation of lithium tetrasulﬁde.
Equations (8), (12) and (15) make it possible to calculate the
number of solvent molecules required for the solvation of lithium
tetrasulﬁde, as well as the molar ratio of sulfur and solvent ensuring
complete reduction of sulfur. Studies that we performed previously
[45] have shown that the solvate number of lithium ion in lithium
tetrasulﬁde in sulfolane solutions is 2. In this case, the solvate
number of lithium tetrasulﬁde is 4, while the molar ratio of the
solvent to elementary sulfur is 1:1.
To estimate the possible depth of electrochemical reduction of
sulfur in the high voltage plateau, we performed calculations on the
effect of average solvate number of lithium ion and support salt
concentration on the number of free solvent molecules that can
solvate the lithium ions in lithium tetrasulﬁde formed (Table 1,
Equations S.1 & S.2 are given in Supplementary materials). It fol-
lows from the calculations that electrochemical reduction of sulfur
to lithium tetrasulﬁde in electrolytes with the lithium perchlorate
concentrations studied and with the electrolyte content of 6.7 ml
per 1 mg of S (4 ml per 1 mA h(S)) is only possible if the average
solvate number of lithium ions is close to 2. In other words, in the
case of sulfolane, the minimum required value of solvate number is
2. The calculation results are in good agreement with the experi-
mental data. In fact, as follows from Fig. 1, electrochemical reduc-
tion of sulfur to lithium tetrasulﬁde occurs even in electrolytes with
high lithium perchlorate concentrations. However, the reduction
depth is limited by the number of free solvent molecules. For
example, at a salt concentration of 2.0M, the amount of free mol-
ecules of sulfolane in the electrolyte is 5.7M. This amount of free
sulfolane molecules is sufﬁcient to fully solvate lithium ions in the
system if the solvate number of the lithium ion is 2. Sulfolane, due
to the presence of two equivalent oxygen atoms, can act as a
bidentate ligand, and one molecule of sulfolane can saturate two
coordination sites in the solvation shell of lithium ion.
Table 1 summarized the value of calculated discharge capacity
that can be reached at high voltage stage. These results show is
there enough amount of free molecules of solvent (in this case
sulfolane) in the electrolyte to solvate the generated lithium tet-
rasulﬁde. If the free solvent molecules are not sufﬁcient to
completely reduce the sulfur to lithium tetrasulﬁde, then on the
discharge curves of lithium-sulfur cells, the capacity value obtained
at the high voltage plateau, is less than the theoretical value of
418 mAh/g(S).
A comparison of the calculated and experimentally obtained
discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells shows that the formation
of lithium tetrasulﬁde is possible in all studied electrolyte
solutions:
 if the average solvate number of lithium ion is 2, the formation
of lithium tetrasulﬁde is possible in all the electrolyte systems
studied;
 if the average solvate number of lithium ion is 3, the formation
of lithium tetrasulﬁde is possible in electrolytes containing from
0.1M to 1.0M lithium perchlorate
 if the average solvate number of lithium ion is 4, the formation
of lithium tetrasulﬁde is possible in electrolytes containing from
0.1M to 0.5M lithium perchlorate.

The dependence of the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells on the
concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane is bell-shaped
with a maximum at a salt concentration of about 1M (Fig. 2). The
isotherm of the speciﬁc ionic conductivity of lithium perchlorate
solutions in sulfolane has a similar shape (Fig. 6). The match of the
positions of maxima on the concentration dependence of the ca-
pacity of lithium-sulfur cells and on the isotherm of lithium
perchlorate solution conductivity indicates that the processes of
ion transfer in the electrolyte solution and the depth of electro-
chemical reduction of sulfur are controlled by the same factors - the
mobility and concentration of lithium ions.
On the one hand, the increase in the support salt concentration
increases the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte system, and on
the other hand, it decreases the mobility of lithium polysulﬁdes due
to the increase in viscosity (Fig. 6).
We believe that the depth of the electrochemical reduction of
lithium polysulﬁdes is determined to a greater extent by the rate of
charge transfer than by the transport properties of the medium.
The rate of charge transfer is controlled by the species of lithium
polysulﬁdes in electrolyte solutions, which depend on the con-
centration of the support salt. Probably, that is why there is a cor-
relation between the shape of isotherms of the conductivity and the
depth of the electrochemical reduction of sulfur.
The rate of electrochemical reduction of high ordered lithium
polysulﬁdes is greater than the for low ordered lithium polysulﬁdes
indicated on the reactivity control of the electrochemical reduction
of lithium polysulﬁdes. At the same time, the mobility of high or-
dered lithium polysulﬁdes is less than the mobility of low ordered
lithium polysulﬁdes.
The rate of electrochemical reduction of lithium polysulﬁdes
with different order can be judged from the value of the internal
resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at low and high depths of
discharge, which is mainly determined by the resistance of charge
transfer. A low value of the internal resistance of lithium-sulfur
cells at low discharge depths and a greater resistance at high
discharge depths (Fig. 7a) indicate greater rate of reduction of sulfur
and high ordered lithium polysulﬁdes compared to the reduction
rate of low ordered lithium polysulﬁdes.
Of course, the mechanisms of electrochemical transformations
of sulfur and lithium polysulﬁdes in lithium-sulfur cells, especially
with a limited electrolyte content, requires detailed study. The
assumption made by us is only a hypothesis and needs additional
experimental conﬁrmation.
The reactivity of various species of sulfur and lithium poly-
sulﬁdes decreases in the series: S > LiSn > Li2Sn > LiSn > Sn2. The
reactivity of sulfur and lithium polysulﬁdes is determined by the
electron density on the polysulﬁde chain. In the case of elemental
sulfur, all sulfur atoms are neutral, so elemental sulfur has the
greatest reactivity. In the radical species of sulfur LiSn, the negative
charge on the polysulﬁde chain is compensated by the positive
charge of the lithium cation. Due to the peculiarities of the elec-
tronic structure, the radical species of lithium polysulﬁdes have a
greater reactivity than neutral ones, such as Li2Sn. Ionic species of
sulfur have a lower reactivity than neutral ones due to the presence
of a negative charge on the polysulﬁde chain. The higher the den-
sity of the negative charge, the lower the reactivity.
The decrease in the discharge voltage at the high voltage stage
with the increase in the support salt concentration (Fig. 3) is
apparently due to the suppression of the homolytic dissociation of
high-ordered lithium polysulﬁdes. This results in the decrease in
the concentration of neutral and radical species of lithium poly-
sulﬁdes that whose reactivity is higher than that of molecular
species.
The increase in discharge voltage (Fig. 3) and the increase in the
depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur at the low voltage
stage (Fig. 2), which observed in the ascending region of the
Qdisch. ¼ f(Csalt) dependence, indicate that the increase in lithium
perchlorate concentration in the electrolyte solution results in the
increase in the reactivity of sulfur into low-ordered lithium poly-
sulﬁdes (n  4). The increase in Li2Sn reactivity is apparently due to
an equilibrium shift of electrolytic dissociation of lithium poly-
sulﬁdes (Equations (1) and (2)) towards the formation of molecular
species of lithium polysulﬁdes that possess higher reactivity than
ionic species.
The subsequent decrease in the sulfur reduction depth in the
descending region of the Qdisch. ¼ f(Csalt) dependence can be
explained by a considerable decline of the transport properties of
the electrolyte system due to the increase in its viscosity (Fig. 6). It
is further decreased at high concentrations of lithium perchlorate
(above 2М) by the shortage of free solvent molecules required for
the solvation of lithium polysulﬁdes.
The solvation energy of the lithium ion in electrolyte systems
with different concentrations of lithium perchlorate practically
does not change, therefore discharge voltage is determined to a
greater degree by the kinetics of the electrode processes than by
the thermodynamics of solvation of the lithium ion. Similar studies
were performed in electrolyte systems based on lithium tri-
ﬂuoromethanesulfonate (CF3SO3Li) and the nature of the anion of
the support salt practically does not affect the general laws of
electrode processes.
The discharge voltage on the lithium-sulfur cell is determined
by the difference in electromotive force and the sums of over-

the electrolyte (IR component) (see Equation (21)).

4.5. Effect of the support salt concentration on the resistance of
lithium-sulfur cells
The effect of the concentration of support salts on the reactivity
of various species of sulfur and lithium polysulﬁdes can be deter-
mined from the dependence of the DC resistance of lithium-sulfur
cells vs. their depth of discharge. The internal resistance of lithium-
sulfur cells is composed of three constituents: the electrolyte
resistance plus the charge transfer resistance on the positive and
negative electrodes (Equation (22)):
Rcell ¼ Rel þ RLi þ RS
(22)
where: Rcell is the DC resistance of a lithium-sulfur cell, Ohm,cm2;
Rel is resistance of the electrolyte solution, Ohm,cm2; RLi is the
charge transfer resistance on the lithium electrode, Ohm,cm2; and
RS is the charge transfer resistance on the sulfur electrode,
Ohm,cm2.
The dependence of the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells on the
depth of discharge is a curve with a complex shape where three
regions corresponding to different discharge depths can be distin-
guished (Fig. 7a). It is convenient to use the results of impedance
studies [49] to analyze the contributions of various components to
the total resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at various discharge
depths. As impedance studies have shown, at small discharge
depths of 5e15% (1st region) the main contributions to the resis-
tance of lithium-sulfur cells are made by the charge transfer
resistance on the positive electrode in the electrochemical reduc-
tion of high-ordered lithium polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn; n > 4) and by the
resistance of passivating layers on the electrodes. As high-ordered
lithium polysulﬁdes are reduced, the total concentration of
lithium polysulﬁdes in the electrolyte solutions increases, which
considerably decreases the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte
system. Therefore, at depth of discharge of lithium-sulfur cells
about 25e35%, the increase in their resistance is observed, with the
main contribution coming from the resistance of the electrolyte
solution. This region corresponds to the complete reduction of
elementary sulfur to soluble lithium polysulﬁdes. Therefore, the
dependence of resistance on the depth of discharge passes through
a maximum caused by the low electrolyte conductivity due to its
saturation with lithium polysulﬁdes.
Upon further discharge of lithium-sulfur cells, electrochemical
reduction of lithium tetrasulﬁde and low-ordered lithium poly-
sulﬁdes occurs. Since low-ordered lithium polysulﬁdes possess
higher mobility and affect the viscosity of the electrolyte system
more weakly, the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte system in-
creases, hence the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells decreases.
Therefore, the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at depths of
discharge of 60e80% is determined more considerably by the
charge transfer resistance in the reduction of low-ordered lithium
polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn; n < 4) and the resistance of passivating layers
on the sulfur electrode than by the electrolyte resistance and the
charge transfer resistance on the lithium electrode.
Thus, the effect of the salt concentration on the reactivity of
lithium polysulﬁdes can be determined from the resistances in the
minimum in the 1st and 3rd regions of the dependence of resistance
on the depth of discharge of lithium-sulfur cells.

perchlorate in sulfolane on the internal resistance of lithium-sulfur
cells at different depths of discharge: 0.10 DoD (or 10%), 0.32 DoD
(or 32%), and 0.75 DoD (or 75%).
As one can see from Fig. 7b, the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells
at the high voltage stage (0.10 DoD) at moderate concentrations of
lithium perchlorate (up to 2M) nearly does not depend on the salt
concentration but decreases at the low voltage stage (0.75 DoD),

4.6. Effect of the support salt concentration on the cycling
performance of lithium-sulfur cells
One of the most complex problems that needs to be resolved for
creating commercially acceptable lithium-sulfur batteries is that
their capacity fast decreases during charge-discharge cycling.
The decrease in the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells during
cycling results from the simultaneous action of several factors, the
main of which are listed below:
 sulfur redistribution over the volume of lithium-sulfur cells;
 sulfur transfer from the back-side space of the positive electrode
to the front-side space;
 sulfur encapsulation (as lithium sulﬁde) in the pore space of the
carbon component;
 passivation of the carbon component surface of the positive
electrode;
 decomposition of electrolyte system components (solvents and
support salt anions).
The impact of these factors is determined by the concentration
of the support salt. As the support salt concentration increases, the
rate of capacity depletion decreases, while the capacity of lithium-
sulfur cells containing a high-concentrated solution increases
rather than decreases in the initial stage of cycling. One of the
reasons of the decrease in the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells during
cycling may lie in the passivation of the surface of carbon particles
by hardly soluble products of electrochemical reactions - sulfur
during the charge and lithium sulﬁde during the discharge. If this
hypothesis is correct, the decrease in the rate of capacity depletion
of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling with the increase in the sup-
port salt concentration can be explained by hindrance of sulfur and
lithium sulﬁde crystallization in high-concentrated electrolyte
solutions.
4.7. Effect of the support salt concentration on the coulomb
efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells
The fact that the active material of the positive electrode of
lithium-sulfur cells is located in electrolyte solutions results in a
very undesirable phenomenon, i.e., direct reactions of sulfur and
lithium polysulﬁdes, dissolved in the electrolyte, with the metallic
lithium electrode. That decreases the coulomb efﬁciency of elec-
trochemical processes in lithium-sulfur cells.
The rates of these reactions are determined by two factors: the
transport properties of the electrolyte system and the transport
properties of the surface electrolyte layer on the lithium electrode.
The composition and properties of the solid electrolyte on the
lithium electrode surface and the transport properties of electrolyte
systems are determined, to a considerable extent, by the concen-
tration of the support salt. As the support salt concentration in the
electrolyte increases, the viscosity of the electrolyte system in-
creases exponentially (Fig. 6). Dissolution of lithium polysulﬁdes in
the electrolyte increases the viscosity of the electrolyte system even
further.
Sulfur contained in molecular species of lithium polysulﬁdes can
be transferred only by diffusion, whereas sulfur within charged
species (polysulﬁde anions) can be transferred both by diffusion
and migration. Since molecular species of lithium polysulﬁdes are
larger in size than ionic ones, the rate of sulfur transfer within
lithium polysulﬁde molecules is lower than that within ionic par-
ticles. Therefore, as the support salt concentration increases, the
overall (diffusional and migrational) mobility of sulfur-containing
particles decrease due to the increases in the viscosity of the
electrolyte system.
Changes in the ionic composition of the electrolyte and in the
mobility of sulfur-containing particles will also affect the compo-
sition of the surface ﬁlm on the lithium electrode, and hence its
transport properties.
It follows from the experimental data obtained (Fig. 5) that the
coulomb efﬁciency quickly decreases at initial stages of lithium-
sulfur cell cycling (5e10 cycles) in electrolyte solutions with low
and moderate lithium perchlorate concentrations (0.5e1.5М) and
then almost does not change. In high-concentrated electrolyte so-
lutions (with LiClO4 concentrations above 1.5M) the coulomb efﬁ-
ciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells is higher than in less
concentrated solutions and remains nearly unchanged during
cycling.
High values of coulomb efﬁciency (more than 100%) in the high
concentrated electrolyte (2.8M LiClO4) are explained by the fact
that the experimentally determined discharge capacity of lithium-
sulfur cells is greater than the charge capacity of the cells. This is
probably due to the fact that in high-concentrated electrolytes,
because of their high viscosity, only a part of sulfur in the positive
electrode is partly electrochemically reduced at the ﬁrst cycles.
During cycling, the part of sulfur electrochemically reduced in-
creases, which leads to the increase in the discharge capacity of
lithium-sulfur cells and the decrease in coulomb efﬁciency.
The decrease in coulomb efﬁciency at initial stages of cycling in
electrolytes with low and moderate concentrations of lithium
perchlorate can be due either to improvement of the transport
properties of the surface ﬁlm on the lithium electrode or to a
growth of the lithium electrode surface due to dispersion of
metallic lithium. It is possible that both factors act simultaneously.
Identiﬁcation of the reasons of the decrease in the coulomb efﬁ-
ciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells requires additional experi-
mental studies.
The increase in the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-
sulfur cells with the increase in concentration of support salt is
most likely due to the slowness of interelectrode sulfur transfer due
to the increase in the viscosity of electrolyte solutions [50].
The decrease in the self-discharge (shuttle) current density of
lithium-sulfur cells with the increase in the viscosity of solutions of
lithium perchlorate in sulfolane (Fig. 5b) may indicate that the in-
crease in the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells
with the increase in the concentration of the support salt is mainly
due to the delay in interelectrode transfer of sulfur and lithium
polysulﬁdes.
5. Conclusions
The concentration of lithium salts in electrolyte solutions
(lithium perchlorate in sulfolane) has a considerable effect on the
properties and regularities of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells. As the
concentration of the support salt increases, the discharge capacity
of lithium-sulfur cells ﬁrst increases, then decreases. The increase
in the discharge capacity mainly occurs due to the increase in the
capacity of lithium-sulfur cells at the low voltage stage. The
maximum discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells is reached at

lithium perchlorate concentration in sulfolane equal to 1М. The
dependence of the discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells on the
support salt concentration well correlates with the ionic conduc-
tivity isotherm of LiClO4 solutions in sulfolane. The depth of elec-
trochemical reduction of sulfur is limited by the number of free
solvent molecules.
As the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane in-
creases, the reactivity of high-ordered lithium polysulﬁdes de-
creases, while that of low-ordered ones ﬁrst increases but starts to
decrease at LiClO4 concentrations above 1.5М.
The capacity of lithium-sulfur cells changes in a complex way
during cycling, depending on the support salt concentration. The
smallest change in the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells during
cycling is observed at 2.4M concentration of the support salt in the
electrolyte.
The increase in the concentration of the support salt results in
the increase in the coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur
cells. At concentrations of the support salt above 2.4М, the
coulomb efﬁciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells is close to 100%.
We believe that the concentration of support salts affects the
