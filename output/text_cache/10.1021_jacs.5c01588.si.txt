1. Experimental section 
1.1. Raw materials 
All the raw materials, including carbon nanotubes (CNTs), graphene (G), sulfur 
(S8), titanium nitride (TiN), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), 
lithium nitrate (LiNO3), lithium sulfide (Li2S), carbon paper (CP), N-methylpyrrolidone 
(NMP), sodium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (NaTFSI), sodium chloride (NaCl), 
deuterated chloroform (CDCl3), acetonitrile (ACN), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), 
formamide (FA), ethanediamine (en), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), 1,2-dimethoxyethane 
(DME), di-isopropyl sulfide (DIPS), di-isopropyl ether (DIPE), hexyl methyl ether 
(HME), deionized (DI) water, poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, HSV-300), 
polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard), polyethylene (PE) separator (Celgard), 
aluminum (Al) foil, copper (Cu) foil, and lithium (Li) foil were purchased from 
commercial sources and directly used without further purification.  
 
1.2. Solution and electrolyte preparation  
DIPS, DIPE, or HME and DOL/DME (v/v = 1:1) mixed solvents were blended in 
various volume ratios and adopted as the DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions, each containing 
1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI. To prepare the Li2S6-added DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions, Li2S and 
S8 were mixed in the DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions with a molar ratio of 8:5, and stirred 
at room temperature until all the solids were dissolved. The Li2S6 concentration was set 
to be 1.0 mol[S] L−1. Spectroscopic experiments including donor number measurements, 
NMR, and Raman tests were based on the DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions and Li2S6-added

DIPE/DIPS/HME contents no more than 50% can still dissolve 2.0 wt% LiNO3. 
 
1.3. Spectroscopic experiments  
For spectroscopic experiments of liquid samples, 23Na nuclear magnetic resonance 
(NMR) spectra were recorded on a JNM-ECZ400 NMR spectrometer with 256 
scanning times to measure the donor number. Concretely, the NMR tubes were coaxial 
types, where 10 mmol L−1 NaCl aqueous solutions as the internal reference were filled 
in the inner tube, and the targeted solutions to be tested were filled in the outer tube. 
7Li-NMR spectra were recorded on the same NMR spectrometer with 16 scanning times 
to detect the LiPS surrounding environments in solutions. In detail, the NMR tubes 
were coaxial types, where pure CDCl3 solvents as the deuterated reagents were filled 
in the inner tube to lock the field, and the Li2S6-added DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions were 
filled in the outer tube. For the Raman spectra tests, the DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions or 
Li2S6-added DIPS/DIPE/HME solutions were filled into a quartz glass cuvette, and the 
spectra were recorded with 532 nm He–Ne laser in a Horiba Jobin Yvon LabRAM 
HR800 Raman spectrometer.  
For spectroscopic experiments on solid samples, the X-ray diffraction (XRD) 
patterns were collected on a Bruker D8 Advanced diffractometer with Cu-Kα radiation 
at 40.0 kV and 120 mA as the X-ray source. Morphology characterization was

1.4. Electrochemical measurements 
1.4.1. Electrochemical tests on model systems 
For LiPS redox kinetics evaluation, Li2S6 symmetric cells were chosen as the 
model systems, where the CNT-coated Al foil current collectors were prepared as the 
electrodes for cell assembly. Specifically, CNTs and PVDF with a mass ratio of 10:1 
were added into NMP. The mixture was sonicated for 2.0 h and stirred for another 2.0 
h to obtain a homogeneous slurry. Then, the slurry was coated onto Al foils and dried 
at 60oC for 12.0 h. The areal mass loading of the CNTs was controlled to be 0.20 mg 
cm−2. The CNT-coated Al foil electrodes were finally obtained after being punched into 
disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm. To construct Li2S6 symmetric cells, two identical 
CNT-coated Al foil electrodes were assembled in a standard 2032-coin cell with a piece 
of PP separator (19.0 mm in diameter) and 40 μL Li2S6-added DIPS/DIPE/HME 
solutions. 
To probe the characteristic kinetic parameters in homogeneous reactions, 
techniques including electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and distribution of 
relaxation time (DRT) analysis were applied to the Li2S6 symmetric cells. Concretely, 
EIS was performed using the potentiostatic mode at the open-circuit voltage on a 
Solartron 1470E electrochemical workstation. A sinusoidal voltage with an amplitude 
of 10 mV and a frequency range from 0.1 Hz to 10 kHz was applied. A high and low

1.5. Li–S pouch cell assembly and evaluation 
The TiN electrocatalysts were directly introduced into the S/C cathodes by being 
mixed in the slurry together. Typically, 11.2 g sulfur powder, 4.20 g CNT, and 0.71 g 
TiN were mixed and ball-milled thoroughly. The mixture was then melted under 
vacuum at 155°C for 2.0 h to obtain a composite. The composite and PVDF binder with 
a mass ratio of 9:1 were dispersed in NMP to form a homogeneous slurry. The slurry 
was then double-side coated on Al foils and dried at 60oC for 24 h. The areal sulfur 
loading was around 7.5 mgS cm−2 on a single side. 4 × 7 cm2 electrodes were cut and 
directly used as the sulfur cathodes in pouch cells. The measured thickness and porosity 
of the cathode for pouch cells are around 140 μm and 60%, respectively. 
The anode of the Li–S pouch cells was double-sided Li metal sheets (75 μm 
thickness on a single side) rolled on Cu current collectors with a size of 4 × 7 cm2. For 
the assembly of Li–S pouch cells, the cathodes and anodes were stacked layer by layer 
with PP membrane (for 2 Ah-level pouch cells) or PE membrane (for 5 and 10 Ah-level 
pouch cells) as the separator. The electrodes were controlled in parallel connection by 
using an electrode lug terminal. The electrolyte for the Li–S pouch cells was 
DIPS/DOL/DME mixed solvents dissolved with 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 5.0 wt.% 
LiNO3. The pouch cells were tested on the LAND multichannel battery cycler between 
the voltage range of 1.70–2.55 V. The energy density was calculated based on the total

1.6. Theoretical simulations 
1.6.1. Density functional theory calculations 
Graphene was regarded as the reaction substrate in this work. Li2S4 was treated as 
the single molecule, soluble LiPS radicals were treated as radicals adsorbing on 
graphene substrate, and insoluble Li2S and Li2S2 were treated as crystals. The LiPS 
radicals are represented by LiSx· radicals (x = 1 or 2) and interact with the substrate. 
Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​jacsat/​article-supplement/​3736459/​pdf/​ja5c01588_si_001/​ by TSINGHUA UNIV user on 21 September 2026

<<PAGE 9>>
SI9 
The structure of Li2S2 was taken from a theoretical hypothesized structure3. All the 
simulations on the intrinsic kinetics model constructions were performed in the frame 
of density functional theory (DFT)4 methods using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation 
Package (VASP)5. The Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE)6 exchange–correlation 
functional within a generalized gradient approximation (GGA)7 was implemented. The 
plane-wave cut-off energy was set at 520 eV, and all structures were optimized to a 
force tolerance less than 2×10−2 eV Å−1 and the total energy convergence lower than 
10−5 eV. The k-point of adsorption and crystal configurations were set to be 8 × 8 × 1 
and 7 × 7 × 7, respectively, which was verified by our previous work8. DFT-D3 method 
was used to include the van der Waals force9. A vacuum layer of 20 Å was adopted in 
the z-axis to minimize the interaction between the adjacent layers. The explicit solvation 
model was considered to describe the impact of solvation. The solvation model allows 
DME and DIPS/DIPE/HME molecules (i.e., 2DME, 2DIPS/DIPE/HME, or 
DME+DIPS/DIPE/HME) to interact with the soluble Li2S4 molecule and LiSx· radicals 
on the substrate, respectively. The binding energy for LiPS radical was defined as the 
total energy of the adsorption configuration minus the sum of the energies of the 
substrate and the solvated radicals. 
The electrostatic potential calculations of the five solvating electrolytes (DOL, 
DME, DIPS, DIPE, and HME) were conducted by Gaussian (G09) program10 with 
Becke’s three-parameter hybrid method using the Lee–Yang–Parr correlation functional 
(B3LYP) at 6-311++G(d,p) level11. Frequency analysis was performed to ensure the 
ground state of optimized molecules. The Natural Bond Orbital (NBO) charge analysis

1.6.2. The preferential reaction pathway for DFT calculations 
Based on our previous work, the preferential pathway in this work is the Li2S4 
symmetrical decomposition by calculating the descriptors in stage diagrams13, 14. The 
detailed elementary hypothesized heterogeneous reactions from Li2S4 to Li2S are as 
follows: 
 
(sol)2-Li2S4 + (Li+ + e−) + * → (sol)2-LiS2∙* + Li2S2 
(Reaction 1) 
 
(sol)2-LiS2∙* + (Li+ + e−) → Li2S2 + * + 2sol 
(Reaction 2) 
 
Li2S2 + (Li+ + e−) + * + 2sol → (sol)2-LiS∙* + Li2S 
(Reaction 3) 
 
(sol)2-LiS∙* + (Li+ + e−) → Li2S + * + 2sol 
(Reaction 4) 
Therein, the * represents the graphene substrate, and (sol)2 represents two solvent 
molecules coordinating with LiPSs. Considering the reaction stoichiometric numbers, 
the six-electron-transfer process from Li2S4 to Li2S can be extended to the following 
six steps based on above four elementary reactions: 
 
(sol)2-Li2S4 + (Li+ + e−) + * → (sol)2-LiS2∙* + Li2S2 
(Step I) 
 
Li2S2 + (sol)2-LiS2∙* + (Li+ + e−) → 2Li2S2 + * + 2sol 
(Step II) 
 
2Li2S2 + (Li+ + e−) + * + 2sol → (sol)2-LiS∙* + Li2S + Li2S2 
(Step III) 
 
Li2S + Li2S2 + (sol)2-LiS∙* + (Li+ + e−) → 2Li2S+ Li2S2 + * + 2sol (Step IV) 
 
2Li2S + Li2S2 + (Li+ + e−) + * + 2sol → (sol)2-LiS∙* + 3Li2S 
(Step V) 
 
3Li2S + (sol)2-LiS∙* + (Li+ + e−) → 4Li2S + * + 2sol 
(Step VI) 
Notably, the core reactions of Step III and V are totally identical, and the elementary

totally identical, and the elementary reaction therein is the Reaction 4. Therefore, all of

1.6.3. Equilibrium potential calculations 
The equilibrium potential (Eeq) of Li2S4 conversion is associated with the ΔG of the 
overall reaction in the following equation: 
 
Li2S4 + 6Li+ + 6e− → 4Li2S 
(eq. 2) 
The reference electrode was set to be the Li metal electrode. The ΔG for above 
conversion was defined using: 
 
ΔG = 4G(Li2S) − G(Li2S4) − 6G(e−) − 6G(Li+) 
(eq. 3) 
The free energy of Li2S (G(Li2S)) was obtained from its crystal structure. G(Li2S4) 
was acquired from the molecular structure. The reaction (Li+ + e−) → Li is at 
equilibrium in the condition of 0 V vs. Li/Li+ electrode. The free energy of the ion–
electron pair (Li+ + e−) is equal to G(Li) at 0 V vs. Li/Li+, which is similar to the method 
of hydrogen electrode15. Therefore, the Gibbs energy can be expressed at any electrode 
potentials as follows:  
 
G(Li+) + G(e−) = G(Li) − eE 
(eq. 4) 
Therein, the G(Li) represents the Gibbs free energy of Li solid and E is the electrode 
potential vs. Li/Li+ electrode. Notably, the equilibrium potential Eeq equals to E when 
the value of ΔG equals zero, and the calculated value of Eeq is 2.19 V.

1.6.4. Calculations of Gibbs free energy changes in elementary reactions 
The Gibbs free energies of radicals on the active sites were defined as the Gibbs 
free energy changes of Reactions 1 and 3: 
 
ΔG(LiS2∙*) = G(LiS2∙*) + G(Li2S2) − G(*) − G(Li+) − G(e−) − G(Li2S4) (eq. 5) 
 
ΔG(LiS∙*) = G(LiS∙*) + G(Li2S) − G(*) − G(Li+) − G(e−) − G(Li2S2) (eq. 6) 
where G(LiS2∙*) and G(LiS∙*) are the free energies of adsorbed LiS2∙ and LiS∙ 
complexes. G(Li2S2) and G(Li2S) were considered in solid states. Following above 
rules, the Gibbs free energy changes for Reactions 1 to 4 can be expressed as follows: 
 
ΔG1 = ΔG(LiS2∙*) 
(eq. 7) 
 
ΔG2 = − ΔG(LiS2∙*) + 2G(Li2S2) − 2G(Li+) − 2G(e−) − G(Li2S4) 
(eq. 8) 
 
ΔG3 = ΔG(LiS∙*) 
(eq. 9) 
 
ΔG4 = − ΔG(LiS∙*) + 2G(Li2S) − 2G(Li+) − 2G(e−) − G(Li2S2) 
(eq. 10) 
To avoid the heavy calculation of vibrations, the Gibbs free energies of all species 
in the above equations are approximated with total energies due to the small 
contribution of zero-point energy and the entropy change in solid-state conditions16.  
 
1.6.5. Overpotential calculations. 
To give a quantitative comparison, the calculated overpotential (η) is introduced to 
evaluate the intrinsic reactivity of LiPSs solvated by 2DME, DME+HME, or 2HME. 
The η is determined by the potential-determining step, which is the least exothermic 
step. Taking these considerations into account, η can be expressed as

SI13 
 
η = Eeq − min(ΔEi) 
(eq. 11) 
where Eeq represents the equilibrium potential. The ΔEi (i= 1,2,3, or 4) is the potential 
of an elementary reaction under the condition of 0 V vs. Li/Li+, which is defined as: 
 
ΔEi = −ΔGi / e 
(eq. 12) 
where ΔGi is the Gibbs free energy change of an elementary reaction. Note that the ΔEi 
is a function of ΔG(LiS∙*) and ΔG(LiS2∙*). Therefore, the η can be directly reflected 
by ΔG(LiS∙*) and ΔG(LiS2∙*), which are greatly affected by the coordinating solvents. 
Notably, since we adopted the same calculation method and model in our previous 
work14, the DFT calculation results for the LiPSs with HME coordination are the same, 
including the calculated overpotentials, energy variation plots, and the volcano plots.
