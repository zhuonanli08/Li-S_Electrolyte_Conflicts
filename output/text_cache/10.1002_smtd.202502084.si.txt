Experimental Procedures
Electrolyte preparation: The study was conducted using 1.2 M solutions of lithium salts SL with TTE as a co-solvent, mixed at a volumetric ratio of 1:1.5, forming an SSE system, as described in our previous work [1]. To investigate the effect of salt concentration, five different LiFSI/LiTFSI salt ratios were selected, as follows: 1) 1.2 M LiTFSI, 2) 0.2 M LiFSI + 1 M LiTFSI, 3) 0.45 M LiFSI + 0.75 M LiTFSI, 4) 0.6 M LiFSI + 0.6 M LiTFSI, and 5) 1.2 M LiFSI (referred to in the text as LiTFSI, DSE20, DSE45, DSE60, and LiFSI, respectively). For comparison, a DME/DOL-based electrolyte was prepared by dissolving 1 M LiFSI and 5 wt.% LiNO₃ in a solvent mixture of DME/DOL (3:1, by mol), referred to as DD 3:1 LN.
Cathode preparation: Sulfur cathodes were prepared following the procedure from previous works [1,2]. The cathode composition comprises 64 wt.% sulfur, 26 wt.% carbon materials, and 10 wt.% polymeric binder. The binder was composed of 5 wt.% sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and 5 wt.% styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) dispersed in water as the solvent. The carbon fraction was divided into 16 wt.% Ketjen black (EC-600JD) and 10 wt.% of the in-house synthesised graphene-based activated carbon composite, ResFArGO, whose synthesis is described elsewhere [3]. Sulfur/carbon composites were prepared by melt diffusion at 155 °C for 12 hours under an argon atmosphere to ensure homogeneous sulfur infiltration. The final cathode loading was 4 mgs cm−2.
Physicochemical characterisations: The morphology of lithium deposits on copper substrates was characterised via SEM using a FEI Quanta250 microscope. Surface composition analysis of cathodes and anodes from full cells was performed by XPS using a Phoibos 150 spectrometer with a non-monochromatic Mg Kα source (hν = 1253.6 eV).
To evaluate the LiPS dissolution ability of the electrolytes, stoichiometric quantities of solid S8 and Li2S were mixed in each electrolyte to prepare 0.5 M Li2S6 saturated solutions. The resulting solutions were filtered through 0.45 μm PTFE syringe filters. The filtered samples were first evaluated visually using optical imaging to observe the colour evolution of the samples due to the LiPS solubility. In addition to visual inspection, UV-Vis spectroscopic measurements were performed to further assess the dissolution process. The saturated Li2S6-containing solutions were diluted 10 times and characterized using a UV–vis spectrophotometer (UV-2600, Shimadzu). Measurements were conducted in a sealed cuvette over a wavelength range of 200–800 nm, with each spectrum representing an average of 50 scans (0.1 s integration time).
Ionic conductivity measurements: Ionic conductivity of both pristine and Li2S6-saturated electrolytes was measured using a two-point platinum electrode immersed in 1 mL of electrolyte by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy.
Electrochemistry measurements: The electrochemical tests were performed in CR2032 coin-type cells assembled in an Ar-filled glovebox. Li°||Li° symmetric and Li°||Cu half cells were prepared to test the stability of the electrolyte against lithium using 14 mm diameter lithium disks, 16 mm diameter copper disks, and 16 mm diameter separator (Celgard 2500), filled with 60 μL of electrolyte. Symmetric cells were tested at increasing current densities with five plating/stripping cycles of 2 hours each at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 mA cm−2. The 4 mAh cm-2 Li depositions on Cu were performed by plating Li at a current density of 0.4 mA cm-2 for 10 h. The calculation of the deposition/dissolution of lithium vs. copper was performed following the Aurbach method with 10 cycles of plating/stripping. The test starts with a Li deposition at 0.4 mA cm-2 for 10 h, then follows stripping-plating cycles of 0.4 mA cm-2 for 3 h each. Finally, a stripping till 1 V to assure the complete removal of the Li plated [4].
In-situ optical microscopy characterizations were performed by using a designed model cell [5]. In detail, two Li° electrodes (effective area: 9 mm2) fixed on titanium current collectors are placed in an H-shaped slot milled on a poly(ether-ether-ketone) sample holder, spaced 1 mm apart. Two titanium screws are in contact with titanium current collectors to conduct the model cells with battery cyclers (Land CT2001A, Wuhan LAND Electronics, China). The model cells were assembled in a glove box and transferred to an optical microscopy system (Bjscistar, China). The areal capacity for the single plating/stripping process of Li°|LHCE|Li° cells is set to 1  mAh cm−2.
For the Li-S cell performance, 13 mm diameter sulfur cathodes with high sulfur loadings (4 mgs cm−2), were used. A fixed electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio (E/S) of 7 μL mg−1 was maintained. Long-term cycling was performed at C/10 after 5 preconditioning cycles at C/20, with a voltage range of 3.0–1.2 V for SL-based electrolytes and 2.6–1.7 V for DD 3:1 LN electrolyte.
