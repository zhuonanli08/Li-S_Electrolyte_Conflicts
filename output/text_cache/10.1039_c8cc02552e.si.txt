Experimental section:
Preparation and characterization of S@pPAN materials. The S@pPAN materials were 
prepared as the reported method.1 Sublimed sulfur powder (99.99%, Adamas-beta) and PAN 
(Mw=150,000, Sigma-Aldrich) were mixed intensively in a mass ration of 8:1 and heat treated at 
300 °C for 9 h under argon atmosphere to get S@pPAN samples with sulfur content of 46%. The 
sulfur content was determined by elemental analysis (Perkin Elmer PE 2400 II). 
Preparation and characterization of the electrolytes. Lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF6) and 
lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide Li[N(SO2F)2] (LiFSI) were purchased in battery grade from 
BASF Battery Materials and were used as received. Ethylene carbonate (EC), dimethyl carbonate 
(DMC), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), and tetraethylene glycol dimethyl 
ether (TEGDME) were purchased from Shenzhen CAPCHEM Technology Co. Ltd. Dibutyl ether 
(DBE) (99.3%, anhydrous) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Inc. and stored with 4 Å 
molecular sieves for 72 hours in an argon-filled glove box before use. The electrolytes of 1 M 
LiPF6/EC-DMC (1:1, v/v), 1 M LiFSI/DME, 1 M LiFSI/DOL, 1 M LiFSI/DOL-DME (1:1, v/v), 1 
M, 2 M, 3 M and 4 M LiFSI/DBE were prepared by dissolving predetermined amounts of lithium 
salts into solvents in an argon filled glove box. The ionic conductivity of electrolyte was measured 
by a conductivity meter (DDS-301A, Shanghai INESA Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd., China) at 
25 °C. The viscosity of electrolyte was measured on programmable rheometer (DV-III Ultra, 
Brookfield, USA) at 25 °C.
Electrochemical characterization. The electrochemical window of electrolytes were examined 
by cyclic voltammetry (CV) inside the glove box using electrochemical work station (CHI650C, 
China) with a Pt disc electrode as working electrode and lithium sheet as both counter electrode 
and reference electrode. The Pt disc electrode was polished with Al2O3 powder and rinsed with 
anhydrous ethanol before use. Coulombic efficiency of lithium deposition/stripping was tested by 
Li | Cu cells. Typically, a constant amount of Li (1 mA h cm-2) was firstly deposited on Cu 
substrate and then stripped until the cut-off voltage of 1.0 V vs. Li/Li+ at the same current density 
(0.5 mA cm-2). Sulfur cathodes were prepared as follows: S@pPAN, Super-P, and NaCMC with a 
weight ratio of 80 : 10 : 10 were mixed to homogeneous slurry in water. The resulting slurry was 
coated on aluminum foil and then dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C overnight. The typical sulfur 
mass loading was about 1.2 mg cm-2. Electrochemical experiments were carried out using CR2016 
coin cells, which were assembled in an argon-filled glove box (H2O, O2 < 0.1 ppm, Mbraun) with

Celgard 2400 (Celgard, USA) separator and pure Li foil (Alfa Aesar, 99.9%, 200 um in thickness) 
anode. Controlled amount of electrolytes (15 μl mg-1sulfur) were added in the coin cells by pipette 
to insure the consistency of testing condition. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were 
obtained on a PARSTAT 4000 electrochemical workstation in the frequency range from 100 kHz 
to 0.1 Hz with an exciting potential amplitude of 5 mV. Galvanostatic charge/discharge was 
conducted using a Land CT2001A multi-channel battery testing system in the fixed voltage range 
of 1.0-3.0 V (vs. Li/Li+) at room temperature. The current rate was calculated based on the 
theoretical capacity of S (1.672 A g-1) and the specific discharge/charge capacities were based on 
the sulfur weight. 
Electrode characterization. The morphologies of lithium metal electrodes were observed by a 
field emission SEM (Nova NanoSEM 450, FEI company, USA). Cells were disassembled in the 
glove box and the obtained electrodes were thoroughly washed three times with anhydrous DME 
to remove any electrolyte salt residuals. To avoid exposure to air, the dried samples were sealed in 
an air-isolating container and transferred quickly into the SEM equipment under the protection of 
Ar flow. X-ray photo electron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed using an X-ray photoelectron 
spectrometer (AXIS ULTRA DLD, Kratos Analytical Ltd., UK) with a monochromatic Al Ka 
source (1486.6 eV).
DFT Calculations. The density functional theory (DFT) with the B3LYP exchange-correlation 
potential2 was employed by Gaussian 09 package.3 The basis set 6-311g(d) was used for C, H, O, 
N, S, and Li elements. Visualization of the LUMO and HOMO was done with Gaussian View in 
0.03 electron/bohr^3 Isosurface.
