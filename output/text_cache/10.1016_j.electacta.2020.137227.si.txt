Full chromatograms showing S2- and S22- species
As explained in the main text, the developed HPLC method enabled detection of methylated polysulfide species as well as elemental sulfur. In Fig. 1 and Fig. 2, the chromatograms are shown in the range of 8 to 14 minutes, including polysulfide species from Me2S3 to Me2S8 and elemental sulfur. The retention times of Me2S2 and Me2S were 6.0 min and 2.1 min, respectively. The two peaks were not ideal for further evaluation. 

Fig. S1: a) Galvanostatic potential during the first cycle of a battery cell built with a “conventional” electrolyte and the corresponding chromatograms (measured at 230 nm) of polysulfide and sulfur species obtained from b) cathode and c) separator samples. The potential curve in a) is an example from a battery cell used to obtain the sample for chromatographic determination of polysulfide and sulphur species after one discharge and charge. Chromatograms for each SOC were obtained from a separate battery cell. The vertical scaling of the chromatograms is intended to show the SOC of the sample they represent. There are some minor deviations in this graphical representation – e.g. the third sample was a battery cell stopped just as the potential reached the low voltage plateau and so the cathode chromatogram consequently shows a small peak for the Me2S species.

Separator colour comparison


Fig. S3: An example of separator colour after cell disassembly for the “conventional” and “fluorinated” electrolyte: top row shows separators extracted from a cell, which was discharged at 0.1C to 1.5 V vs. Li/Li+. The bottom row shows separators extracted from a cell, which was discharged to 1.5 V vs. Li/Li+ and charged to 3.0 V vs. Li/Li+ at a current of 0.1 C. Note that the separator of the “fluorinated” electrolyte charged cell has a slight yellow tint, which likely corresponds to dissolved sulfur.


Quantification of S22-, S32- and sulfur.
For sulfur, S32- and S22- species, preparation of standard solutions (in the case of polysulfides these were methylated polysulfides) enabled us to quantify their concentration and determine the detection limit for the prepared analysis method.
