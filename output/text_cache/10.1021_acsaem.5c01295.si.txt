EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS
Electrolyte preparation
Lithium bis(trifluorosulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, Solvionic, 99.9%), lithium nitrate (LiNO3, Alfa 
Aesar, 99.95%), and lithium difluorophosphate (LiPO2F2, ChemFish, 99.9%) were used as 
received. Battery grade solvents, including dimethoxy ethane (DME), and dioxolane (DOL), 
supplied by Gotion Inc. were dried with 4 Å molecular sieves before use. Different electrolytes 
were prepared in an Ar-filled glovebox box with <1 ppm H2O and O2 and stirred overnight to 
ensure a homogenous solution. Their compositions and abbreviations are listed in Table S1.
Table S1. Electrolyte composition used in this study.

Dual additive
1 M LiTFSI, 0.3 M LiPO2F2, 0.2 M LiNO3 in DME/DOL (1:1 in 
vol.)
Electrolyte characterization
The ionic conductivity of the prepared electrolytes was measured using a BioLogic MCS 10 
system over a temperature range of 20-60 oC. 
The viscosity of the electrolytes was measured using a high-throughput viscometer (VROC® 
Initium One Plus, RheoSense) over the same temperature range of 20-60°C. A 100 µL sample of 
each electrolyte was automatically measured within the viscometer.

The reduction stability of these electrolytes was evaluated in a coin cell with carbon paper as the 
working electrode and Li metal as the counter electrode. The cyclic voltammetry was performed 
on these cells over a voltage range from open-circuit voltage (OCV) to 0.5 V for three cycles at a 
scan rate of 0.1 mV s-1 using Biologic VSP Potentiostat.
Coin-cell assembly and electrochemical characterization
Sulfur-based cathodes with a high sulfur loading ( 4 mg cm−2) were prepared following our 
previous work.1 The coin-cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glovebox, using Li chip (250 µm) 
as anode, sulfur-based cathode, and electrolyte dropped on the polyethylene separator (20 µm). 
The amount of electrolyte for each cell used lean electrolyte condition (E/S = 4 mL gS−1). 
The cell performance was galvanostatically measured at 0.05 C for the initial cycle and 0.1 C 
(1C = 1000 mA g-1) for subsequent cycles in a voltage range of 1.8 – 2.8 V at 30 oC, using a Landt 
tester. The charge-discharge capacity of Li-S batteries was derived from the weight of sulfur 
content (64 wt.%).
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was used to investigate the impedance evolution 
of symmetrical Li Li cells, operating at a current density of 0.5 mA cm⁻² and an area capacity of 
1.0 mAh cm⁻². EIS measurements were carried out using a Biologic VSP instrument, over a 
frequency range from 1 MHz to 10 mHz, with a perturbation amplitude of 10 mV.
Postmortem analysis 
Cycled Li|S coin cells were disassembled in an argon-filled glovebox at the fully discharged 
state after 10 cycles to retrieve the lithium anode and sulfur cathode for SEI/CEI characterization.
