Preparation of Co-NC
Dissolve 0.03 mmol Co (NO3)2•6H2O and 1 g of peptone in 2 mL of deionized water, then place it in the refrigerator to freeze for 12 h until it forms ice cubes. Freeze dry for 12 h, and mix the dried sample with 10 g NaCl of solvent. Ball mill at 550 rpm for 3 h. After ball milling, heat the mixture in a tube furnace under N2 atmosphere at a heating rate of 5 ℃ min-1 to 900 °C, and perform high-temperature cracking for 1 h. Cool to room temperature, wash the carbonized product, and dry it for later use.
Preparation of S@Co-NC
Co-NC and sublimated sulfur were mixed and ground for 30 min in a mass ratio of 2:3. Then, argon gas was filled into the glove box, and then the product S@Co-NC was obtained by reaction in a 155 ℃ oven for 12 h.
Physical characterization
Using the German Carl Zeiss Ultra Plus scanning electron microscope (SEM), images were taken from different areas. The British VG Escalab 210 X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to analyze the chemical composition of the sulfur cathode surface after battery cycling. The UV-5800 PC UV-visible spectrophotometer (UV-vis) was used, with a wavelength range of 220-600 nm.
Electrochemical performance test
Weigh S@Co-NC, conductive carbon black Super P, and binder PVDF (7:2:1) by mass ratio, dissolve them in NMP, mix thoroughly, and coat the aluminum foil current collector. Dry for 12 h in a 60 ℃ oven to obtain the sulfur cathode. Lithium metal serves as the anode, Celgard 2400 as the separator, and the reference cell uses 1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME = 1:1Vol% with 1.0% LiNO3 as the electrolyte (the experimental cell adds different concentrations of HTN to the above electrolyte). Assemble the button cell using a CR2032-type stainless steel battery case in a glove box filled with Ar gas. Unless otherwise specified, the conventional sulfur loading in the electrodes is approximately 1.2-1.4 mg cm-2. The electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio is about 40 μL mg-1. For Li||Li symmetric cells, replace the positive electrode sheet with a lithium sheet for assembly.
Constant current charge-discharge tests were conducted on the Xinwei battery system with a voltage range of 1.7-2.8 V. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were performed on the CHI 650D electrochemical analyzer. The EIS test frequency range was from 100 kHz to 10 mHz. All electrochemical tests were carried out at room temperature. The scan rate and voltage range for CV measurements were 0.1 mV s-1 and 1.7-2.8 V, respectively.
