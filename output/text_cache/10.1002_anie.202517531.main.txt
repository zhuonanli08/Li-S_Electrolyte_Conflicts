Anion-Induced Bridged Electrolyte Design Strategy Mitigates
Capacity Decay in Lithium–Sulfurized Polyacrylonitrile Batteries
Shuang Wu+, Xinyi Liu+, Mao Guo, Jinze Hou, Zhimeng Hao, Kai Zhang, Haixia Li,
Zhenhua Yan, Yong Lu,* and Jun Chen*
Abstract: Previous strategies have improved the compat-
ibility of sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) in ether-
based electrolytes by constructing a relatively stable
interphase, but the electrode degradation caused by
sluggish kinetics has limited the practical implementation
of SPAN cathodes. Here, we propose an anion-induced
bridged electrolyte (AIBE) design strategy to simultane-
ously achieve anode/cathode compatibility, anion-derived
interphase formation, and fast Li+ transfer kinetics in
Li||SPAN batteries through utilizing small-sized nitrate
(NO3−) anions with multidentate coordination propen-
sity. One NO3−could coordinate with multiple Li+
ions to promote the formation of bridged complexes
([Li+–NO3−–Li+]) with reduced Li+–Li+ distances. These
densely packed ion pairs create an anion-dominated
solvation structure and fast Li+ interfacial reaction
kinetics. Simultaneously, the optimized AIBE facilitates
the formation of a robust inorganic-rich interphase to
suppress electrode degradation. This electrolyte mitigates
SPAN cathode decay, enabling a Li||SPAN full cell to
retain 80.5% capacity after 500 cycles. An Ah-level pouch
cell (50 µm Li, 4.0 mA h cm−2 SPAN, lean 1.52 g Ah−1
electrolyte) achieves 99.7% capacity retention after 95
cycles. This work elucidates the fundamental role of
NO3−anions in modifying the solvation structure and
interphase composition/structure, offering novel perspec-
tives for advanced electrolytes for Li||SPAN batteries.
Introduction

of portable electronic devices and electric vehicles because
of their high energy density and long-term cyclability.[1–4]

The transition metals in cathode materials for the existing
lithium-ion batteries face some challenges, such as the limited
resources and rising prices, which have forced researchers to
explore new materials for next-generation batteries.[5–8] The
utilization of sulfur as a low-cost, green, and renewable cath-
ode material is widely recognized to address the aforemen-
tioned problems.[9] However, the conventional C/S composite
cathodes generally exhibit unsatisfactory electrochemical per-
formance due to intractable problems, including lithium poly-
sulﬁde (LiPS) cross-contamination issues,[10] active species
aggregation or electrode passivation,[11] and inferior cycle
life under practical conditions.[12,13] Consequently, sulfurized
polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) with the advantages of high con-
ductivity and no self-discharge is considered as a promising
direction to ameliorate the abovementioned obstacles.[3,14,15]
Although the SPAN represents a promising alternative to
conventional transition-metal-based and C/S composite cath-
odes, the combination of Li metal anode and SPAN cathode
still faces rigorous challenges due to the LiPS shuttle effect
and consequent anode corrosion, which substantially impedes
their practical development and commercialization.
Electrolyte optimization strategies, including carbonate-
based polar electrolytes (CBPEs), weakly solvating elec-
trolytes (WSEs), high-concentration electrolytes (HCEs),
and localized high-concentration electrolytes (LHCEs), have
attracted signiﬁcant research interest due to their propensity
for forming relatively stable electrode/electrolyte interphase
(EEI). Unfortunately, CBPEs have insufﬁcient Coulombic
efﬁciency (CE < 98%) toward Li metal owing to the
participation of carbonate solvents in the solvation structure,
resulting in a continuous capacity decay of full cells.[16] WSEs,
HCEs, and LHCEs have proven effective in enhancing the
interaction of Li+-anions, thus improving the Li CE (>99%)
through constructing anion-derived interphase. Yet, the low-
polarity solvents used in WSEs show weak ability for lithium
salt dissociation, resulting in limited Li+ transport in bulk
electrolytes.[17,18] On the other hand, HCEs or LHCEs based
on polar solvents still face the bottleneck of sluggish Li+
interfacial reaction kinetics.[19,20] These electrolytes inevitably
suffer from inferior Li+ transport issues and subsequent
electrode degradation, making it difﬁcult to ensure that
the lifespan of Li||SPAN full cells exceeds 300 cycles. In
summary, the current designed electrolytes are difﬁcult to
simultaneously meet the formation of anode/cathode compat-
ibility, anion-derived interphase, and fast Li+ transfer kinetics,
presenting a great challenge for next-generation long-life
Li||SPAN batteries.

Herein, we propose a unique anion-induced bridged elec-
trolyte (AIBE) to tackle the previously mentioned challenges
and provide a deep understanding of the solvation structure
evolution induced by the participation of nitrate (NO3−)
anions. Notably, NO3−possesses a planar triangular structure
with multiple binding sites to Li+, and the relatively negative
minimum electrostatic potential (ESP) enables the formation
of bridged complexes ([Li+–NO3-–Li+]) with short Li+–Li+
distances (<5.5 Å). The optimized AIBE induced by the
bridged mechanism achieves an anion-dominated solvation
structure and fast Li+ transport kinetics, which is beneﬁcial for
constructing robust EEI and alleviating electrode degradation
after long cycling. As a result, the cell with AIBE demon-
strates exceptional electrochemical performance, achieving
a high Li plating/stripping CE of 99.14% in the Li||Cu half
cell. Notably, a record-long lifespan of 500 cycles in Li||SPAN
full cell while retaining 80.5% of its capacity. Furthermore,
the engineered electrolyte formulation exhibits remarkable
cycling stability, achieving negligible capacity decay (∼0.3%)
in an Ah-level Li||SPAN pouch cell over 95 discharge/charge
cycles. This work provides new electrolyte design guidelines
for realizing long-life Li||SPAN batteries.
Results and Discussion
Design Principles of AIBEs
Conventional CBPEs possess solvent-dominated solvation
structures and are compatible with SPAN cathodes by forming
polycarbonate-rich cathode–electrolyte interphase (CEI), but
induce severe parasitic reactions at the Li anode surface,
leading to accelerated capacity fade and structural degrada-
tion (Figure 1a). In contrast, ether-based electrolytes have
improved Li compatibility but exhibit fundamental limitations
in Li||SPAN batteries, such as excessive solubility of LiPSs,
LiPSs shuttle-induced degradation of the solid electrolyte
interphase (SEI), and subsequent anode corrosion.[21–24]
Although WSEs, HCEs, and LHCEs with anion-dominated
solvation structures could promote the anion-derived F-rich
CEI to relieve the above obstacles, the sluggish Li+ interfacial
reaction kinetics hinder the further application of these
electrolytes in practical Li||SPAN batteries (Figure 1b,c). It is
known that anions display a strong impact on the electrolyte
structure and further affect the evolution of EEI. The fun-
damental prerequisite for optimizing Li+-anion coordination
ratio through anion engineering is that one anion should
have the ability to coordinate with multiple Li+. Meanwhile,
anions should have relatively small space dimensions, which
may facilitate the formation of compact ion pair aggregates
and ensure the fast transport of cations in electrolytes. NO3−
possesses a planar triangular structure with multiple binding
sites to Li+, and the relatively negative ESPmin enables the for-
mation of [Li+–NO3-–Li+] complexes in electrolytes. Paired
with lithium bis(ﬂuorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) and ether sol-
vents to form AIBEs, possessing excellent Li compatibility
and ensuring fast ion transport kinetics (Figure 1d).
It is worth noting that the solubility of lithium nitrate
(LiNO3) is closely related to the ESP distribution and donor
number (DN) of the solvent.[25,26] ESPmin is generally associ-
ated with the afﬁnity of Li+-solvent, whereas ESPmax pertains
to molecular polarity. Firstly, the ESP distribution function
of organic molecules was used to screen solvents (Figures 1e
and S1, and Table S1). The |ESPmax|/|ESPmin| ratios of these
solvents are all below 1, indicating their ability to dissociate
lithium salts. Furthermore, some solvents selected in this work
exhibit |ESPmax|/|ESPmin| ratios in the range of 0.4–1 and show
high reactivity with lithium metal. This contrasts sharply with
ethers, which have consistently low ratios (below 0.4) and
demonstrate good lithium anode compatibility. It is generally
believed that only solvents with a higher DN than 22.2 kcal
mol−1 (NO3−) have greater solubility for LiNO3, such as TEP
or DMSO, but these solvents have poor Li compatibility.
Therefore, a low |ESPmax|/|ESPmin| but a moderate DN set
the primary criteria for solvent selection (Figures 1f, S2, and
Table S2). Applying the criteria to several solvents, a series of
ethers, 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), tetrahydrofuran (THF),
and 1,2-diethoxyethane (DEE) were identiﬁed as prime
candidates to design AIBEs. The dissolution of LiNO3 was
directly observed through optical photographs (Figure S3).
The highest concentration of LiNO3 can reach 0.5 M under
the solubilizing effect of 2 M LiFSI.[27]
Analyzing the Bridged Mechanism
The multidentate coordination propensity of NO3−anions
is revealed by the ESP distribution, which is obtained by
using density functional theory (DFT) calculations.[28,29] The
NO3−anion has the most negative ESPmin compared to
FSI−anion and DEE molecule, implying that it is more
likely to bind more cations (Figures 2a and S4).[30] The
multiple binding sites enable the NO3−anion to act as
intermediates connecting multiple Li+ ions, promoting the
formation of Li+-anion structures. The solvation structures
of 2.5 M LiFSI (denoted as 2.5F) and 2 M LiFSI-0.5 M
LiNO3 (denoted as 2F-0.5N) DEE-based electrolytes were
ﬁrst elucidated through Raman spectra and nuclear mag-
netic resonance (NMR) analysis. The solvation feature of
FSI−anions undergoes signiﬁcant modiﬁcation upon the
introduction of NO3−anions, manifesting as a reduction in
solvent separated ion pair (SSIP) populations, accompanied
by an increased proportion of contact ion pair (CIP) and
aggregate (AGG) conﬁgurations (Figures 2b and S5).[31] This
structural evolution is corroborated by NMR spectroscopy,
which reveals a distinct downﬁeld shift in the
7Li signal
(Figure S6). These experimental results directly reﬂect the
enhanced interaction between Li+ ions and anions resulting
from the introduction of NO3-.
Detailedly, the solvation structures at the nanometer scale
were examined by molecular dynamics (MD) simulations
(Figure S7). The MD parameters for various molecules
and anions were generated by Sobtop.[35] As illustrated in
Figure 2c, the coordination number (CN) of Li+-O(FSI−)
and Li+-O(DEE) exhibits a slight decrease. This reduction
may arise from the competitive coordination of Li+ with
NO3−, which dominates the solvation environment due to
its stronger interaction with Li+. However, the observed

average and maximum cluster size, suggesting the formation

To validate the results of the simulations, we con-
ducted synchrotron-based X-ray scattering experiments. The
microscopic structures in the 2.5F and 2F-0.5N DEE-based
electrolytes were investigated by wide-angle X-ray scattering
(WAXS) with a beam energy of 10.75 keV. The peaks
appearing at 0.32 and 1.37 Å−1 indicate the structural
properties of pure solvent (Figure S9). Upon lithium salt
dissolution, a distinct scattering feature emerged within
Q = 0.5–1.2 Å−1, indicative of solvation cluster formation
(Figure 2e). Impressively, the 2F-0.5N system exhibited this
peak at a relatively reduced scattering vector (Q = 0.88 Å−1),
corresponding to a characteristic correlation distance of
r = 0.71 nm as determined by the Ehrenfest equation
(Q·r = 2π).[37] This difference in Q-position relative to the
2.5F electrolyte (Q = 0.94 Å−1, r = 0.67 nm) demonstrates
that NO3−introduction effectively enlarges solvation cluster
dimensions, thereby corroborating the results of theoretical
calculation.
Combining experimental and theoretical results can obtain
the following bridged mechanism. When NO3−coordinates
a single Li+ through monodentate binding to form [NO3-–
Li+–FSI−–Li+] complexes, it introduces steric repulsion
that marginally increases Li+–Li+ separation in [Li+–FSI−–
Li+] complexes (Figure 2f,g). However, the multidentate
coordination capability of NO3−anions dominates in 2F-
0.5N DEE electrolyte, favoring bridging conﬁgurations like
[Li+–NO3-–Li+] with reduced Li+–Li+ distances (<5.5 Å)
(Figure 2h). Importantly, the DEE-based electrolyte induced
by the NO3−bridged mechanism sacriﬁces less intrinsic ion
transport kinetics (Figure S10), forming a more compact
ion pair aggregate while increasing the cluster size, which is
beneﬁcial for suppressing LiPS dissolution and constructing
a robust interphase. As shown in Figure S11, the results of
LiPS solubility tests and in situ UV–vis spectroscopy indicated
that adding LiNO3 effectively suppressed LiPS dissolution.

degradation of the cathode (Figure S12a) and shuttles to the
lithium metal anode, where they engage in parasitic reactions,
leading to a severe deterioration of the anode’s surface
morphology (Figure S12b). In comparison, the Li anode and
SPAN cathode cycled in 2.5F-0.5N DEE electrolyte maintain
relatively complete and uniform structure, suggesting the

Physicochemical Investigation of the Li Metal and SPAN
Cathode

critical in facilitating the stable cycling of Li metal batteries.

cm−2, the morphology of plated Li in different electrolytes
was characterized by using a focused ion beam-scanning
electron microscopy (FIB-SEM). The deposited layer in

in AIBE-DEE is thinner and more homogeneous than the

Following a comprehensive analysis of electrolyte proper-
ties and anode stability in AIBEs, we assessed the compat-
ibility of these electrolytes with SPAN cathode (S content:
36.3 wt.%, Table S3) using Li foil (450 µm) paired with SPAN
cathode (1.3 mg cm−2) to assemble half cells. Compared
with 2.5F-based electrolytes, these AIBEs with [Li+–NO3-–
Li+] complexes exhibit signiﬁcantly improved SPAN cathode
stability (Figure 5a). These coin cells were cycled at 1 C rate,
with capacity retention (1000 cycles) ranking as AIBE-DEE
(93.9%) > AIBE-THF (85.2%) > AIBE-DME (82.7%),
which suggests the successful inhibition of the LiPSs dis-
solution. The evolution of the interfacial impedance plays

Conclusion
In summary, we proposed an AIBE design strategy to address
the long-standing challenge of sluggish Li+ transport kinetics
and electrode degradation in conventional electrolytes for
SPAN cathode. Central to this strategy is the identiﬁcation
of solvents with a low |ESPmax|/|ESPmin| (<0.4) and moderate
electron donation ability, which ensures the dissociation of
LiNO3 and achieves relatively high Li compatibility. The supe-
riority of AIBE-DEE involves high intrinsic ionic conductiv-
ity and fast charge transfer kinetics. Meanwhile, the multiple
binding sites of NO3−anion promote the formation of densely
packed ion pairs, facilitating the formation of anion-derived
inorganic-rich interphases. As a proof of concept, a rationally
designed AIBE-DEE achieves a high Li plating/stripping
CE up to 99.14% along with outstanding cycling stability in
50 µm Li||4.0 mA h cm−2 SPAN full cell over 500 cycles
with 80.5% capacity retention. Furthermore, the AIBE-DEE
demonstrates exceptional cycling stability in an Ah-level
pouch cell, maintaining almost no capacity decay over 95
cycles. This work not only advances the development of ether-
based electrolytes for Li||SPAN batteries but also provides a
unique design concept for further electrolyte engineering.
