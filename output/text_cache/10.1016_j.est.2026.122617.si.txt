Experimental
Preparation of dual-reaction interlayer. The dual-reaction interlayer was prepared by a ball-milling method and coated on a Celgard 2400 membrane (a polypropylene (PP) separator) using a casting process. First, Ketjen Black (KB), 2, 5-dimercapto-1, 3, 4-thiadiazole (DMTD), and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder were mixed at a mass ratio of 100 mg: 5 mg: 37.5 mg, and ball milled for 1 h. Second, 25 mg of single-walled carbon nanotube (SWCNT) solution and an appropriate amount of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) were added dropwise into the ball milled mixture and stirred thoroughly to form a homogeneous slurry. The resulting precursor slurry was uniformly coated onto one side of PP separator using a doctor blade with a thickness of 20 μm. Finally, the coated separator was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h, denoted as DRI@PP. The mass loading of dual-reaction interlayer was kept at 0.52 mg cm⁻².
Preparation of KB/SWCNT interlayer. The KB/SWCNT interlayer was prepared with the same method of dual-reaction interlayer. First, KB and PVDF binder were mixed at a mass ratio of 100 mg: 37.5 mg, and ball milled for 1 h. Second, 25 mg SWCNT solution and an appropriate amount of NMP were added dropwise into the ball milled mixture and stirred thoroughly to form a homogeneous slurry. The resulting precursor slurry was uniformly coated onto one side of PP separator using a doctor blade with a thickness of 20 μm. Finally, the coated separator was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h, denoted as KB/SWCNT@PP. The mass loading of KB/SWCNT was kept the same as dual-reaction interlayer.
Preparation of p-DMTD interlayer. The p-DMTD interlayer was coated on PP separator by a casting process. First, DMTD nanoparticles and PVDF binder were mixed at a mass ratio of 100 mg: 37.5 mg, and ball milled for 1 h. Second, an appropriate amount of NMP were added dropwise into the ball milled mixture and stirred thoroughly to form a homogeneous slurry. The resulting precursor slurry was uniformly coated onto one side of PP separator using a doctor blade with a thickness of 20 μm. Finally, the coated separator was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h, denoted as p-DMTD@PP. The mass loading of p-DMTD was kept the same as dual-reaction interlayer.
Preparation of sulfur cathode. Super P (SP) and sulfur powder were mixed at a mass ratio of 3:7 in a Teflon container and heated at 155 °C for 12 h to obtain the SP/S composite. Subsequently, the SP/S, additional SP, and PVDF binder were mixed at a weight ratio of 7:2:1 and dispersed in NMP to form a homogeneous slurry. The resulting slurry was uniformly coated onto carbon-coated aluminum foil and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h. The areal sulfur loading was maintained at 1.5-2.0 mg cm-2, except for high sulfur loading/lean electrolyte tests. 
Interaction experiments of DMTD and Li2S6 electrolyte. First, 0.8 g sulfur powder and 0.23 g lithium sulfide (99.5%, Alfa Aesar) were mixed and dissolved in 10 mL 1, 2-dimethoxyethane (DME)/dioxolane (DOL) (volume ratio of 1:1). The mixture was stirred at 60 °C in a glove box to obtain the 0.5 M Li2S6 electrolyte. Second, the 0.5 M Li2S6 solution was diluted to 0.01 M and injected into two glass tubes. One glass tube was added into 20 mg DMTD. The other glass tube was set as a control group with the same volume of Li2S6 electrolyte only. Finally, two glass tubes were placed in a glove box for 4 h and digital photos were taken before tests and after 4 h. 
Materials Characterization. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Verios G4 UC, 10 kV) was used to characterize the morphologies and structures of samples. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images were taken by FEI Tecnai F20 microscope at 200 kV. In-situ X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were performed using a XRD Brucker AXS D2 Phaser diffractometer equipped with a Cu Kα radiation source (λ = 0.154056 nm). The diffraction data were collected over a 2θ range of 20° to 40° with a step size of 0.02° min-1. Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was conducted using a Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS5 spectrometer under an argon atmosphere to characterize the surface functional groups of the materials. Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis was performed using an Agilent Infinity II QTOF G6545. The system utilizes an electrospray ionization source to ionize compound molecules, and the mass-to-charge ratios (m/z) of the resulting ions are analyzed based on their time of flight within the mass detector. Prior to analysis, the samples were immersed in high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) grade acetonitrile inside a glovebox to avoid exposure to air and moisture. The prepared solutions were sealed and then transferred to the instrument for testing. Chromatographic separation was conducted using a C18 column (300 × 8.8 mm) with a mobile phase composed of acetonitrile and water (90:10, v/v) at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min.
Preparation of Li-S coin cells. All electrochemical tests were conducted using CR2025 coin cells. The sulfur cathode was cut into a square plate with a side length of 10 mm. The anode consisted of a lithium metal disc (16 mm in diameter, 0.6 mm thick), and the separator was a Celgard 2400 polypropylene (PP) membrane with a diameter of 19 mm. The electrolyte comprised 1 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) and 0.2 M lithium nitrate (LiNO3) dissolved in a 1:1 (v/v) mixture of 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME). All cells were assembled in an argon-filled glovebox with sulfur cathode, Li foils, DRI@PP, KB/SWCNT@PP, p-DMTD@PP or PP separator, and the electrolyte mentioned above. The common electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) in most coin cells was controlled to be 15 μl mg−1. Assembled cells were rested under open-circuit conditions for 12 hours to ensure thorough wetting of the electrodes before electrochemical measurements.
Electrochemical Measurements. The electrochemical performances were measured using coin cells. Galvanostatic charge-discharge tests were measured on LAND CT3001K galvanostatic charge-discharge instrument. The voltage range for charge-discharge was 1.7-2.8 V. 1 C corresponds to 1675 mA g-1. All the experiments were done at room temperature, and all potentials are referenced to Li/Li+.
Theoretical Calculations. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were implemented in the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [1, 2]. The electron interactions were treated with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), supplemented by DFT-D2 semiempirical correction for dispersion interaction [3-5]. The energy cutoff for the plane wave basis expansion was set to 500 eV. Geometric optimization was carried out using a Γ-centered k-point mesh of 1×1×1 for Brillouin zone sampling. The energy and force convergence criteria were 10-5 eV and 0.01 eV/Å, respectively. The adsorption energies (Eads) of the Li2S6 were obtained by the following equation (eq 1): Eads = E(*Li2S6) – E* – E(Li2S6), where E(*Li2S6), E*, and E(Li2S6) are the energy of the substrate with the adsorbed Li2S6, the substrate and the single Li2S6 molecule, respectively. The charge density difference was calculated as (eq 2): Δρ = ρ(*Li2S6) – ρ* – ρ(Li2S6), where ρ(*Li2S6), ρ*, and ρ(Li2S6) are the charge density of the substrate with the adsorbed Li2S6, the substrate, and the single Li2S6 molecule, respectively.

Charge-discharge reactions of sulfur cathode in DRI@PP and PP:
To elucidate the detailed reaction mechanisms, the discharge and charge mechanisms of sulfur cathodes with DRI@PP and PP separator were investigated, using Li2S6 as the representative intermediate. These two systems exhibit distinct pathways as follows:
For the conventional discharge process with PP separator, the reduction begins with three S8 molecules reacting with eight Li+ ions and eight electrons to generate four Li2S6 molecules (3S8+8Li⁺+8e⁻→4Li2S6). Subsequently, one Li2S6 molecule undergoes further reduction with ten Li+ and ten electrons to form six Li2S molecules (Li2S6+10Li⁺+10e⁻→6Li2S).
In the case of DRI@PP, the initial reduction step remains unchanged (3S8+8Li⁺+8e⁻→4Li2S6), but the subsequent steps diverge significantly. Specifically, seven Li2S6 molecules react with forty-eight [C2N2S3]²⁻ units, involving a multi-electron transfer process, to form the polymerized intermediate C16N16S31Li2 (7 Li2S6+48[C2N2S3]²⁻−98e⁻→6C16N16S31Li2+2Li⁺). These C16N16S31Li2 intermediates are then further reduced with 14Li⁺ and 14e⁻ to yield the final discharge products―C16N16S24Li2 and seven Li2S molecules (C16N16S31Li2+14Li⁺+14e⁻→C16N16S24Li2+7Li2S).
During the charge process, with PP separator, Li2S undergoes direct electrochemical oxidation to regenerate elemental sulfur (8Li2S→16Li⁺+S8).
In contrast, with DRI@PP, the oxidation follows a polymer-mediated pathway. Six Li2S molecules react with one molecule of discharge product C16N16S24Li2, yielding two depolymerized products: C6N6S12Li2 and C10N10S18Li2, along with the release of ten Li⁺ and ten electrons (6Li2S+C16N16S24Li2→C6N6S12Li2+C10N10S18Li2+10Li⁺+10e⁻). This transformation effectively disrupts the stable Li2S framework and promotes its oxidation through a thermodynamically more favorable pathway, thereby enhancing the overall electrochemical kinetics.
