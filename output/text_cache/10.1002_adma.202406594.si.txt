Discussion S2. High-voltage and sloping first-discharge curve of SPAN – causes and implications.
Pure SPAN, which contains only covalently-fixed sulfur, generally has a sulfur content below 40% and a specific capacity below 550 mAh g-1. The first discharge of this pure SPAN usually shows a low-voltage and flat plateau due to the high overpotential for the first lithiation. After activated, the discharge curves become more sloped in the following cycles. This type of SPAN is widely reported in the literature, which may have led to the impression that the first discharge of SPAN always appears flat at a very low potential.
Recently, it has been recognized that to achieve higher SPAN capacity, the synthesis process can be tailored (by lowering the heating temperature and/or shortening the heating time) to retain certain amounts of weakly-bonded and/or elemental sulfur in the SPAN matrix. Due to its labile nature, this type of sulfur requires less activation and tends to be active at a relatively high potential upon discharging, which explains the high-voltage and sloping first-discharge curve of high-capacity SPAN (i.e., specific capacity around 700 mAh g-1), regardless of the electrolyte. Indeed, it has been observed that the first discharge voltage of SPAN increases with its sulfur content and specific capacity (Journal of the American Chemical Society 2023, 145(17), 9624-9633 – Figure S4; Journal of Power Sources 2019, 412, 670-676 – Figure S2). Other works also reported that the first discharge of high-capacity SPAN is sloped and generally starts around 1.9-2.0 V (ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 2024, 16(16), 20618-20625; Journal of The Electrochemical Society 2021, 168(11), 110504; ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 2020, 12(30), 33702-33709). The high-voltage and sloping first-discharge curve suggests the presence of non-covalent sulfur, which is needed to achieve high specific capacity. 
Compared to pure SPAN, stabilizing SPAN with embedded weakly-bonded/elemental sulfur is more challenging because of its high reactivity. Conventional electrolytes, such as carbonate-based ones, cannot form a good CEI to suppress side reactions between the weakly-bonded/elemental sulfur and the electrolyte, resulting in continuous capacity fade and inferior CE during subsequent cycles. (Angewandte Chemie International Edition 2019, 58(3), 791-795).
The fact that the high-voltage and sloping first-discharge curve was observed in our electrochemical data (Figure 5d) is consistent with our reported high specific capacity of SPAN (> 700 mAh g-1), highlighting the good compatibility of our electrolyte TD with high-capacity SPAN (i.e., even when weakly-bonded/elemental sulfur is present).
