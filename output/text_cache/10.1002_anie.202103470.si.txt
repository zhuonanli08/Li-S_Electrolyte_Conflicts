I. Supplementary Text for Experimental Section 
1. Raw materials. Li metal foils (500 and 50 μm) were purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. 
Diisopropyl ether (DIPE, 99%, Sigma-Aldrich), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, battery grade, 
DoDoChem), 1,3-dixolane (DOL, battery grade, DoDoChem), lithium bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) 
imide (LiTFSI, >99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich), and LiNO3 (99.99% metals basis, Aladdin) were used as 
received without further purification. 
2. The preparation of electrolyte. PSE, i.e., 1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME (1:1, by vol.), and EPSE, i.e., 
1.0 M LiTFSI in DIPE/DOL/DME (3:3.5:3.5, by vol.), were prepared in an argon-filled glove box. No 
LiNO3 and PSs are in PSE or EPSE if no specific explanation. 
3. S/C composite cathode. S/carbon nanotubes (S/CNT) composite were prepared by a facile ball-
milling process. Typically, 60.0 mg CNTs and 140.0 mg S were mixed. Then, the S/CNT composite 
was further ball-milled with polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in N-methyl 
pyrrolidone (NMR) solvent to form a homogeneous slurry. The as obtained slurry was directly coated 
on an aluminum current collector by a doctor blade, which was further dried at 60°C for 12 h. The 
S/CNT electrode was then available after punching into disks with diameters of 13.0 mm. The sulfur 
content in the whole electrode is 63% and the S loading was 1.2 and 4.0 mgS cm−2 for electrochemical 
measurements. All materials were kept and used in an argon-filled glove box with oxygen and water 
contents below 1.0 ppm. 
4. The immersion test of Li in different electrolytes with PSs. The mixture of S and Li2S with a 
molar ratio of 7:1 was added into PSE or EPSE electrolyte to prepare the electrolyte with Li2S8. In order 
to exclude the effect of SEI in blocking PSs, Li foil (500 μm) was firstly immersed into EPSE without 
PSs for 8 h to form SEI. Then, the Li foil with SEI was immersed into PSE with PSs for 24 h in order to

evaluate the ability of SEI in blocking PSs. The color change of the solution was recorded by a high-
resolution camera. 
Li foil was immersed in 5 mL EPSE and PSE electrolyte with 2.5 mM Li2S8 for 24 h, respectively, 
in order to assess the stability of PSs in bulk electrolyte against Li metal. The color change of the 
solution was recorded by a high-resolution camera. 
5. Electrochemical measurements. Li | Cu cells were assembled in standard 2025-coin cells with PSE 
or EPSE. 40 μL electrolyte was added. Li foil (500 μm-thickness, 16 mm in diameter), Cu foil (17 mm 
in diameter), and polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard 2400, 19 mm in diameter) were used. Cells 
were tested using a Neware battery cycler (CT-4008t, Shenzhen, China) with a capacity of 1.0 mAh 
cm−2 at a current density 1.0 mA cm−2. To observe Li deposition and stripping morphology, Li | Cu cells 
were disassembled after 5 cycles. 
Li–S batteries were assembled with mild and practical conditions. Under mild conditions, a low 
loading S cathode (1.2 mgS cm−2), a thick Li foil (500 μm), and much electrolytes (25 μL) were used to 
test cycling performance and shuttle current. Cells were tested using a Neware battery cycler (CT-4008t, 
Shenzhen, China) and monitored in galvanostatic mode within a voltage range of 1.7 to 2.6 V at 0.1 C. 
Li metal anodes were disassembled and observed after cycling. In addition, 2 wt.% LiNO3 was added in 
electrolytes to further protect Li metal anode. Cells were then tested at a voltage range of 1.7 to 2.6 V at 
0.5 C. Under practical conditions, a high loading S cathode (4.0 mgS cm−2), a thin Li foil (50 μm), and a 
low E/S ratio (7.5 μL mgS−1) are used. 2 wt.% LiNO3 was added in electrolytes. Li–S batteries were 
tested in a voltage range of 1.7 to 2.6 V at a current density of 0.1 C (1 C=1675 mA g−1). Cells were 
tested at 25°C in a thermostatic chamber. 
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was carried out with amplitude of 10 mV over the 
frequency range from 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz.

6. Pouch cells. A Li–S pouch cell (1.6 Ah, 7.0 × 4.0 cm2) was fabricated in a dry room at a dew point of 
−40°C with a high loading S cathode on Al foil (6.1 mgS cm−2 every single side), a thin Li foil on Cu 
foil (50 μm every single side), and a low E/S ratio (3.0 μL mgS−1). To assemble a pouch cell, cathode 
and anode electrodes were stacked layer-by-layer with PP separator. The ratio of DIPE in electrolyte 
can be adjusted according to the test conditions of pouch cell. The pouch cell was tested in a voltage 
range of 1.8 to 2.55 V at 0.025 C. Testing was performed at 25°C in a constant temperature oven. The 
above conditions meet the requirements of a pouch cell of 300 Wh kg−1 theoretically as reported.[1] The 
practical mass of the pouch cell is 10.82 g including all components of a pouch cell, and the practical 
specific energy of the pouch cell is 301.4 Wh kg−1. 
7. Shuttle current test. For the measurement of shuttle current, cells were firstly galvanostatically 
charged to 100% state of charge at 2.9 V, then discharged to different voltage stage 2.90, 2.60, 2.45, 
2.43, 2.40, 2.39, 2.38, 2.37, 2.36, 2.35, 2.34, 2.25, and 2.20 V, and switched to potentiostatic mode. The 
potential of the cathode will drop steadily due to the continuous shuttle of PSs if there was no external 
current. Hence, the shuttle current was determined as the applied steady-state potentiostatic current to 
offset the potential drop. 
8. Materials Characterizations. To observe the surface of Li foils, Li | Cu cells were disassembled 
after 5 cycles, and then electrodes were washed by pure DME for three times to obliterate the residual 
electrolyte. The electrodes were dried thoroughly in the glove box. The electrodes were protected in an 
argon-filled home-made container to avoid contact with air during the transferring process before SEM 
and XPS tests. The morphologies of electrodes were characterized by a scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM, JSM 7401F, JEOL Ltd., Japan) operated at 3.0 kV. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) 
experiments were carried out on scanning X-ray microprobe (Quantera SXM, ULVAC-PHI. INC) 
operated at 250 kV, 55 eV with monochromated Al Kα radiation. Ar+ sputtering rate for the XPS depth-
profiling calibrated on SiO2 surface is ~20 nm min–1. The sputtering time is the time of Ar+ sputtering 
on SEI to investigate the components at the different depths in SEI.

9.3. Force fields. We employ the force filed combination suggested by Joachim Dzubiella et al.[3-4] in 
the present work. To be more specific, the OPLS-AA[5] force filed for DOL, DME, and DIPE; the 
CL&P[6] force filed for TFSI–; parameters from Dang et al.[7] for Li+; the DREIDING force filed with 
modified charges suggested by Joachim Dzubiella et al.[4] for PS chains, were followed. 
Geometric combination rules are deducted to get the Lennard-Jones (LJ) interactions for unlike 
atoms. To encounter the electronic polarizability, the Coulomb interactions of ions are treated by the 
electronic continuum correction (ECC)[8-11] method by rescaling the ionic charges. In this approach, the 
partial ionic charges, 
iq , are rescaled down by square root of high-frequency dielectric permittivity of 
bare solvent ()[3].  can be calculated from the refractive index (n) using

(n) for their mixtures are calculated based on the geometric mixing rule.[3] The rescaling factor, , is 
1.3907 and 1.3854 for PSEs and EPSEs systems, respectively in the present work.

9.4. Simulation details. All simulations are conducted by GROMACS (version 2019.1)[12] software 
package. The equations of motions are integrated by the Leap-Frog algorithm[13] with a time step of 2 fs. 
An energy minimization was conducted using the steepest descent algorithm until the maximum force is 
less than 1000 kJ mol–1 nm–1[14]. We firstly heated up the system to the temperature of 400K and cooled 
it down to 298.15K with the time interval of 3 ns. An equilibration run of 10 ns with NPT (i.e., a fixed 
number of molecules, a constant pressure, and a constant temperature) algorithm at a constant pressure 
of 1 bar and temperature of 298.15 K, followed by a production run of 10 ns with NVT (i.e., a fixed 
number of molecules, a constant volume, and a constant temperature) algorithm at a constant 
temperature of 298.15K is carried out in this study. System pressure is controlled by the Berendsen[15] 
barostats with a time constant of 1.0 ps for the equilibration processes. The system temperature is 
controlled by the Berendsen[15] thermostats with a relaxation time of 0.1 ps for the equilibration and 
production processes. The atomic and molecular trajectories in the production stage are saved every 500 
steps (1 ps) for data analysis. 
The particle-mesh Ewald (PME)[16] method with a Fourier spacing of 0.12 nm and a 1.0 nm real-
space cut-off are used to calculate the electrostatic interactions. The LINCS algorithm[17] is used to 
constrain the bond length and angle. All snapshots are presented by the visual molecular dynamics 
(VMD) package.[18]
