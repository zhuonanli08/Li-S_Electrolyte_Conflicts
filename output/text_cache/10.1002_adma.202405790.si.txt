Experimental section
Synthesis of Cu-2-PF6. Cu-2-PF6 was first prepared by anion replacement route. Binuclear copper-based precursor (Cu-2) was synthesized by using 1, 10-phenanthroline and copper (II) chloride dihydrate as the reactants, deionized water and ethanol with a volume rate of 1:10 as the solvent. The mixture was sealed in a Teflon-lined autoclave (Anhui CHEMN Co. Ltd.) and maintained at 120 ℃ for 24 h. Then the reaction was cooled down to room temperature and green Cu-2 crystals were collected after filtration and washing with a mixture of icy isopropyl alcohol and water. After the Cu-2 was dissolved in deionized water, KPF6 as the anion exchanger at 10 equivalents was added into the solution. The precipitated light blue Cu-2-PF6 powder was attained after the further washing and drying. 
Preparation of Cu-2-T. Cu-2-PF6 power was dissolved in 1 mL electrolyte containing 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME)/1,3-dioxolane (DOL) solvent, 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 to prepare the catalysts of Cu-2-T. The Cu-2-T contents were 0.10, 0.25 and 0.40 wt.% by changing the usage of Cu-2-PF6 power. 
Carbon coated separator. 180 mg Super P and 20 mg polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) were mixed with N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP) solvent to form a slurry, followed by coating on one side of PP separator. After drying at 60 °C for 12 h, the one-side carbon-coated PP separator was further punched into small disks with a diameter of 19 mm prior to use. 
Preparation of S/C cathode. The Super P and sulfur powers were mixed with a mass ratio of 1:3. Then the mixture was sealed into a glass bottle and heated at 155 ℃ for 6 h under Ar atmosphere. The S/C composite was thoroughly ground before the preparation of S/C cathode slurry. Then S/C composite, super P and LA133 binder were stirred at a mass ratio of 8:1:1 until the formation of the viscous slurry. The as-prepared slurry was coated on a piece of Al foil and then dried at 60 ℃ under vacuum condition before punched into disks with 13 mm diameter. 
Materials characterizations. Single crystal structures of samples were collected on a Bruker SMART APEX-II CCD detector system. The single crystals of Cu-2-T were obtained by virtue of solvent evaporation method. In details, 2 mL Cu-2-T with a concentration of 0.25 wt.% is kept in an Ar filled glove box at room temperature without sealing. Green Cu-2-T crystals were finally precipitated after 5 days. Fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR) of Cu-2-PF6 and Cu-2-T were collected on a Nicolet iS50 Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscope. Ultraviolet-visible (UV–vis) spectra of the Cu-2-T samples were detected on a Shimadzu UV1900 UV–vis spectrometer. Morphologies of samples were inspected by Zeiss sigma 500 scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Raman spectra were obtained on INVENIO-R spectrometer and Horiba Lab RAM HR Evolution with 532 nm laser to detect the solvation. Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) images were collected on a Alysum Research Cypher ES Atomic Force Microscope. Morphology evolutions of Li||Li symmetric configurations with different electrolytes during lithium deposition process were observed by the YUESCOPE YM710TR optical microscope in a home-made optical cell (Beijing scistar tech. Co. Ltd). Liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS) tests were performed on LC-MS  (8040, Shimadzu). 
Li2S nucleation and dissociation tests. 0.2 mol L–1 Li2S8 solution was first attained by mixing sulfur and Li2S at a molar ratio of 7:1 in tetraglyme solvent. Commercial carbon paper (CP) was punched into small disks with a diameter of 13 mm. Super P powder was dispersed into ethanol and then loaded on CP (0.2 mg per piece). For the control samples, cells were fabricated by employing CP with super P as the cathode, lithium foil as the anode, 20 μL Li2S8 (0.2 mol L–1) solution (blank sample) as the catholyte and 20 μL LiTFSI (1.0 mol L–1) solution without Li2S8 as the anolyte. As for the test sample, Cu-2-T in Li2S8 electrolyte as the catholyte with other procedures in coordance with the control samples. Then the cells were first galvanostatically discharged to 2.06 V at a constant current of 0.112 mA and then discharged potentionstatically at 2.05 V until the current was below 10–5 A. As for the Li2S dissociation tests, the cells were first galvanostatically discharged at the current of 0.112 mA until voltage to 1.7 V. Subsequetly, the cells were charged at the voltage of 2.35 V until the current to 10–5 A.
Shuttle current measurements. The cells were assembled by employing Celgard 2500 membrane as the separator, Li foil as the anode, a mixture of DME/DOL solution with 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 0.25 wt%. Cu-2-T as the electrolyte. The assembled cells were first galvanostatically charged to 2.8 V at a rate of 0.5 C and maitained for 1000 sencond before galvanostatically discharged to 2.38 V at a rate of 0.5 C. Chronoamperometric shuttle current measurements were then performed at 2.38 V with a running time of 10000 senconds. The electrolyte without Cu-2-T was used as a conrol for the shuttle current measurements. 
Assembly of symmetric cells. Symmetric cells were assembled by applying CP as the working and counter electrodes, and Cu-2-T in 20 μL Li2S6 with a concentration of 0.2 mol L–1 as the electrolyte. The control sample was made by using 20 μL Li2S6 as the electrolyte. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were conducted between –1 to 1 V at a scan rate of 10 mV s–1.
Li plating/stripping tests. Cu||Li cells were assembled by using Li foil as the anode, Cu foil as the cathode. Cu-2-T with a content of 0.25 wt.% acts as the electrolyte. As a control, a mixture of DME/DOL solution containing 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 as the blank electrolyte was also used (Canrd. Technoogy Co. Ltd). Cu||Li cells were discharged at a current density of 0.5 mA cm–2 and a capacity of 0.5 mA h cm–2, then striped at 0.5 mA cm–2. And the Li||Li cells were assembled with two pieces of Li foils as the working electrodes. Cu-2-T and blank electrolyte were also used for the tests of Li||Li cells.
Electrochemical evaluations. The sulfur content in the whole cathode was 60 wt.%, and mass loadings of 1.4–1.6, 6.5 mg cm–2 for routine and high-sulfur-load batteries, respectively. The coin-type batteries were assembled by employing carbon-coated separator, Li foil as the anode. Cu-2-T with different contents serve as the electrolytes. As a control, a mixture of DME/DOL solution containing 1.0 mol L–1 LiTFSI and 2.0 wt.% LiNO3 as the blank electrolyte was also utilized to detect the battery performance. The electrolyte/sulfur ratios were respectively 12.0 and 4.5 μL mg–1 for the routine and high-load batteries. Galvanostatic discharge/charge, rate and cycling performance measurements were carried out on a Neware Battery Measurement System in the voltage window of 2.8–1.7 V. The curves of cyclic voltammetry (CV), linear scanning voltammetry (LSV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were recorded on a Metrohm Autolab G204 electrochemical workstation.
Synchrotron radiation X-ray 3D nano-CT tests. Synchrotron radiation three-dimensional nano-computed tomography (X-ray 3D nano-CT) tests were carried out on the beamline BL07W at the National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory (NSRL), Hefei, China. The flux and spatial resolution were 2 × 1010 Phs s−1 and 30 nm, respectively. The powder samples originate from the disassembled cells after Li2S nucleation tests. Under pure argon atmosphere, the sample was loaded onto a piece of carbon-free formvar film (100 mesh), and dried at room temperature. The loaded formvar film was further placed into the vacuum chamber for imaging tests. Two-dimensional (2D) tomograms were collected at a title-angle range of −60o to 60o with an interval of 1o. The exposure time was 2 seconds. The as-achieved 2D tomograms were reconstructed into 3D visualized images by virtue of X Radia XMR reconstructor software. Further 3D visualization and segmentation of the reconstructed images were performed on an Avizo Fire VSG software (Visualization Sciences Group, Bordeaux). 
Small angle neutron scattering tests. Small angle neutron scattering (SANS) tests were performed on the SANS-Suanni spectrometer at China Mianyang Research Reactor (CMRR) to achieve the structure information of lithium anode samples. Samples were first collected from the worked Li||Li symmetric cells at 1 mA cm–2 for 50 cycles. After washing with DME solution and naturally drying in an Ar-filled glove box, the lithium anodes were sealed into two pieces of Al-plastic films for SANS analysis. The neutron wavelength used in this experiment was 0.53 nm. The sample-to-detector distances of 1.5 and 9.9 m were selected. BerSANS software was applied to convert the original data to one-dimension (1D) data.
Theoretical calculations. All of the calculations were based on the density functional theory (DFT) and performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) code.1,2 The electron exchange-correlation interaction was parameterized by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional.3,4 A kinetic cutoff energy of the plane-wave basis expansion was set to 500 eV, and electron spin polarization was considered in all of the calculations. A vacuum space of 20 Å was employed to avoid periodic interactions between the slab and its period images. For geometric relaxation, the convergence criteria for energy and force were less than 10-5 eV and 0.05 eV/Å, respectively. The van der Waals (vdW) correction with the Grimme approach (DFT-D3) was included in the interaction between single molecule/atoms and substrates.5 One graphene layer acting as the basic planar structure unit of carbon-based materials was used as a control for theoretical simulations. A supercell of graphene containing 6 × 6 unit cells that contain 72 Carbon atoms was used to construct the adsorption model. The Monkhorst-Pack k-point grids of 3 × 3 × 1 were used to sample 2D Brillouin zones for the graphene supercell, respectively. The energy barriers of Li2S decomposition and Li atom diffusion were calculated by using the climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method.6
