Tuning the solubility of polysulfide for constructing practical lithium-sulfur battery

Experimental section
Synthesis of porous carbon (PC)
The carbon was synthesized by template-base fabrication methods using silica nanoparticles. The preparation method refers to the previous work which was completed by Liu [1]. Typically, 5 g colloidal silica was added to 45 ml H2O2 solution (3%), and then 2 g pyrrole was added into the solution. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 10 min to form a homogeneous solution. After that, 4 ml FeCl3 solution (0.01 M) was added to solution drop by drop for initiating polymerization of pyrrole. After stirring at room temperature for 24 h, pyrrole gradually polymerized and formed polypyrrole colloids. The mixture was then dried at room temperature. The solid product was collected and annealed at 800 ℃ in a tube furnace for 5 h in an argon atmosphere with a heating rate of 5 ℃ min-1. The product then was etched in hot NaOH solution to remove the silica template. HCl was used to remove the iron impurities. The carbon product was collected after filtration, repeated washing with water and ethanol. After drying the product, the mesoporous carbon was prepared.
Synthesis of SeS7
The sulfur powder and selenium powder were mixed in a molar rate of 7:1, then transfer the mixture to the oven. The mixture was heated to 260 ℃ and held for 24 h before being cooled to room temperature naturally. The SeS7 was collected.
Synthesis of SeS7/PC [2,3]
For the typical synthesis of SeS7/PC composite with a loading of 66%, the as-synthesized PC and SeS7 were mixed at a weight ratio of 1:2 and then grind for 30 min. after that, the mixture was put into the oven at 260 ℃ for 12 h to get a homogeneous distributed of SeS7 in PC. The SeS7/PC was collected. The theoretical specific capacity of prepared SeS7 is 1410 mAh g-1.
Synthesis of the cathodes
A working electrode was prepared by mixing the SeS7/PC composites, Super P, carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and polymerized styrene butadiene rubber (SBR) which were working as binder in a weight ratio of 80:10:5:5 in water solvent. The slurry was pasted onto an Al foil and then dried in a vacuum oven at 70 ℃ for 12 h. The SeS7/PC cathode was cut into disks (Φ 8.0 mm) with an active content of 53.3%. 
Prepare of electrolytes
Electrolytes were prepared by dissolving 1.0 M LiTFSI in DME/DOL (1:1, v/v), which was denoted as DME/DOL. The electrolyte composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in DME/DOL/HFE (1:2:1, v/v/v) was denoted as DME/DOL/HFE. The electrolyte composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL /HFE (1:1, v/v) was denoted as DOL/HFE. All of the LiTFSI and solvents were purchased from DoDoChem (Suzhou). Li2S and S8 (1:5, by mol.) were added into the DME/DOL, DME/DOL/HFE and DOL/HFE electrolyte to prepare the Li2S6 electrolyte. All electrolytes were prepared in an argon-filled glove box with O2 and H2O contents below 0.1 ppm.
Material characterizations
The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) studies were conducted on JSM-7600F SEM instrument at an accelerating voltage of 10 kV. The low-magnification transmission electron microscopy（TEM）was carried out on a JEM2100 instrument at 200 kV. Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of different samples were obtained on Empyrean using a Cu-Kα source at 40 kV. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted on Thermo Scientific K-Alpha instrument. The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) test was conducted on a Pyris1 TGA (PerkinElmer Instruments) in a temperature range of 30-600 ℃, with a heating rate of 10 ℃ min-1. BET surface area and pore size distribution measurements were performed with nitrogen absorption/desorption at 77 K on 3H-2000PM1 instrument of BeiShiDe company. the degassing condition was 250 ℃ for 2 h. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) were conducted on AscendTM 600 MHZ (Bruker instruments). Raman measurements were carried out on a LabRAM HR800 of Horiba JobinYvon company with an exciting laser of 532 nm. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) were conducted on Nicolet iS50 of thermo scientific company. Ultraviolet-visible spectra (UV) test were conducted on UV-2550 of Shimadzu company.
Electrochemical measurements
2032 coin cells were assembled with above sulfur electrode as the cathode, a lithium metal as the anode, and Clegard 2325 as the separator. The electrolyte volume was 25 ul. The cells were assembled in an argon atmosphere glovebox (<0.1 ppm of O2 and H2O) and cycled at a rate of 0.2C on a Land battery cycler over a voltage of 1.7-2.8 V. Galvanostatic measurements were made using a LAND-CT2001A instrument at room temperature. Li||cycled-Li batteries were assembled in 35μl DME/DOL with a new Li metal and a Li metal which has cycled 10 times in three electrolytes. cyclic voltammetry measurements were performed on a CHI660e electrochemical workstation at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1 between 1 and 3 V.
Density functional theory (DFT) calculation
The DFT calculations were carried out in Gaussian (G16) [4] program with Becke’s three-parameter hybrid method using the Lee-Yang-Parr correlation functional (B3LYP). The def2svp was employed for geometry optimization and a larger basis set of def2tzvp was used for the calculation of single point energy. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation
MD simulation was taken by Gromacs software [7], and their ratio is as follows (Table S1). We used the OPLS-AA force field [8], which is suitable for electrolyte solution. The force field parameters were generated with LigParGen tool [9]. RESP2 (0.5) charges [10] was used and atomic charges of all ions were multiplied by scale factor 0.8 to correct the polarization effect of ions [11]. Simulation boxes were built by Packmol software [12] and then submitted to energy minimization using the steepest descent method. The equilibrium simulation was carried out with NPT ensemble at 298.15 K and 1bar for 10 ns; The production simulation was carried out with NVT ensemble at 298.15 K for 10 ns; The simulation boxes were visualized by VMD software [13].

Fig. S1. (a) XRD patterns of commercial Se, S and as-prepared SeS7. (b) Raman spectra for commercial Se, S and as-prepared SeS7.

XRD patterns of commercial Se, S and as-prepared SeS7 was reveal in Fig. S1a. the patterns of SeS7 is different from commercial Se and S, which illustrates that a new species has been synthesized successfully. Raman spectra of the as-prepared SeSx solid solutions are also shown in Fig. 1b. This figure also includes the spectra of commercial S and Se for comparison. To the commercial sulfur, the peaks centered at 151,215,474 cm-1 are assigned to species of S-S bonds. For commercial Se, the 238 cm-1 correspond to trigonal Se. the peaks near 350 cm-1 were assigned to Se-S stretching vibrations which was observed in the SeSx solid solutions. It is consistent with previous reports which verified the successful preparation of SeSx solid solution [14]. 

Fig. S2. Morphology of PC characterization by (a) TEM and (b) SEM
The XRD patterns of SeS7/PC is similar to PC, at the same time, the peaks of SeS7 was disappear in the SeS7/PC. It is revealed that the active material was completely deposited into the pores of mesoporous carbon. The TG curve illustrated the thermal behavior of active material and SeS7/PC composite was studied using curves obtained from TGA-DTA. The measurement was carried out from 30-600 ℃ at 10 ℃/min under N2 atmosphere. It is observed that the active material in the sample started to evaporate at 220 ℃ and completely evaporated around 550 ℃. A major weight loss of 65.4 wt% is observed at the temperature range of 220 ℃-550 ℃, which is attributed to the evaporation of SeS7. From this weight loss, the actual active material loading amount is confirmed in the prepared SeS7/PC composite.
