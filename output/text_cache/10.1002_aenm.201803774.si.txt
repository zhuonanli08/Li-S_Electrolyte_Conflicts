Experimental Section  
Electrolytes and Materials:  
The salts of lithium bis (ﬂuoro sulphonyl) imide (LiN(SO2F)2, LiFSI) and lithium bis 
(triﬂuoromethane sulphonyl) imide (LiN(SO2CF3)2, LiTFSI), the solvent of 1,2-dimethoxyethane 
(DME), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), and 1H,1H,5H-octafluoropentyl-1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl ether 
(OFE) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used as received without further purification. 
Porous Carbon and CMK-8 were provided by ACS Material. The electrolytes were prepared by 

<<PAGE 3>>
dissolving the lithium salt into the mix solvents with various certain volume ratios in Argon-
filled glovebox. Specifically, a certain volume ratio of 95: 5 for the solvents of OFE: DME were 
mixed together, then 1M LiFSI salt was added into the mix cosolvent to obtain the 1M 
LiFSI/OFE+DME5 (abbreviated as “DME5”) electrolyte (herein, the “M” is equal to the 
Molsalt/Litersolvents). Similarly, the 1M LiFSI/OFE+DME15 (abbreviated as “DME15”) and 1M 
LiFSI/OFE+DME50 (abbreviated as “DME50”) electrolytes can be obtained in the same way 
with solvent volume ratio of OFE: DME of 85: 15 and 50: 50, respectively. For another contrast 
electrolyte of 1M LiTFSI/DOL+DME (abbreviated as “DOL+DME”), it was composed of 1M 
LiTFSI salt in a mixed solvent of DOL/DME (v/v=1:1). The sulfur@carbon composite electrode 
(S@C) was prepared by a melting sulfur diffusion method. Detailly, a certain amount of 
elemental sulfur was uniformly grounded with the porous carbon (PC) and CMK-8 (mass ratio of 
PC: CMK-8 =1: 1), following sealed in a glass tube under vacuum and heated at 155 oC for 15h 
to obtain the S@C composite. 
Structural Characterization:  
Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded by Bruker D8 (Bruker AXS Inc., USA) 
using Cu KĮ line radiation source. Thermogravimetric Analysis (TG) was carried out under 
Argon gas with the heating rate of 5oC/min. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images were 
recorded on a Hitachi SU-70 field emission scanning electron microscope (S4800, HITACHI, 
Japan). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was achieved on a ESCALAB 250 X-ray 
monochromator with an Ar+ ion sputtering gun, which can well adjust different etching time for 
test samples. The viscosity of electrolytes was measured with an AR2000 stress-controlled 
rheometer (TA Instruments), running at room temperature on a cone-and-plate geometry (20 mm 
diameter, 2° cone angle). Specifically, a solvent trap was used to minimize evaporation of the 

<<PAGE 4>>
electrolytes while measured. The experiments were conducted under different steady shears, and 
the apparent viscosity was measured as a function of the shear rate. The experiment for lithium 
polysulﬁde dissolution was carried out as following: a mole ratio of Li2S and S (1:7) were 
uniformly mixed in an Argon-filled glovebox, then added into the DME solvent, and waited for 
two weeks to ensure the Li2S8 saturation state. The ignition and combustion experiment for 
electrolytes were recorded with high-speed digital camera (a Vision Research Phantom v 12.1 
digital camera); a small piece of glass fiber membrane was soaked with ~ 0.5 mL electrolyte and 
stabilized by alligator clip, followed by immediate ignition with a butane lighter.  
Electrochemical Measurements:  
The working electrode preparation: S@C composite, conductive carbon, polyvinylidene 
diﬂuoride (PVDF, binder) with a weight ratio of 8:1:1 were mixed in certain amount of N-
methylpyrrolidinone (NMP) to obtain homogeneous slurry after 4 hours stirring. The slurry was 
coated onto the thin aluminum foil and dried at 65 °C in vacuum overnight. The sulfur loading is 
about 1-2 mg/cm2 on each electrode discs. The coin cells (CR2032) were assembled in an argon-
ﬁlled glove box with the as-prepared cathode electrode, pure lithium metal foil as anode 
electrode, one sheet of polypropylene microporous ﬁlm (PP Celgard) as separator. The 
Electrochemical performance were tested with Arbin Battery test instrument (BT2000, Arbin 
Instruments, USA) at a voltage range of 1.0-3.0 V versus Li/Li+ at room temperature (23 ± 
0.5 °C). The galvanostatic intermittent titrations (GITT) were also performed by alternating the 
current density of 0.1 A/g for 20 min along with open circuit voltage periods of 2 hours after 15 
discharge/charge activation cycles at the same current density of 0.1 A/g. The CE of Li 
stripping/plating in various electrolytes was invested in Li|Cu coin cells (CR2032), employing Li 
foil as both the counter and reference electrode, Cu foil with a same diameter of 14 mm as the 

<<PAGE 5>>
substrate for Li metal stripping/deposition, and PP ﬁlm as separator. Different current densities 
for Li metal plating/stripping process was set under the same deposition capacity of 1.0 mAh cm-
2 for Li metal on Cu foil, with a stripping voltage up to 1.5 V versus Li/Li+. Simultaneously, the 
same Arbin stations were also used to conduct the electrolyte cycling stability test in Li|Li coin 
cell, with the similar experimental procedure of Li|Cu cells except the replacement of Li metal 
foil to Cu foil. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were performed on Gamry instrument 
with a scan rate of 0.05 mV/s. The ion conductivity of electrolyte was tested by a conductivity 
meter equipped with 2-electrode Pt probe. The Li+ transference number was measured by a 
combing alternating-current (AC) impedance and direct-current (DC) polarization using Li|Li 
coin cell.
