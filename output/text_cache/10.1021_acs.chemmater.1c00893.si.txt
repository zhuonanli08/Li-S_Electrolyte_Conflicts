Supporting Discussion
Comparing the FTIR results in Figure 2c to the vibrational frequencies acquired though 
DFT calculations in Table S1, there are a key differences that shed light on the in-situ reaction. 
In the calculated values shown above, there are strong signals occurring at 1,710 and 1,660 cm-1, 
respectively, for LiTFA and NH4TFA. These signals correspond to the asymmetric stretching 
vibrations of the C-O and C=O bonds in the trifluoroacetate structure. These theoretical results 
very closely match that seen in the experimental results in Figure 2c. The broad peak of 
NH4TFA in the experimental data is attributed to additional vibrational modes that occur in the 
NH4+ structure at 1,736 cm-1 in the DFT results. LiTFA should theoretically not actually show 
any other signals in this range; the other peak present in the experimental spectrum at ~ 1,690 
cm-1 may be due to a contaminant, likely water, as the salt is very hygroscopic. This also 
explains why the spectra in the “Li2S6 + NH4TFA” case predominantly displays the correct 
signal at 1,725 cm-1, and much less of the lower wavenumber contaminant signal that is present 
in the pure LiTFA spectra. The resultant LiTFA that forms in-situ is likely higher purity than that 
in the commercial LiTFA sample.
