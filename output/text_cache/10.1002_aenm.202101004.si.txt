1. Experimental Section 
1.1. Preparation of KB/S composites and KB/S cathode 
KB/S composite was prepared by mixing KB (KJ EC-600, US) with sulfur powder 
(99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich) at a mass ratio of 3:7. The mixture was then transferred to a 

<<PAGE 3>>
sealed steel reactor and heated at 150 °C for 9 h and then 300 °C for 2 h. The obtained 
KB/S composite has a sulfur content of 69 wt% according to thermogravimetric (TG) 
analysis. To prepare electrodes, slurry casting method was employed onto carbon 
coated aluminum foil. The slurry was prepared by mixing active material, acetylene 
black, and SA (sodium alginate) with a mass ratio of 8:1:1. The electrodes were cut 
into disks with a diameter of 10 mm after dried at 60 ℃ over 12 h under vacuum. The 
sulfur loading in cathode was 1-1.2 mg cm-2. High sulfur loading cathode with 3.1 and 
7.2 mg cm-2 were also prepared by same method.  
1.2. Electrochemical measurements 
The carbonate-based electrolytes used in this study were prepared by dissolving 
LiTFSI in a mixture of Diethyl carbonate (DEC) and

also prepared by dissolving 1 M Lithium Bis(trifluoromethane)sulfonimide (LiTFSI)

multichannel potentiostat 3/Z (VMP3). Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) test was

1.3. Physical characterization 
The micromorphology of the electrode was characterized by scanning electronic 
microscopy (FESEM, Hitachi S-4800) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, 
H-7650)). Raman spectra were collected by a Horiba Scientific LabRAM HR Raman 
spectrometer operated with an incident laser beam at 532.03 nm. The viscosity of the 
electrolyte was measured using a viscometer (SNB-1) at 25 ℃. The Fourier transform 
infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) of the electrode were collected by the Nicolet iS10. 
UV-vis absorption spectra of the solution were collected using a Perkin Elmer 
LAMBDA 1050 spectrophotometer. The contact angle measurements of different 
electrolyte on the separator were carried out by contact angle meter (JCY-2). TOF‐
SIMS tests were conducted using a TOF‐ SIMS IV machine (ION‐ TOF GmbH, 
Germany). The ionic conductivity of the electrolyte in temperature range of 30-80 ℃ 
was measured by the conductivity meter (DDSJ-319L). 7Li-NMR spectra were 
obtained on a Bruker Avance III-400 spectrometer. 7Li chemical shifts are referenced 
to 1 M LiCl (aq) at 0.0 ppm. For X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis, 
the electrode was disassembled in an Ar-filled glove box to avoid exposure to air or 
moisture and transferred to an XPS system. Ex-situ synchrotron-based X-ray 

<<PAGE 5>>
absorption near edge structure (XANES) spectra was carried out at the Canadian 
Light Source (CLS). Sulfur K-edge was collected on the Soft X-ray 
Microcharacterization Beamline (SXRMB) by total electron yield (TEY) mode. The 
electrode was disassembled and sealed in glovebox under Argon to avoid oxidation. 
Subsequently, the samples were transferred to beamline for test. Ionic conductivities 
of the electrolytes combined with separators were measured using electrochemical 
impedance spectroscopy (EIS) on a versatile multichannel potentiostation 3/Z 
(VMP3). Symmetric cells with two stainless steel (SS) electrodes and five Celgard 
2400 separators (PP, thickness: 25 μm) with 100 μL of electrolyte were assembled.

Figure S1. The optical image of 0.1 M LiTFSI in TTE solvent. 
The performance of Li-S battery using electrolyte with LiTFSI in pure TTE was also 
investigated. As shown in the Figure S1, the TTE cannot completely dissolve the 
LiTFSI at a very low concentration of 0.1 M. The lack of ion transport pathway in this 
electrolyte leads to stagnant electrochemical reaction. The S cathode therefore cannot 
work in the electrolyte with LiTFSI in pure TTE.

Figure S3. 7Li-NMR spectra of (a) LiTFSI and dilute solution with 1 M LiTFSI in 
single solvent, (b) electrolyte with different LiTFSI concentration and LHCE with 
HCE: TTE=1:1. 
As shown in the Figure S3a, the 7Li-NMR spectrum in DEC shows sharper peak and 
more positive chemical shift than that in FEC. In addition, multiple 7Li-NMR peaks 
indicate the existence of multiple Li+ solvate structure with DEC. In the Figure S3b, 
there are also multiple peaks in dilute electrolyte with 1 M LiTFSI, suggesting 
complex coordination environment of Li+. With the increasing concentration, 
significant negative chemical shift of 7Li-NMR spectra is observed. It reveals that the 
Li+ ions are more shielded upon addition of Li salt due to the stronger interactions 
between Li+ ions and TFSI- anions [1]. In the HCE, the limited solvent and anion may 
lead to the decreased multiple Li+ solvate structure. The addition of TTE causes a 
positive shift of the 7Li-NMR spectra, indicating a more deshielded effect of TTE 
which is in agreement with the earlier finding[2].

Figure S13. FT-IR spectra of the cycled electrode and other contrast sample. 
Figure S13 shows the FT-IR spectrum of the cathode after one cycle and other 
contrast sample obtained in the 700 to 2000 cm-1 wave number range. The peaks at 
864, 1269, 1420 cm-1 correspond to the Li-O, C-C, and COO- bonds[3], indicating that 
organic component consist of COO- and O-Li is formed on the electrode surface. In 

<<PAGE 13>>
addition, strong absorbance peak near ∼1600 cm-1 due to thiocarbonate arise[4]. The 
reaction product of the DEC and Li2S6 shows similar absorption peak, indicating the 
deposit on the electrode surface results from the reaction between lithium polysulfides 
and carbonate solvent. 
 
 
 
Figure S14. (a) Time-dependent EIS of Li-S cell during the first cycles, (b) the 
Nyquist plots of the cell after 10 cycles and its equivalent circuit. 
The time-dependent EIS test was carried out during the discharge/charge process as 
shown in Figure S14a. The Nyquist plots are consisted of one high-frequency 
semicircle and an inclined low-frequency tail, which represents the charge transfer 
resistance (Rct) and the semi-infinite Warburg impedance (W1), respectively[5]. After 
10 cycles, it exhibits two semicircles at high-frequency, and it is well fitted by the 
equivalent circuit shown in Figure S14b inset. The first semicircle represents the of 
CEI film (Rf)[6] with a value of 11 Ω, and the medium-frequency semicircle is related 
to the Rct with a value of 15 Ω. The time-dependent EIS test indicates that the CEI 
film forms and becomes stable after few cycles.

A localized high concentration carbonate-based electrolyte is developed for Li-S batteries 
based on conventional KB/S materials. The cathode demonstrates a solid-phase reaction 
route by forming a CEI film via nucleophilic reaction between LiPSs and carbonate 
solvents. Li-S cell exhibits stable cycling performance at 2 C by maintaining a discharge 
capacity of 570  mAh g-1 after 600 cycles.
