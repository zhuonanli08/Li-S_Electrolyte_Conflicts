ABSTRACT
We evaluated lithium–sulfur battery performance across realistic conditions and identified critical trade-offs between energy output and 
durability. The results outline a pathway toward more scalable battery technologies.
Lithium–sulfur (Li–S) batteries offer high theoretical energy density (1,672 mAh g⁻1) and low material cost, yet their practical deployment 
remains limited by poor scalability under lean-electrolyte and high sulfur-loading conditions. Many reported electrolyte strategies achieve high 
capacities in coin cells but rely on excess electrolyte and low areal loading, obscuring constraints critical for pouch-cell operation. Here, we 
present a data-driven benchmarking framework to evaluate Li–S performance across coin and pouch configurations by jointly analyzing sulfur 
loading, electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio, and delivered capacity. Using literature data from 2022 to 2025, we construct ternary performance 
landscapes and apply principal component analysis (PCA) to identify governing trade-offs. Two orthogonal axes emerge: a practicality-driven 
axis linking high sulfur loading, low E/S ratio, and high areal capacity, and a stability-driven axis associated with capacity retention and fade 
rate. Their weak coupling reveals a persistent gap between energy density optimization and durability. Importantly, this work establishes a 
statistically grounded, data-centric framework that transforms fragmented literature into actionable design space. A feasible operating win-
dow— ~ 7–10 mg cm⁻2 sulfur loading and ~ 1.7–2.8 µL mg⁻1 E/S—yields ~ 5.5–7.5 mAh cm⁻2 areal capacity with stable cycling, providing 
quantitative benchmarks for scalable Li–S systems.

Introduction
Lithium–sulfur (Li–S) batteries have emerged as one of the 
most promising next-generation energy storage systems due 
to their exceptionally high theoretical specific capacity (1,672 
mAh ­g−1) and the potential to surpass the energy density limits 
of conventional lithium-ion technologies.1–3 The low cost and 
earth abundance of sulfur further enhance the appeal of Li–S 
chemistry for applications ranging from electric vehicles to 
large-scale grid storage.4–6 In practical operation, however, 
realizing theoretical performance remains challenging due 
to intrinsic electrochemical phenomena. Key issues include 
the polysulfide shuttle effect, which leads to active material 
loss and rapid capacity fading, and the insulating nature of 
sulfur and its discharge products, which hinders electronic 
and ionic transport.7–9 Moreover, substantial volume changes 
during cycling and the instability of the lithium metal anode 
exacerbate degradation pathways that impede sustainable 
performance.10,11
A central bottleneck in advancing Li–S systems toward com-
mercialization lies in the interplay between sulfur loading, elec-
trolyte utilization, and achievable capacity. High sulfur loadings 
are essential for maximizing energy density at the cell level, yet 
increasing loading often results in lower active utilization due 
to sluggish reaction kinetics and electrolyte starvation.12–14 The 
electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio quantitatively captures elec-
trolyte consumption relative to active material mass and has 
been recognized as a critical metric for assessing practical rele-
vance.12,15 Conventional laboratory studies typically employ high 
E/S ratios and low sulfur loadings to facilitate reaction kinetics 
and polysulfide solubility; however, such conditions artificially 
inflate cell performance metrics while masking limitations that 
become pronounced under lean-electrolyte and high-loading 
regimes.12,16 As a consequence, although individual materials 
and electrolyte strategies have demonstrated remarkable gravi-
metric capacities in coin cells, translating these gains into practi-
cal pouch-cell formats with lean-electrolyte conditions remains 
an outstanding challenge.17–19
This discrepancy between laboratory performance and prac-
tical scalability has been increasingly emphasized in recent 
literature.20–22 Reviews have highlighted that electrolyte sol-
vation structure, additive chemistry, and interfacial engineer-
ing are critical for stabilizing lithium metal and suppressing 
polysulfide shuttling under lean-electrolyte conditions. Strate-
gies such as localized high-concentration electrolytes, ­LiNO3 sta-
bilization, DOL/DME–LiTFSI systems, and engineered cathode 
architectures have demonstrated promising results. However, 
most reported advancements remain evaluated within narrowly 
defined performance windows, often prioritizing either gravi-
metric capacity or cycling stability without a holistic assessment 
of multi-variable trade-offs governing real-world scalability.
Recent literature (2022–2025) has increasingly focused on 
rational electrolyte design and performance benchmarking, 
examining how solvation structures, additives, and solvent sys-
tems influence polysulfide behavior, interfacial stability, and 
ultimately cell-level energy metrics.20–22 Reviews highlight the 
importance of optimizing electrolyte composition to balance 
polysulfide shuttle suppression and lithium metal stability while 
reducing electrolyte excess without sacrificing capacity.23–30 
Despite this progress, a holistic, data-driven assessment of the 
trade-offs inherent in real-world operation has been lacking, par-
ticularly in terms of the multi-dimensional relationships among 
sulfur loading, E/S ratio, and gravimetric capacity across diverse 
benchmarks.31 Establishing such a framework is essential for 
identifying electrolyte strategies that genuinely advance Li–S 
battery performance toward commercial feasibility.
Importantly, despite significant progress in electrolyte chemis-
try and electrode engineering, the field lacks a quantitative, data-
driven framework capable of simultaneously interrogating sulfur 
loading, E/S ratio, and delivered capacity across diverse cell for-
mats and operating regimes.31 Existing benchmarking approaches 
typically compare individual metrics in isolation, limiting the abil-
ity to distinguish genuinely scalable strategies from electrolyte-rich 
laboratory constructs. Recent efforts have underscored the impor-
tance of lean-electrolyte design for practical Li–S batteries, yet a 
comprehensive multivariate evaluation linking these parameters 
across coin and pouch cells remains absent.
To address this gap, we introduce a systematic, data-driven 
benchmarking framework that integrates sulfur loading, E/S 
ratio, and capacity into ternary performance landscapes, comple-
mented by principal component analysis (PCA) to resolve domi-
nant trade-offs governing Li–S scalability. By compiling literature 
data spanning 2022–2025, we construct a multi-year performance 
map encompassing both coin and pouch-cell demonstrations.

results.
In this work, we address this gap through a systematic meta-
analysis of literature reported between 2022 and 2025, integrat-
ing key performance variables into a single analytical space. The 
objective is not to propose a new chemistry or architecture, but to 
establish a statistically informed design and benchmarking frame-
work that defines realistic operating boundaries and identifies con-
vergence trends toward practical conditions. Such a framework is 
essential for bridging the gap between laboratory-scale demonstra-
tions and deployable lithium–sulfur systems.
Results and discussion
Data processing
To ensure consistency across the compiled dataset, electro-
lyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratios reported in both volumetric (µL mg⁻1) 
and gravimetric (g g⁻1) units were standardized to a volumetric 
basis. For studies reporting E/S in gravimetric units, conversion 
was performed using :
where ρelectrolyte represents the electrolyte density (g mL⁻1). 
When explicitly reported, literature values were used; other-
wise, a representative density of ~ 1.2 g mL⁻1 was assumed for 
ether-based electrolytes (e.g., DOL/DME–LiTFSI systems). This 
assumption introduces minimal variation relative to the broader 
dataset trends and does not affect the overall conclusions of the 
multivariate analysis.
In this work, the ternary representation is used as a normal-
ized visualization framework rather than a compositional dia-
gram. The three variables—sulfur loading, E/S ratio, and areal 
capacity—are independently normalized to enable comparative 
mapping within a bounded simplex, without implying conserva-
tion or compositional summation.
Principal component analysis was performed in standardized 
Cartesian space. The principal component directions shown in 
the ternary plots represent visual projections of these trends 
onto the normalized parameter space and are intended as quali-
tative guides rather than strict regression fits within barycentric 
coordinates.
Scope and positioning of the study
This study is designed as a quantitative meta-analysis rather 
than an experimental investigation. The dataset is compiled 
from peer-reviewed lithium–sulfur battery studies published 
between 2022 and 2025, focusing on reports that provide suf-
ficient detail on sulfur loading, electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio, and 
areal capacity.
The intent of this approach is to enable cross-study compara-
bility and statistical interpretation of performance trends that 
are not accessible through individual experimental reports. 
While no new experimental data are generated, the methodology 
allows identification of latent correlations, trade-off regimes, 
and feasible operating boundaries that emerge only when data 
are analyzed collectively.
Such data-centric approaches are increasingly recognized as 
essential tools for guiding experimental design in complex elec-
trochemical systems, where multiple interdependent parameters 
govern performance.
It is important to emphasize that the objective of this analy-
sis is not to introduce incremental material-level improvements, 
but to provide a system-level understanding of performance 
constraints in lithium–sulfur batteries. By aggregating and ana-
lyzing literature data within a unified framework, the present 
study reveals emergent structure in the performance landscape, 
enabling identification of realistic operating regimes that are not 
apparent from isolated studies. This perspective shifts the focus 
from reporting peak performance metrics to understanding scal-
able and practically achievable conditions.

Performance landscape of 2023 lithium–sulfur electrolytes 
in coin cells (0.1–0.5 C)
To systematically evaluate electrolyte progress in lithium–sul-
fur (Li–S) batteries, we constructed a ternary performance land-
scape using reported 2023 literature data for coin cells tested at 
moderate rates (0.1–0.5 C), integrating sulfur loading, electro-
lyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio, and delivered areal capacity (Fig. 2). 
This multivariate representation enables simultaneous visuali-
zation of electrolyte economy, sulfur utilization, and capacity 
scaling within a unified framework.
The majority of reported studies cluster within the central 
region of the ternary map, corresponding to moderate-to-high 
sulfur loading, moderate E/S ratios, and consequently moder-
ate areal capacity. This concentration indicates that most 2023 
investigations remained within conventional experimental 
windows, prioritizing balanced electrochemical stability over 
aggressive scaling conditions. Only a limited number of studies 
extend toward extreme operating points.
Notably, configurations achieving low E/S ratios combined 
with elevated sulfur loading trend toward higher areal capacity 
along the PC1 trajectory (y = 0.9217x − 0.0863), reflecting coor-
dinated optimization between sulfur utilization and electrolyte 
reduction. Conversely, architectures operating at high E/S ratios 
with low sulfur loading are associated with reduced areal capac-
ity, underscoring the limited scalability of electrolyte-rich test-
ing conditions.
The positive slope of PC1 highlights the intrinsic coupling 
between sulfur loading and areal capacity under moderately 
balanced electrolyte conditions. However, progression toward 
higher areal capacity requires a carefully managed trade-off 
between sulfur mass loading and electrolyte availability. Exces-
sive electrolyte masks transport limitations, while aggressive 
electrolyte reduction without structural optimization suppresses 
sulfur utilization.
Overall, the 2023 ternary map reveals that the most impactful 
advances in Li–S coin-cell research are those that move beyond 
conventional electrolyte-rich configurations and approach 
leaner conditions while maintaining meaningful areal capacity. 
These studies represent early indicators of progress toward real-
istic Li–S cell configurations compatible with higher energy–den-
sity standards and eventual pouch-cell translation.

Principal component analysis of Li–S design–performance 
trade‑offs
To systematically interrogate the coupled effects of cell design 
parameters and electrochemical performance metrics, we performed 
a principal component analysis (PCA) on lithium–sulfur (Li–S) bat-
tery data reported in the literature between 2022 and 2024 (Fig. 5). 
The analysis incorporated six standardized variables: electrolyte-
to-sulfur ratio (E/S), sulfur loading, areal capacity, coulombic effi-
ciency (CE), capacity retention, and capacity fade rate. Only literature 
reports that explicitly disclosed all six parameters were included to 
ensure internal consistency of the multivariate analysis. The dataset 
was therefore curated to include only studies with internally consist-
ent and complete reporting of these parameters, ensuring compara-
bility across data points and preserving the physical interpretability 
of the multivariate analysis. Capacity retention and fade rate were 
standardized to account for variations in reported cycle endpoints 
across the literature. Capacity retention values were extracted at the 
reported cycle number for each study, while capacity fade rate was 
calculated as the normalized percentage capacity loss per cycle (or 
per 100 cycles) based on the initial and final reported capacities over 
the corresponding cycling interval. This approach preserves data-
set breadth while enabling consistent comparison of stability trends 
across studies with differing cycle life definitions. All variables were 
subsequently standardized prior to PCA to ensure comparability. This 
approach enables a compact representation of the dominant correla-
tions within the dataset while preserving the physical interpretabil-
ity of the underlying electrochemical constraints.32–36 Details of this 
analysis are provided in Supplemental Information.
Because PCA eigenvectors are sign-invariant, the loading 
vectors were oriented such that: ensuring physically meaning-
ful “Practicality” and “Stability” axes.
The resulting PCA biplot reveals two orthogonal axes that 
together explain approximately 66.5% of the total variance 
in the compiled dataset. The first principal component (PC1, 
39.6% variance explained) is dominated by positive contri-
butions from sulfur loading and areal capacity and a negative 
contribution from E/S ratio, indicating that PC1 captures a 
practicality-driven design axis. Movement along PC1, there-
fore, reflects the transition from electrolyte-rich, low-loading

Data‑derived feasible operating window
To identify practical and scalable operating conditions, we 
screened the compiled 2022–2024 Li–S literature dataset for 
configurations simultaneously exhibiting high areal capacity, 
high sulfur loading, lean-electrolyte usage, and meaningful 
cycling stability. Using percentile-based filters anchored to the 
dataset distribution (areal capacity ≥ 75th percentile, reten-
tion ≥ 60th percentile, sulfur loading ≥ 60th percentile, and 
E/S ≤ median), we obtained a subset that defines a feasible prac-
tical–stable regime (Fig. 6). This regime corresponds to E/S ≈ 
1.7–2.8 µL ­mg−1 and sulfur loading ≈ 6.9–9.9 mgₛ cm⁻2, yielding 
areal capacities ≈ 5.5–7.5 mAh cm⁻2 with ~ 82–89% retention 
over ~ 215–394 cycles (fade rates ~ 2.9–7.8% per 100 cycles). 
These results support a feasible operating window where elec-
trolyte starvation is avoided while maintaining transport and 
interfacial stability under practically relevant areal capacities.
Electrolyte and electrode chemistry characteristics 
within the feasible window
The data-driven feasible operating window identified here 
(E/S ≈ 2–3 µL ­mgS
−1 and sulfur loading ≈ 7–10 mg ­cm−2) repre-

consistently been demonstrated in recent literature. Impor-
tantly, the reports populating this envelope share common elec-
trolyte and electrode chemistry features that enable simultane-
ous electrolyte minimization and high areal sulfur utilization.
Across the feasible window, the dominant electrolyte class 
remains ether-based systems, most commonly binary DOL/
DME mixtures containing LiTFSI salt (typically ~ 1 M). These 
electrolytes provide favorable polysulfide solvation–transport 
kinetics and sufficiently low viscosity to sustain sulfur redox 
reactions under lean-electrolyte constraints. In nearly all 
practical-loading studies within this regime, ­LiNO3 is also 
employed as a critical additive to stabilize the lithium metal 
anode by promoting formation of a passivating solid–electro-
lyte interphase (SEI) and suppressing parasitic polysulfide 
shuttle reactions. Thus, the scalable window is largely defined 
by LiTFSI–DOL/DME–LiNO3 baseline chemistries, reflecting 
their continued role as the most mature electrolyte platform 
for high-loading Li–S operation.
From an electrode architecture perspective, cells achiev-
ing sulfur loadings above ~ 7 mg ­cm−2 within E/S ≤ 3 typically 
rely on engineered cathode scaffolds that maximize sulfur 
utilization while preserving ionic accessibility. These include 
porous carbon hosts (mesoporous carbons, carbon nanotube 
networks, graphene-based frameworks), catalytic or polar sur-
face modifiers (e.g., metal sulfides, nitrides, or oxides), and 
binder systems optimized for mechanical integrity at high 
mass loading. Such cathode designs are essential to mitigate 
transport limitations and maintain electrode-level conductiv-
ity when electrolyte volume is severely restricted.
Notably, while gel-polymer and quasi-solid-state electro-
lytes are frequently associated with improved cycling stability, 
they appear less represented within the strict lean-electrolyte/
high-loading feasibility envelope, indicating that most scalable 
demonstrations still rely on advanced liquid ether electrolytes 
rather than fully solidified platforms. Overall, the feasible 
operating window derived from literature trends suggests that 
near-term scalable Li–S deployment is most strongly enabled 
by ether-based LiTFSI/LiNO3 electrolytes paired with struc-
tured, polysulfide-regulating carbon–sulfur cathodes designed 
explicitly for electrolyte-limited operation.
In summary, the feasible scalability envelope (E/S ≈ 2–3 
µL ­mgS
−1 and sulfur loading ≈ 7–10 mg ­cm−2) is predominantly 
populated by ether-based DOL/DME electrolytes with LiTFSI 
salt and ­LiNO3_ additive, coupled with high-loading porous 
carbon–sulfur cathodes incorporating polysulfide-regulating 
host architectures.

design, where sulfur utilization, electrolyte economy, and 
electrode architecture must be simultaneously optimized.

PC2: Trade‑off‑dominated transitional regime
PC2 (y =  − 1.4897x + 0.8533), primarily associated with the 
2025 datasets evaluated at 1–5 C, exhibits a pronounced nega-
tive slope, revealing a strong inverse correlation between sulfur 
loading and the E/S ratio. This principal component captures an 
intermediate stage of pouch-cell development characterized by 
deliberate attempts to increase sulfur loading while aggressively 
reducing electrolyte content. However, movement along PC2 
does not produce proportional gains in areal capacity, indicating 
that scaling efforts are constrained by underlying electrochemi-
cal and transport limitations.
These limitations likely stem from intensified polysulfide 
shuttle effects, restricted ionic transport within thick, high-
mass-loading cathodes, and rapid electrolyte depletion under 
lean conditions. As electrolyte content is reduced to improve 
practical energy density, insufficient ionic mobility and poor sul-
fur utilization emerge as dominant bottlenecks. Consequently, 
PC2 defines a trade-off-dominated regime in which improvement 
in one design variable directly penalizes another, preventing bal-
anced performance enhancement.
Architectures located within this region illustrate the intrin-
sic challenges of transitioning from electrolyte-rich laboratory 
constructs to electrolyte-lean pouch configurations without 
simultaneous advances in cathode microstructure engineer-
ing, sulfur confinement strategies, and interfacial stabilization. 
PC2 therefore represents a necessary but incomplete transitional 
phase in Li–S maturation—highlighting the limitations of param-
eter reduction strategies in isolation and underscoring the need 
for integrated electrode–electrolyte co-design to achieve the bal-
anced scaling observed along PC1.
In addition, recent studies have shown that under extreme 
lean-electrolyte conditions, the solubility of lithium polysulfides 
becomes significantly limited, altering the fundamental reaction 
pathway of sulfur conversion.37 Rather than proceeding through 
solution-mediated polysulfide intermediates, the system increas-
ingly shifts toward solid-state nucleation and growth of Li₂S. 
This transition introduces heterogeneous deposition behavior, 
increases interfacial resistance, and leads to incomplete sulfur 
utilization. As a result, capacity fading under lean-electrolyte 
conditions is not only governed by transport limitations and 
shuttle effects, but also by this shift in reaction kinetics, which 
constrains reversible sulfur redox processes. This kinetic transi-
tion provides an additional mechanistic basis for the trade-off-
dominated behavior captured along PC2.
It is important to note that lithium metal anode thickness 
and the corresponding negative-to-positive (N/P) capac-
ity ratio are not consistently reported across the literature 
datasets analyzed in this study. In many cases, cells are con-
structed with excess lithium, which can partially compensate 
for lithium consumption and suppress apparent capacity fad-
ing. Under lean-electrolyte conditions, this effect becomes 
particularly significant, as lithium depletion and interfacial 
instability are major contributors to degradation. Conse-
quently, variations in lithium excess may influence the stabil-
ity trends captured along the PC2 axis, potentially masking 
intrinsic limitations associated with sulfur utilization and elec-
trolyte transport. These observations underscore the need for 
standardized reporting of N/P ratios in future studies to enable 
more comprehensive benchmarking of lithium–sulfur battery 
performance.

However, unlike the integrated scaling trajectory observed 
along PC1, progress along PC3 does not consistently translate 
into high areal capacity under lean-electrolyte constraints. 
While stability metrics improve, sulfur utilization at high 
mass loading remains limited by transport and structural fac-
tors. PC3, therefore, represents a chemistry-dominated opti-
mization stage that enhances interfacial stability and cycling 
durability but precedes the architectural and systems-level 
innovations required for practical, high-loading pouch-cell 
implementation.

performance.

Implications for practical energy density scaling
The ternary-PCA framework provides a critical insight into 
the true scaling behavior of Li–S pouch cells: high gravimetric 
capacity alone is insufficient to indicate meaningful progress 
toward practical energy–density realization. Advancement 
toward the PC1 domain—representing engineering-viable 
architectures—occurs only when reductions in E/S ratio and 
increases in sulfur loading translate into proportional and sus-
tained gains in areal capacity. In this multivariate context, areal 
capacity emerges as the decisive validation metric for pouch-
level scalability.
Architectures that fail to exceed approximately ~ 4 mAh cm⁻2 
under lean-electrolyte conditions remain fundamentally con-
strained, regardless of elevated specific (gravimetric) capacity 
values achieved under electrolyte-rich testing environments. 
The PCA results demonstrate that practical energy density is gov-
erned not by isolated capacity improvements but by balanced co-
optimization of sulfur utilization and electrolyte economy under 
realistic constraints.
Accordingly, commercialization-relevant Li–S pouch cells 
demand coordinated engineering solutions rather than incre-
mental chemical modifications alone. These include precise 
cathode porosity control to maintain ionic accessibility at high 
mass loading, effective electrolyte confinement strategies to pre-
vent starvation under lean conditions, interfacial stabilization 
of lithium metal to suppress shuttle-induced degradation, and 
mechanical robustness capable of sustaining thick sulfur elec-
trodes during repeated cycling. Only through such integrated 
electrode–electrolyte co-design can Li–S systems transition from 
laboratory-scale demonstrations to scalable, manufacturable 
energy storage platforms.

Conclusion
In this work, we established a comprehensive data-driven 
benchmarking framework to systematically evaluate lithium–sul-
fur (Li–S) battery scalability across both coin and pouch-cell for-
mats by integrating sulfur loading, electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) 
ratio, and delivered capacity within a unified ternary–PCA analyti-
cal approach. By compiling and interrogating multi-year literature 
data (2022–2025), we identified distinct design trajectories gov-
erning Li–S performance and quantitatively resolved the trade-offs 
between practical energy–density scaling and cycling stability.
Our analysis demonstrates that pouch-cell viability cannot 
be inferred from gravimetric capacity alone. Instead, scalable 
performance emerges only when increases in sulfur loading and 
reductions in electrolyte content translate into proportional 
gains in areal capacity under lean-electrolyte conditions. Two 
orthogonal design axes were resolved: a practicality-driven axis 
coupling high sulfur loading, reduced E/S ratios, and elevated 
areal capacity, and a stability-driven axis associated with reten-
tion and degradation behavior. The weak coupling between 
these axes reveals a persistent decoupling between laboratory-
optimized energy metrics and long-term operational durability.
Importantly, we define a data-derived feasible operating 
window—sulfur loading of approximately 7–10 mg cm⁻2 and E/S 
ratios of ~ 1.7–2.8 µL mgₛ⁻1—corresponding to areal capacities 
of ~ 5.5–7.5 mAh cm⁻2 under sustained cycling. Architectures 
operating outside this envelope either depend on electrolyte-
rich conditions that artificially inflate performance metrics or 
encounter transport and stability limitations that restrict mean-
ingful scaling.
These findings underscore that commercialization-relevant 
Li–S pouch cells require integrated electrode–electrolyte co-
design strategies rather than incremental additive refinement 
alone. Critical enablers include optimized cathode porosity 
and sulfur distribution, electrolyte confinement compatible 
with lean operation, enhanced interfacial ion transport in thick 
electrodes, and mechanical stabilization capable of sustaining 
high mass loading without structural degradation.
By quantitatively defining practical design boundaries instead 
of isolated performance milestones, this work provides actionable 
engineering guidance for technology translation. The multivari-
ate framework introduced here enables objective benchmarking 
of electrolyte economy and sulfur utilization under realistic con-
straints, offering a rigorous decision-support tool for advancing 
Li–S batteries from laboratory validation toward manufacturable 
pouch-cell platforms suitable for electric mobility, aviation elec-
trification, and grid-scale energy storage applications.
Looking forward, the advancement of data-driven bench-
marking in lithium–sulfur batteries critically depends on the 
adoption of standardized reporting practices across the research 
community. Key parameters—including areal capacity, electro-
lyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio, sulfur loading, and negative-to-positive 
(N/P) capacity ratio—should be consistently reported in future 
studies, particularly for pouch-cell configurations. The absence 
of such standardized metrics currently limits cross-study compa-
rability and can obscure intrinsic performance trade-offs under 
practical operating conditions. Establishing unified reporting 
guidelines will enhance the quality, reproducibility, and univer-
sality of Li–S battery datasets, enabling more rigorous evaluation 
of scalable designs and accelerating the transition toward com-
mercially viable energy storage technologies.
This work establishes a data-driven framework for evaluating 
lithium–sulfur battery performance, shifting the emphasis from 
isolated experimental achievements to system-level feasibility. 
Although no new experimental data are introduced, the study 
provides a quantitative foundation for benchmarking, compari-
son, and design optimization across the field. By defining a sta-
tistically grounded feasible operating window, this work enables 
more rigorous assessment of emerging materials and architec-
tures, supporting the transition of lithium–sulfur technology from 
laboratory-scale demonstrations toward practical deployment.
