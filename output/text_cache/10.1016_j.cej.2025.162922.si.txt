Experimental Section
Materials: Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, >99.9%), sodium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (NaTFSI, 99.99%), and poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, 99.9%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. 1,3-Dioxolane (DOL) (battery grade) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) (battery grade) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Co., Ltd. Hydrazine hydrate and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) were purchased from Tianjin Damao Chemical Reagent Factory. Polypropylene (PP) membrane (Celgard 3501) was purchased from Shenzhen Sentai Technology Co., Ltd. Hexamethylphosphoramide (HMPA), LiNO3 (99.99% metals basis), and sulfur (99.95%) were obtained from Aladdin Co., Ltd. Li metal foil was purchased from China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd. Acetylene black (99.99%) was purchased from Strem Chemicals, Inc. HMPA was purified by vacuum distillation. Other materials were used as received.
Preparation of the S@RGO cathode: Graphene oxide (GO) was synthesized using the modified Hummers’ method [1]. GO was converted into its reduced form (RGO) by chemical reduction using hydrazine hydrate, followed by thermal treatment. First, a Teflon-lined autoclave (50 mL) was charged with a suspension of GO (20 mL, 5.62 mg mL1) and hydrazine hydrate (2.5 mL), and then placed an oven set at 180 °C for 12 h. The product was collected by filtration, washed with deionized water, freeze-dried for 48 h, and then thermally treated using a tube furnace at 800 °C for 2 h under an atmosphere of Ar. Sulfur was incorporated into the RGO by thermally annealing a mixture of sulfur and RGO (3:1, w/w) at 155 °C for 12 h [2]. The obtained composite was designated as S@RGO. To prepare the S@RGO cathode, the S@RGO composite (40.24 mg), acetylene black (5.26 mg), and PVDF (5.06 mg) were ground using a mortar and pestle for 10 minutes, followed by dispersing the mixture in NMP (300 L) to form a S@RGO slurry. The S@RGO cathode was prepared by coating the S@RGO slurry on Al foil and then dried in a vacuum oven set at 45 °C for 24 h.
Preparation of the electrolytes: The DOL/DME electrolyte was prepared by dissolving LiTFSI (2.8709 g, 10 mmol) and LiNO3 (0.2622 g, 2 wt%) in a mixture of DOL (5 mL) and DME (5 mL). The DOL/HMPA electrolyte was prepared by dissolving LiTFSI (2.8709 g, 10 mmol) and LiNO3 (0.2622 g, 2 wt%) in a mixture of DOL and HMPA (10 mL in total). The HMPA loading was 4 vol%, 6 vol%, 8 vol%, or 10 vol% and the resultant electrolytes were designated as DOL/HMPA-4, DOL/HMPA-6, DOL/HMPA-8, and DOL/HMPA-10, respectively. All electrolytes were prepared in an Ar-filled glove box.
Characterization: SEM measurements were performed on a scanning electron microscope (Phenom, XL G2 Desktop) that operated at an accelerating voltage of 10 kV. XPS data were collected on an X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (Thermo Fisher, NEXSA) using monochromated Al K radiation (1486.7 eV). The Raman spectra were obtained on a Raman microscope (Horiba Scientific, XploRA PLUS). To prepare the samples that were characterized using SEM or XPS, the Li anode and the sulfur cathode were isolated after disassembling a tested Li//Li symmetric cell (after 5 charge/discharge cycles) and a tested Li−S cell (after 10 charge/discharge cycles), respectively. The Li anode and sulfur cathode were washed with DOL to remove any impurities, followed by drying for 5 h in a glove box. The electrochemical measurements were performed on an electrochemical workstation (Princeton, PMCCH808A) and a battery testing system (LAND, CT2100A). 
The DN value of HMPA was measured using 23Na NMR spectrometry [3]. NaTFSI was first dissolved in a series of solvents, including acetonitrile (DN=14 kcal mol1), DME (DN=20 kcal mol1), pyridine (DN=33 kcal mol1), dimethyl sulfoxide (DN=30 kcal mol1), triethylamine (DN=55 kcal mol1), and HMPA. The concentration of NaTFSI was 5 mmol L1. A solution of NaCl in D2O (1 M) was sealed in a capillary tube (0.3 mm diameter) and used as an external reference. The 23Na NMR shifts of NaTFSI in the different solvents were measured. The DN value of HMPA was obtained by measuring the chemical shift of 23Na NMR signal followed by linear correlation to the DN value of the solvent (see Figure S1).

Li//Li symmetric cells were prepared using Li foils as the electrodes, PP membranes as separator, and either the DOL/HMPA or the DOL/DME electrolyte (40 μL). The rate capability and cycling stability tests of the Li//Li symmetric cells were performed on a battery testing system. The rate capability was tested at a current density that ranged from 0.5 to 5 mA cm−1 with a plating/stripping capacity of 1 mAh cm−2. The cycling stability was measured at a current density of 1 or 5 mA cm−2 with a plating/stripping capacity of 1 mAh cm−2.

Li−S cells were prepared using the S@RGO cathode, a PP membrane as the separator, Li foil as the anode, and either the DOL/HMPA-6 or the DOL/DME electrolyte (40 μL). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves and EIS data of Li−S cells were obtained on an electrochemical workstation. Charge/discharge, rate capability and cycling stability data of Li−S cells were obtained on a battery testing system. To measure the leakage current, a Li−S cell was subjected to an initial discharge/charge cycle over a potential range of 1.7 and 2.7 V, followed by discharging to 1.7 V and then being charging at a constant potential of 2.2, 2.35, 2.4, 2.5, or 2.6 V for 20 h. The stabilized current was considered as the leakage current.

The visible cells were assembled using the S@RGO cathode, a PP membrane as the separator, Li foil as the anode, and either the DOL/HMPA-6 or DOL/DME electrolyte (10 mL). The assembly and measurement of the visible cells were performed in an Ar-filled glovebox.
Molecular Dynamics: Density functional theory (DFT) calculations and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed in this study. The Gaussian code was employed to calculate the coordination structures and binding strengths of Li+ with different solvent molecules, electrostatic potential, and the energy levels of the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO). The binding strengths were calculated using the following the equation: 

E = Etotal−Eion−Esol,

where Etotal, Eion, and Esol are the total energy of solvent coordinated with Li+, the energy of Li+, and the energy of solvent, respectively. The DFT calculations were carried out at the B3LYP level with the 6-311G(d,p) basis set. MD simulations were performed using a large-scale atomic/molecular massively parallel simulator code, and the interatom interaction was described by the all-atom optimized potentials for liquid simulations force field. The velocity-verlet algorithm with a fixed time step of 0.5 fs was used to integrate the equations of motion. To estimate the Li+ solvation structures in the two kinds of electrolytes, Li+ and solvent molecules, in ratios similar to those used in the experiments, were randomly placed in the simulation box. The electrolytes were then minimized with the steepest descent method, followed by 50-ns simulation under the isothermal–isobaric ensemble (NPT) at the temperature of 298 K and pressure of 1 atm.
