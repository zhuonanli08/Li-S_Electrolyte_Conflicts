a b s t r a c t
Based on the dissolution and conversion mechanism of lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries, insulating solid
short-chain polysulﬁdes (Li2S2/Li2S) will continuously passivate and corrode the active interface of cath-
ode and anode, which seriously affects its performance. Herein, ammonium benzenesulfonate (NH4BS) is
proposed as a soluble ammonium salt to dissolve Li2S in the ether electrolyte, according to the inductive
effect of NH4
+ cation and O atom on Li-S bond. This is beneﬁcial to alleviate the interface problem of elec-
trodes and irreversible loss of active materials. Noticeably, soluble Li2S regulates its deposition behavior
from 2D to 3D, which is conducive to the more effective use of conductive surface. Moreover, the addition
of NH4BS can increase the dissociation degree of long-chain polysulﬁdes, so that the diffusion rate and
reaction kinetics of active substances are improved. Proﬁting from these functions, the Li-S cells with
NH4BS act out excellent cyclic stability in the long cycle of 0.5 C and 2 C. Under the extreme conditions
of high sulfur loading and low electrolyte-sulfur ratio, the cells with NH4BS can cycle stably for 196
cycles, which signiﬁcantly prolongs the battery life. The proposal of NH4BS broadens a new idea to solve

1. Introduction
With the development of portable mobile devices, electric vehi-
cles and smart grid applications, the lower-cost and higher-energy
density energy storage systems urgently need to be developed
[1,2]. Sulfur is abundant in nature, low in price and friendly to the
environment [3]. In addition, the theoretical speciﬁc capacity of Li-
S batteries using sulfur as cathode active materials reaches up to
1675 mAh g1, attributing to the multistep conversion of sulfur
(S8) to lithium sulﬁde (Li2S) [4,5]. However, S8 and the ﬁnal discharge
product Li2S are both electronically insulating solids [6], which
accounts for the poor sulfur redox reversibility. The huge volume
expansion of the conversion between S8 (2.07 g cm3) and Li2S
(1.66 g cm3) will also easily cause the collapse of the electrode
structure [7]. Moreover, during charging/discharging, the high-
order lithium polysulﬁdes (LPSs, i.e., Li2Sn, 4  n  8) will trigger
the notorious ‘‘shuttle effect” [8]. As the ‘‘shuttle effect” occurs, the
uncontrolled deposition of insulating Li2S will continuously passivate
the cathode conductive surface [9], which is the major reason for
generation of dead sulfur and rapid capacity fade of Li-S cells [10].
Meantime, the dissolved long-chain LPSs can be reduced to Li2S/
Li2S2 on the Li metal anode, resulting in the corrosion of anode
[11]. In order to solve these problems, researchers have made great
efforts in cathode modiﬁcation [12,13], electrolyte formula [14,15],
interfacial regulation of lithium metal anode [16,17].
From the perspective of electrolyte additives, molecular binders
which can react with LPSs to form precipitation is the most com-
mon means to suppress the ‘‘shuttle effect”. Oligoanilines [18],
binphenyl-4,4’-dithiol [19], bis(4-nitrophenyl)carbonate [20] and
2, 5-dichloro-1, 4-benzoquinone [21] are all reported as molecular
binders to improve the performance of Li-S cells. However, the
strategy of molecular binder cannot alleviate the problem of the
passivated electrode caused by the uncontrolled deposition of
Li2S. Alternatively, increasing the solubility of Li2S in the elec-
trolyte may be a feasible approach. Previously, NH4NO3 [22] and
NH4I [23] have been proved to promote the dissociation and disso-
lution of Li2S, but they are insoluble in the basic electrolyte and
high-donicity
solvents
such
dimethyl
sulfoxide
(DMSO)
and
tetramethylene sulfone (SL) has to be introduced. This brings a
problem that the violent reactivity of the high-donicity solvents
with Li anode hinders stable cycling of cells [24], which makes a
request for an additive dissolving in the basic ether electrolyte
and promoting the solubility of Li2S.
Herein, we reported ammonium benzenesulfonate (NH4BS) as
an electrolyte additive, which can dissolve in the basic electrolyte
without cosolvent. Through the inductive effect of NH4
+ cation and
O atom (from sulfonate group) on Li-S bond in Li2S, NH4BS can pro-
mote the dissociation of long-chain and short-chain polysulﬁdes in
the electrolyte, which could improve the reaction kinetics and alle-
viate the passivation of electrode surface. In addition, the solubility
of Li2S is also conductive to reducing the corrosion of Li anode and
loss of active material. Based on protective effects on surfaces of
anode and cathode, NH4BS signiﬁcantly elevates the comprehen-
sive electrochemical performance of Li-S cells.
2. Results and discussion
Most of ammonium salt additives are insoluble in basic ether
electrolyte, which requires high-donicity solvents, such as SL and
DMSO to help dissolve [22,23]. While NH4BS exhibits a satisfying
solubility of 1.5 wt% in the base electrolyte at room temperature
(Figure S1), so there will be no violent reactivity between high-
donicity solvents and Li anode. In order to determine the interac-
tion between NH4BS and Li2S, the theoretical model based on den-
sity function theory was used [25]. As shown in Fig. 1a, the length
of Li-S bond in Li2S is 2.101 Å, the length of N-H bond in NH4BS is
1.019 Å and 1.474 Å respectively. When NH4BS and Li2S coexist in
theoretical model, the system will be optimized towards the low-
est energy state. In the optimization result, Li-S bond and N-H bond
extend to 2.230 Å and 1.118 Å respectively. Notably, the calcula-
tion not only veriﬁes the hydrogen bond inductive effect between
NH4
+ and Li2S, but also ﬁnds that the S-O bond in NH4BS extending
from 1.474 Å to1.513 Å. Ulteriorly, the Mayer bond order of S-H
and Li-O bonds between NH4BS and Li2S are calculated [26]. Solu-
bility can be understood as an energy-driven complexation pro-
cess, and Mayer bond order can judge the interaction strength
and bonding trend between quantum atoms, which is used to
study the dissolution behavior of substance [27,28]. As displayed
in Fig. 1b, the Mayer bond order values between each S and H
(0.363), Li and O (0.841) cannot perfectly meet the requirements
for the formation of stable bonds, but the values are obviously
away from 0, revealing that there is strong interaction between
NH4BS and Li2S [29]. The visual experiment conﬁrms the results
of theoretical calculation that the addition of NH4BS can indeed
help
the
dissociation
and
dissolution
of
Li2S
in
electrolyte
(Fig. 1c). When the same amount of Li2S (8 mg) is added into the
electrolyte with/without NH4BS (2 mL) and stirred for 20 min,
Li2S can be dissolved in the electrolyte with NH4BS, while Li2S is
insoluble and the solution is turbid in the base electrolyte. For fur-
ther analysis, Li2S and NH4BS are dissolved in DMSO d6 for nuclear
magnetic resonance (NMR). Fig. 1d reveals the 1H resonance of NH4
+
cation shifting to the low frequency region (from 7.2 ppm to
0.64 ppm) [30], as NH4BS coexists with Li2S. The shift of 1H reso-
nance indicates that the strong interaction between electronega-
tive S2 (from Li2S) and closely aggregated positive H+ (from
NH4
+) produces electron shielding to H atom [22]. As for 7Li spectra,
Fig. 1e displays the obvious shift (from 1.99 ppm to 1.02 ppm)
with introducing NH4BS, which attributes to the inductive effect
of NH4
+ on Li2S, along with the coordination between O atom (from
sulfonate group) and Li+ [31].
Li2S is an electronic insulating solid [32], which will continu-
ously passivate the surface of conductive substrate during the
cycle. The solubilization of Li2S by NH4BS additive can reduce the
passivation of cathode surface in principle, so the second platform
(for the conversion of soluble long-chain polysulﬁdes to insoluble
short-chain polysulﬁdes) on the ﬁrst cycle charge–discharge curve
of the cell with NH4BS is much longer than that of blank cell at
0.5 C (Fig. 2a) [33]. Ulteriorly, we assemble Li2S8|Li cells with/with-
out NH4BS to investigate the deposition behavior of Li2S on the
same
conductive
substrate
by
chronoamperometry
[34,35].
Fig. 2b presents the chronoamperometry curves for the discharging
process of Li2S8|Li cells. Notably, the cell with NH4BS (1115 s)
reaches the highest constant current earlier than the blank cell
(1915 s), suggesting NH4BS is beneﬁcial to improve the kinetics
of polysulﬁdes conversion from long-chain to short-chain. In addi-
tion, it can be seen that the deposition capacity of the cell with
NH4BS reaches 632.5 mAh g1, which is higher than that of the
blank cell (580.2 mAh g1). The increase of deposition capacity

Previously, researchers mainly focused on the dissolution of
short-chain polysulﬁdes (Li2S/Li2S2) in the study of solubilizer
[1,22,32,37], but rarely discussed the inﬂuence on long-chain poly-
sulﬁdes. The degree of dissociation of active materials will affect
the diffusion rate of ions and then affect the reaction kinetics of
the battery. Li2S4 is selected as a typical long-chain polysulﬁde
[38]. The density functional theory model is used to estimate the
dissociation degree of long-chain polysulﬁde (Li2S4) when NH4BS
exists. As shown in Fig. 3a, the bond length of S-Li in Li2S4 is
2.384 Å, which can be extended to 2.991 Å with receiving the
action of NH4

NH4BS contributes to the dissociation of long-chain polysulﬁdes. In
addition, it can be observed that the formation speed of Li2S4 is
accelerated and the solubility is increased in electrolyte with NH4-

higher initial capacity and possess better capacity retention, which
is consistent with the experiment results that NH4BS regulates the
3D deposition of Li2S and eliminates its irreversible accumulation.

Due to the shuttle effect, long-chain polysulﬁdes will diffuse to
the Li anode and be reduced to short-chain polysulﬁdes (Li2S/Li2S2)
on its surface, resulting in the corrosion of Li anode [40]. NH4BS can
help Li2S dissolve into the electrolyte, which can not only alleviate
cathode passivation, but also reduce anode corrosion. The cells
with/without NH4BS after 150 cycles at 0.5 C are assembled, and
their Li anodes are tested by SEM, EDS and XPS. As can be seen
from Fig. 5d-f, the surface of Li anode with NH4BS remains dense
and smooth, while coral dead lithium appears on that of blank cell
(Fig. 5a-c). In addition to the morphology of surface, the cross-
sectional SEM image (Fig. 5g-h) and EDS mapping (Fig. 5i-j) of Li
anodes also reveal that NH4BS is conductive to mitigating the cor-
rosion of Li anode. Compared with the blank cell, the corrosion
depth with NH4BS is nearly 30 lm less. Furthermore, the chemical
environments of sulfur on the Li anodes with/with NH4BS after 150
cycles at 0.5 C are analyzed by XPS, as shown in Fig. 5k. It is obvi-
ous that the signal of Li2S2 (163.2162.1 eV) signal on Li anode
with NH4BS is much weaker than that without NH4BS, indicating
that the addition of NH4BS effectively reduces the accumulation
of short-chain sulﬁdes on the surface of Li anode [41]. Other signal
peaks on XPS spectra are from thiosulfate and the S-O bond of SO2-
CF3, which are products of decomposition of lithium salts and the
side reaction between polysulﬁdes and Li anode [42,43]. In conclu-
sion, the solubilization function of NH4BS on short-chain sulﬁdes
can indeed relieve the corrosion of Li anode.
3. Conclusion
In summary, NH4BS can dissolve short-chain polysulﬁdes (Li2S2/
Li2S) and effectively alleviate the passivation of cathode and anode,
through the inductive effect of NH4
+ cation and O atom on Li-S
bond. Notably, this inductive effect also can increase the dissocia-
tion degree of long-chain polysulﬁdes, which is rarely concerned in
the past. The increase of polysulﬁdes dissociation degree can
improve the diffusion rate of active material and reaction kinetics.
Based on these functions, NH4BS endows Li-S cells more stable cyc-
lic performance and superior rate performance. Even under the
extreme conditions of high sulfur loading and low electrolyte-
sulfur ratio, the Li-S cell achieves the stable 196 cycles. In addition,
NH4BS as a soluble ammonium salt avoids poor compatibility
between high polar solvent and lithium negative electrode. How-
ever, NH4BS cannot inhibit the ‘‘shuttle effect”, so there is still
rapid capacity decay at the initial stage of the cycle, which needs
to further complemented with other functional additives to better
promote the practicality of Li-S batteries.

4.3. Electrochemical measurements
The CR2025 coin cells are assembled in a glove box, S/C com-
posite as the cathode, polypropylene as the diaphragm and lithium
sheet as the anode. For low sulfur loading cells, the electrolyte is

standing 12 h at 25 C, the cells are cycled in 1.72.8 V versus
Li/Li+ and conducted GITT test on the LANDAN test system. The
cells for observing the deposition morphology of Li2S directly takes
Li2S8 in the electrolyte as active materials (25 lL-0.1 M-Li2S8 + 1

rates (0.1 mV/s, 0.2 mV/s, 0.3 mV/s, 0.4 mV/s, 0.5 mV/s) are per-
formed on electrochemical workstation in a voltage range.

4.5. Theoretical calculation
The theoretical models based on density functional theory are
used to probe the interaction between NH4BS and polysulﬁdes
(Li2S and Li2S4). The theoretical calculations are executed by the
software gaussian 16 under the functional b3lyp and basis set 6-
31+g(d) level [25,44]. Ulteriorly, DFT-d3 dispersion correction is
adopted to make the results more accurate [45]. In addition, Mayer
bond order of S-H bond and Li-O bond between NH4BS and Li2S is
obtained by calculating the single point energy under the func-
tional b3lyp and basis set 6-311g(d) level [46,47], and then all elec-
tronic wavefunction is analyzed in multiwfn package [48].
