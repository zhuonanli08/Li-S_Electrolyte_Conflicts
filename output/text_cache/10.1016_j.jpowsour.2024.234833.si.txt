Li-S battery assembly and testing: 
XC-72 (commercial carbon black) and sulfur powder were mixed in a mass ratio of 3:7 and ground. The mixture was then heated at 155 °C under an argon atmosphere for 12 h to deposit sulfur, resulting in a sample with a sulfur content of 70 wt.%. The sample was mixed with SuperP conductive carbon black (20 wt.%) and PVDF (10 wt.%) and dissolved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The mixture was coated onto aluminum foil current collectors and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h to remove the solvent. The dried electrode was then cut into disks with a diameter of 12 mm. The sulfur content in the positive electrode was 49%. Unless otherwise specified, the typical sulfur loading in the electrode was approximately 1.2~1.4 mg cm-2. Celgard 2400 was used as the separator, and lithium metal was used as the anode. The aforementioned electrolyte, either without additives or containing 2,3-DCP, 2,5-DCP, or 2,6-DCP, was added. Unless otherwise specified, the electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio (E/S) was fixed at 30 μL mg-1. Button cells were assembled using stainless steel cell cases (2032 type) in an argon-filled glove box. During electrochemical performance testing, the current rate and specific capacity calculations were based on the sulfur mass in the cathode (1 C = 1675 mA g-1). The batteries were tested on a Neware battery testing system with a voltage range of 1.7~2.8 V. Cycle voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed using a CHI 650D electrochemical analyzer. The CV measurements were conducted with a scan rate of 0.1 mV s-1 and a voltage range of 1.7~2.8 V, while the EIS measurements were performed over a frequency range of 100 kHz to 0.01 Hz. The ion conductivity of the electrolyte was measured using a conductivity meter equipped with platinum electrodes. To ensure the accuracy and repeatability of the data, multiple tests were conducted for each sample while continuously monitoring their stabilities during testing. The values reported in the manuscript are the averages derived from these multiple tests to underscore the reliability of the ionic conductivity measurements.

Electrolyte preparation 
The blank electrolyte was prepared using a basic electrolyte for lithium-sulfur batteries (LSBs), which consists of a solution of 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.1 M LiNO3 in a mixture of DOL (dioxolane) and DME (dimethoxyethane) in a volume ratio of 1:1. To prepare electrolytes with 2,3-dichloropyrazine (2,3-DCP), 2,5-dichloropyrazine (2,5-DCP), and 2,6-dichloropyrazine (2,6-DCP), these compounds were dissolved in the above-mentioned electrolyte at concentrations of 30, 40 and 50 mM.

Experiments of Li2S nucleation 
CR2032 coin cells were assembled using XC-72 electrode for the symmetrical cell, lithium metal anode, and Celgard 2400 separator. 0.50 mol L−1 [S] of Li2S8 dissolved in tetraglyme with 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI was prepared as basic electrolyte. Additional 40 mM pyrazine additives were added to the basic solution to obtain a polysulfide catholyte. 20 μL catholyte was dropped onto XC-72 electrode, while 20 μL blank electrolyte that was otherwise identical but without polysulfides was added to the anode compartment. To study the nucleation kinetics of Li2S, the assembled cell was first discharged at a constant current from 0.112 mA to 2.06 V, followed by constant potential discharge until the current dropped below 0.01 mA, while maintaining a voltage of 2.05 V to ensure complete Li2S nucleation.

Physical characterization
The materials and solvents used in the study were purchased from commercial sources (such as Aldrich, Canrd, Dodochem) without further purification. Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) was performed using a German Car Zeiss Ultra Plus microscope. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was carried out using a British VG Escalab 210 instrument with a Mg 300-W X-ray source.

Theoretical calculations
In this work, we employed the density functional theory (DFT) method using the Gaussian 16 program. Calculations were performed using the B3LYP functional and the 6-31G(d) basis set. All structures were optimized and verified, and no imaginary frequencies were obtained. The energy levels of their lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) were calculated at the same level of theory. The Gibbs free energy was used during all present discussions. The stationary points as local minima or transition states were characterized by analyzing vibrational frequency within the same theoretical level. Multiwfn was used for rendering and visualization, and the visual molecular dynamics (VMD) software was employed for visualization purposes.
