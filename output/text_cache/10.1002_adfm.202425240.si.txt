I. Supporting text
1. Experimental section
1.1 Electrolyte and electrode preparation
The electrolyte was prepared and stored inside a glovebox filled with high-purity argon (water and oxygen both <0.01 ppm). LiPF6 (Lithium hexafluorophosphate, DodoChem), LiTFSI (lithium bis((trifluoromethyl)sulfonyl)azanide, DodoChem), LiNO3 (lithium nitrate, DodoChem) were used as the salts. DOL (1,3-dioxolane, Adamas) and DME (ethylene glycol dimethyl ether, Adamas) were used as the solvents. The nominal stoichiometry of Li2S8 solution was prepared by mixing S (sulfur, Merck) and Li2S (lithium sulfide, Canrd) with a molar ratio of 7:1 in DOL/DME (1/1=v/v), followed by magnetic stirring for 24 h. The 1.0 M LiTFSI and 1 M LiNO3 were ordinally added into DOL/DME and were shocked by a shaker for 15 min. 
1.2 Electrolyte and electrode characterization
Atomic absorption spectrometer (ZEEnit700P) measurements were conducted based on the saturated Li2S8 solutions after 24 h. Raman spectroscopy (HRS-500) were measured by adding electrolyte into cuvette. Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (ADVANCE NEO 500) was measured by adding electrolyte into Coaxial nuclear magnetic tube, and 0.01 M LiPF6 in D2O was used as the internal reference. The ionic conductivity and viscosity of electrolytes were measured by Ion conductivity meter (SevenCompact S230-USP/EP-CN) and viscometer (Anton Paar MCR102e Rheometer), respectively. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were conducted on a Thermo Scientific ESCALAB 250Xi scanning X-ray microprobe with a monochromatic Al Kα X-ray (1486.6 eV) source.\
1.3 Electrochemical performance testing
Li||S, Li||Li, and Li||Cu cells were assembled in 2025-coin type cells inside a glovebox and tested at 25°C on Neware battery test system. Separator was Cegard 2400 pp (polypropylene, DodoChem). Typically for the sulfur cathode, 12.0 g CNTs and 24.0 g S were mixed and kept in a glass bottle at 160°C for 4 h. The S/CNT mixture was further ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP solvent to form a homogeneous slurry. After that, the obtained slurry was directly coated on an Al current collector by a doctor blade, which was further dried at 60°C for 12 h. The S/CNT electrode was then available after punching into disks. The areal sulfur loading of cathode was ~1.1 mg cm−2. The lithium metal anode takes a piece of lithium metal sheet with 500 μm. The electrolyte of symmetrical cells was about 40 μL to ensure the separator and electrodes were fully wetted and the E/S ratio of Li||S was 15 mL g−1 (S). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed with an amplitude of 10 mV over the frequency from 0.01 to 106 Hz in electrochemical workstation. 
1.4 Theoretical simulations
MD simulations were conducted using the Large Scale Atomic/Molecular Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS) code and the Optimized Potential for Liquid Simulations-all atom (OPLS-AA) force field [1-3]. The simulation model containing 495 DOL, 337 DME, 70 LiTFSI, and 70 LiNO3 was constructed for solvation structure analysis. Models containing 495 DOL, 337 DME, and 35, 70, 105, or 140 LiTFSI were constructed for calculating dielectric constants according to the method developed in literature[4]. The parameters of solvent molecules were generated by the LigParGen web server except for adopting the advanced restrained electrostatic potential (RESP2) atomic partial charges, which were obtained based on electrostatic potential (ESP) charges using the Multiwfn program [5-9]. The parameters of Li+ and TFSI− were obtained from Jensen et al.[10] and Doherty et al.[11], respectively. The initial atomic coordinates were generated with Packing Optimization for Molecular Dynamics Simulations (Packmol) program [12] and the solvation structures were visualized by VESTA [13]. 
The periodic boundary conditions (PBCs) were applied in all three directions for all simulations. A cutoff of 12 Å was used for both van der Waals interactions and long-range correction (particle–particle particle-mesh) of Coulombic interactions. The time step was fixed to be 1 fs. All electrolyte models were first equilibrated in an NPT ensemble using the Parrinello–Rahman barostat for 2 ns to maintain a temperature of 298.15 K and a pressure of 1 atm with time constants of 0.1 and 1.0 ps, respectively [14]. After that, the electrolytes were heated from 298.15 to 500.15 K within 0.5 ns, and maintained at 500.15 K for 0.5 ns. Subsequently, the models were annealed from 500.15 to 298.15 K within 0.5 ns and equilibrated at 298.15 K in an NPT ensemble for another 4 ns. A 7 ns production run was finally conducted in an NVT ensemble under Nose–Hoover thermostat[15]. Only the final 5 ns were adopted to analyze the solvation structures and calculate the dielectric constants.
The binding energy between Li+ and anions were computed by DFT calculations (𝐸b, defined as formula 1 where 𝑀 and 𝑁 can be Li+ or anion), which were performed in Gaussian (G09) program. Becke’s three-parameter hybrid method using the Lee–Yang–Parr correlation functional (B3LYP) and 6-31+G(d, p) basis set was chosen[16]. The solvation effect was considered by the SMD model[17].
The Gibbs reaction free energy was calculated by the GGA-PBE method. The Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) function was adopted to describe electron exchange and correlation energy. The cut-off energy for plane-wave basis was set to 400 eV to ensure the precision of calculations and the total energy convergence criterion for energy was 10−5 eV, with the force convergence thresholds set at 0.02 eV Å−1 for geometric optimization. Meanwhile, the COSMO solvent model was added to describe the dielectric constant.
