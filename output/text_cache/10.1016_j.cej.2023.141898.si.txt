Dual-Mediation Pathways Promote Redox Kinetics for High-Loading Lithium–Sulfur Batteries under Lean Electrolyte
Kaifeng Li, Lufei Wang, Qihang Yang, Sheng-Qi Guo and Mengmeng Zhen∗

Tianjin Key Laboratory of Clean Energy and Pollution Control, School of Energy and Environmental Engineering, Hebei University of Technology, Tianjin 300071, PR China.


Preparation of sulfur cathode:
CMK-3 and sulfur powers with a weight ratio of 3:7 were added in 20 mL CS2 solutions and then supersonic vibration until the CS2 solution evaporates completely. The mixture was heated 160 °C for 17 h in tube furnace under Ar atmosphere to get S@CMK-3 composites. As-prepared composites, Super P and PVDF were mixed (weight ratio of 8:1:1) in NMP solution to form a slurry. Then, the slurry was coated on carbon cloth and then dried in an oven at 60 °C for 12 h. The mass loadings of sulfur were 2.8 mg cm−2 ~ 7.3 mg cm−2. From Fig. S8, the sulfur content in S@CMK-3 cathodes was tested to be ~68.6 wt% by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA).
Adsorption Tests of Li2S6 solution and CoCp2 mediators:
5.0 mM Li2S6 solution was obtained by dissolving Li2S and S (molar ratio of 1:5) into DME and DOL (1:1 vol) solution under vigorous stirring at 80°C for 24 h. 20 mg as-prepared heterogeneous mediators were added into 5.0 mL Li2S6 solution. The adsorption tests of CoCp2 mediators are similar to Li2S6 solution.
Assembly of Symmetric Cells:
Symmetric cells were assembled with two same electrodes of as-prepared heterogeneous mediators. 0.2 M Li2S6 solution (in DME/DOL) solution containing 1.0 M LiTFSI and 1 wt % LiNO3 or 20 mM CoCp2 was used as the electrolyte. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests were carried out at scan rates of 0.5 mV s–1 and 20 mV s–1 on the CHI760E electrochemical workstation between −1.0 V and 1.0 V. 
LSV Tests: 
LSV was performed in a three-electrode cell. As-prepared heterogeneous mediators coated on glassy carbon as the working electrode, Pt as the counter electrode, Ag/AgCl electrode as the reference, and 0.1 M Li2S/methanol solution with or without CoCp2 mediators as the electrolyte. The three-electrode cell was measured at electrochemical workstation at a scan rate of 0.5 mV s−1 between −0.7 and −0.1 V.
Electrochemical Measurements: 
The electrolyte was 1.0 M LiTFSI and 1 wt % LiNO3 dissolved in the mixture of DOL and DME (1:1 ratio by volume). As-prepared sulfur cathode as cathode, Li foil as anode and as-prepared heterogeneous mediators as interlayer materials coated on Celgard 2400 separator were assembled into 2032-type coin cells. The electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) was kept at ~12 μL mg-1 or 4.5 μL mg-1 per cell. The cycling performance was tested in an electrochemical window of 1.7 to 2.8 V (versus Li/Li+) at different current densities (1 C = 1675 mA g−1) on LANHE-CT2001A. The gravimetric specific capacities and areal capacity were calculated based on sulfur. Cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) in the range of 10 mHz–100 kHz measurements were carried out on electrochemical workstation (CHI760E). 
Nucleation and Dissolution of Li2S 
0.5 M Li2S8 solution was obtained by dissolving Li2S and S (molar ratio of 1:7) in tetraethylene glycol solvent under vigorous stirring at 60°C for 48 h. As-prepared heterogeneous mediators, Super P and PVDF (weight ratio of 8:1:1) were dispersed in NMP and coating on the Al foil with a diameter of 12 mm and dried at 60 °C for 12 h to be used as cathode. 25 μL blank electrolyte or CoCp2 mediators containing electrolyte was added into the Li side and 25 μL Li2S8 solution were added into the cathode side. The cell was discharged galvanostatically to 2.06 V at 0.112 mA, and then discharged potentiostatically at 2.05 V until current decreased to 10-5 A. To investigate the dissolution of Li2S, fresh cells were first discharged at a current of 0.10 mA to 1.80 V, and subsequently discharged at 0.01 mA to 1.80 V until full conversion of LiPSs into solid Li2S. Then, the cells were potentiostatically charged at 2.40 V for the dissolution of Li2S into LiPSs until charge current was below 10-5 A. 
Materials Characterization: 
The morphology and microstructure of the prepared heterogeneous mediators were characterized by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, XL30 ESEM-FEG, USA) at 10.0 kV, transmission electron microscope (TEM, ARM200F, Japan), HRTEM (JEOL, JEM–1200 EX), and a High-angle annular dark field-scanning transmission electron microscopy (TECNAI G2F20). The N2 adsorption-desorption data was measured at 77 K using a 30 Micromeritics ASAP 2020 C to determine the specific surface area, pore volume and pore size distribution. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy was performed by Thermo escalab 250Xi spectrometer with a base pressure better than 5×10-7 Pa for analysis. X-ray powder diffraction (XRD, Ultima IV-185) patterns were collected with CuKα radiation and Raman spectroscopy (LabRAM HR Evolution, France) were collected with 473 nm laser source. UV-vis spectroscopy of the solutions was collected by an ultraviolet and visible spectrophotometer (UV-3600). 
Computational Methods:
All the DFT calculations were conducted based on the Vienna Ab-inito Simulation Package (VASP) [S1, S2]. The exchange-correlation effects were described by the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) method [S3, S4]. The core-valence interactions were accounted by the projected augmented wave (PAW) method [S5]. The energy cutoff for plane wave expansions was set to 480 eV, and the 3×3×1 Monkhorst-Pack grid k-points were selected to sample the Brillouin zone integration. The vacuum space is adopted 15 Å above the surfaces to avoid periodic interactions. The structural optimization was completed for energy and force convergence set at 1.0×10-4 eV and 0.02 eV Å-1, respectively.
