C/S composite cathode 
The carbon-sulfur composite was synthesized using the melt-in-diffusion method. Sulfur powder and porous carbon powder (Ketjen Black (KB) 600JD) were mixed at 7:3 wt % and homogenized using a mortar for over 20 minutes. The mixture was placed in a Teflon container, which was then purged with argon gas using a glove box and subsequently sealed. The sealed container, filled with argon gas, was placed inside a stainless-steel autoclave and heated at 155 ℃ for 12 hours. After cooling to room temperature in air, the resulting C(KB)/S powder was further ground using a mortar to obtain the final composite powder.

< Approximate Composition of DMI/DEE Electrolytes Exhibiting Local Phase Separation>
In case of the DEE:DMI ratio of 1:1 and an E/S ratio of 50, we simulated the condition in which LiPSs are fully dissolved by preparing an electrolyte with the same DEE:DMI ratio (1:1) containing LiPSs. The corresponding change in the DEE:DMI ratio was then monitored using Raman spectroscopy. As shown in Fig. S19, the fraction of free DMI decreases markedly upon LiPS dissolution. Quantitatively, the ratio of Ifree-DMI/Ifree-DEE is approximately 11 in the absence of LiPSs, but decreases to 1.7 after LiPS dissolution. Considering that the initial 1:1 mixture corresponds to a ratio of 11, back-calculation indicates that the ratio of DEE:DMI shifts to ~6.5:1 (Table S4). This value is sufficient to induce phase separation, consistent with our experimental observation that phase separation occurs when DEE:DMI = 6:4 (i.e., 60% DEE in Fig. S10).
Based on this back-calculation, when the initial DEE:DMI ratio is 2:8, complete dissolution of LiPSs during discharge would shift the DEE:DMI ratio to ~1.6:1 (Table S5). This corresponds to 61.5% DEE, which is expected to induce phase separation under these conditions.
