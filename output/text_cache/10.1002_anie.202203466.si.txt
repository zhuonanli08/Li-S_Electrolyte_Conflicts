Section 1: Materials & Methods 
1.1 Materials 
The following chemicals were used as received without any treatment. Styrene (99%, Sigma-Aldrich, 
contains 4-tert-butylcatechol as stabilizer), potassium persulfate (K2S2O8, 99 wt%, Sigma-Aldrich), sodium 
hydroxide pellets (99%, VWR), zinc nitrate hexahydrate (Zn(NO3)2·6H2O, 99%, Sigma-Aldrich), cobalt 
nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO3)2·6H2O, 99% Sigma-Aldrich), 2-methylimidazole (99%, Sigma-Aldrich), 
methanol (99%, Scharlau), ethanol (99%, Scharlau), tetrahydrofuran (stabilized with 250 ppm BHT, GR 
grade, Duksan), polyvinylpyrrolidone (40 k, TCI), ammonia (28%, VWR), thiourea (99%, Sigma-Aldrich), 
Sulfur powder (reagent grade, ~100 mesh particle size, Sigma-Aldrich), Selenium powder (~100 
mesh, >99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich).  
 
 

<<PAGE 4>>
1.2 Methods 
1.2.1 Synthesis of three-dimensional polystyrene (3d PS) template 
In a typical synthesis, styrene was firstly washed by 10 wt.% NaOH solution to remove the stabilizer. 
Afterward, a solution prepared by adding 65 mL washed styrene and 2.5 g polyvinylpyrrolidone in 500 mL 
deionized water was bubbled with N2 for 30 min, and the mixture was further fluxed at 75 ˚C under N2 for 
another 30 min to get the solution A. The solution B was prepared by dissolving 1.0 g K2S2O8 in 50 mL 
deionized water under stirring. Then solution B was injected into solution A, and after stirring at 75 ˚C for 
24 h, the white PS nanosphere can be collected by centrifuging. The white product was then transferred 
into a 60 ˚C oven to remove the remaining solvent. After collecting the PS nanosphere, 5.0 g PS was fully 
dispersed in 50 mL ethanol by ultrasonication to get the suspension solution, which was then filtered under 
vacuum to get the 3d PS template. The as-prepared 3d PS template was further dried in a 60 ˚ C oven 
overnight to remove the solvent.  
1.2.2 Synthesis of the ordered macroporous sulfur host (OMSH) 
In a typical synthesis, the precursor solution was prepared by dissolving 3.62 g Zn(NO3)2∙6H2O, 0.9 g 
Co(NO3)2∙6H2O, and 3.75 g 2-methylimidazole in 20 mL methanol. After degassing under vacuum for 10 
min, the 3d PS template was soaked into the precursor solution for 1.0 h. Then, the 3d PS template was 
dried and further degassed under vacuum for 30 min to remove the solvent and gas. Afterward, the 3d PS 
template was soaked in the solution prepared by mixing 10 mL methanol and 10 mL NH3∙H2O for 24 h. 
Then, the sample was dried in a 60 ˚ C oven under vacuum overnight to remove the solvent, and the 
fragments were added into 100 mL tetrahydrofuran solvent for 6 h to remove the 3d PS template. Next, the 
3d ordered macroporous zeolitic imidazolate framework (3d OMZIF) can be collected by centrifuge and 
washed with tetrahydrofuran several times. The 3d OMZIF was then heated at 800 ˚C under N2 for 2 h with 
a heating rate of 5 ˚C min-1. Then, the black powder and 5.0 g thiourea were placed in a tube furnace and 
were heated at 800 ˚C under N2 for another 1 h with the heating rate of 5 ˚C min-1 to the OMSH material. 

<<PAGE 5>>
1.2.3 Preparation of OMSH-Se/S cathode composite 
The preparation of the OMSH-Se/S cathode was based on a molten diffusion method. Typically, 0.1 g 
OMSH, 0.04 g Se powder, and 0.36 g S powder were ground into a fine powder mixture. Then, the mixture 
was transferred into an Ar-protected autoclave and heated in a 155 ˚C oven for 12 h to get the OMSH-Se/S 
cathode composite.  
1.2.4 Characterization methods 
The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) characterizations were conducted on a JEOL-6700 SEM 
instrument with an accelerating voltage of 10.0 kV. The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and the 
corresponding energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) were conducted on a JEOL-2010 TEM 
instrument at 200 kV. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) characterizations were conducted on 
Physical Electronics PHI 5600 multi-technique system. The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) 
characterization was conducted on a TGA Q5000 instrument in the temperature range of 25-450 ˚C with a 
heating rate of 1˚C min-1. Time-of-Flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) characterizations 
were conducted on a TOF.SIMS 5 (IONTOF GmbH) instrument and the imaged area was 30 µm × 30 µm. 
After cycling, the cells were disassembled inside an Ar-filled glove box to collect the cycled electrodes. 
Meanwhile, the cycled electrodes were sealed inside a vacuum sealing box to avoid the contamination of 
moisture and then transferred into the ToF-SIMS equipment. The in situ high-energy X-ray diffraction 
(HEXRD) characterization was conducted at beamline 17-BM-B of the Advanced Photon Source at 
Argonne National Laboratory, and the wavelength of the X-ray was 0.45185 Å. The two-dimensional 
patterns were calibrated using a standard LaB6 sample and converted to one-dimensional patterns using the 
GSAS-Ⅱ software[1]. The in situ X-ray absorption fine structure spectroscopy of the Se K-edge was 
conducted at beamline 20-BM-B. The X-ray absorption spectra data were processed with the Athena and 
Artemis software package.  
1.2.5 Electrochemical test methods 

<<PAGE 6>>
The cathode slurry was prepared by mixing the OMSH-Se/S composite, Ketjenblack, and polyvinylidene 
fluoride binder based on the weight ratio of 8:1:1 in a suitable amount of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone solvent. 
The slurry was then cast on aluminum foil and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 ˚C overnight. The CR-2032 
coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box, using lithium metal foil as the anode, Celgard 2400 as 
the separator, and the OMSH-Se/S as the cathode. The 2-dimethoxyethane (DME)-based electrolyte used 
in this work was composed of 1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI) in 1:1 v/v 1,3-
dioxolane (DOL)/DME with 0.1 M lithium nitrate additive. The 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-
tetrafluoropropyl ether (HFE)-based electrolyte used in this work was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in 1:1 
v/v DOL/HFE with 0.1 M lithium nitrate additive. The electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio of all coin cells was 
controlled to be 10 µL mgs-1. The Li-Se/S half cells were galvanostatic cycled within the voltage range of 
1.2-3.0 V at different currents on the Neware CT-4008W testing system. The cyclic voltammograms test 
was carried out on a Solartron Analytical 1470 System between 1.2 and 3.0 V (vs. Li/Li+) at 0.1 mV s-1. 
1.2.6 Pouch cell assembly methods 
The slurry was prepared by mixing 62.5 mg OMSH-Se/S, 7.8 mg Ketjenblack, and 7.8 mg polyvinylidene 
fluoride binder in a suitable amount of NMP solution, which was cast on a 30 mm × 40 mm aluminum foil. 
After drying in a 60 ˚C oven under vacuum overnight, an Al tab was riveted on the Al foil, and a Ni tab 
was riveted on the 30 mm × 40 mm Cu foil. The pouch cell was assembled in an Ar-filled glove box, and a 
piece of 30 mm × 40 mm Li metal foil with a thickness of 200 µm (or 40 µm) was rolled on the Cu foil as 
the anode. Then a layer of Celgard 2400 separator was put between the cathode and anode, and a calculated 
amount of electrolyte was injected. Afterward, the pouch cell was sealed under vacuum.
