Li||Li cycling at higher current density
The potential transients for Li stripping and deposition in symmetrical cells at higher current densities (0.5 mA cm-2) are shown on Fig. S1. The experiment was stopped if the signal became noisy, indicating internal micro short-circuits, which happened after 6.5 cycles for the electrolyte and lowest catholyte concentration and 20.5 cycles for 0.1 M catholyte solution. For the highest polysulfide concentration tested, cell failure was reached in the first cycle and the transient is not shown.


Fig. S1: Potentials during stripping and deposition experiment on Li||Li symmetrical cells using different concentrations of polysulfides added to the electrolyte solution. All cells were assembled with two 2 cm2 Li electrodes, one layer of Celgard 2320 separator, and 20 µL of different concentrations of polysulfides in 1 M LiTFSI in TEGDME:DOL 1:1 (v:v) supporting electrolyte. Stripping and deposition experiment was carried out at 0.5 mA cm-2 current density, each half cycle to the capacity of 2 mAh. 

Coulombic efficiency of stripping and deposition of Li (Li||SS cells)
In order to determine the Coulombic efficiency of stripping and deposition of Li, Li||Cu cells are usually used, although they could not be applied here, since copper is known to react with polysulfides[1–4], which would change the composition and response of such cells. Instead of copper, stainless steel discs were used, as proposed elsewhere[5]. Unfortunately, the Li||SS cells did not reach the performance evident from Li||Li symmetrical cells, since the signals showed micro short-circuits in early cycling. Only the data up to the cell failure are therefore shown below (Fig. S2).
Fig. S2a shows the first stripping of Li from the Li anode and deposition onto the stainless steel surface. The cells assembled with catholyte solutions exhibit a plateau around 2.0 V vs. Li/Li+, indicating formation of Li2S from the polysulfides present. The overpotential in the negative part of this half cycle follows the same trend as seen from Li||Li symmetrical cells’ electrochemical response (Fig. 1). Cell death first occurred in the cell with no polysulfides, with the performance increasing towards the cell with the highest polysulfide concentration (Fig. S2b). For this cell, the efficiency could also be determined to be in the range of 75% (Fig. S2c).


Fig. S2: Coulombic efficiency determination for Li||SS stripping and deposition in different polysulfide concentrations. The cell setup was the same as in Li||Li symmetrical cells with the exception of replacing one of the Li anodes with a 2 cm2 SS disc. The current used was 0.1 mA cm-2, the discharge was limited with time (2 h) while the charge part of the cycling was limited with the potential of 1 V vs. Li/Li+. a) and b) show the potential transients for the first discharge and first cycle, respectively, while c) shows the calculated Coulombic efficiency.
Post-mortem analysis of Li||Li cells
After 60 cycles of stripping and deposition experiment and impedance measurements (Figs. 2, 3), the cells were disassembled inside the glove-box. Apart from the obvious differences in the colour of the separators, the analysis also shows that the electrolyte and the experiment with the lowest polysulfide concentration produce Li deposits, which are easily peeled away from the electrodes (Fig. S3, shown as grey spots on the separators). This indicates a presence of a substantial amount of dead lithium.


Fig. S3: Disassembled cells with different concentrations of polysulfides in the catholyte after 60 cycles of stripping and deposition at 0.1 mA cm-2 and impedance spectroscopy analysis (Figs. 2, 3).

Morphology of Li deposits in the cell cycled with 1.0 M Li2S8 catholyte
The surface of the Li electrode, which underwent stripping and deposition in a cell with a highly concentrated polysulfide solution showed no signs of dendrite growth. The morphology differed from spots, where wrinkling of the surface occurred (Fig. 4, Fig.S4 – red rectangle bracket) and spots, where small porous growths were evident (Fig. S4 - blue and yellow rectangles). Those porous growths were sensitive to the electron beam and would begin to melt away at higher voltages or prolonged scanning, which, together with resemblance to already published reports on Li2S deposit morphology[6,7], indicated those are not dendrites, but rather short-chain polysulfide precipitations.


Fig. S4: Li surface morphology after stripping and deposition in 1.0 M Li2S8 catholyte solution showing no dendritic growth and short chain polysulfide precipitation on the electrode surface.

Morphology of Li surface after pre-treatment with 1.0 M Li2S8 catholyte
Similar to the extended cycling regime, short term cycling of the Li electrodes in the highly concentrated catholyte solution did not exhibit any dendritic growth (Fig. S5a and b). To provide a clear difference between the observed Li2S deposits and dendritic growth, a Li||Li symmetrical cell with 1.0 M Li2S8 catholyte was discharged for one half-cycle with a 0.1 mA cm-2 current for a 5-times extended time period (10 h instead of 2 h). As evident from Figs. S5c and d, the working electrode of this cell exhibited some cases of dendritic growth with Li2S deposits surrounding said Li formations, clearly showing the difference in morphology.


Fig. S5: a) and b) different magnifications of the Li surface after pre-treatment with 1.0 M Li2S8 (10 full cycles at 0.1 mA cm-2 density with each half cycle lasting 2 h); c) and d) different magnifications of a Li working electrode taken from a Li||Li symmetrical cell built with 1.0 M Li2S8 onto which Li was deposited with a current density of 0.1 mA cm-2 for 10 hours.

Comparison of Li metal anode performance with or without pre-treatment
As discussed with Fig. 5b, a more correct comparison between the non-treated electrode and the high polysulfide concentration pre-treated electrode would be to perform 10 cycles of pre-treatment in an electrolyte not containing polysulfides. Since the pre-treatment was conducted with the same current density as the extended stripping and deposition experiment, important data could be extracted from Fig. 5b itself. As shown in Fig. S6, the curve showing the performance of the non-treated lithium can be shifted for 10 cycles, which now account for the pre-treatment. Although this data treatment does not account for disassembly of the cell, one can deduce that the cell disassembly and reassembly would most likely only damage the electrode surface, slightly hindering its performance.


Fig. S6: Investigation of lithium pre-treatment with high concentration of polysulfide solution: extended stripping and deposition experiment at 0.1 mA cm-2, showing a comparison in performance between pre-treated Li and Li without pre-treatment




Coulombic efficiency of stripping and deposition of Li (Li||Cu cells)
Since pre-treatment of Li  with 1.0 M Li2S8 allowed for exclusion of polysulfides from subsequent performance testing, the Coulombic efficiency determination was attempted on these cells using the more standard cell configuration of Li||Cu cells (as explained earlier copper could not be used in polysulfide containing cells due to Cu2S and CuS formation[1–4]).
Although cycling could be extended in comparison to Li||SS cells (Fig.S2), long term performance still could not be evaluated due to short-circuits and cell death. As shown on Fig. S7, efficiency of the cell after pre-treatment was improved, although the average change was from 65% efficiency to 68% efficiency, resulting in only a 5% improvement.


Fig. S7: Coulombic efficiency determination for Li||Cu stripping and deposition in 1 M LiTFSI in TEGDME:DOL 1:1 (v:v) electrolyte with and without pre-treatment with a high concentration of polysulfides. The cell setup was the same as in Li||Li symmetrical cells with the exception of replacing one of the Li anodes with a 2 cm2 Cu disc. The current used was 0.1 mA cm-2, the discharge was limited with time (2 h) while the charge part of the cycling was limited with the potential of 1 V vs. Li/Li+.
