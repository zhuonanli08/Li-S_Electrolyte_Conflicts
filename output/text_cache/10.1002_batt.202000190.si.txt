1. Determination of maximum polysulfide (PS) solubility with UV-Vis spectroscopy 
Electrolyte preparation was performed in an argon filled glovebox (H2O and O2 < 0.01 ppm). The 
electrolyte preparation of the electrolytes without PS is described in the main part of the publication. To 
achieve saturation of the different electrolytes with PS, 0.5 M Li2S (Sigma-Aldrich, Inc.) and 0.4375 M 
S8 (Sigma-Aldrich, Inc.) (= 0.5 M Li2S8) were mixed with the electrolytes without PS and stirred for 
4 days at 50 °C. Afterwards, the remaining solids were filtered out with a syringe filter (PTFE, 0.2 µm, 
Carl Roth GmbH + Co. KG). 
For determination of maximum PS solubility, one calibration and one dilution series per electrolyte was 
prepared. The calibration solutions were produced by mixing a stock solution of 0.1 M Li2S8 (Li2S + S8) 
in tetramethylene sulfone (TMS, Kishida Chemical Co., Ltd.), a stock solution of 5.5 M lithium 
bis(trifuoromethane)sulfonimide (LiTFSI, Gotion, Inc.) in TMS, 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-
tetrafluoropropyl ether (TTE, Daikin Chemical Europe GmbH) and pure TMS and stirring overnight. 
Because in some electrolytes (especially with high TTE content) Li2S8 is not the most stable modification, 
sometimes elemental sulfur partially precipitated. In these cases, the precipitation was filtered with a 
syringe filter (PTFE, 0.2 µm).  
Due to the many balanced polysulfide modifications and the indeterminability of the individual 
concentrations, the concentration of polysulfides should be indicated as concentration of Li2Sx. A 
presentation of the different solutions with adjusted PS concentrations for the calibration series can be 
found in Table S1. These series were used to calculate a correlation between PS concentration and 
absorbance.

All solutions were characterized by UV-Vis spectroscopy. Therefore, a quartz glass cuvette (High 
Performance, Hellma GmbH & Co. KG) with an optical path length of 1 mm was filled with 
approximately 300 µl solution in an argon filled glovebox and sealed with parafilm (Bemis Company, 
Inc.) and carried immediately in an argon filled box to the UV-Vis spectrometer. The measurements were 
performed with the UV-Vis spectrometer Cary 5000 (Agilent Technologies, Inc.) in a wave length range 
between 300 nm and 800 nm. UV-Vis spectra of all solutions are depicted in Figure S1. Based on the 
Lambert-Beer law, the absorbance at a wavelength is directly proportional to the concentration. Therefore 
it is possible to calculate a calibration curve and then determine the concentration in an unknown sample. 
For TMS / TTE (1:1) and TMS / TTE (2:1) the wavelength for calculation (395 nm and 410 nm 
respectively) was chosen based on a shoulder in the absorption spectra. In these regions the absorbance 
variation is smaller and therefore a determination more precisely. For TMS / TTE (4:1) and TMS, this 
procedure could not be applied due to the higher PS concentration and therefore higher absorbance in 
these electrolytes. It would be possible to dilute the solutions more, but this would make the concentration 
determination less accurate. Therefore, 460 nm (TMS / TTE (4:1)) and 520 nm (TMS) was used as 
wavelengths for calculation.

5. Operando transmission X-ray microscopy (TXM) 
To study the Li growth behavior in the electrolyte of TMS / TTE with polysulfides under low current 
density and micro-scale down to several micrometer, operando TXM visualization was conducted using a 
Li/Li plastic cell and discharged with a current of 0.2 mA cm-2 for 1 h similar to our previous work[1]. 
Figure S11 shows the initial state and the state after Li plating, the operando TXM Li plating process can 
be found in Video V1. The Li foil on the right-hand side of Figure S11a shows lighter contrast due to less 
X-ray absorption of metallic Li, and darker color on the left referring to the electrolyte, respectively. It is 
found that the surface of the Li foil is rather rough and corrugated at the initial state. After Li deposition, 
the plated Li shows mossy and filament structure with porosity between each other, which is similar to 
the SEM image of lithium electrode cycled in TMS / TTE (1:1) + PS. 
 
Figure S11: Mosaic image of Li surface at the (a) initial state and (b) after Li plating process using Li/Li plastic cell and TMS/TTE 
(1:1) with PS as electrolyte. 
Cells with TMS / TTE (1:1) electrolyte without PS were also tested and a nonuniform, porous deposition 
can be found (Figure S12 and Video V2) which is in good accordance with the SEM images.  
 
 
Figure S12: Mosaic image of Li surface at the (a) initial state, (b) after Li plating, and (c) after Li stripping process using Li/Cu 
plastic cell and TMS / TTE (1:1) without PS as electrolyte. The white scale bar at the left bottom of each image is 10 µm.

7. Operando Raman spectroscopy  
To further investigate the effect of PS additives in TMS / TTE (1:1) on SEI formation mechanism, 
operando Raman spectroscopy was performed. Since a SEI is instantaneously formed when electrolyte 
contacts with metallic Li due to its low reduction potential, the set-up is adopted, and a Li/Cu cell was 
employed to create fresh surface for SEI formation and monitor the chemical composition evolution of Cu 
surface upon cathodic potential sweep. The results are shown in Figure S19. The Raman spectra of Li/Cu 
cell using TMS / TTE (1:1) without PS additive show consistent features along the potential sweeping 
from OCV to -0.5 V. The bands at 276 and 742 cm-1 can be assigned to the vibration frequency of 

<<PAGE 12>>
11 
LiTFSI. Meanwhile, the Raman signals at 310, 389, 441, 675, 871, and 968 cm-1 can be attributed to 
TMS.  
 
 
Figure S19: Operando Raman spectra upon cathodic potential sweep in a Li/Cu plastic cell using (a) TMS/TTE (1:1) and (b) 
TMS/TTE (1:1) with PS as electrolyte. 
However, as for the Li/Cu cell using TMS / TTE (1:1) with PS, a broad band evolves during the potential 
range from 1.7 V to 1.0 V at ~290 cm-1 and not two clearly distinguishable peaks, like in the electrolyte 
without PS. This peak may be related to formation of long-chain polysulfide S62- as calculated from the 
previous work[2]. Nevertheless, it should be noted that polysulfides with different chain length have 
complex signals between 100~500 cm-1 and it is challenging to assign this Raman band to a specific 
polysulfide species without additional investigation. In addition, a black layer is grown during this 
potential region as shown in the digital images in Figure S20, suggesting the potential formation of a 
passivation layer from the reactions between polysulfides and Cu or polysulfides and Li. In addition, CuS 
might emerge; however, both the Raman signals of CuS at 270 and 474 cm-1 are not observed during the 
measurements. Furthermore, Raman signals decrease after passing through 0.5 V, also indicating that the 
Raman scattering from the Cu surface is inhibited by the passivation layer. To conclude, the addition of 
PS in TMS/TTE (1:1) leads to the formation of a different SEI or passivation layer, with characteristic

signatures in the Raman spectra, but further investigation needs to be carried out to unambiguously 
identify the chemical composition using complementary techniques.
