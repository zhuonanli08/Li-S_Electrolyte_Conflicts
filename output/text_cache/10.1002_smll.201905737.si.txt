Figure S10. HOMO-LUMO energies of DOL, DME, and TTE. XPS analysis of lithium metal 
immersed in TTE solvent after 10 second Ar+ etching to remove the surface layer oxidized by air.  
 
To verify the source of SEI component, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and 
highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) was calculated (Figure S10a). It is widely known that 
lower LUMO suggests the molecular is good electron acceptor, and the higher HOMO suggest the 
molecular is hard to be oxidized. The LUMO of DME and TTE is close to each other means they 
have the same tendency to decompose on the surface of lithium metal. For further verification, 
lithium metal was immersed in TTE solvent for 2 hours at ambient temperature. After 10 s Ar+ 
etching to remove the surface layer oxidized by air before the lithium metal was moved into the XPS 
chamber,  XPS analysis was carried on to detect the surface component of li metal. It can be seen 
from Figure S10 b-d, the C-C/C-H, -CF3, LiF, LiCO3, Li2O, and organic O-C-O peaks appear on the 

<<PAGE 16>>
surface of Li metal surface, almost the same with Li metal anode from the cycled Li/Li cells using 
TDE electrolyte. The results of HOMO-LUMO calculation and XPS analysis prove that TTE can 
participates the SEI formation. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

<<PAGE 17>>
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure S11. a) Long term cycling performance of Li/Li symmetric cells using TDE electrolyte 
without LiNO3 additive. EIS analysis of Li/Li cells using TDE electrolyte during cycling, b) the 
overpotential changes at first several cycles; c-h) EIS plots of Li/Li symmetric cells at different 
cycles.  
The TDE electrolyte contains tiny amount of LiNO3 additive (0.025M), as is shown in Figure S11 a, 
the Li/Li cells using LiNO3-free electrolyte shows stable voltage profiles, almost without obvious 
change during cycling. For the TDE electrolyte contains tiny amount of LiNO3 (Figure S11 b), the 
EIS plot of Li/Li symmetric cells was tested at different cycles, as is shown in Figure S11 c-h, it was 
demonstrated that during the platting/stripping process, the overpotential of Li/Li cells continues to 

<<PAGE 18>>
decrease and finally stabilizes at a certain value. When the overpotential continues to decrease and 
stabilizes at around 8 mV, there are still two arcs in the impedance spectrum, thus this couldn’t be 
internal short-circuit. The voltage profiles change in the initial cycles can caused by the decomposing 
of tiny amount of LiNO3 in the TDE electrolyte. During the initial several cycles, LiNO3 can be 
reduced to LiNxOy, Li2O species, and fast ion conductor such as Li3N and Li3N derivatives might be 
generated, thus the impedance of electrolyte/electrode surface can be decreased. Besides, the 
decreasing polarization during Li+ platting/stripping of Li-Cu cells using TDE electrolyte (Figure S4, 
Figure S5) is also a strong proof of the SEI layer generating process. Previous reports[6] 
demonstrated that the presence of LiNO3 could remarkably decrease the impedance of Li electrodes, 
which is well consistent with our results.

Figure S12. Long term cycling performance at 60 oC and the corresponding SEM morphology of 
Li/Li symmetric cells using a,b) LS002 electrolyte and c,d) TDE electrolyte. 
The cycling of Li/Li symmetric cells at 60 oC using TDE and LS002 electrolyte is shown in Figure 
S12. As we can see from the plot, the overpotential at 60 oC is much lower than that of room 
temperature, which could be ascribed to the the lower electrolyte viscosity enables better 
electrolyte/electrode contact and faster charge transfer kinetics at elevated temperature[7].

electrolyte burns severely.
