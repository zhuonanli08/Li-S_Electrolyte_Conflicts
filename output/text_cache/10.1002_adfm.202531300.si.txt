1. Experimental Section
Chemical reagent
In this work, NH4I was purchased from Hebei Bailing Wei Super Fine Material Co., Ltd. S8 was purchased from Merck Co., Ltd. Porous carbon was purchased from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. 1-methyl-2- pyrrolidinone was purchased from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. The base electrolyte was purchased from Aladdin Reagent (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. All reagents were analytical-grade pure and were used as received without further purification.
Preparation of S/C cathode
Porous carbon and sulfur are mixed according to the weight ratio of 3:7 and ground evenly. Then the mixture is heated in the muffle furnace under 155 ℃ for 12 h to fully immerse sulfur in to mesoporous carbon, and the S/C composite material is obtained. The 70% S/C composite material, acetylene black (Canrd) and polyvinylidene fluoride (Canrd) are added into 1-methyl-2- pyrrolidinone (Aladdin, 99%) according to the weight ratio of 7:2:1, and fully stirred to prepare a uniform slurry. Subsequently, the slurry is coated on aluminum foil and dried in an oven at 60 ℃ for 12 h. Finally, the S/C cathode can be obtained by cutting the dried electrode into a disc with a diameter of 1.33 cm2 with a tablet press. 
Preparation of the targeted electrolyte
The base electrolyte is 1 M lithium bis (trifluoromethanesulfony) imide (LiTFSI, Aladdin, 99.9%) and 1 wt.% LiNO3 (Aladdin, 99.9%) in the mixture solvent with 1, 2-dimethoxythane (DME, Aladdin, 99.5%) and 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL, Aladdin, 99.5%) (v/ v = 1: 1). The electrolyte with NH4I, (jkchemical, 99%) can be obtained by adding NH4I in the above base electrolyte according to the weight ratio of 0.1 wt.%, 0.25 wt.%, 0.5 wt.% and 1 wt.%.
Material characterization
The morphologies of samples were characterized by field-emission scanning electron microscopy images (SEM, SIGMA 500) and X-ray photoelectron spectrum (XPS, PHI 5000 Versa Probe, Japan) was obtained to characterize the compositions of the samples. Raman spectra were recorded on a Thermo Fischer DXR with 532 nm diode laser excitation at room temperature. The transformation mechanism of LiPSs during discharge was studied by in-situ ultraviolet-visible spectroscopy (PE lambda 750).
In-situ ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) spectra measurement: The preparation of sulfur-containing cathode materials is the same as before and then cut into rectangles (0.5 cm×1 cm). The sulfur area mass loading on the electrode was ≈1.6mg cm−2. In an Ar full-filled glove box, the in-situ UV-vis cells were assembled using pristine S/C composite electrode as the cathode, and Li metal as the anode, using self-made in-situ test tubes. Then further sealed in Li-S electrolyte (3 mL), for the experimental group, the electrolyte contained NH4I, whereas the control group used electrolyte without NH4I. The in-situ cuvette cell was galvanostatically discharged at 0.1 C on an electrochemical workstation. UV-vis spectra were recorded every 10 min with a wavelength range of 200-700 nm from the beginning of the discharge until the end of the discharge for batteries.
Electrochemical measurements
The CR2032 coin batteries are assembled in a glove box, S/C composite as the cathode, polypropylene as the diaphragm and lithium sheet as the anode. The electrolyte is added at the electrolyte-sulfur (E/S) ratio of 20:1. After standing 12 h at 25 ℃, the batteries are cycled in 1.70-3.00 V versus Li/Li+ and conducted GITT test on the Neware test system. The batteries for observing the deposition morphology of Li2S directly takes Li2S8 in the electrolyte as active materials (12 μL-0.1 M-Li2S8 +10 μL-electrolyte with/without NH4I), carbon paper as cathode collector, and carries out constant current discharge at 0.1 C in the Neware test system. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements in a voltage range 1.7-3.0 V (vs. Li/Li+) at different scanning rates (0.1 mV/s, 0.2 mV/s, 0.3 mV/s, 0.4 mV/s, 0.5 mV/s) are performed on electrochemical workstation in a voltage range.
Theoretical calculations
All density functional theory (DFT) calculations were executed using Gaussian 16 software.[1] Geometry optimizations were systematically conducted at the B3LYP[2] /def2tzvp[3,4] level of theory, incorporating Grimme’s DFT-D3 dispersion correction[5-7] to account for van der Waals interactions. Convergence criteria were set to default thresholds for force constants (<0.00045 Hartree/Bohr) and nuclear displacements (<0.0018 Å). Subsequent harmonic vibrational frequency calculations at the same theoretical level confirmed structural stability by verifying the absence of imaginary frequencies (ν > 0 cm⁻¹), thereby ensuring all optimized geometries represent true local minima on the potential energy surface. To maintain theoretical consistency, single-point energy evaluations were performed on the optimized geometries using the identical B3LYP/def2tzvp methodology.
The binding energy (BE) was quantified via the energy difference equation:
 BE=E complex − ( Ea +Eb )
where E complex denotes the total energy of the supramolecular complex, while Ea and Eb correspond to the individual energies of the isolated components in their optimized geometries.
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations for the solvation structure of non-electrolytes were conducted by using the Gromacs.[8] The forced field parameters were obtained from OPLS force fields. The velocity-verlet method was used as an integral method and the integration time step was 1 fs.[9] A cutoff radius of 15 Å was applied in the MD simulations of vdW and electrostatic interactions. The standard periodic boundary condition was used in all simulations. Particle-particle particle-mesh (PPPM) solver was applied to describe the long electrostatic interactions.[10] First, energy minimization was performed to obtain a stable system. After minimization, each system was then equilibrated under the NPT ensemble at a constant temperature of 298.15 K for 5 ns. Finally, each system was simulated for the last 2000 ps under NVT ensemble for data collection. Nosé–Hoover thermostat/barostat algorithm was used to control the temperature and pressure of each system in the NPT and NVT ensemble.[11,12]
