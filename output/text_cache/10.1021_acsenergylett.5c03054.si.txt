EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Preparation of electrolytes
Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI, battery grade) was obtained from Nippon Shokubai and 
dried overnight at approximately 100 ºC under vacuum prior to use. 1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME, 
battery grade) was purchased from Gotion and used as received. 1,1-Difluoro-2-(2-
methoxyethoxy)ethane (DFE), 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (TTE, 
99%), bis(2,2,2-trifluoroethyl) ether (BTFE, 97%), and 1,2-bis(1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethoxy)ethane 
(BTFEE, 97%) were acquired from SynQuest Laboratories. These solvents and diluents were dried 
using preactivated molecular sieves until their moisture content was confirmed to be less than 20 
ppm via Karl Fischer titration. Localized high-concentration electrolytes (LHCEs) were prepared 
stepwise following the specific molar ratios provided in the manuscript (1Salt-1Solvent-2Diluent). 
Initially, the LiFSI salt was dissolved in the solvating solvent (either DFE or DME) until fully 
dissolved, after which the diluent was added to complete the preparation. All procedures were 
conducted inside an MBraun glovebox filled with purified argon (Ar), maintaining oxygen and 
moisture levels below 0.1 ppm.
Preparation of SPAN
Polyacrylonitrile (PAN, Sigma-Aldrich) and sulfur powder (325 mesh, Alfa Aesar) were mixed at 
a mass ratio of 1:4 by ball milling. The mixture was then heated at 450 °C for 6 h under an argon 
atmosphere. The obtained product was subsequently heat-treated at 250 °C for 24 h under argon 
to remove residual sulfur, yielding sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN). 
Electrochemical tests

CR2032 coin cell kits from MTI Corporation were utilized for electrochemical performance 
testing. The ionic conductivities of the LHCEs were measured using a Bio-Logic MCS system. 
The average Coulombic efficiency of the electrolyte was determined in Li||Cu cells using a 
modified Aurbach method which included the following steps: 5 mAh cm−2 of Li metal was 
electrochemically deposited onto a bare Cu electrode at a current density of 0.5 mA cm−2, and fully 
stripped to a cut-off voltage of 1 V (vs. Li/Li+). Next, Li metal of 5 mAh cm−2 was deposited as a 
reservoir, and Li was stripped and redeposited under 1.0 mAh cm−2 at 0.5 mA cm−2 for ten cycles. 
Finally, all remaining Li metal was completely stripped till 1 V at 0.5 mA cm−2. Li||Li symmetric 
cells were tested by cycling a capacity of 8.0 mAh cm−2 at a current density of 1.6 mA cm−2 and a 
capacity of 8.0 mAh cm−2 at a current density of 1.6 mA cm−2, respectively. The interphase 
resistance was analyzed through AC impedance measurements using a VSP Classic (Bio-Logic) 
over a frequency range of 10−2 to 106 Hz at room temperature. The Sand's capacities of the 
electrolytes were determined using Sand's formula.1
The SPAN cathode was prepared using SPAN powder, carbon black (Super P), and 
carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) binder in a weight ratio of 8:1:1, coated onto a carbon-coated 
aluminum (Al) current collector. The cathode’s active material loading was approximately 3.5 
mAh cm−2. The cells incorporated a 20 µm polyethylene (PE) separator (Energy Tech Solution), 
30 µL of electrolyte, and a 210 µm thick Li metal chip as the anode. Li||SPAN cells were first 
formed in two formation cycles at C/20 and then cycled at C/5 (where 1C = 3.5 mA cm−2) at 25 
°C under a voltage range of 1.0 to 3.0 V. For the higher areal capacity of SPAN (7 mAh cm−2), 5 
g (Ah)−1 of electrolyte was used with 100-µm thick Li metal. Cycling performance was evaluated 
using three parallel coin cells for each electrolyte formulation. The single layer pouch cell was 
assembled with 8 mAh cm−2 of SPAN, 100-µm thick Li metal and 2.5 g (Ah)−1 electrolyte. The

Structural/Physicochemical Characterizations
The polysulfide (PS) solubility test was performed with 0.1 M Li2S6 at 55 °C for 20 h inside the 
Ar-filled glovebox. The supernatants from the solvating solvents were transferred into cuvettes 
and analyzed by using a UV−Visible spectrophotometer (UV−2401PC, Shimadzu) at a scan rate of 
1 nm s−1, covering a wavelength range from 700 to 300 nm. For the post-mortem analyses, the 
electrodes were collected after the coin cells were disassembled, gently washed with DFE or DME 
solvent to remove the electrolyte residues, and dried under vacuum to remove all solvents. The 
surface and cross-sectional images were observed on a Helios scanning electron microscopy 
instrument. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted using a Physical Electronics 
Quantera scanning X-ray microprobe, with the resulting spectra analyzed through CasaXPS 
software. For each sample, XPS spectra were obtained from two different surface locations. The 
sulfur K-edge spectra were measured using X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) at 8-BM at 
NSLS II in Brookhaven National Laboratory in the fluorescence mode. For the experiments, the 
cycled electrodes were cut into 3 × 3 mm2 pieces and sealed between Kapton and Mylar films 
before loading to the sample stage. All XAS were measured in fluorescence mode and analyzed 
using the Athena software package.2
Simulations
All calculations were performed following the Hybrid ab initio and reactive molecular dynamics 
(HAIR) scheme, which uses a sequence of ab Initio Molecular Dynamics (AIMD), used to describe 
Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​aelccp/​article-supplement/​5152813/​pdf/​nz5c03054_si_001/​ by TSINGHUA UNIV user on 23 September 2026

<<PAGE 5>>
accurately the localized electrochemical reactions, followed by ReaxFF reactive dynamics step, to 
better represent the longer-range and longer-time chemical reactions in the electrolyte along with 
the mass transfer. This approach also reduces the need of extremely accurate ReaxFF parameters 
as the local bond-breaking description is done by the AIMD steps.3, 4
All AIMD calculations were performed using the density functional theory (DFT) method 
as implemented in the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP – version 6.3.2),5-7 with the 
plane-wave basis set including spin polarization to describe the valence electrons using a cutoff 
energy of 400 eV. The treatment of the core-electron interactions was done with the recommended 
projector augmented wave pseudopotentials (PAW),8, 9 i.e. Li_sv, S, O, N, F, C, and H. We used 
the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized gradient approximation (PBE-GGA)10, 11 and the 
integration of Brillouin zone was done with the gamma centered Monkhorst-Pack grid method,12 
as well as the Gaussian smearing method with an energy smearing set to 0.05 eV allowed for 
determining the electronic occupancies in all calculations. The energies and forces convergence 
criteria were set to 10−4 eV and 0.02 eV/Å, respectively, while long-range van der Waals 
interactions were incorporated using the Grimme’s D3-dispersion correction.13, 14  The AIMD 
calculation steps run an isothermal stabilization at constant-temperature, constant-volume (NVT 
ensemble) set at 330 K  for 0.5 ps, and uses a 1.2 fs time-step; this relatively long time-step is 
possible after increasing the hydrogen mass to its tritium isotope.15 The Nose-Hoover thermostat 
is set to 0 to control the temperature, and Newton's equations are integrated with the Verlet 
algorithm.
Reactive molecular dynamics (ReaxFF MD) steps were performed using the open-source 
software LAMMPS (version 29 Aug 2024),16 employing a ReaxFF reactive force field specifically 
parameterized for interactions among C, H, O, S, Li, F, and N atoms, with a focus on  describing 
Downloaded from pubs.​acs.​org/​aelccp/​article-supplement/​5152813/​pdf/​nz5c03054_si_001/​ by TSINGHUA UNIV user on 23 September 2026

<<PAGE 6>>
FSI− anions and organic solvents interfacing with lithium surfaces.3 In ReaxFF, bond orders are 
computed based on interatomic distances and partial energies, incorporating penalty, lone pair, 
valence, and torsional contributions, which are updated at every timestep. Van der Waals 
interactions are modeled via a Morse potential, while atomic charges are determined through a 
charge equilibration scheme17-22 using Coulombic interactions.23 This charge equilibration method 
estimates the partial charge on each atom by minimizing the system's electrostatic energy, 
effectively solving a large sparse linear system.21 The ReaxFF MD steps run with the NVT 
ensemble at a constant temperature of 330K using the default Nosé−Hoover thermostat24 with a 
time step of 0.25 fs for a total of 5 ps. All systems run at least 41 HAIR cycles, featuring 225.5 ps 
long.
