Experimental Section
Chemicals and materials. 4-Fluorobenzenethiol (F-PhSH, 98%+, Adamas-beta), sulfur (99.5%, Aladdin), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI, DoDoChem), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, DoDoChem), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, DoDoChem), commercial Li-S electrolyte (1.0 M LiTFSI/0.15 M LiNO3 in DME:DOL=1:1 v/v, DoDoChem), Li metal foil (Cellithium), and carbon disulfide (CS2, 99%, J&K) were purchased and used as received.

Methods
F-PhSLi synthesis: The synthesis of the F-PhSLi was carried out according to our previous report.[1] F-PhSLi was prepared by dissolving 500 μL F-PhSH in 500 μL DME in an Argon-filled glove box. A piece of lithium foil (diameter: 15.6 μm, thickness: 450 μm) was added in the solution to react overnight with small bubble generated. After the reaction was completed, the solvent was evaporated under vacuum at room temperature for 10 h, and then heated at 80 ℃ under vacuum for 20 h to ensure complete removal of DME. The prepared F-PhSLi is a light yellow powder.
Preparation of cathode and electrolyte: commercial bucky paper was cut into 1.13 cm2 discs (diameter: 12 mm) and dried at 100 ℃ in a vacuum oven for 12 h as the current collector. Sulfur powder was dissolved in CS2, then the solution was dropped on the carbon paper and the cathode was obtained after evaporating the solvent at 60 ℃ for 12 h in a vacuum oven. For the pouch cell, the bucky paper was cut into squares of 3 × 3 cm2 and the sulfur loading is 40.6 mg. 1.0 M F-PhSLi and 0.15 M LiNO3 were dissolved in DOL/DME (v:v=1:1) for comparison with commercial Li-S electrolyte. 0.5/2.0 M F-PhSLi and 0.15 M LiNO3 in DOL:DME=1:1 v/v was used to compare the effect of F-PhSLi concentration on performance. 1.0 M F-PhSLi and 0.5 M LiNO3 in DOL:DME=1:1 v/v was used in the high mass loading/high-rate cell. 1.0 M F-PhSLi, 0.5 M LiNO3, and 0.5 M LiTFSI in DOL:DME=1:1 v/v was used in the pouch cell.
Detection of the cathodic sulfur content remaining after the reaction with F-PhSLi electrolyte: 20 μL 1.0 M F-PhSLi electrolyte was dropped on the sulfur cathode (S loading is 1.2 mg) and then washed by dropping 5 mL DME solvent until the solvent is almost colorless; the washed sulfur cathode was dried under vacuum and then assembled with lithium metal anode using LiTFSI electrolyte. The assembled cell was cycled at a constant current of 0.1 mA and voltage range of 1.8-3.0 V.
Characterizations. 7Li NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker AVIII500 system at room temperature. A 1.0 mol L-1 sample of LiCl in deionized H2O was used as the reference (0 ppm). 1 M F-PhSLi dissolved in DOL/DME solution and the S+1 M F-PhSLi DOL/DME solution were used to prove the chemical reaction. Raman spectra were obtained on Lab RAM HR Evolution Laser Raman spectrometer in the range of 200-800 cm-1. The solutions were sealed in the quartz mold in the Argon-filled glove box to avoid exposure to air during the test. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) measurements were conducted on Zeiss Sigma 500 and Phenom FEG (cross-sectional SEM). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was conducted on a Thermo SCIENTIFIC ESCALAB 250Xi with a monochromatic Al Kα radiation. The samples were washed with pure DME to remove the residual salts before the SEM and XPS tests. Ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (UPLC-QTof-MS, Waters Acquity UPLC coupled with a Xevo G2-XS QTof mass spectrometer) was used to analyze the reactive and charge/discharge products. The reactive products and the cycled cathode were immersed in DME, then the obtained solutions were transferred out of the glovebox and tested in atmospheric pressure solids analysis probe (ASAP) positive mode with the first-order mass spectrometry model.
Electrochemical measurement. CR2032 coin cells were assembled by sandwiching Celgard 2400 as a separator between the lithium metal anode and sulfur cathode in the Ar-filled glovebox (H2O<0.1ppm, O2<0.1ppm). The amount of electrolyte on both cathode and anode is 20 μL. For the blank cells, the sulfur-loaded carbon paper was replaced by the one without sulfur, and others are the same. The cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.8 V and 3.0 V on a LANHE battery cycler. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were performed on Bio-Logic VMP-3 with a scan rate of 0.05 mV s-1. For the pouch cell, the cathode and electrolyte mentioned above were used, and the size of the lithium anode and separator are 3 cm × 3 cm and 4 cm × 4 cm. The amount of electrolyte in the pouch cell is 400 μL and the voltage range is 1.7-3.0 V. For the low-temperature tests, the assembled cells were first placed into an incubator at -20 ℃ for 1 h before testing to ensure that the internal temperature of the cells reached -20 ℃, and then cycled at 0.1 C with a voltage range of 1.8-3.0 V. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were performed with a Bio-Logic VMP-3 impedance analyzer in the frequency range of 1000 kHz to 0.1 Hz with an amplitude of 5 mV.
Kinetics measurement. The activation energies for various lithium polysulfides conversion steps during the discharge process were calculated according to the Arrhenius equation by using the charge transfer resistance (Rct) that derived from the EIS profiles (Figure S5 and S6). The cells were first discharged to 1.8 V and recharged to 3.0 V, and then discharged to the specific voltages at 0.1 C rate. EIS was measured at specific voltages in the frequency range of 1000 kHz to 0.1 Hz with an amplitude of 5 mV. An incubator was used to control the temperature during the EIS measurements.
Current-time transients (CTTs) tests: the Li2S nucleation and growth in the two electrolytes were studied by the combination of galvanostatical and potentiostatical charge-discharge process. The Li-S cells were assembled with the sulfur cathode, lithium metal anode and PP membrane separator. And 20 μL electrolytes were added to the cathode and anode, respectively. The assembled cells were first discharged to 1.8 V and recharged to 3.0 V, and discharged to 2.08 V at 0.1 C rate, and then potentiostatically discharged at 2.0 V until the current was below 10-5 A. The deposited current collector was then taken out and washed by DME to observed the deposition morphology of Li2S.
Potentiostatic intermittent titration technique (PITT) tests: the fully discharged cells were obtained to study the dissolution of Li2S during the charging process. The assembled Li-S cells were galvanostatically discharged at 0.1 mA to 1.8 V and then potentiostatically discharged at 1.8 V for 4.0 h to ensure thorough conversion of Li2S. The above cells were charged via PITT tests. Typically, the fully discharged cells were potentiostatically charged from 2.10 to 2.60 V with a step size of 0.02 V. The cut-off condition at each step was that the current is less than 20 μA. The current was recorded to study the delithiation process of Li2S. The full process was performed on Bio-Logic VMP-3.
Theoretical simulations. The ground state of Li2Sn and F-PhSnLi (n=4, 6, 8) were calculated in at PBE0/def2-TZVP level with the use of Gaussian 16 program.[2] The influence of the solvation medida was mimicked using the solvation model based on density (SMD)[3] with static dielectric constant of DME was set to be 7.07 at 298.15 K.[4]
