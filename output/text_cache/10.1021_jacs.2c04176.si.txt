1. Experimental section 
1.1. Raw materials 
All raw materials, including carbon nanotubes (CNTs), graphene (G), sulfur (S8), 
molybdenum disulfide (MoS2), lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), 
sodium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (NaTFSI), 1-butyl-1-methylpyrrolidinium 
bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (Pyr14TFSI), lithium trifluoromethanesulphonate 
(LiTfO), methyl trifluoromethansulfonate (TfOMe), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), lithium 
iodide (LiI), lithium sulfide (Li2S), carbon paper (CP), N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP), 
1,3-dioxolane (DOL), 1,2- dimethoxyethane (DME), deionized (DI) water, 
tetrahydrofuran (THF), deuterated chloroform (CDCl3), poly(vinylidene fluoride) 
(PVDF, HSV-300), polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard 2400), aluminum (Al) foil, 
and lithium (Li) foil were purchased from commercial sources and directly used without 
further purification.  
 
1.2. Operando visualized gelation and gelation-inhibition 
1.2.1. Visualized gelation 
To prepare the solution for visualized gelation, 1.0 mmol LiTFSI, NaTFSI, 
Pyr14TFSI, LiTfO, or TfOMe was dissolved in 1.0 mL DOL solvent and denoted as 
DOL with LiTFSI, NaTFSI, Pyr14TFSI, LiTfO, or TfOMe. 
To observe the gelation process, 1.0 mL DOL with LiTFSI, NaTFSI, Pyr14TFSI, 
LiTfO, or TfOMe was added to 50 mg MoS2 and stood for 12 h. Optical images were 
taken using an operando digital camera at different time.

1.2.2. Visualized gelation-inhibition 
1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI dissolved DOL/DME (v/v=1:1) mixed solvents was adopted 
as the blank solution. To prepare the solution for visualized gelation-inhibition, LiI 
solutions were prepared by adding LiI into the blank solution. The concentration of LiI 
was 0.10 mol L−1. 
To observe the gelation-inhibition process, 1.0 mL LiI solution was added to 50 
mg MoS2 and stood for 12 h. Optical images were taken using an operando digital 
camera at different time. 
 
1.3. Material characterization 
1.3.1. Characterization of the gel 
The gel was firstly washed with DOL to rinse the disulfide powders. Fourier 
transform infrared spectrometry (FTIR) analysis was carried out on a NEXUS 870 
spectrograph within a wavenumber range from 400 to 4000 cm−1 to investigate the 
functional groups of the gel. 13C and 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra 
were recorded on a JNM-ECZ400 NMR spectrometer with CDCl3 as the deuterated 
solvent and tetramethylsilane as the internal reference to determine the structure of the 
gel. Gel permeation chromatography (GPC) analysis was carried out using a Waters 
2695 instrument to detect the molecular weight of the gel. The GPC instrument was 
equipped with a differential refractometer detector (Waters 2414 RI Detector) and a 
laser light scattering detector (WYATT TREOS II). Tetrahydrofuran was used as the

maintained at 35°C.  
 
1.3.2. Characterization of the MoS2 electrocatalyst 
Morphology characterization was performed on a JSM 7401F (JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, 
Japan) scanning electron microscope (SEM) operated at 3.0 kV and a JEM 2010 (JEOL 
Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) transmission electron microscope (TEM) operated at 120.0 kV. 
High-angle annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscope (HAADF-
STEM) images and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopic (EDS) mapping were carried 
out on a double spherical aberration corrected TEM Titan Cubed Themis G2 300 (FEI, 
Portland, US). The working voltage of the STEM was 300.0 kV.  
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectra were recorded using an 
ESCALAB 250Xi (Thermo Fisher) to analyze the component and chemical states of 
MoS2. The binding energies of the XPS profiles were calibrated by the carbon 1s peak 
at 284.6 eV. For the Raman spectra tests, the samples were spread on a quartz glass 
sheet and the spectra were recorded with 633 nm He–Ne laser in a Horiba Jobin Yvon 
LabRAM HR800 Raman spectrometer. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were 
collected on a Bruker D8 Advanced diffractometer with Cu-Kα radiation at 40.0 kV and 
120 mA as the X-ray source.

To prepare the Li2S6 electrolyte, Li2S and S8 were mixed in the blank solution (1.0 
mol L−1 LiTFSI dissolved DOL/DME mixed solvent) with a molar ratio of 8:5, and 
stirred at room temperature. The Li2S6 concentration was 1.0 mol[S] L−1. To prepare the 
Li2S8 electrolyte, Li2S and S8 were mixed in the blank solution dissolving 2% LiNO3 
with a molar ratio of 8:7 and stirred at room temperature. The Li2S8 concentration was 
also 1.0 mol[S] L−1. In addition, LiI dissolved in the above Li2S6 or Li2S8 electrolyte was 
denoted as LiI+Li2S6 electrolyte and LiI+Li2S8 electrolyte, respectively, and the 
concentration of LiI was 0.10 mol L‒1. 
 
1.4.2. Preparation of MoS2 coated Al foil 
For kinetic evaluation on the liquid–liquid, liquid–solid, and solid‒liquid 
processes, the MoS2 electrocatalysts were coated onto Al foil current collectors to 
prepare the electrodes for cell assembly. Specifically, MoS2, PVDF, and G with a mass 
ratio of 50:1:10 were added into NMP. The mixture was sonicated for 2.0 h and stirred 
for another 2.0 h to obtain a homogeneous slurry. Then, the slurry was coated onto Al 
foils and dried at 60oC for 12.0 h. The areal mass loading of the MoS2 electrocatalyst 
was controlled to be 0.50 mg cm−2. The MoS2 coated Al foil electrodes were finally 
obtained after being punched into disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm. 
 
1.4.3. Liquid–liquid conversion kinetics evaluation 
Li2S6 symmetric cells were constructed for liquid–liquid conversion kinetic 
evaluation following a previously validated method.1 In details, two identical MoS2

PP separator (19.0 mm in diameter) and 40 μL Li2S6 electrolyte or LiI+Li2S6 electrolyte. 
Cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests 
for the Li2S6 symmetric cells were carried out on a Solartron 1470E electrochemical

1.4.4. Liquid–solid conversion kinetics evaluation 
To study the Li2S nucleation kinetics, Li2S8 cells were assembled with a piece of 
MoS2 coated Al foil electrode as the cathode, a 600 μm Li metal foil as the anode (16.0 
mm in diameter), a piece of PP membrane as the separator (19.0 mm in diameter), and 
Li2S8 electrolyte or LiI+Li2S8 electrolyte. Specifically, 40 L Li2S8 electrolyte or 
LiI+Li2S8 electrolyte was added to the cathode, while 5 L blank electrolyte without 
Li2S8 was added to the anode for electrode wetting. The Li2S8 cells were discharged 
galvanostatically to 2.12 V and then potentiostatically discharged at 2.14, 2.12, 2.10, 
2.08, 2.06, and 2.04 V for Li2S nucleation and growth. The voltage dropped to the 
following stage when the potentiostatic time exceeded 6.0 h.

1.5. Fabrication of MoS2 separators 
MoS2 separators were obtained via filtration. Specifically, 75.4 mg MoS2, 7.5 mg 
G, and 9.2 mg PVDF binder were dispersed in 30.0 mL NMP by ultrasonication for 1.0 
h. Then the dispersion was filtered through a PP membrane and dried at 70 ℃ for 24.0 
h. The areal mass loading of the MoS2 electrocatalyst was 1.0 mg cm−2. The separators 
were then pouched into small disks with a diameter of 19.0 mm. 
MoS2 separator with the gel layer (denoted as MoS2 separator with gel) was 
obtained via an immersion process based on the MoS2 separator. Specifically, a MoS2 
separator was immersed in the blank solution (1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI dissolved in 
DOL/DME mixed solvent) for 12.0 h. Then the separator was dried at room temperature 
for 24.0 h. 
 
1.6. Li–S coin cell assembly and evaluation 
Sulfur cathode was fabricated as follows. 60 mg CNT and 140 mg S8 were mixed 
in a batch and ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a 
homogeneous slurry. The slurry was then coated on Al foils using doctor blades and 
dried at 60℃ for 24 h. The areal sulfur loading was 1.2 mgS cm−2 for regular evaluation 
and 4.4 mgS cm−2 for evaluation under harsh conditions. The sulfur cathodes were

punched into small disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm. 
Electrochemical performance was evaluated using standard 2025 coin cells for 
regular evaluation or 2032 coin cells for evaluation under harsh conditions. The coin 
cells were assembled with a piece of sulfur cathode, a Li metal foil as anode (16.0 mm 
in diameter and 600 μm in thickness), and a PP membrane separator, a MoS2 separator, 
or a MoS2 separator with gel (19.0 mm in diameter). 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI and 2 wt.% 
LiNO3 dissolved in DOL/DME (v/v = 1:1) mixed solvent was adopted as the blank 
electrolyte. LiI dissolved in the above blank electrolyte was denoted as the LiI 
electrolyte. The LiI concentration was 0.10 mol L‒1. The electrolyte to sulfur (E/S) ratio 
was 15.7 or 7.7 μLelectrolyte mgS−1 for the coin cells with sulfur cathodes of 1.2 or 4.4 
mgS cm−2, respectively. The coin cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.7−2.6 
V on a LAND multichannel battery cycler under different C-rates (1 C = 1672 mA g−1). 
 
1.7. Li–S pouch cell assembly and evaluation 
The MoS2 electrocatalysts were directly introduced into the sulfur cathodes. 
Typically, 5.6 g sulfur powder, 2.0 g CNT, and 0.20 g MoS2 were mixed and ball-milled 
with PVDF binder with a mass ratio of 9:1 in NMP for 2.0 h to form a homogeneous 
slurry. The slurry was then double-side coated on Al foils and then dried at 60oC for 24 
h. The areal sulfur loading was 7.1 mgS cm−2 on a single side. 4 × 7 cm2 electrodes were 
cut and directly used as the sulfur cathodes in pouch cells. 
The anode of the Li–S pouch cell was double-side Li metal sheets (75 μm thickness 
on a single side) rolled on Cu current collectors with a size of 4 × 7 cm2. For the

PP membrane as the separator. The electrodes were controlled in parallel connection by

L−1 LiI, 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI, and 5.0 wt % LiNO3 dissolved in DOL/DME (v/v = 1:1) 
mixed solvents. The E/S ratio was controlled to be 3.5 and 3.0 mgelectrolyte mgsulfur−1 for

calculated based on the total mass of the pouch cells including anodes, cathodes, the 
electrolyte, separators, Al current collectors, Cu current collectors, tabs, and the

1.8. Theoretical Simulations 
All the calculations were carried out using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation 
Package (VASP)2 in the framework of Density functional theory (DFT).3 The 
generalized gradient approximation (GGA)4 with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerh (PBE)5 
functional was implemented to describe the electron exchange and correlation energy. 
The plane-wave cut-off energy was set to 520 eV, and all the structures were optimized 
with 2×2×1 k-point sampling to a force tolerance less than 2×10−2 eV Å−1 and the total 
energy convergence lower than 10−5 eV. DFT-D3 method was used to include the van 
der Waals force.6 A slab model, containing seven-layer MoS2 and a 3×1×1 supercell,
