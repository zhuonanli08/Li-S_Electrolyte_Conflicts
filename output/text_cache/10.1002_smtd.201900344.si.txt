I. Experimental Section 
1.1. Raw materials 
All raw materials, including lithium sulfides (Li2S), sulfur (S), cobaltocene 
(CoCp2), nickelocene (NiCp2), 1,1'-bis(diphenylphosphino) ferrocene (DPPF), carbon 
paper (CP), N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP, Sinopharm), 1,2-dimethoxyethane 
(DOL), 1,2-diamethoxyethane (DME), deionized (DI) water, poly(vinylidene fluoride) 
(PVDF, HSV-300), polypropylene (PP) separator (Celgard 2400), aluminum (Al) foil, 
lithium (Li) foil, lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfony)imide (LiTFSI), and lithium 
nitrate (LiNO3) are purchased from commercial sources and used without further 
purification 
 
1.2. Materials characterization 
The morphologies of Li2S deposition were characterized by a JSM 7401F (JEOL 
Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) scanning electron microscopy (SEM) operated at 3.0 kV. The 
pore-size distribution and Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) specific surface area of the 
samples were measured by N2 isothermal adsorption/desorption at 77K using 
Autosorb-IQ2-MP-C system. The pore size distribution and pore volume were 
calculated by quenched solid state density functional theory (QSDFT) method using 
the adsorption branch. The crystal structure of Li2S-CP was identified by an X-ray 
powder diffractometer (D8-Advance, Bruker, Germany) at 40.0 kV and 120 mA with 
Cu-Kα radiation. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the samples were recorded on 
an X-ray powder diffractometer (D8 Advance, Germany) at 40.0 kV and 120 mA with 
Cu-Kα radiation. 
 
1.3. Electrolyte and polysulfide preparation 
1.0 moL L−1 LiTFSI and 0.3 moL L−1 LiNO3 dissolved in DOL/DME (v/v=1:1) 
mixed solvents was adopted as the blank electrolyte. The redox mediators (RMs), 
including CoCp2, NiCp2, and DPPF, were added to blank electrolyte, and new 
electrolyte with a concentration of 20 mmoL L−1 (20, 50 and 100 mmol L−1 for CoCp2)

was obtained after stirring for 24 h.  
Both Li2S4 and Li2S8 were selected as prototype of polysulfides. The mixture of 
Li2S and S with a molar ratio of 1:3 and 1:7 were added into blank electrolyte to 
prepare Li2S4 (3.3 mmoL L−1 [S]) and Li2S8 (2 moL L−1 [S]) catholyte, respectively. 
To investigate the mediation of RM, CoCp2 was added to above polysulfide 
electrolyte and the concentration was controlled at 20 mmoL L−1. All above 
procedures were conducted in an Ar-filled glove box. 
 
1.4. Cell assembly and electrochemical performance 
Two types of cell were assembled. One of sulfur-free electrodes (donated as Li | 
CP cells) for model discharge, including cyclic voltammetry (CV) experiment and 
diffusion measurement. The other is routine Li | S cells with carbon/sulfur blended 
composite electrodes. 
For the former, polysulfide or RM solution was used as catholyte. commercial 
CP was cut into small pieces with a diameter of 13.0 mm and used as current collector 
for different catholyte, which possesses a thickness of 290 m (Figure S8), a specific 
surface area of 6.863 m3 g−1, and abundant mesopores and macropores (Figure S9). 
Lithium foil and CP were assembled into a standard 2025 cell with PP separators. 20 
μL catholyte was dropped onto CP electrode, while 10 μL blank electrolyte was added 
in the anode compartment. 
For Li | S cells, in a typical procedure, carbon/sulfur composite was prepared 
through ball-milling.[1] A mixture of 60.0 mg multi-walled carbon nanotubes and 
140.0 mg sulfur was ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP 
solvent. The slurry was casted onto aluminum (Al) foil and dried for 12.0 h at 60°C to 
prepare sulfur cathode. The coated Al foil was cut into small disks with a diameter of 
13.0 mm. The areal sulfur loading of 1.3 mg cm−2 was applied for most 
electrochemical measurements, and that of 4.8 mg cm−2 was employed for lean 
electrolyte test. The carbon/sulfur cathode disk and lithium anode were assembled in 
standard 2025 coin cell separated by PP separators. The amount of electrolyte was

precisely controlled with an electrolyte-to-sulfur (E/S) ratio of 16 and 4.7 
μL(electrolyte)/mg(sulfur) for cells with sulfur loadings of 1.3 and 4.8 mg cm−2. To verify 
the role of CoCp2 in the discharge process, cells discharged to 1.7 V were 
disassembled, injected with 10 μL fresh electrolyte, reassembled and re-discharged at 
0.1 C. The cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.7–2.8 V on a Neware 
multichannel battery cycler under different C-rates (1 C = 1672 mA g–1). CV tests 
were conducted on a Solartron 1470E electrochemical workstation at a scanning rate 
of 0.1 mV s−1 for Li | S cells and 10–50 mV s−1 for Li | CP cells. 
 
1.5. Measurement of diffusivity

ip is the peak current (A), A is the electrode area (cm2), C is the concentration (moL 
cm−3, here referring to 20 mmol L−1 Li2S4 and CoCp2), ν is the scan rate (V s−1, here 
adopting 10–50 mV s−1), D is the diffusion coefficient (cm2 s−1), n is the number of 
transferred electrons (6 for Li2S4 and 1 for CoCp2), and α is transfer coefficient (0.5 
based on the assumption that the peak current is gained from the reversible process).[2]
