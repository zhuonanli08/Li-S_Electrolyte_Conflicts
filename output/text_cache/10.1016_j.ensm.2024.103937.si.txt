Confined Biomimetic Catalysts Boost LiNO3-Free Lithium-Sulfur Batteries via Enhanced LiTFSI Decomposition



















Experimental section
Materials: The following chemicals and solvents are purchased and used without further purification. Indium nitrate hydrate (In(NO3)3.xH2O, 99.99% metals basis, Aladdin), 2-Aminoterephthalic acid (NH2-H2BDC, AR, >98.0%, Aladdin), Iron(II) phthalocyanine (FePc, AR, >98.0%, Aladdin), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, AR, 99.5%, Aladdin), Ethanol (EtOH, AR, H2O<0.3%, Aladdin) and Distilled water.

Syntheses of FePC@NH2-MIL-68: After 30 minutes of ultrasonic, the In(NO3)3.xH2O (250 mg) NH2-H2BDC (125 mg) and FePC (196 mg) are completely dissolved in solution of DMF (6.5 mL). Then, the homogeneous solution is transferred to a 25 mL glass bottle and placed in the oven at 85℃ for 6 hours. The obtained crystals are soaked in EtOH for 24 hours to replace the guest molecules. The resultant products were collected and cleaned by EtOH and DMF, then the yellow powder NH2-MIL-68 was obtained by drying in vacuum oven [1].

Preparation of FePC@NH2-MIL-68 cathode: CNTs–S cathode was prepared via a conventional melting-diffusion treatment [2]. The sulfur powder (>99.99%, Aladdin) was mixed with commercial multiwalled CNTs (>95%, inside diameter, 3–5 nm, Aladdin) at a mass ratio of 4:1 by ground milling. The obtained mixture was stirred in carbon disulfide (CS2, >99.9%, Aladdin) for 12 h, followed by heating at 155℃ for 12 h in a vacuum oven, which allowed sulfur to immerse into the CNTs and form CNTs-S composites. The 80 wt% of above CNTs-S composite, 14 wt% CNT conductive additive, 5 wt% polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF, Sigma-Aldrich) and 1 wt% FePC@NH2-MIL-68 were then mixed in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP, >99.5%, Aladdin) solvent to produce homogeneous slurry and cast onto a Al foil with a scraper blade, followed by drying at 60℃ in vacuum for 12 h. FePC and NH2-MIL-68 cathodes can be prepared by a similar method. Finally, the obtained cathode was punched into a disk of 14 mm in diameter to prepare the CNT-S/FePC@NH2-MIL-68, CNT-S/FePC, CNT-S/NH2-MIL-68 and CNT-S (abbreviated as FePC@NH2-MIL-68, FePC, NH2-MIL-68 and CNT) cathodes. The average sulfur loading of the cathode was ~1.2 mg cm−2 for the normal electrochemical measurements. The sulfur loading electrodes of 7.9 mg cm−2 were also prepared for the high sulfur mass loading experiments.

Li−S cell assembly and electrochemical measurements: The CR2025 coin cells were assembled using FePC@NH2-MIL-68, FePC, NH2-MIL-68 or CNT cathode, Li anode, Celgard 2400 separator and traditional electrolyte (1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) in 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME):1,3-dioxolane (DOL) = 1:1 (v:v)) in an Ar-filled glovebox (<0.1 ppm O2 and H2O, Universal, MIKROUNA) [3]. The E/S usage used was controlled at ~15 μL mg−1 for 1.2 mg cm−2 and 5.5 μL mg−1 for high sulfur loading electrodes. Cyclic voltammetry (CV, potential range: 1.6−2.8 V (vs. Li/Li+), sweep rate: 0.1 mV s−1) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS, frequency range: 0.01 Hz−200 KHz, AC voltage amplitude: 5 mV) measurements were performed on an electrochemical workstation (CHI 760E) at an ambient temperature (30℃) [4]. The rate, cycling and galvanostatic charge/discharge curves were recorded with a Neware CT-4008T-5V20mA-164 system between 1.6 and 2.8 V (vs. Li/Li+) under various C-rates (1 C=1675 mAh g−1) at room temperature. The capacity of Li−S battery was calculated according to the sulfur mass of the cathode. The galvanostatic cycling of symmetric Li||Li cells was carried out under capacity control (1 mA cm−2/1 mAh cm−2) and was evaluated by the voltage evolution profiles.

Material characterization: XRD patterns were collected at room temperature from a D8 Advance powder diffractometer (Bruker, Germany) with Cu Kα radiation (40 kV, 40 mA, λ = 1.54 Å) at a speed of 10° min-1 and a step size of 0.025°. The JSM-6700F field emission scanning electron microscope and TECNAI G2F20 transmission electron microscope were used to observe the morphology and structure of various electrodes. XPS data were obtained by using an ultrahigh vacuum setup equipped with a monochromatic Al-K X-ray source (10 mA, 15 kV) and a high-resolution Gammadata-Scienta SES 2002 analyzer. The binding energy scale was calibrated to the C 1s peak (close to 284.8 eV), and the diameter of the analysis area was approximately 500 mm. To determine the surface chemical state of the electrode under cycling, the battery with a certain discharge/charge depth was disassembled in a glove box and sealed in a vacuum bottle, which was quickly transferred to the XPS chamber (a basic pressure of 2×10-9 mbar) for ex-situ XPS measurements.

Shuttle current test: The cells were galvanostatically charged/discharged for 3 cycles at 0.2 C. Then the cells were discharged to 1.7 V and charged to 2.35 V, and then switched to potentiostatic mode. The current decay process was recorded and fitted to obtain the shuttle current density [5].

Visualized adsorption of LiPSs: Li2S6 was selected as a representative long-chain LiPS, which was prepared by mixing Li2S and sulfur powder at a molar ratio of 1:5 in DOL/DME (1:1, v/v) solution by intense stirring at 60℃ for 24 h [6]. Then, 10 mg of FePC@NH2-MIL-68, FePC or NH2-MIL-68 was put into the 2 mL of the as-prepared Li2S6 solution (1.5×10−3 M). After soaking 8 h for sufficient adsorption, the digital photographs were taken and the sediments were separated and dried at 60℃ for 48 h for the following XPS analysis.

Raman spectroscopic measurements: Raman spectra were acquired using an inVia Raman spectrometer (Renishaw, UK) at 532 nm with a 50 mW Ar-ion laser. The spectra of the electrolyte were recorded from 80 to 3150 cm−1, and all liquid samples were sealed in capillary glass tubes. Laser power was set at 500 mV, and 400 scans were accumulated with a resolution of 4 cm−1. 

In-situ FT-IR spectroscopy measurement: In-situ FT-IR spectra were carried out on Thermo Fisher iS50. The FePC@NH2-MIL-68 slurry was coated on a carbon paper film. Then the carbon paper, Li sheet and porous Celgard 2400 membrane were used as cathode, anode and separator, respectively, for assembling new Li–S cell. According to the Lambert–Beer law, the attenuation of infrared light passing through a thin layer of electrolyte at an angle of 45° before reaching the electrode surface is estimated to be ~0.3. Before collecting infrared data, the battery was charged and discharged for 2 cycles in the potential range of 1.6–2.8 V (vs. Li/Li+) with a sweep rate of 0.1 mV s−1. Subsequently, the measurement was performed with an accumulation time of 10 s and recorded every 0.1 V.

Methodological details of simulations: All the density functional theory (DFT) and ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) calculations were carried out using Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP) [7-9]. We employed the projected augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotential within the generalized gradient approximation with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) parameterization to describe the exchange-correlation functionals. A cutoff energy of 500 eV was adopted for the plane-wave expansion. We further constructed the optimized structure of FePC@NH2-MIL-68. 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL), dimethoxyethane (DME) and lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) molecules were packed in the vacuum space randomly to simulate the electrolyte environment. The DFT-D3 method was adopted to control the correction of Van der Waals (vdW) interaction. The convergence tolerance for energy and force was set at 10−5 eV and 10-2 eV Å−1, respectively.
