Recently, we have proposed a new 
strategy to improve the energy density and 
cycling performance of the Li-S battery 
using a functional electrolyte with dime-
thyl disulfide (DMDS) as cosolvent.[29,30] 
On the one hand, DMDS can promote an 
alternate reaction pathway through the 
formation of dimethyl polysulfide and Li 
organosulfides as new intermediates and 
reduced products. This new path offers 
better redox kinetics, avoids the forma-
tion of viscosity-raising long-chain Li 
polysulfides, and therefore promises high 
utilization of active materials at low E/S ratios. On the other 
hand, DMDS with an SS bond can also function as an elec-
trochemically active component in the electrolyte to contribute 
extra capacity. Additionally, DMDS-based functional electro-
lyte has been demonstrated to provide Li-S battery with excel-
lent capacity retention through a built-in automatic discharge 
shutoff mechanism in sulfur cathodes that reduces unfavorable 
Li2S formation. By using this functional electrolyte, high areal 
sulfur loading cathodes (4 mg cm−2) with an extraordinary 
capacity of around 1000 mAh g−1 can be achieved under a 
rather low E/S ratio of 5 mL g−1. As has been demonstrated in 
the DMDS-containing electrolytes, dimethyl trisulfide (DMTS) 
is a primary electrochemical reaction intermediate with higher 
reactivity than DMDS.[29] Moreover, DMTS is a commercially 
available organosulfide reagent and the SSS bonds provide 
DMTS with higher theoretical capacity (851 mAh g−1) than 
DMDS (570 mAh g−1).[29,31] All these advantages drive us to 
explore whether a functional electrolyte with DMTS as cosol-
vent will work to provide higher cell capacity and better cycling 
performance for Li-S batteries.

The lithium–sulfur (Li-S) battery offers a high theoretical energy density 
of ≈2600 Wh kg−1 and low cost, positioning it as a promising candidate 
for next-generation battery technology. However, problems including 
disastrous Li polysulfides dissolution and irreversible Li2S deposition 
have severely retarded the development of Li-S batteries. To solve these 
issues, a functional dimethyl disulfide (DMDS)-containing electrolyte was 
recently reported that promotes an alternate electrochemical reaction 
pathway for sulfur cathodes by the formation of dimethyl polysulfides and 
Li organosulfides as intermediates and reduction products, leading to 
significantly boosted Li-S cell capacity with improved cycling reversibility 
and stability. Here, dimethyl trisulfide (DMTS), a primary discharge–
charge intermediate in the DMDS-containing electrolyte, which is also 
a commercially available reagent, is further investigated as a cosolvent 
in functional electrolytes for Li-S batteries. Due to the higher theoretical 
capacity of DMTS and its better reactivity with Li2S than DMDS, a 25 vol% 
DMTS-containing electrolyte enables Li-S batteries with even higher cell 
capacity and improved cycling performance than using previous optimal 
50 vol% DMDS-containing electrolyte.
Batteries
As a promising candidate for high-energy and low-cost battery 
technology, the lithium–sulfur (Li-S) battery has been extensively 
studied over the last decade.[1] However, it still confronts critical 
drawbacks such as low practical energy density and dissatisfac-
tory cycling performance that mainly arise from a high electro-
lyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio required in cells, as well as a low utilization 
of sulfur capacity and rapid capacity fading of sulfur cathodes.[2] 
The deteriorated performance of sulfur cathodes is mainly due to 
the insulating nature of sulfur, dissolution of Li polysulfide inter-
mediates, large volume change of sulfur species during discharge/
charge process, and irreversible deposition and segregation of 
Li2S from conductive substrates.[3,4] To date, many advanced 
approaches have sought to ensure sufficient contact between 
active sulfur species and the conductive substrates as well as 
mitigate Li polysulfide migration and its parasitic reactions with 
Li anode. Among these are constructions of highly conductive

Li-S battery with a carbon/sulfur (C/S) composite cathode 
was used to explore the superiority of DMTS-containing elec-
trolytes. All the coin cells tested contained ≈1 mg of sulfur and 
10 µL of electrolyte, resulting in an E/S ratio of ≈10 mL g−1. 
As shown in Figure 1a, Li-S batteries using a 25 vol% DMTS-
containing electrolyte exhibited a voltage profile very similar to 
the DMDS-containing electrolytes. It has been widely known 
that sulfur cathodes show two typical discharge plateaus at 
2.4–2.3 and ≈2.1 V in conventional electrolyte, which corre-
sponds to the reduction of solid S8 to soluble polysulfides, and 
polysulfides to insoluble sulfides, respectively. In contrast, as 
shown in Figure 1a, only a slope between 2.4 and 2.1 V and 
a plateau at ≈2.1 V were observed in both DMTS- and DMDS-
containing electrolytes, demonstrating that sulfur cathodes 
undergo a similar electrochemical pathway. Besides, the cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) results (Figure S3, Supporting Informa-
tion) of Li-S battery in 25 vol% DMTS-containing electrolyte 
have also demonstrated such similarity. However, slight dif-
ference exists between two CV results. First, the slope region 
in the DMTS-containing electrolyte shows both higher voltage 
and capacity than in the DMDS-containing electrolyte. This 
indicates the formation of dimethyl polysulfides intermediates 
with longer SS chains than DMTS (the main intermediate in 
DMDS-containing electrolyte) in the DMTS-containing elec-
trolyte due to the reaction of sulfur and DMTS.[32,33] Second, 
the optimal DMTS content is found to be only ≈25 vol%, much 
less than 50 vol% for the DMDS-containing electrolyte. Inter-
estingly, C/S cathodes in the 25 vol% DMTS-containing elec-
trolyte showed an even higher initial discharge capacity of 
≈1100 mAh g−1composite, than ≈950 mAh g−1composite in 50 vol% 
DMDS-containing electrolyte (Figure 1a), and almost double 
the capacity in the conventional electrolyte (≈600 mAh g−1 in 
our previous report[29]) under similar testing conditions. With 
higher DMTS content (i.e., 50 vol%), we observed severe polari-
zation and even less capacity. This could be attributed to higher 
viscosity and/or lower conductivity of the 50 vol% DMTS-
containing electrolyte (3.99 mS cm−1) compared to 25 vol% 
DMTS-containing electrolyte (5.37 mS cm−1) (Table S1, Sup-
porting Information). Moreover, after 50 cycles at C/10 rate, the 
C/S cathode still maintained a higher sulfur-specific discharge 
capacity of ≈765 mAh g−1composite in the 25 vol% DMTS-con-
taining electrolyte than ≈705 mAh g−1composite in the 50 vol% 
DMDS-containing electrolyte (Figure 1b). The above results 
fully demonstrated that DMTS-based functional electrolyte 
could promote sulfur cathodes with superior performance than 
the DMDS-based electrolyte.
The superiority of DMTS-based functional electrolyte is 
directly related to its higher theoretical capacity and reactivity 
compared to DMDS. First, the higher theoretical capacity of 
DMTS provides extra capacity to sulfur cathodes. With the 
addition of only 25 vol% of DMTS, the C/S cathodes showed 
even higher initial capacity and better capacity retention than 
with 50 vol% DMDS-based electrolyte. Second, the higher 
reactivity of DMTS with sulfur and Li2S formed the alternate 
electrochemical pathway more fluently during cycling, which 
is more beneficial to the cycling performance of sulfur cath-
odes. Of particular note, the irreversible capacity loss caused 
by insoluble Li2S deposition and separation of active material

by the presence of DMTS. The solubility of Li2S in 25 vol% 
DMTS and 50 vol% DMDS-containing electrolytes was tested 
to demonstrate that DMTS possesses higher reactivity with 
Li2S than DMDS. As shown in Figure 2, after adding Li2S into 
25 vol% DMTS-containing electrolyte, Li2S powder was dis-
solved and the color of the system changed from light yellow 
to dark red, indicating the formation of polysulfide species. On 
the contrary, for the 50 vol% DMDS-containing electrolyte, no 
color change was observed after the addition of Li2S and the 
Li2S powder precipitated at the bottom of the vial. The solu-
bility test verified that the existence of DMTS in the electrolyte 
could enhance reversibility of Li2S by partially dissolving Li2S 
deposition, a factor which is very important to improve cycling 
performance of sulfur cathodes and which is also responsible 
for its higher capacity retention. Increasing DMTS content to 
50 vol% does not further increase the capacity of C/S cathode 
and leads to relatively poor cycling performance, probably 
due to its low conductivity and the severe polarization, as can 
be seen in the discharge/charge curves (Figure 1). Therefore, 
the 25 vol% DMTS-containing electrolyte was used for further 
electrochemical testing.
Our previous study showed that the DMDS-based electrolyte 
not only provided sulfur cathodes with high capacity but also 
enabled enhanced capacity retention through a built-in auto-
matic discharge shutoff mechanism by tuning carbon to sulfur 
ratio in the sulfur composite cathodes to reduce unfavorable Li2S 
formation.[30] Cathodes with high sulfur content produce more 
Li organosulfides with better redox kinetics and reversibility, 
creating an automatic shutoff mechanism for the discharge 
process to mitigate the detrimental irreversible formation of 
insoluble Li2S. Thus, both excellent cyclability and high capacity 
were observed for cathodes with high sulfur content by using 
the DMDS-based functional electrolyte. Here, the C/S cathodes 
with different content of sulfur (0, 40, 50, and 70 wt%, which 
were labeled as OMC-S0, OMC-S40, OMC-S50, and OMC-S70) 
were also investigated using the 25 vol% DMTS-containing 
electrolyte, as shown in Figure 3. Similarly, a rapid capacity 
fading was observed for C/S cathodes with sulfur content less 
than 40 wt%. A highest initial capacity of ≈1100 mAh g−1composite 
with the best capacity retention of ≈850 mAh g−1 after 50 cycles 
was observed for the OMC-S70 cathode, with the same trend 
but higher value than using the 50 vol% DMDS-containing elec-
trolyte. The S/C cathode without sulfur (OMC-S0) also showed 
a high initial capacity (≈1000 mAh g−1) and reversible capacity 
between 900 and 600 mAh g−1 during 50 cycles, corresponding 
to the electrochemical performance of pure DMTS itself. These 
again verified that DMTS can provide extra capacity for the 
Li-S cell. The above results, therefore, indicate that the 25 vol% 
DMTS-containing electrolyte can efficiently boost cell capacity 
and enable cathodes with high sulfur content to achieve better 
cycling performance. Finally, extended cycling (288 cycles) 
in 25 vol% DMTS-containing electrolyte at a higher C/3 rate 
using the high-sulfur-content cathode (OMC-S70) was also 
conducted (Figure 4), demonstrating excellent retention of dis-
charge capacity over 600 mAh g−1 during extended cycling. The 
OMC-S70 cathode showed a stable cycling performance with a 
fading rate as low as around 0.11% cycle−1 for all 288 cycles, or 
0.096% cycle−1 for the last 100 cycles. Compared to the DMDS-
containing or conventional 1,3-dioxolane/1,2-dimethoxyethane 
(DOL/DME) electrolyte, all these results highlight the advan-
tages of DMTS-containing electrolyte which will considerably 
promote the development of high-energy-density Li-S batteries.
In conclusion, we reported a functional electrolyte system 
using DMTS as cosolvent for Li-S batteries. DMTS functions 
similar to DMDS to promote an alternate electrochemical reac-
tion pathway for sulfur cathodes and to enable good cycling per-
formance for cathodes with high sulfur content by way of an 
automatic discharge shutoff mechanism. Thanks to its higher 
theoretical capacity and reactivity than DMDS, a 25 vol% DMTS-
containing electrolyte enables sulfur cathodes with higher dis-

Experimental Section
Sulfur Cathode Fabrication: Sulfur cathodes were prepared by 
conventional blade-casting method. In detail, an ordered mesoporous 
carbon (OMC) was synthesized according to a previously reported 
method and used as conductive frameworks.[8] To prepare sulfur/carbon 
(C/S) composites, sulfur was mixed with OMC under different weight 
ratio using a mortar. Then the sulfur and OMC mixture was heat treated 
in sealed vials at 159 °C for 10 h. The as-obtained OMC-S composite 
with different sulfur content (0, 40, 50, and 70 wt%), labeled as OMC-S0, 
OMC-S40, OMC-S50, and OMC-S70, was then mixed with Super P 
carbon and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in a mass ratio of 80:10:10 
and dispersed in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) to form a slurry. The 
sulfur cathode was at last fabricated by casting slurry onto carbon-
coated aluminum foil, dried overnight at 55 °C under vacuum and then 
punched into round pieces with a diameter of 12 mm. The sulfur loading 
of the cathode was controlled at ≈1 mg cm−2.
Electrolyte Preparation: The DMTS-containing electrolyte was prepared 
by dissolving 0.1 m LiNO3 and 1 m Li bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) 
imide (LiTFSI) in a mixture of DMTS, DOL, and DME with two different 
volume ratios, namely, 50 vol% DMTS (v/v/v, 2/1/1) and 25 vol% DMTS 
(v/v/v, 2/3/3).
Materials and Electrochemical Characterization: 1H NMR spectra 
were collected on a Bruker DRX-400 instrument. The peaks of THF-d8 
(99.5% atom of D, δ = 1.73 and 3.58 ppm) were used for calibration. The 
electrochemical performance of Li-S batteries with different electrolytes 
was evaluated using CR2016 coin cell on Arbin BT2000 or Land CT2001a 
battery testers. Li foil was employed as anode. Celgard 2323 was used as 
the separator. The amount of electrolyte was controlled at 10 µL. Coin 
cell assembly was performed in an Ar-filled glove box with H2O and O2 
level below 0.1 ppm. To test cycling performance, the electrochemical 
voltage window for discharge/charge was set at 1.6–2.6 V. Cyclic 
voltammetry of Li-S coin cell in 25 vol% DMTS-containing electrolyte 
was performed at a scan rate of 0.05 mV s−1 from 2.8 to 1.6 V.
