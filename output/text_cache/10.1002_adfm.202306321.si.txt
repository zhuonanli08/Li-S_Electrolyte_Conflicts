Experimental Section  
Preparation of electrolytes: ZnI2 purchased from Adamas was added into the 
electrolyte (1.0 M solution of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) 
with 1 wt% LiNO3 in DME/DOL mixture (1:1, vol.)) and stirred for 20 min in an 
Ar-filled glove box to form the uniform solution. 
 
Materials and electrode preparations: The HCG was purchased from Suzhou 
Tanfeng Graphene Technology Co. Ltd. For the HCG/S preparation, the S was 
infiltrated into HCG using a melt-diffusion strategy at 160 °C for 24 h, forming 
well-contacted and homogeneous HCG/S composites. The cathode materials were 
prepared by mixing HCG/S, acetylene black, and polyvinylende fluoride (PVDF) 
binder in a weight ratio of 8 : 1 : 1 in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The slurry was 
then coated onto the aluminum foil and dried at 50 oC for 12 h. Afterwards, the dried 

<<PAGE 3>>
 
2
coated aluminum foil was punched into disks with a diameter of 14 mm for Li-S coin 
cell use, and the loading of the active-material was 1.3 to 1.5 mg cm-2. For Li2S6 
symmetric cells, the cathode materials were produced in the same procedure as Li-S 
cells, except for replacing the HCG-S composites with HCG. As for the pouch cells, a 
conductive HCG/S paper was used as the cathode. In a typical procedure, 100 mg 
HCG as the surface modifier and dispersant mixed with 1152.1 mg Na2S2O3 in NMP, 
ultrasonicated, and then magnetically stirred. HCl (29.2 ml, 5.0 M) solution was 
added dropwise into the above well-mixed solution under strong magnetic stirring and 
sonicated for 2 h to obtain a uniform HCG/S solution. Finally, the HCG/S composite 
was obtained by centrifugation, followed by drying overnight. Rectangles with a size 
of 90 mm × 120 mm were cut as the working cathodes for the Li-S pouch cells. 
 
Electrochemical measurements: All the cells were assembled in an Ar-filled 
glove box below 1.0 ppm. CR-2025 coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove 
box using the HCG/S-coated aluminum foil as the cathode, lithium foil as anode, and 
Celgard 2400 as the separator. The LSV cells are assembled using stainless steel as 
the electrodes. All the cells are assembled in an Ar-filled glove box with oxygen 
concentration below 1.0 ppm. 
The Li2S deposition experiment was measured with assembled cells using carbon 
paper as the cathode and lithium foil as the anode. In the cell assembly process, 20 𝜇L 
electrolyte containing 0.2 M Li2S6 was added on the carbon paper side and another 20 
𝜇L electrolyte with/without ZnI2 was dropped on the Li foil side. The cell was first 

<<PAGE 4>>
 
3
discharged at constant current to the 2.06 V and then discharged with the constant 
voltage at 2.05 V until the current was below 10−5 A for the nucleation and deposition 
of Li2S. 
The Li2S6 symmetric cells were assembled utilizing the sulfur-free HCG/S as the 
cathode and adding 40 𝜇L 0.2 M Li2S6 solution as the electrolyte. Another 40 𝜇L 
electrolyte with/without ZnI2 was dropped on the separator. The CV profiles of 
symmetric cells were tested at the scan rate of 10 mV s−1 between -1.0 and 1.0 V. 
The galvanotactic discharge-charge results of these cells were obtained using the 
LAND CT3002A battery testing system. The GITT was tested in the LAND 
CT3002A battery testing system at a constant current of 0.2 C with a relaxation time 
of 2 min every 5 min. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance 
spectra (EIS) were measured in the CHI660E electrochemical work station. The 
frequency range of the EIS test is between 100 kHz and 0.01 Hz with 5 mV 
amplitude. 
 
Characterizations: The morphologies of Li depositions and Li2S depositions 
were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7001F). The SEI 
components of Li-S cells were further characterized through X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS, AXIS, SUPRA+). Ultraviolet-visible spectrophotometry (UV, PE 
Lambda 950) was employed to detect the signal change of I−
, I3
− and Li2S6 in solution. 
TGA was measured at a heating rate of 10 °C min-1 with a N2 atmosphere using a 
TAQ 600 thermo-gravimetric analyzer.

Computational details 
Quantum chemical calculations were performed using the ORCA 5.0.1 program 
package. For the frontier molecular orbital energy, electrostatic energy, the overall 
reaction energy and the binding energy, B97-3c was first used to optimize the 
molecular structure for a stable configuration and then r2SCAN-3c was performed to 
calculate the single-point energy and output molecular orbital state, electrostatic 
energy state. The overall reaction energy change (∆E) was defined as the final single 
point energy difference between reactants and products, namely 
∆E = Eproducts − Ereactants 
For the binding energy, Eb was defined as 
Eb = Emolecular1+molecular2 −Emolecular1 −Emolecular2 
where the Emolecular1+molecular2, Emolecular1 and Emolecular2 refers to the final 
single point energy of the optimized configuration of the binding state of molecular 1 
with molecular 2, the separated state of molecular 1 and molecular 2, respectively. 
Analyses of free energies, binding energies and reaction energies were carried 
out with DFT by using Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP).[1] The 
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional of generalized gradient approximation 
(GGA) was used to describe electron-electron exchange correlations.[2-3] The projector 
augmented wave (PAW) potential was used to describe the electron-ion 
interactions.[4-5] 550 eV was chosen as the cutoff energy of the plane-wave basis set. 
The convergence criterion of energy was set to be 10−5 eV, and that of force was set to 
be 0.01 eV Å-1. A hexagonal 6 × 6 × 1 supercell with a vacuum slab of 20 Å in the

vertical (z) direction was constructed to model the graphene substrate. A 
Gamma-centered 3 × 3 × 1 k-point was sampled for the Brillouin zone integration. 
Van der Waals corrections were considered in this work. For the case of evolution of 
LiPSs without catalyzing of ZnI2, stepped reaction energy, Er was defined as 
following: 
Er = 2ELi2Sx/2_G − ELi2Sx_G −2ELi – EG 
where ELi2Sx/2 _G , ELi2Sx_G , ELi , EG are the total energy of Li2Sx/2 molecule 
adsorption on the graphene, Li2Sx molecule adsorption on the graphene, chemical 
potential of Li and total energy of graphene substrate, respectively. For the case of 
evolution of LiPSs catalyzed by ZnI2, Er was defined as 
Er = 2ELi2Sx/2_Zn_G − ELi2Sx_Zn_G − 2ELi − EZn_G 
where ELi2Sx/2_Zn_G, ELi2Sx_Zn_G and EZn_G are the total energy of Li2Sx/2 molecule 
bound with ZnI2 adsorption on the graphene substrate, Li2Sx molecule bound with 
ZnI2 adsorption on the graphene substrate, and ZnI2 adsorption on graphene, 
respectively.
