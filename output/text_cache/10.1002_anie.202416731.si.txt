Experimental Procedures 
1. Materials 
1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME), 1,2-Dibutoxyethane (DBE) and dipropyl ether (PE) were purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (TCI). 
All of these solvents were dried with 4 Å molecular sieves for two days in the Ar-filled glovebox at 25 ºC before use. Lithium 
bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) and lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI) salts were purchased form Dodochem (Suzhou) 
Ltd without any treatment. The ‘M’ in this work means molar salt in liter solvent. To synthesize sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN), PAN 
and Sulfur were mixed in an agate mortar at a mass ratio of 1:4. The mixture was heated in an argon-filled furnace at 450 °C for 6 h 
with a ramping rate of 10 °C min-1. Pyrene-4,5,9,10-tetraone (PTO) and perylene 3,4,9,10-tetracarboxylic dianhydride (PTCDA) were 
purchased commercially. Ketjen black (KB) was bought from Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. Poly (acrylic acid) 
(PAA) and Guar Gum (GG) were purchased from Aladdin and TCI, respectively. 
 
2. Preparation of electrodes 
The SPAN slurry was prepared by mixing SPAN (80 wt%), KB (10 wt%), PAA (5 wt%) and GG (5 wt%) in deionized water. The PTO 
slurry was prepared by mixing PTO (60 wt%), KB (30 wt%) and polyvinylidene fluoride binder (PVDF, 10 wt%), and the PTCDA electrode 
was prepared by casting the slurry consisting of PTCDA, KB, and PVDF at a weight ratio of 70: 20: 10 in N methyl-2-pyrrolidinone 
(NMP). These slurries were cast onto carbon-coated aluminum foil, dried at 80 or 120 °C for 12 h under vacuum. By controlling the 
coating thickness, the cathode electrode with different loading can be obtained. 
 
3. Material characterization 
Raman spectra of different samples was conducted on a HORIBA XploRA PLUS with 785 nm excitation wavelength. In-situ Raman 
spectra of Li||SPAN batteries with different electrolytes during discharge/charge processes were obtained by Raman spectroscopy 
(inVia Qontor, excitation wavelength, 785 nm). 17O and 19F nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were performed with Bruker 
AVANCE III 400 MHz instrument. The deuterated reagent used in NMR spectra is deuterium oxide (D2O) (17O), and 1 M trifluoroacetic 
acid (TFA) in D2O (19F). Infrared spectroscopy (IR) tests were obtained with Bruker Tensor Ⅱ Sample Compartment RT-DLaTGS. A 
Li||SPAN quartz cell was used for the in-situ UV-vis spectroscopy (Agilent Technologies, Cary 60). The micromorphology of Li metal 
was investigated by using field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (JEOL JSM-7900F). Focused Ion Beam Scanning 
Electron Microscope (FIB-SEM, CARL ZELSS Crossbeam340) was utilized to examine the uniformity of lithium metal deposition. The 
detailed interfacial characteristics were obtained by High-Resolution Transmission Electron Microscopy (HRTEM, Talos F200X G2). X-
ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) depth profiling was performed on the Krato AXIS Supra+. All peaks were charge referenced 
using C 1s binding energy of 284.8 eV. 
 
4. Electrochemical Measurement 
The electrochemical performance and characterization were tested by CR2032-type coin cells on the Land battery tester (CR3002A, 
Wuhan, China). All coin cells were fabricated in an argon-filled glovebox with O2 and H2O < 0.1 ppm. Celgard 2400 was used as 
separator.

of 10 mV. Li||Li symmetric cells are discharged and charged for ten cycles at 1.0 mA cm-2, and then subjected to the EIS tests in a wide 
temperature range from -40 to 40 °C for the calculation of activation energy.

The cyclic voltammetry (CV) of Li||Cu cells were carried on a solartron 1470E CellTest workstation: Li||Cu half cells at a scan rate of 
0.5 mV s-1. The first scan proceeds from the open circuit potential (OCV) to 0 V versus Li/Li+ (solid lines), whereas the following scans 
are between 1.0 and 0 V versus Li/Li+ (dashed lines). 
Li||Cu CEs: 12 mm diameter 500 μm Li foil, one layer of 16 mm celgard 2400 separator, 14 mm diameter of Cu foil and 40 μL electrolyte 
were used. For Aurbach method, the Cu surface was conditioned by plating 5 mAh cm-2 of Li and stripping to 1 V at 0.5 mA cm-2. Then 
a Li reservoir of 5 mAh cm-2 was plated onto Cu, followed by 10 cycles of Li plating and stripping at 0.5 mA cm-2 and 1 mAh cm-2. Finally, 
all Li on Cu was stripped to 1 V at 0.5 mA cm-2. For long term CEs, the half cells were cycled at 1 mA cm-2 and 1 mAh cm-2.  
Li deposition morphology on Cu was studied by depositing 5 mAh cm-2 of Li at 0.5 mA cm-2 in Li||Cu half cells. After Li deposition, the 
cells were disassembled and the Cu electrodes were washed with solvents. 
Li|| SPAN cells: 12 mm diameter Li foil (500μm for half cells and 50 μm for full cells), one layer of 16 mm celgard 2400 separator, 10 
mm diameter SPAN cathode (1.0 mg cm-2 for half cells and 5.0 mg cm-2 for full cells) and 40 μL electrolyte were used. For long term 
cycling, the half cells were cycled between 1.0 V and 3.0 V at different rates (1 C = 650 mAh g-1) discharge/charge current density (two 
cycles of activation at 0.5 C or 0.1 C). The full cells were cycled between 1.0 V and 3.0 V with 0.2 C (25 °C, 50 °C) or 0.05 C (-50 °C) 
charge/discharge current density (two cycles of activation at 0.1 C or 0.02 C). 
Li||SPAN pouch cells: For Ah-scale pouch cell, seven pieces of double-sided cathode (4.8 cm*5.8 cm, 3 mAh cm-2) and eight pieces of 
50 μm Li foil on Cu substrate (5 cm*6 cm) were used in a dry room at a dew point of -50 °C, whose volume of electrolyte (F-PE) was 
2.5 g Ah-1. Another single-layer pouch cell was assembled with single pieces of SPAN cathode and Li foil on Cu substrate. The 
specifications of the Ah-scale Li||SPAN pouch cell is provided in Table S6, respectively. 
 
5. Theoretical calculation 
All the molecular structures and complexes were optimized at the level of B97-3c. [69-71] The binding energy of various species were 
evaluated at the B3LYP-D3/aug-cc-pvtz level. [70-75] All DFT calculations were carried out with ORCA 5.0.3.[75-82] The CPCM is used to 
customize different solvents, the specific parameters are shown in Table S1. In addition, the diethyl ether (ε = 4.1) was used to represent 
the DBE solvent not explicitly included in quantum chemistry calculations. The intermolecular binding energies (∆E) between LiPS 
(Li2S/Li2S2) and different solvents are calculated through considering counterpoise correction. The conductor-like polarizable continuum 
model (CPCM) was used to present different electrolyte environment.[83] The calculation formula is as follows:  
∆E = Ecluster - ELiPS - Esolvents 
where Ecluster, ELiPS and Esolvents represent the total energy of the cluster, LiPS and solvents, respectively.  
The Gibbs free energy change (∆G) of LiPS-solvent complexes formation was calculated to compare the solubility differences. The 
∆G was defined as follows:  
∆G = G(solvent-LiPS) - G(solvent) - G(LiPS) 
The shermo program was used in the Gibbs free energy calculation.[84] 
The visualization of calculation results was utilizing Multiwfn 3.8,[85] VMD [86] and CYLview (http://www.cylview.org). The Gromacs 
2020.6 package with periodic boundary conditions was utilized to conduct molecular dynamics simulations.[87] The OPLS-AA force field 
was used for all solvent molecules. The molecular ratio of the corresponding electrolytes is displayed in Table S2. The LigParGen 
server was used to generated topology files, bonded and Lennard-Jones parameters.[88] The advanced restrained electrostatic potential 
(RESP2) atomic partial charges were used, which were generated by the Multiwfn program based on electrostatic potential charges.[89] 
The FSI- descriptions was obtained from previous work.[90] The first step of simulation is energy minimization using steepest descent
