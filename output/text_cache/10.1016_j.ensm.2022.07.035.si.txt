1.1. Raw materials 
2 
All the raw materials including sulfur (S), lithium sulfide (Li2S), lithium 
3 
bis(trifluromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), 1,3-dioxolane 
4 
(DOL), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), diisopropyl ether (DIPE), multi-walled carbon 
5 
nanotubes (MWCNTs), copper (Cu) foils, aluminum (Al) foils, polypropylene (PP) 
6 
membranes (Celgard 2400), poly(vinylidenefluoride) (PVDF), N-methylpyrrolidone 
7 
(NMP), lithium (Li) metal foils, graphene (G), and 2032 cell shells were purchased 
8 
from commercial sources and directly used without further purification.

1.2. Assembly and electrochemical test of Li–S pouch cells 
11 
1.2.1. Fabrication of sulfur cathodes and lithium anodes 
12 
Sulfur and MWCNTs were first mixed in a batch with a mass ratio of 7:3. The 
13 
mixture was then ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form 
14 
a homogeneous slurry. The obtained slurry was then coated on both sides of Al foils. 
15 
Sulfur cathodes were cut into 4.0 cm × 7.0 cm electrodes after being fully dried at 60°C 
16 
for 24.0 h. The sulfur content was 63 wt.% and the areal sulfur loading was 7.3 ± 0.2 
17 
mgS cm−2 on a single side. Lithium anodes were fabricated by rolling lithium metal foils 
18 
on both sides of Cu foils and then cut into electrodes of the same size as the sulfur 
19 
cathodes. The thickness of the lithium foil was 75 μm on a single side.

1.2.2. Assembly of Li‒S pouch cells 
1 
The separator used in pouch cells was Celgard 2400 PP membranes. The basic 
2 
electrolyte was 1.0 mol L−1 LiTFSI dissolved in a mixed solvent of DOL and DME 
3 
(v/v=1/3) with 5 wt% LiNO3 additives. 
4 
To assemble a Li‒S pouch cell at 4 Ah level, eight sulfur cathodes and nine lithium 
5 
anodes were stacked layer-by-layer separated by the PP membranes. All the electrodes 
6 
were set in parallel connection and then put in a package. Then, the basic electrolyte 
7 
was injected into the pouch cell and the electrolyte to sulfur ratio (E/S ratio) was 3.0 
8 
gelectrolyte mgS−1. After that, the pouch cell was vacuumed (‒65 kPa) and sealed for 
9 
further electrochemical tests. 
10 
 
11 
1.2.3. Electrochemical test of Li‒S pouch cells 
12 
The as-assembled Li–S pouch cells were rested for 12.0 h and then 
13 
galvanostatically cycled between 1.70‒2.55 V on a Land multichannel battery tester 
14 
(Wuhan Land Electronic Co., Ltd.). The current density was 0.025 C (1 C = 1000 mA 
15 
gS−1). The cycling test was stopped at the discharged state (stopped at 1.70 V) or the 
16 
charged state (stopped at 2.55 V) when the Li‒S pouch cells exhibited rapid capacity 
17 
decay. 
18

1.3. Failure analysis of 400 Wh kg−1 Li‒S pouch cells 
6 
1.3.1. Disassembly of Li‒S pouch cells 
7 
The Li–S pouch cells were disassembled via ceramic scissors within two hours by 
8 
the end of the electrochemical test. To remove the residual electrolyte and precipitated 
9 
species on electrodes for further characterizations, the electrodes were washed with 
10 
DIPE or DME until the washing solvent was colorless. Concretely, DIPE can only 
11 
dissolve lithium salts but cannot dissolve lithium polysulfides while DME can dissolve 
12 
both lithium salts and lithium polysulfides. Therefore, DIPE was used to remove the 
13 
residual lithium salts while DME was used to remove both lithium salts and lithium 
14 
polysulfides. Consequently, three kinds of cathodes and anodes (unwashed, DIPE 
15 
washed, and DME washed) were obtained at the charged state or the discharged state, 
16 
respectively. All the above procedures were conducted in an Ar-filled glove box with 
17 
water and oxygen contents below 0.1 ppm. 
18

1.3.3. Electrochemical tests of the cycled electrodes 
8 
Standard 2032 coin cell configuration was used to evaluate the electrochemical 
9 
performance of the cycled electrodes. The unwashed cathodes and anodes were 
10 
punched into small disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm and one side of the electrode was 
11 
scraped off. Fresh PP membranes were punched into disks with a diameter of 19.0 mm 
12 
and used as the separators. 
13 
The residual capacity of the cycled cathodes and anodes at the charged state or the 
14 
discharged state was first quantitatively evaluated in coin cells. The cycled electrodes 
15 
served as the cathode while a fresh lithium foil was used as the anode in the coin cells. 
16 
70 μL basic electrolyte was dropped on the unwashed cathodes or anodes while 5 μL 
17 
basic electrolyte was added on the fresh lithium anode. The Li | unwashed cathode cells 
18 
were discharged galvanostatically at 0.238 mA to 1.70 V, in which the areal current 
19 
density (0.183 mA cm−2) was the same as that in the Li‒S pouch cells. The discharge 
20 
capacity was calculated based on the sulfur loading of pouch cell (7.2 mgS cm−2). 
21 

<<PAGE 5>>
SI5 
Similarly, the Li | unwashed anode cells were galvanostatically charged at 0.238 mA to 
1 
1.00 V to make sure the residual active lithium in the cycled anodes was fully stripped. 
2 
The electrochemical kinetics of the cycled cathodes were further evaluated. A 
3 
cycled charged cathode and an uncycled fresh lithium foil were assembled in coin cells. 
4 
70 μL basic electrolyte was dropped on the cycled charged cathode and 5 μL basic 
5 
electrolyte was added on the uncycled lithium anode. The E/S ratio was 8.0 μL mgS−1 
6 
to ensure enough electrolyte. The coin cells were galvanostatically cycled between 
7 
1.70‒2.60 V at the current density of 0.025 C or 0.05 C. 
8 
Similarly, the cycling stability of the cycled anodes was evaluated. An uncycled 
9 
fresh sulfur cathode and a cycled charged anode were assembled in coin cells. The areal 
10 
loading of the uncycled fresh sulfur cathode was 7.2 mgS cm−2. 70 μL basic electrolyte 
11 
was dropped on the uncycled fresh sulfur cathode and 5 μL basic electrolyte was added 
12 
on the cycled charged anode. The E/S ratio was also 8.0 μL mgS−1 to ensure enough 
13 
electrolyte. The coin cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1.70‒2.60 V at the 
14 
current density of 0.025 C. All the electrochemical tests were performed on a Neware 
15 
battery test system (Shenzhen, China).

1.3.4. Reinjecting electrolyte into the cycled Li‒S pouch cells  
18 
Mixed solvent of DOL/DME (v/v=1/3) with or without 5 wt% LiNO3 additive was 
19 
prepared as the reinjected electrolyte, respectively. The cycling test of the Li‒S pouch 
20 
cells was stopped at the discharged state when the capacity of the pouch cells decayed 
21 
rapidly. The pouch cells were unsealed and then reinjected with the as-prepared 
22 

<<PAGE 6>>

electrolyte with or without LiNO3. The amount of the reinjected electrolyte followed 
1 
an E/S ratio = 1.0 gelectrolyte gS−1 (corresponding to 3.27 ± 0.09 g electrolyte for each 
2 
pouch cell). Then, the pouch cells were re-vacuumed (‒65 kPa) and sealed. The 
3 
reinjected Li‒S pouch cells were galvanostatically cycled again between 1.70‒2.55 V 
4 
on the Land multichannel battery tester at the same current density of 0.025 C after 
5 
resting 2.0 h.
