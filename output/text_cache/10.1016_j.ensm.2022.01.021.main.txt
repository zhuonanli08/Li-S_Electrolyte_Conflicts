a b s t r a c t 
Commercial application of lithium-sulfur batteries (LSBs) largely depends on precise regulation of complex sulfur 
redox reactions. Herein, polymer organosulfur compounds (POCs) are designed as promising redox mediators for 
the ﬁrst time. The mesoporous yolk-shell POCs with covalently linked short-chain sulfur are coated on commer- 
cial PP separators to regulate the redox behavior of sulfur. The short-chain sulfur could spontaneously interact 
with polysulﬁdes to reversibly regulate the electrochemical conversions of polysulﬁdes, aﬀord faster sulfur redox 
kinetics and improve the deposition of Li 2 S. POCs could also interact with lithium ions to stabilize the lithium- 
metal anode. Hence, the POC endows the commercial sulfur cathode with an outstanding rate performance of 
790 mA h g − 1 at 4.0 C and a higher capacity retention up to 87% within 1000 cycles at 2.0 C. The LSB with 
a high-sulfur-loading of 6.8 mg cm − 2 and a small electrolyte/sulfur ratio of 5.6 uL mg[S] − 1 is also synthesized, 
achieving an excellent capacity of 5.9 mA h cm − 2 after cycling. 
1. Introduction 
Lithium-sulfur batteries (LSBs) are regarded as the most promis- 
ing next-generation energy storage devices for commercial applica- 
tions (above 500 Wh kg − 1 ) due to their extremely high energy density 
(2600 Wh kg − 1 ), however, their long-chain polysulﬁdes intermediates 
are highly soluble in electrolytes, inducing the shuttle eﬀect and limit- 
ing greatly their practical applications [1–4] . Even so, the existence of 
long-chain polysulﬁdes could intrinsically adjust the neutralization and 
disproportionation reactions during the cycling process by improving 
the deposition behavior of Li 2 S and corresponding electrochemical per- 
formances [5–8] , this self-regulation eﬀect cannot be well controlled 
due to the continuous change of the local concentration and species 
of polysulﬁdes during the cycling process [ 9 , 10 ]. It is reported that re- 
dox mediators could eﬀectively regulate the electrochemical behavior of 
polysulﬁdes [11–13] . Meini et al. ﬁrstly explored metallocenes as redox 
mediators, which can eﬀectively reduce the delithiation overpotential of 
Li 2 S, thereby promoting the oxidation reaction of Li 2 S [14] . Similarly, 
most of the extrinsic redox mediators can only regulate a relatively sin- 
gle electrochemical reaction step, while the electrochemical reactions at 
other potentials still remain poor kinetics [ 15 , 16 ]. Hence, an ideal re-

dox mediator should reversibly interact with polysulﬁdes and possess 
stronger redox regulation capabilities for the overall electrochemical

Sulfur-containing compounds could be promising candidates to reg- 
ulate electrochemical behaviors of polysulﬁdes, because of their ex- 
pected aﬃnity and structure similarity to polysulﬁdes. As reported, 
small molecular organosulfur compounds (SMOCs) could spontaneously 
react with polysulﬁdes [20] . As redox mediators of LSBs, SMOCs could 
attend the electrochemical reactions of polysulﬁdes and improve their 
corresponding kinetics, achieving high electrochemical performances 
[21] . However, most of SMOCs are in liquid state and could dissolve 
in electrolytes, causing the shuttle eﬀect and the instability of the elec- 
trochemical processes [22] . Recently, polymer organosulfur compounds 
(POCs) have been reported to react reversibly with polysulﬁdes, making 
them promising as novel polysulﬁde redox mediators [ 23 , 24 ]. More im- 
portantly, POCs are more stable in the battery system than SMOCs, and 
they could regulate the redox behavior of polysulﬁdes more safely and 
eﬃciently without the presence of serious shuttle eﬀect. As new poly- 
sulﬁde redox mediators, it is imperative to explore the electrochemical 
mechanisms of POCs for high-performance LSBs.

TOF-SIM spectra, DFT calculation and in-situ XRD. 
2. Results and discussion 
Fig. S1 illustrates, schematically, the preparation process of sCA 
organosulfur compound and its modiﬁcation of PP fabric as the sepa- 
rator of LSBs. The sCA compound is fabricated by a self-sacriﬁced tem- 
plate method followed by an inverse sulfurization process [24] . Firstly, 
aminophenol-formaldehyde resin is polymerized and coated onto 
CNTs (Fig. S2a), generating a core-shell structured CNT/aminophenol- 
formaldehyde (CA) compound (Fig. S2b). As reported previously, the 
formed aminophenol-formaldehyde (AF) would hinder the transfer of 
the ammonia catalyst and the monomers to the interior, leading to the 
high polymerization degree of the external AF and the low polymer- 
ization degree of the internal resin. The AF with a high polymerization 
degree cannot be dissolved, while the oligomer of the internal AF resin 
can be etched with acetone solvent (Fig. S2c) [25] . Finally, the CA com- 
pound and sulfur powder are mixed and thermally annealed to generate 
a yolk-shell structured sCA compound with a diameter of ∼150 nm (Figs. 
S2d, S3 and 1 a). The STEM image and the corresponding element map- 
ping ( Fig. 1 b, c) show that S element is uniformly dispersed in the sCA 
compound. The sCA compound displays an amorphous structure except 
for the CNTs ( Fig. 1 d), implying that no sulfur powder is remaining in 
the sCA compound. For comparison, a carbonized yolk-shell structured 
CA (cCA) compound without the presence of sulfur is also prepared, and 
its morphology is similar to that of sCA (Fig. S4). 
Diﬀerent from CA and cCA compounds, the XRD pattern of sCA com- 
pound exhibits a broad peak from 15° to 30° (Fig. S5a), indicating that 
sCA has an amorphous structure. Besides, obvious S peaks in the sur- 
vey XPS spectrum of sCA (Fig. S5b) and additional C-S (400 cm − 1 ) and 
S-S (480 cm − 1 ) bonds in the Raman spectrum of sCA ( Fig. 1 e) conﬁrm 
the covalent linkage of sulfur element to the sCA polymer matrix [26] . 
In contrast to sulfur powder, the S 2p 1/2 and S 2p 3/2 peaks of sCA red 
shift to 164.5 and 163.3 eV ( Fig. 1 f), further demonstrating the covalent 
linkage of sulfur chains to the polymer matrix [27] . 
As shown in the time of ﬂight-secondary ion mass (TOF-SIM) spec- 
trum ( Fig. 1 g), the presence of the fragments of C 2 S − ( m/z = 56), COS − 
( m/z = 60), S 2 
− ( m/z = 64) and S 3 
− ( m/z = 96) in the sCA compound 
indicates that the covalently linked sulfur should be in the form of short- 
chains (S x , x ≤ 3) [28] , leading to an aﬃnity and structure similarity to 
polysulﬁdes. Longer-chain sulfur species are not observed in the sCA 
compound even if the x-axis of TOF-SIM spectrum is extended (Fig. S6). 
As shown in the TGA curve of the sCA (Fig. S7), the mass-loss below 
420 °C is 6.1%, and the total sulfur in sCA is 49.2% based on the ele- 
mental analysis. Therefore, the calculated ratio of the bridge sulfur to 
the terminal sulfur is ca. 0.14 in the sulfur chain. The ratio should be 
0 if the sulfur chain is -C-S-S-C; while the ratio should be 0.5 if the sul- 
fur chain is -C-S-S-S-C. It means that the prepared sulfur-chain of sCA 
should contain less than 3 sulfur atoms. Fig. S8 gives a probable chemi- 
cal structure of sCA. Besides, the sCA compound exhibits a mesoporous 
structure with a speciﬁc surface area of 72.1 m 2 g − 1 and an average 
pore diameter of 16.7 nm ( Fig. 1 h). Thus, the mesoporous yolk-shell 
structure would physically impede the diﬀusion of polysulﬁdes, thereby 
increasing the residence time of the polysulﬁde in sCA. Consequently, 
the short-chain sulfur structure could have more time to regulate the 
conversion of polysulﬁdes. sCA, as a redox mediator, is coated onto a 
PP fabric separator with the help of a PVDF binder, generating a sCAP 
separator ( Fig. 1 i). As shown in Fig. S9, 6 𝜇m-thick sCA is evenly loaded 
on the PP separator with a small sCA loading of 0.3 mg cm − 2 . As the sCA 
compound could be tightly attached onto the PP separator, the resultant 
sCAP separator is structurally stable even after folding and unfolding 
processes ( Fig. 1 j). More importantly, the short-chain sulfur structure 
of sCA is well retained in the sCAP separator (Fig. S10). As shown in 
three-dimensional (3D) TOF-SIM mapping, the S 2 − and S 3 − fragments 
are uniformly distributed in the sCAP separator ( Fig. 1 k). These results 
show that the redox behavior of polysulﬁdes could be well regulated in 
the whole sCAP separator [29] . Additionally, the contact angle of PP sep- 
arator reduces obviously after coating with the sCA compound ( Fig. 1 l), 
which facilitates the transport of Li + and promotes the electrochemical 
kinetics. 
The reaction mechanisms of sCA with polysulﬁdes are investigated 
in detail on the basis of the expected aﬃnity and structure similarity of 
short-chain sulfurs to polysulﬁdes ( Fig. 2 ). For both sCA and cCA, the 
color and the UV-vis absorption intensity of the Li 2 S 4 solution are weak 
after the adsorption ( Fig. 2 a), due to the N heteroatoms derived from 
the aminophenol-formaldehyde matrix [30] . However, the strong inter- 
action of the short-chain sulfur of sCA with polysulﬁdes could remove 
the soluble Li 2 S 4 more eﬃciently, leading to a nearly colorless Li 2 S 4 
solution and a much weaker absorption intensity of Li 2 S 4 than cCA. 
The Li 1s and S 2p spectra of Li 2 S 4 after the adsorption with sCA fur- 
ther demonstrate the strong interaction between sCA and polysulﬁdes 
( Fig. 2 b, c). The Li 1s spectrum of Li 2 S 4 could be divided into two peaks: 
the strong peak represents the Li-S bond and the another peak of Li-O 
bond is caused by the oxidation in air [31] . After the adsorption with 
sCA, Li 1s peak blue shifts, and two new peaks representing Li-N bond 
and C-S x -Li bond appear, while the Li-S bond disappears. These results 
indicate that the short-chain sulfur in sCA could spontaneously react 
with Li 2 S 4 to form the C-S x -Li chains [32] . Besides, both the S T − 1 /S B 0 
signal (S T 
− 1 and S B 
0 represent typical terminal sulfur and bridge sul- 
fur, respectively) of Li 2 S 4 and the C-S/S-S bond of sCA move to higher 
binding energies after the adsorption of Li 2 S 4 , demonstrating the spon- 
taneous reaction between sCA and Li 2 S 4 [33] . 
The reaction behavior of sCA with polysulﬁdes is further investigated 
by the Li 2 S 6 symmetric cell measurements ( Fig. 2 d). Compared to cCA, 
the CV curve of sCA exhibits two obvious pairs of redox peaks with much 
stronger current response at ± 0.44 and ± 0.8 V, which are assigned to 
the promoted electrochemical oxidation/reduction of Li 2 S 6 [34] . There 
is nearly no current response in the absence of Li 2 S 6 in the sCA com- 
pound. It implies that the spontaneous and reversible regulation process 
of the short-chain sulfur in sCA with polysulﬁdes boosts the Li 2 S 6 con- 
version eﬀectively. Even at varied scan rates, the sCA compound can 
also regulate the redox conversion of Li 2 S 6 well (Fig. S11). As expected, 
the spontaneous reaction should regulate the redox behavior of polysul- 
ﬁdes, thereby suppressing the shuttle eﬀect and promoting the electro- 
chemical kinetics of polysulﬁdes. 
The nucleation characterization of Li 2 S is a powerful method for re- 
vealing the redox regulation ability of sCA [35] . Both sCA and cCA ex- 
hibit sharp peaks ascribed to the nucleation process of Li 2 S ( Figs. 2 e, 
S12). However, sCA exhibits a much faster Li 2 S nucleation process 
(3510 s) and a larger nucleation capacity (313.7 mA h g − 1 ) than those 
(4190 s and 260.9 mA h g − 1 ) of cCA, demonstrating that the sCA com- 
pound could accelerate the conversion of polysulﬁdes well, which is also 
supported by the deposition morphology of Li 2 S. As shown in Fig. 2 f,

To further conﬁrm the electrochemical mechanisms of POCs, the 
ﬁrst-principles calculations on the basis of the density functional theory 
(DFT) and in-situ XRD patterns are used to substantiate the spontaneous 
interaction of sCA toward polysulﬁdes ( Fig. 3 ). Fig. 3 a shows six inter- 
mediate states in the consecutive reaction process between polysulﬁdes 
and sCA. First, S 8 is adsorbed on the N active site by N-S bonds; and 
lithium ions are inserted into the ring structure of S 8 with the assistance 
of adjacent short-chain sulfur to form Li 2 S 8 molecules, accompanying 
with newly formed C-S x -Li bonds between Li 2 S 8 and short-chain sulfur 
[39] . Due to the successive interaction between sCA and Li 2 S n ( n = 1, 
2, 4, 6, 8), the chain length of Li 2 S n becomes shorter, conﬁrming the 
regulation eﬀect of short-chain sulfur on the conversion of polysulﬁdes 
[40] . Based on the DFT analysis, the regulation of polysulﬁde conver- 
sion should be reversible. For comparison, in the absence of sCA, there is 
nearly no chemical interaction between Li 2 S n and the carbon substrate 
to facilitate the polysulﬁdes conversion ( Fig. 3 b). 
The reversible regulation eﬀect is further conﬁrmed by the in situ 
XRD patterns. The initial detected peaks are assigned to the character- 
istic peaks of sulfur [41] , which become weaker in the upper-discharge 
plateau during the discharge process ( Fig. 3 c). Simultaneously, the peaks 
of polysulﬁdes appear at around 24-25° [42] , and shift slightly toward 
higher 2 𝜃angles with gradually decreasing intensities in the lower- 
discharge plateau. Moreover, a new characteristic peak of Li 2 S at ∼27°
emerges along with the weaker polysulﬁdes peaks, indicating that the 
Li 2 S deposition occurs at the very early discharge stage [43] . Subse- 
quently, the intensity of Li 2 S continuously increases and ﬁnally reaches 
a maximum at the fully discharged state. During the charge process, 
the Li 2 S is progressively oxidized to polysulﬁdes, and then transformed 
to elemental sulfur close to the end of the charge [44] . These results 
demonstrate that sCA could reversibly regulate the redox behavior of 
polysulﬁdes and improve the utilization of sulfur, which are also con- 
sistent with the results of XPS and CV analysis ( Fig. 2 b–d). As shown 
in the in situ XRD analysis of the battery with the cCAP separator (Fig. 
S15), with the decrease of the discharge voltage, the peak of the ele- 
mental sulfur disappears and the peaks of polysulﬁdes appear at around 
24-25°, but the intensities of the polysulﬁde peaks are much lower than 
those of the battery with the sCA-modiﬁed separator. Furthermore, at 
the end of the discharge process, no obvious Li 2 S peak is observed. This 
phenomenon further conﬁrms the advantage of the sCA on regulating 
the sulfur redox reactions. Here, the sCA serves as the insoluble redox 
mediators to promote the electrochemical conversion of sulfur. During 
the process, several bonds are formed between sCA and polysulﬁdes, 
making the conversion process of the sulfur diﬀerent from that with- 
out catalysts. The DFT calculations show that the free energies for Li 2 S n 
species with sCA are much lower than those with the carbon cloth sub-

Thanks to the short-chain sulfur, the sCA compound could not only 
regulate the redox behavior of polysulﬁdes, but also stabilize the lithium 
metal anode ( Fig. 4 ). As shown in the galvanostatic discharge/charge 
(GDC) curves of symmetric Li-Li batteries at 1 mA cm − 2 with a capacity 
of 1 mA h cm − 2 ( Fig. 4 a), the batteries with PP and cCAP separators 
lose their stabilities quickly after 50 and 100 h cycling. As expected, the 
battery with the sCAP separator exhibits the most stable cycling perfor- 
mances at both 1 and 2 mA cm − 2 ( Figs. 4 a, S16), and its overpotential 
is as small as 8 mV after 300 h cycling ( Fig. 4 a), indicating that sCA 
could stabilize lithium metal more eﬀectively than cCA [46] , which is 
also supported by the result of Li-Cu batteries. As shown in Fig. 4 b, the 
coulombic eﬃciencies of PP and cCAP batteries decay obviously after 
only 50 cycles at 1 mA cm − 2 with the capacity of 1 mA h cm − 2 . In con- 
trast, the coulombic eﬃciency of sCAP battery remains stable at over 
99% within 100 cycles. 
Moreover, the sCAP battery displays the smoothest voltage dip with 
an ultralow overpotential of only 52 mV ( Fig. 4 c), much smaller than 
those of cCAP battery (76 mV) and bare PP battery (131 mV). The N 
and O heteroatoms in sCA and cCA could increase the aﬃnity to lithium 
ions and make the uniform distribution of lithium ions, thereby reduc- 
ing the nucleation overpotential of lithium ions [47] . More importantly, 
the short-chain sulfur in the polymer matrix of sCA could also inter- 
act with lithium ions, further reducing the nucleation overpotential and 
stabilizing the lithium metal anode. Therefore, sCA exhibits the small- 
est charge transport and interface impedance, which could be reduced 
further after cycling, because the interaction of sCA with lithium ions 
could decrease the interface impedance and charge transport impedance 
( Figs. 4 d, S17 and Table S1) [48] . As expected, the lithium deposition 
morphology is improved. After the continuous stripping and plating of 
lithium metal with cycles, the lithium metal surface of sCAP battery still 
remains smooth, and no obvious dendrites are observed ( Figs. 4 g, S18). 
For comparison, the surface of the lithium metal in the PP battery turns 
broken and a large number of dendrites are formed ( Fig. 4 e) [49] , while 
huge spherical-like dendrites are formed in the cCAP battery ( Fig. 4 f) 
due to the interaction between lithium ions and N and O heteroatoms of 
the polymer matrix [50] . All these results demonstrate that sCA eﬀec- 
tively stabilizes the lithium metal interface and improves the stability 
of the lithium metal anode. 
The eﬀect of the redox regulation on polysulﬁdes is also investigated 
by the kinetics analysis of batteries with the sCAP separator ( Fig. 5 ). 
The CV curves with diﬀerent separators, all exhibit two cathodic peaks 
and one anodic peak, constituting the reversible redox process of sulfur 
cathodes ( Fig. 5 a). Compared to cCAP and PP batteries, sCAP battery 
displays the highest onset and peak voltages in the discharge process, 
as well as the lowest onset and peak voltages in the charge process, 
conﬁrming that sCA has the promising ability to eﬀectively enhance the 
electrochemical kinetics by regulating the electrochemical behavior of 
polysulﬁdes [51] . Additionally, the CV curves of sCAP battery exhibit a 
smaller polarization than those of the other two at varied scan rates from 
0.05 to 0.35 mV s − 1 ( Figs. 5 b, S19a and S19b), further demonstrating 
that sCA promotes the electrochemical kinetics of sulfur cathode.

Beneﬁting from the eﬀective polysulﬁdes conversion regulation and 
lithium metal stabilization of sCA, the LSB with the sCAP separator ex- 
hibits outstanding electrochemical performances ( Fig. 6 ). As shown in 
the GDC proﬁles at 0.2 C (Fig. S20), the LSB with the sCAP separa- 
tor typically displays two discharge plateaus and one charge plateau, 
which agrees well with its CV curves. Additionally, almost no overpo- 
tential does exist in the discharge/charge curves, demonstrating that 
sCA could eﬀectively boost the electrochemical kinetics of the LSB by 
regulation of the polysulﬁde conversion [55] . Moreover, the LSB with 
the sCAP separator exhibits the best cyclability compared to those with 
cCAP and PP separators at a low current of 0.5 C and a high current of 
1.0/2.0 C ( Fig. 6 a–c). Besides, they also exhibit a much better cyclabil- 
ity than those batteries with bare PVDF-modiﬁed separators, conﬁrming 
that the positive functions derive from the sCA rather than PVDF (Fig. 
S21). Additionally, the better cyclability of the LSB with the cCA (ca. 
11% N content) modiﬁed separator, compared to the LSB with the PP 
separator, is attributed to the N doping. Furthermore, the LSB with sCA 
(ca. 5.6% N content) modiﬁed separator has much better cyclabilities 
than those with the cCA modiﬁed separator, which is ascribed to the 
short-chain sulfur. The signiﬁcant improvement derives from the sCA 
modiﬁed separator rather than cCA modiﬁed separator, indicating that 
the short-chain sulfur plays a major role in improving the capacity and 
cyclability with cycles. Especially at 2.0 C, even after 1000 cycles, the 
reversible capacity still remains 824.4 mA h g − 1 with a capacity decay 
ratio as low as 0.013% per cycles compared to the ﬁrst cycle capacity of 
947.6 mA h g − 1 , much better than those reported previously (Table S2). 
As reported, sCA could serve as a novel cathode for high-performance 
LSBs [ 23 , 24 ]. In the present work, when a LSB is assembled with the 
sCAP separator in the absence of the sulfur cathode, its reversible ca- 
pacity is just ∼40 mA h g − 1 (Fig. S22), demonstrating that the role of 
sCA is to regulate the conversion of polysulﬁdes and stabilize the lithium 
metal rather than the capacity contribution of sCA. 
Furthermore, the LSB in the presence of sCAP exhibits excellent 
rate performances ( Fig. 6 d). Its capacity is 1196.5, 1026.2, 948.3 and 
871 mA h g − 1 at 0.2, 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 C, respectively. Even at 4.0 C, the 
capacity can still reach 790 mA h g − 1 . When the current density returns 
to 0.2 C, its capacity can well recover to 1073.9 mA h g − 1 , implying the 
outstanding rate performance. Their corresponding GDC proﬁles keep 
similar at varied current densities of 0.2 C to 4.0 C (Fig. S23), further 
indicating the exceptional electrochemical kinetics [56] . 
Encouraged by the marvelous electrochemical performances, a sCAP 
LSB with high sulfur loading and lean electrolyte is prepared ( Fig. 6 e). 
After cycling, the capacities can remain 5.9 and 4.0 mA h cm − 2 at high 
sulfur loadings of 6.8 and 4.6 mg cm − 2 with small electrolyte/sulfur ra- 
tios of 5.6 and 8.3 uL mg[S] − 1 , respectively. All the capacities are higher 
than the commercial lithium-ion batteries standard of 3.0 mA h cm − 2 . 
The corresponding GDC proﬁles with the low electrolyte/sulfur ratio 
of 5.6 uL/mg[S] almost keep well consistent, indicating an outstanding 
practical application ability ( Fig. 6 f). Even after 200 cycles, the capacity 
still holds at 3.6 mA h cm − 2 with a sulfur loading of 4.6 mg cm − 2 (Fig. 
S24).

and after 200 cycles at 0.5 C. As shown in Fig. 7 a, the fresh surface of 
sulfur cathode is ﬂat and smooth, however, many big cracks appear af-

to the serious shuttle eﬀect of polysulﬁdes as conﬁrmed by the large 
amount of anchored polysulﬁdes on the surface of PP separator towards 
Li anode side ( Fig. 7 e, f) [57] . cCAP separator does not eﬃciently reduce 
the formation of cracks ( Fig. 7 c), and polysulﬁdes could be observed on 
the surface of cCAP separator towards Li anode side ( Fig. 7 g). Obviously, 
due to the regulation of the redox behavior of polysulﬁdes, the sulfur 
cathode surface with the sCAP separator is still smooth and almost no 
breakage is observed ( Fig. 7 d). Moreover, no obvious polysulﬁdes are 
seen in the back of the sCAP separator ( Fig. 7 h), demonstrating that 
the shuttle eﬀect has been eﬀectively suppressed by sCA. Another posi-

3. Conclusions 
To overcome the disadvantages of small-molecule organosulfur com- 
pounds, for the ﬁrst time, a novel and promising polymer organosul- 
fur compounds (POCs) redox mediators are synthesized to regulate the 
electrochemical behavior of polysulﬁdes. Mesoporous yolk-shell struc- 
tured sCA with short-chain sulfur is synthesized and then coated on the 
PP separator as POCs redox mediators to promote the performance of 
LSBs. On one hand, N heteroatoms in the polymer matrix of sCA could 
chemically anchor polysulﬁdes and the short-chain sulfur could sponta- 
neously react with polysulﬁdes, precisely regulating the redox behavior 
of polysulﬁdes. On the other hand, sCA could interact with lithium ions 
to promote the uniform distribution of lithium ions and further stabilize 
lithium metal. Due to the dual redox regulation eﬀect of sCA, the sCAP 
LSB achieves a high rate capacity of 790 mA h g − 1 at 4.0 C and a high 
capacity retention of 87% within 1000 cycles at 2.0 C. Besides, a battery 
with a high sulfur loading of 6.8 mg cm − 2 and a small electrolyte/sulfur

This work demonstrates a novel and general approach for enhancing the 
sulfur redox kinetics for high-performance LSBs, which can be extended 
to sodium-sulfur batteries, magnesium-sulfur batteries, and so on.
