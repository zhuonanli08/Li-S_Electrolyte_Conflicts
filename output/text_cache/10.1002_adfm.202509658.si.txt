Additional notes: As shown in Figure S3a, the 7Li chemical shift for all four weakly solvating electrolytes are lower than that of the 2 M LiFSI/DME electrolytes, suggesting reduced electron density around Li+ and a transition towards more FSI− coordination, due to the weaker solvation abilities of all four solvents compared to DME. Among the four solvents, solvent B and solvent C are derived from the terminal and middle methylation of solvent A, respectively. However, their solvation abilities are not diminished compared to that of solvent A. It is worth noting that solvent D derived from simultaneously methylating both terminal and central carbons of solvent A, and it exhibits weaker solvation ability than solvent A due to steric hindrance effect. Since the steric hindrance effect is enhanced by the methylation of solvent A, the S-N-S stretching vibration peak of FSI− of the 2 M LiFSI/D electrolyte exhibits a blue shift compared to that of the 2 M LiFSI/A electrolyte, which favors the formation of anion-rich solvation structures (Figure S3b, Supporting Information).

Figure S27. The binding energies of different polysulfide species (Li2S4, Li2S6 and Li2S8) with various solvents (denoted as X for DME, A, B, C, and D).

Additional notes: The binding energy was evaluated based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations performed using the DMol3 package [1]. All calculations were conducted with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation functional within the framework of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), with all atoms fully relaxed [2]. An all-electron double numerical basis set with polarization functions was employed for this study. For geometry optimization, the energy and force convergence thresholds were set to less than 10-5 Ha and 0.002 Ha Å⁻¹, respectively. 
The binding energy (Eb) is defined according to the following equation:
Eb= Esolvent+LiPSs - Esolvent - E LiPSs
Where Esolvent+LiPSs is the total energy of LiPSs adsorbed onto the solvent, Esolvent is the energy of the solvent, and ELiPSs is the energy of the LiPSs.

[1] a) B. Delley, J. Chem. Phys. 1990, 92, 508; b) B. Delley, J. Chem. Phys. 2000, 113, 7756
[2] J. P. Perdew, K. Burke, M. Ernzerhof, Phys. Rev. Lett. 1996, 77, 3865.
