Figure S2: Comparison of coin cells with and without perforated current collectors at the cathode side. Samples were measured 
with the same amount of electrolyte due to volumetric restrictions in the coin cell housing. Because the same absolute electrolyte 
volume was used for cathodes with different sulfur loadings, the cells exhibit different E/S ratios. The observed differences may 
therefore contain contributions from polysulfide concentration and electrolyte transport properties and cannot be assigned 
uniquely to current-collector perforation. 
Figure S1: Microscope image of the perforated current collector without (left) and with coating (right).

Figure S3: Raman spectra acquired with a Raman microscope (Renishaw inVia, 532 nm, 100 % 50 mW, 5s exposure time, 3 
accumulations, Lens: Leica x50 L) at three spatially separated locations of a freshly assembled operando cell containing 
electrolyte E1 (DOL/DME (1:1 vol.), 1 M LiTFSI, 2 wt% LiNO₃). At each location, spectra were recorded through a perforation 
(‘Hole’, top) and on the adjacent coated current-collector land (‘Current collector’, bottom). Raw spectra (left) exhibit different 
fluorescence backgrounds, whereas the background-corrected spectra (right) reproduce the characteristic Raman band positions 
and relative spectral features across all three locations and both sampling geometries. This comparison supports the spatial 
robustness of the spectral fingerprints in the freshly assembled cell but does not imply complete electrode-wide equivalence 
during operando cycling. 
 
 
Figure S4: Z-Hit validation before and after drift and time correction for electrolyte E1. Top: Voltage profile. Middle: Residuals of 
the modulus without correction. Bottom: Residuals of the modulus with correction. The residuals for low frequencies and high 
voltage drift are significantly reduced.
