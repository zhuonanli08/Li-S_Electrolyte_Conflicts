A B S T R A C T
To overcome the issue of the shuttle eﬀect of the lithium-sulfur (Li-S) battery upon repeated cycle process, in this
work, lithium ﬂuoride (LiF) as a coating to form a crosslinked network structure with aramid paper (AP), which
inhibits the diﬀusion and dissolution of polysulﬁdes. LiF interacts with the electrolyte to form an artiﬁcial solid
electrolyte interface (SEI) membrane that hinders the transport of polysulﬁdes; The porous structure of the AP
interlayer allows the LiF/CNT slurry to adhere tightly. The Li-S battery assembled with LiF/AP interlayer dis-
played a high discharge capacity of 885 mAh/g at 1C after 200 cycles, with a low attenuation rate of 0.0302 %
per-cycle, and the speciﬁc capacity of the Li-S battery using the LiF/AP interlayer at 4C is 693 mAh/g. Our
researchs are expected to introduce a new generation of high performance Li-S batteries based on a economical
and eﬀective strategy of LiF/AP composite interlayer.
1. Introduction
In order to cope with the large consumption of fossil fuels and the
ensuing environmental pollution, researchers have been working on the
research of green and eﬃcient electrochemical energy storage tech-
nologies in recent years [1,2]. Li-S batteries have attracted much at-
tention because of their advantages such as high energy density
(2600Wh/kg) and high theoretical speciﬁc capacity (1675 mA h/g)
[3,4]. And sulfur (S) is a promising cathode active material because it
has the advantages of rich reserves and low price compared with other
active materials [5,6]. Despite the many advantages of Li-S batteries,
defects such as the “shuttle eﬀect” and the insulation of S hinder their
large-scale application [7].
Recently, numerous eﬀorts have been improved the electrochemical
performance of Li-S batteries by forming composite cathode materials
with elemental sulfur and sulﬁdes, oxides, carbon materials, and

In this paper, our group found a simple way of modifying AP in-
terlayer by LiF. Compared with pristine Li-S batteries, LiF/AP interlayer
Li-S battery can eﬀectively reduce the impact of shuttle eﬀect and im-
prove the cycle ability (Fig. 1). The lower barrier energy of LiF is able to
make Li+ ion spread over the surface of LiF [12,13], simultaneously,
the addition of LiF can enhance the stability of the SEI ﬁlm and improve
the electrochemical performance of the Li-S battery [14]. Compared
with other substrate materials, the AP interlayer has better conductivity
[15] and high temperature resistance [16], which can improve the
speciﬁc discharge capacity of Li-S batteries. On the other hand,the AP
interlayer with a 3D porous structure can not only contain a large
amount of polysulﬁdes, but also anchor LiF slurry [17,18]. It is sa-
tisfactory that the novel LiF composite interlayer prepared in this re-
search can provide better cycle stability and electrochemical perfor-
mance for Li-S batteries.
2. Experimental
2.1. Preparation of LiF/AP interlayer
Preparation of LiF/AP interlayer consists of two steps, the ﬁrst step
is to prepare AP interlayer. The para-aramid chopped ﬁbers were im-
mersed in 3% sodium dodecyl benzene sulfonate (SDBS) solution for
30 min, then the corresponding proportion of para-aramid pulp ﬁbers
were weighed into 1% polyoxyethylene PEO, average Mv = ∼12,000
solution and stirred for 20 min. The above two suspensions were mixed
and MWCNTs, sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), polyacrylamide (APAM)
were added for high-speed shearing for 3 h. After vacuum ﬁltered and
hot pressing treatment, the AP interlayer was obtained.
The second step is the coating LiF on the AP interlayer. In a typical
slurry method, 40 wt% MWCNTs, 50 wt% LiF, 10 wt% polyvinylidene
ﬂuoride (PVDF) were added to the agate tank, then the mixture was
milled at 150 r/min for 6 h to obtain uniformly dispersed LiF/CNT
slurry. Finally, we obtained a LiF/AP interlayer by vacuum drying for
24 h after coating the LiF/CNT slurry on the AP interlayer.
2.2. Assembling of cell and electrochemical measurements
In order to prepare the working cathode, a uniform slurry con-
taining 70 wt% weighing sulfur (S), 10 wt% MWCNTs, 10 wt% carbon
black (CB), and 10 wt% PVDF dispersed in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone
(NMP) was cast onto aluminum current collector. Then the coated
aluminum current collector was dried at 70 °C under vacuum overnight
to obtain a sulfur cathode. By calculation, the area loading of sulfur was
1.8 mg/cm2, and thickness of sulfur cathodes was 0.35 mm.
The electrochemical performance was carried out by assembling
CR2025-type coin cells with Li metal foil as the reference electrode
[19]. The cells were assembled with LiF/AP interlayer and AP inter-
layer in an argon-ﬁlled glove box (MBRAUN LABSTAR, Germany),
Celgard-2300 ﬁlm was used as the separator and 1.0 M LiTFSI in a
mixed solvent of DOL:DME (1:1,vol.) with 1.0 % LiNO3 was utilized as
the electrolyte. Typically, 1.5 mg of S requires the addition of 50 ul of
electrolyte. Then place the assembled cells at room temperature for
24 h, and cycled between 0.05 and 4 C with a battery tester (CT-3080-
5V3A-S1). The cyclic voltammetry (CV 0.1 mV s −1, 1.6–2.8 V) and the
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS 0.01 - 105 Hz, 5 mV)
studies were carried out with a electrochemical workstation [20]
(PARSTAT 3000A).
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 2(a)-(b) shows MWCNTs have a straight linear hollow structure,
demonstrating that MWCNTs are easily dispersed and facilitate electron
transport. As shown in Fig. 2(c)-(d), the MWCNTs are woven into a
highly interconnected network structure, we can clearly observe that
LiF was distributed among the MWCNTs. Fig. 2(e) shows the AP in-
terlayer has a 3D porous structure that anchors soluble polysulﬁdes and
accelerates the penetration of LiF/CNT slurry. The LiF/AP interlayer
side surface was exhibited from the SEM image in Fig. 2(f), LiF/CNT
slurry adheres tightly to the AP interlayer and the thickness of LiF/AP
interlayer is 0.55 mm. Simultaneously, we performed an Energy Dis-
persive Spectroscopy (EDS) test on the LiF/AP interlayer to characterize
the existence of the F, O, C element in the composite interlayer.
Fig. 2(g)-(h) demonstrates that LiF reacts with the electrolyte to
form a dense sol layer, preventing polysulﬁde from diﬀusing to the
cathode, which can explain the better cycling performance of the LiF/
AP interlayer Li-S battery. After 80 cycles, LiF/CNT slurry on the LiF/
AP interlayer surface shows no signs of falling oﬀ(Fig. 2(i)-(g)), in-
dicating that the interlayer not only has good mechanical properties,
but also has a long service life. Therefore, adding the main component
(LiF) of the SEI ﬁlm can supplement the Li+ ions consumed in the SEI
ﬁlm formation process, which is advantageous for maintaining the
stability of the SEI layer and improving the electrochemical perfor-
mance of the battery.

In order to simulate the migration of polysulﬁde in the electrolyte,
we disassembled the three Li-S batteries in the discharged state after 80
cycles, and the cathodes were soaked in electrolyte. It can be seen from
Fig. 3(g) that the electrolytes of the cathodes doped with MWCNTs, AP
interlayer and LiF/AP interlayer are brown, yellow and colorless, re-
spectively [33]. The cathode of the LiF/AP interlayer remains trans-
parent during the soaking, indicating that the LiF coating prevents the
diﬀusion of higher-order polysulﬁdes to the anode [34], and UV–vis
absorption spectras further proves the previous view (Fig. 3(h)). The
polysulﬁde has a wavelength in the range of about 260–300 nm, and the
peak at 280 nm is attributed to S8 or S6
2−[35]. In the absence of LiF
coating, the AP interlayer can intercept the diﬀused polysulﬁde and
reuse the active material; but the simple mechanical barrier is not
available to hinder the accumulation of polysulﬁde [36]. The absence
of the peak at 280 nm after coating LiF suggest that LiF can inhibit the
formation of polysulﬁdes.
To better understand the eﬀects of composite interlayer on the
electron transfer of the Li-S battery, the kinetic analysis of the LiF/AP
are studied by using CV tests. Fig. 4 shows the voltammogram proﬁles
of LiF/AP interlayer Li-S battery [37]. There are two typical char-
acteristic peaks at 2.05 and 1.94 V, corresponding to the formation of
high-order polysulﬁde Li2Sn (4 ≤n ≤8) and low-order polysulﬁde
(Li2S2, Li2S) [38], respectively. The oxidation peak appears around
2.49 V is corresponds to the conversion of Li2S2 /Li2S to S and poly-
sulﬁdes [39–41]. On the one hand, the area of the second cycle scan
curve is larger than the area of the ﬁrst cycle scan curve due to the
activation of S [42], and LiF/AP interlayer Li-S battery generates SEI
ﬁlm during the ﬁrst scan. On the other hand, the second to fourth cycle
scan curves are substantially coincident, indicating that the LiF/AP
interlayer Li-S battery has good reversibility [43,44]. The above results
show that the LiF/AP interlayer can not only maintain the discharge
speciﬁc capacity of the Li-S battery better, but also avoid the inﬂuence
of the shuttle eﬀect on the cathode.
In order to further understand the eﬀect of composite interlayers on
the electrochemical performance of Li-S batteries, we performed gal-
vanastatic charge-dischargee on three diﬀerent Li-S batteries at dif-
ferent rates (0.05 C–4 C). Fig. 5(a) shows the initial voltage proﬁles of
the LiF/AP interlayer Li-S batteries at 0.05C, the discharge platform at
2.2–2.4 V is associated with the formation of long-chain polysulﬁdes
(Li2Sn, 2 < n ≤8), while the discharge platform at 2.0–2.1 V is asso-
ciated with short-chain polysulﬁdes (Li2S2, Li2S) [45,46]. The discharge
capacities of the cells with MWCNTs, AP interlayer, and the LiF/AP
interlayer are 1103, 1348, and 1529 mA h/g, respectively, and the LiF/
AP interlayer has a signiﬁcantly better battery capacity improvement
eﬀect (Fig. 5(b)). In the low-rate discharge process, both the AP inter-
layer and the LiF/AP interlayer Li-S battery can inhibit the shuttle eﬀect
and increase the discharge speciﬁc capacity to varying degrees.
Nevertheless, under the conditions of large rate discharge, the gap
in electrochemical performance of the three cells are gradually be-
coming apparent (Fig. 5(c)). With the rate increasing from 0.05 C to 4
C, the LiF/AP interlayer Li-S battery has a low capacity loss and sig-
niﬁcant rate performance, which can be attributed to the fast electron/
ion transfer and 3D interlayer structure advantages [47–49]. Compared
with the Li-S batteries using MWCNTs with the ﬁrst discharge speciﬁc
capacity of 296 mA h/g at 4 C, a satisfactory speciﬁc capacity of
693 mA h/g can be obtained for the battery with the LiF/AP interlayer.
Furthermore, the LiF/AP interlayer Li-S battery can recover most of the
capacity under the condition of retesting at 0.2 C. As shown in Fig.5 (d),
the LiF/AP interlayer Li-S battery eﬀectively inhibits the shuttle eﬀect
in constant current charge-discharge at diﬀerent rates (0.05 C–4 C),
which conﬁrms that LiF/AP interlayer inhibits the diﬀusion of poly-
sulﬁde to the Li anode and improves the rate performance.
To evaluate the eﬃciency of the interlayer, we performed long-term
cycling capability on AP interlayer Li-S battery and LiF/AP interlayer
Li-S battery at 1 C. Fig. 6 shows the cycling performance of two dif-
ferent interlayer cells, and it is obvious that the discharge capacity of
the LiF/AP interlayer is higher than that of the AP interlayer. Compared
with the AP interlayer,the LiF/AP interlayer delivers the improved
discharge capacity after the LiF/CNT slurry was coated [50–52]. After
200 cycles, the LiF/AP interlayer displays the capacity retention of

the SEI layer and acts as a shielding layer to keep the integrity of the SEI

In order to better understand the superior electrochemical perfor-
mance of LiF/AP interlayer, we conducted EIS testing on doped
MWCNTs, AP interlayer, LiF/AP interlayer Li-S batteries. As shown in
Fig. 7(a)-(b), the Nyquist diagram consists of a semicircle in the mid-
high frequency region and a diagonal line in the low frequency region,
which represents the charge transfer reaction and the diﬀusion of Li+
ion [54,55]. At the same time, the ﬁrst semicircle represents the re-
sistance of the lithium ion through the solid electrolyte (RSEI), and the
second semicircle represents charge transfer resistance (Rct). The LiF/
AP interlayer exhibits a large Rct before cycling because the special 3D
structure of the LiF/AP interlayer leaves a part of the interior of the
battery [56,57]. However, after 80 cycles, the linear slope of LiF/AP
interlayer is signiﬁcantly more abrupt than MWCNTs and AP interlayer,
indicating that the LiF/AP interlayer has a smaller Rct and provides a
good transport path for Li+ ion [58]. In addition, the lower Rct of the
LiF/AP interlayer than the AP interlayer demonstrates that the com-
posite interlayer formed by the AP interlayer after LiF/CNT coating
enhances electrochemical kinetics and increases usability [59].
4. Conclusion
In summary, LiF/AP interlayer was successfully prepared by a
simple and eﬀective coating way which had been applied in Li-S bat-
tery. Electrochemical testing showed that the LiF/AP interlayer in-
hibited the shuttle eﬀect and increased the discharge capacity. The
results of SEM, UV–vis spectroscopy, and infrared spectroscopy indicate
that the formation of a dense sol layer by LiF in the electrolyte is
beneﬁcial to inhibit the migration of polysulﬁde. At the same time, the
composition and structure of the SEI are optimized thanks to the LiF
coating and enhance the structure and interface stability of the cathode
during charge and discharge. Furthermore, the LiF/AP interlayer could
transfer Li+ ions without inﬂuence due to the lower barrier energy of
