EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Preparation of electrolyte
The electrolyte formulations were prepared by incorporating NiTPP (Macklin, 97%) at concentrations of 0.5 mM, 1 mM, 2 mM, and 3 mM into a conventional ether - based electrolyte system. The base electrolyte was composed of a binary solvent mixture. First, 1,2 - dimethoxyethane (DME, J&K scientific, 99.9%) and 1,3 - dioxolane (DOL, J&K scientific, 99.9%) were mixed in a 1:1 volume ratio. To this solvent mixture, 1M bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide lithium salt (LiTFSI, J&K scientific, 99.9%) and 0.2M lithium nitrate (LiNO₃, Aladdin, 99.9%) were added as the electrolyte salts. For the preparation of the NiTPP-containing electrolytes, the appropriate amount of NiTPP was weighed and added to the pre-prepared conventional ether electrolyte. The resulting mixture was then stirred at room temperature for 8 hours inside an argon-filled glovebox (with O₂ and H₂O levels both below 0.1 ppm) to ensure complete dissolution of NiTPP and to obtain the electrolytes required for the subsequent experimental procedures.
Preparation of sulfur cathode
Sublimed sulfur powder (S), conductive carbon black (Super P), carbon nanotubes, and binder (PVDF) were mixed in an agate ball - mill tank at a mass ratio of 6:2:1:1. A suitable amount of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) was added as the solvent, and the mixture was thoroughly blended. The well mixed blend was subjected to ball-milling for 4 hours to achieve a homogeneous slurry. The resulting slurry was coated onto an carbon-coated aluminum foil using an automatic coating machine. Then it was dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 24 hours to remove the NMP solvent. Finally, the dried electrode was pressed to produce a sulfur electrode sheet with a diameter of 12 mm.
Preparation of polysulfide lithium solution
Preparation of Li2S6 solution
Experiments were conducted in a glove box filled with argon gas (O2, H2O < 0.1 ppm). Sulfur powder and Li2S (molar ratio 5:1) were mixed in 1, 2-dimethoxy-ethane (DME) and 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL) (volume ratio 1:1), and stirred at 25 °C for 12 h to prepare a 0.2 M Li2S6 solution.
Preparation of Li2S8 solution
Similar procedures were conducted with sulfur powder and Li2S (molar ratio 7:1) mixed in tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether, and stirred at 60 °C for 24 h to prepare a 0.2M Li2S8 solution.
Electrochemical experiments
Li-S batteries
The lithium-sulfur battery consists of prepared S cathode, commercial polypropylene diaphragm (PP, Celgard-2400; Diameter 19 mm) and Li foil anode (diameter 16 mm; 0.6 μm thickness) assembled using an ether-based electrolyte with/without NiTPP of the different components described above. 
Li-Cu batteries and Li-Li symmetrical batteries
Li-Cu batteries consisted of copper foil (diameter 12 mm), a PP separator, the prepared ether-based electrolyte, and lithium foil. Li-Li symmetrical batteries used lithium metal as the working electrode, the counter electrode, along with the ether-based electrolyte.
Li2S6 symmetrical batteries
Graphite felt served as the positive and negative electrodes, and 0.2M Li2S6-DOL/DME solution was used as the electrolyte. Each graphite felt received 20 μL of 0.2M Li2S6 solution and 10 μL of ether-based electrolyte, added to the separator to prepare the batteries.
Deposition and dissolution of Li2S
Carbon paper acted as the positive electrode, and lithium foil as the negative electrode, with 0.2 M Li2S8-tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether solution as the electrolyte. Each carbon paper received 20 μL of 0.2M Li2S8 solution, and 20 μL of the prepared ether electrolyte was added to the separator, forming a battery for testing Li2S deposition and dissolution.
The electrochemical workstation (PARSTAT 2273) conducted electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests. EIS measured the frequencies from 100 kHz to 10 mHz, with an AC voltage amplitude was 5 mV. The voltage range for Li2S6 symmetrical batteries was −0.8 to 0.8 V, with CV curves tested at a scan rate of 5 mV s−1. The deposition process discharged batteries at 0.112 mA to 2.06 V, then held at 2.05 V until the current fell below 10−5 A. The dissolution process reduced the voltage to 1.8 V at 0.10 mA and 1.7 V at 0.01 mA, ensuring complete conversion from LiPSs to solid Li2S. The batteries were then charged at a constant potential of 2.35 V until the current was below 10−5 A, ensuring complete dissolution of Li2S.
Material characterization
The surface composition of lithium was analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, THERMO SCIENTIFIC, Al Kα 1486.6eV). The morphology of the experimental samples was characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Quanta 200FEG FEI).
