1. Experiment section
1.1 Materials
N, N′-dimethylpropyleneurea (DMPU) and N, N′-dimethylethyleneurea (DMEU) were 
purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry (TCI). Lithium sulfide (Li2S) was purchased from 
Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. Sulfur powder was purchased from China National 
Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. Thick lithium foil (400 μm) and thin lithium foil (50 μm) were 
purchased from China Energy Lithium Co. Ltd. 1, 2-Dimethoxyethane (DME), 1,3-dioxolane 
(DOL), lithium nitrate (LiNO3), Al current collector, and CR 2032-type coin-cell cases were 
purchased from Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. Lithium 
bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) was kindly supplied by Tinci Materials 
Technology Co. Ltd. The separator (Celgard 2500) was purchased from Celgard, LLC.
1.2 Preparation of electrolytes
The electrolyte was prepared in an argon-filled glovebox (O2 < 0.01 ppm, H2O < 0.01 ppm). 
In detail, 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.4 M LiNO3 were dissolved in the mixed solvents of DOL/DME 
(1:1 by vol., denoted as DD), DMEU/DOL/DME (2:4:4 by vol., denoted as EDD), and 
DMPU/DOL/DME (2:4:4 by vol., denoted as PDD), respectively.
1.3 Materials characterization
Ultraviolet-visible light (UV-vis) spectra were obtained using an absorption spectrometer 
(Shimadzu, UV−2700). Raman spectra were collected by a QE65 Pro spectrometer (Ocean 
Optics) with an emission wavelength of 532 nm. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) 
spectroscopy was applied to assess the chemical environment of O and Li by a 600MHz 
spectrometer (Bruker, AVANCE NEO), in which 0.2 M LiCl in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) 
was used as an external reference set to 0 ppm. The surface morphology of the electrode was 
characterized by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, MAIA3, TESCAN Brno s.r.o.). The 
electrodes were disassembled from cell after cycling, of which the cycled electrodes were rinsed 
by DOL to remove residues and dried under vacuum.

1.4 The electrodeposition and dissolution tests of Li2S
Li||Li2S8 cell was assembled to conduct the electrochemical deposition and dissolution tests 
of Li2S, where the Li2S8 catholyte dripped carbon fiber cloth serves as cathode and lithium 
metal serves as anode. The Li2S8 (0.2 M) catholytes were prepared by mixing S powder and 
Li2S at a stoichiometric molar ratio of 7:1 in DD, EDD, and PDD with 1 M LiTFSI, respectively.
The Li||Li2S8 cell was first discharged to 2.12 V with a current density of 0.112 mA, followed 
by chronoamperometry test at 2.05 V until the current density reaches below 0.01 mA. To 
evaluate the electrodeposition kinetics, the current–time curves of Li2S deposition and growth 
were fitted with the four deposition mathematical models (Table S4). For dissolution tests, the 
Li||Li2S8 cell was first discharged to 1.7 V, and then charged to 2.35 V at 0.112 mA, followed 
by chronoamperometry test at 2.35 V for 20 h.
1.5 Preparation of sulfur cathode
Preparation of Li2S6 cathode. The sulfur powder and Li2S were mixed with a stoichiometric 
molar ratio of 5:1 and dissolved in DME. Li2S6 solution (40 μL) was dropped onto carbon fiber 
cloth disks with a diameter of 12 mm and dried at 60 ºC for 12 h. The active mass loading of 
Li2S6 cathode was controlled at ~1.4 mgs cm−2.
Preparation of CNT/S cathode. Firstly, the sulfur powder and multiwalled carbon nanotubes 
(CNT) were mixed by mechanically ball milling at a weight ratio of 3:1 for 3 h and further 
treated in a sealed vessel at 155 ºC for 12 h to obtain CNT/S composite. Then, CNT/S, acetylene 
black, and PVDF with a mass ratio of 8:1:1 were mixed in N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP) to form 
a uniform slurry. Finally, the slurry was coated onto an Al foil by a doctor blade, dried and cut 
into circular disks with a diameter of 12 mm. CNT/S cathode was applied in both coin and 
pouch cell. The active mass loading of CNT/S cathode was controlled at 2 ~ 9 mg cm–2.

1.8 Theoretical calculations
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation was carried out by the LAMMPS code5 and OPLS-
AA6 force field. The force field parameters were generated by LigParGen web serve.7 The 
parameters for Li+, TFSI−, and S62− were acquired from Jensen et al.,8 Koddermann et al.,9 and 
Rajput et al.,10 respectively. The simulation boxes of 0.1 M Li2S6 + 1.0 M LiTFSI in DD, 0.1 
M Li2S6 + 1.0 M LiTFSI in EDD, and 0.1 M Li2S6 + 1.0 M LiTFSI in PDD were composed of 
72 DOL + 48 DME + 10 LiTFSI + 2 Li2S6, 57 DOL + 39 DME + 19 DMEU + 10 LiTFSI + 2 
Li2S6, and 57 DOL + 39 DME + 16 DMPU + 10 LiTFSI, + 2 Li2S6, respectively. The initial 
atomic coordinates were generated with Packmol11 program and the final constructions were 
visualized by VESTA12 and VMD.13 The steepest descent approach was applied to produce an 
initial energy minimization for each system and the time step was fixed to be 1 fs for all 
simulations. A V-rescale thermostat and Berendsen barostat were used to equilibrate the system 
for an additional 4 ns14 with the last 2 ns being used for the solvation structure analyses.15-16 
The VMD was used to sample the snapshots and analyzed the radial distribution functions 
(RDF) of different electrolytes from the simulation trajectory.

Figure S18. Schematic illustrations of Li2S deposition behavior in (a) E-PDD (b) E-EDD, and 
(c) E-DD.
The electrodeposition mechanism of Li2S was schematically summarized in Figure S18, 
which can be divided the following three stages, including the formation of Li2S cluster, the 
nucleation of Li2S, the growth of Li2S. In E-PDD electrolyte, benefiting from the DMPU with 
high donicity that initiates and stabilizes the additional intermediate (S3•−), the E-PDD 
electrolyte can facilitate the disproportionation/dissociation of LiPSs to produce a large amount 
of Li2S. Thereafter, many nucleation sites of solid Li2S are instantaneously formed upon 
reaching its saturation limit. In addition, owing to the strong affinity between Li2S nuclei and 
LiPSs in high DN electrolyte with low diffusion barrier,23-24 the later formed Li2S will be 
preferentially deposited on the surface of early Li2S nuclei, resulting in 3D growth with porous 
structure. Owing to the limited solubility of Li2S in E-EDD electrolyte, it displays different 
nucleation process in contrast to E-PDD, where the growth process with progressive formation 
of Li2S nucleus results in 3D growth of uneven Li2S granules with island-like morphology. For 
E-DD system, the insoluble Li2S leads to instantaneous nucleation and further evolves into 2D 
film-like passivation layer, which will restrain the electron/Li+ transport with high barriers, 
resulting in sluggish conversion kinetic.
