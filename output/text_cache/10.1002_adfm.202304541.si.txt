1. Experimental Section 
1.1 Raw Materials 
Lithium (Li) metal foils (China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd.), 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME, 
DoDoChem), 
1,3-dioxolane 
(DOL, 
DoDoChem), 
1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl-2,2,3,3-
tetrafluoropropylether (HFE, TCI), hexyl methyl ether (HME, TCI), 1,3,5-trioxane (TXA, TCI), 
lithium bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, DoDoChem), LiNO3 (Aladdin), LiI 
(MACKLIN), Li2S (Alfa), elemental sulfur (S8, Alfa), aluminum (Al) foils, carbon coated 
copper (Cu) foils, carbon nanotubes (CNTs, Cnano), poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, HSV-
300), N-methylpyrrolidone (NMP, Aladdin), and polypropylene (PP) membranes (Celgard 2400) 
were used as purchased without further purification. 
 
1.2. Preparation of Electrolytes 
The control electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME/HFE (2:2:1, by vol.). 
The experimental electrolyte was composed of 1.0 M LiTFSI in the mixture of TXA/DME (1:2, 
by mol.) mixed with HFE (4:1, by vol.). LiNO3 was employed as additives in the electrolytes 
to promote the performance of Li–S full cells. To prepare the electrolyte with 1.0 M[S] Li2S8, 
Li2S and S8 were mixed with a molar ratio of 8:7 in the above electrolytes. All the electrolytes 
were prepared in an argon-filled glove box with H2O and O2 contents below 0.1 ppm. 
 
1.3. Preparation of Sulfur Cathodes 
A mixture of CNT and S8 was fabricated with a weight ratio of 7:3 and then ball-milled 
with PVDF binders with a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP to form a homogeneous slurry. The slurry 
was then cast onto Al foils by doctor blades and dried at 60°C for 12 h to obtain sulfur cathodes. 
The sulfur cathodes were prepared with the areal loading of 1.2, 4.0, or 6.2 mg cm−2 and 
punched into disks with a diameter of 13.0 mm for electrochemical measurements.

1.4. Electrochemical Measurements 
Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was carried out in the cells with Li foils as anodes, 
carbon-coated Cu foils as cathodes, and 40 μL electrolyte. The potential was negatively swept 
from the open circuit potential (OCP) to 0.01 V vs. Li+/Li at the scan rate of 0.1 mV s−1, which 
was conducted on a Solartron 1470E electrochemical workstation. 
To verify the inhibition of the parasitic reactions, lithium–sulfur (Li–S) cells were 
assembled with 600 µm Li anodes (16.0 mm in diameter), 1.2 mg cm−2 sulfur cathodes (13.0 
mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 25 µL electrolyte without LiNO3 
additive. The Li–S cells were tested on a Neware battery cycler (CT-4008T, Shenzhen, China) 
at room temperature. The Li–S cells were firstly galvanostatically charged to 2.90 V and then 
discharged to 2.60, 2.45, 2.43, 2.40, 2.39, 2.38, 2.37, 2.36, 2.35, 2.34, 2.25, and 2.20 V step by 
step to measure the shuttle current. The cells were kept potentiostatically for 1.0 h at each 
voltage step and the shuttle current was determined by the steady-state current at the different 
potentiostatic voltages. Moreover, the Li–S cells were cycled at 0.1 C (1 C = 1672 mA g−1) with 
a voltage range of 1.70–2.60 V to analyze the Coulombic efficiency (CE). Furthermore, Li | Li 
symmetric cells were assembled with 50 μm Li (15.0 mm in diameter) as electrodes, PP 
separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 40 μL electrolyte containing 1.0 M[S] Li2S8 and tested at 
a current density of 1.0 mA cm−2 and an areal capacity of 4.0 mAh cm−2. 
 
1.5. Material Characterization 
In situ electrochemical atomic force microscopy (AFM) was employed to investigate the 
capability of TXA to decompose and form SEI. LiI substituted for LiTFSI in the electrolytes to 
eliminate the interference of Li salt reduction. The in situ electrochemical cell with a three-
electrode system included Li wires as the counter electrode (CE) and the reference electrode 
(RE) and highly oriented pyrolytic graphite (HOPG) as the working electrode (WE). An “O” 
ring with a diameter of 8.0 mm was used to seal the cell. In situ electrochemical AFM

experiments were conducted by combining AFM (Bruker Multimode 8 with a Nanoscope V 
controller) with an electrochemical workstation (Methrohm Autolab, PGSTAT302N) in the Ar-
filled glove box (H2O < 0.1 ppm and O2 < 0.1 ppm). The potential was negatively swept from 
OCP to 0.9 V at the scan rate of 1.0 mV s−1 referred to Li+/Li. A silicon AFM probe (Bruker, 
force constant = 11.8 N m−1, resonant frequency = 240 kHz) was used to scan the surface of 
HOPG with a scan rate of 0.888 Hz (about 288 s for taking one image) in the mode of PeakForce 
Quantitative Nano-Mechanics (PF-QNM). 
Li metal anodes were disassembled from Li–S cells and washed 3 times by DME to 
remove residual electrolyte and dried in the glove box. The morphology was characterized by 
a scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM 7401F, JEOL Ltd., Japan) operated at 3.0 kV. X-
ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) experiments were carried out on a scanning X-ray 
microprobe (Axis Supra+, Kratos Analytical) operated at 250 kV with monochromated Al Kα 
radiation as the X-ray source. The Ar+ sputtering rate was ~130 nm min–1 calibrated on the 
Ta2O5 surface. Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) was conducted to 
investigate the SEI composition on Li metal anodes (PHI nano ToF II, ULVAC-PHI, Inc.). In 
the process of sample transfer, a special transfer vessel was used, which can directly transfer 
the sample from the glove box to the ToF-SIMS vacuum chamber without being exposed to the 
ambient air. The sputter etching was performed using an Ar+ beam (3 kV 100 nA) to obtain 
depth profile. 
 
1.6. Li–S Full Cell Assembly and Battery Performance Evaluation 
The performance of Li–S full cells were evaluated in standard 2032 coin-type cells. Li–S 
coin cells were assembled with ultrathin Li anodes of 50 μm (15.0 mm in diameter), high-areal-
loading sulfur cathodes of 4.0 mg cm−2 (13.0 mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in 
diameter), and 35 µL electrolyte containing LiNO3 additives. In addition, Li–S coin cells with 
ultrahigh sulfur loading were assembled with 600 μm Li anodes (16.0 mm in diameter), 6.2 mg

cm−2 sulfur cathodes (13.0 mm in diameter), PP separators (19.0 mm in diameter), and 85 µL 
electrolyte. The cells were galvanostatically cycled at 0.1 C with a voltage range of 1.70–2.60 
V for cycling performance evaluation on the Neware battery cycler. 
For Li–S pouch cell evaluation, the sulfur cathodes were double-side coated on Al foils 
following the method in section 1.3. The sulfur cathodes and Li metal anodes were cut into a 
size of 4 × 7 cm2 with an areal sulfur loading of around 7.8 mg cm−2 and a Li metal thickness 
of 75 µm on a single side, respectively. The pouch cell was assembled with stacks of double-
side Li metal anodes, double-side sulfur cathodes, and PP separators in a dry room with a dew 
point below −40°C. A low electrolyte to sulfur (E/S) ratio of 3.0 g gS−1 was used. The pouch 
cell was cycled at 0.025 C (1 C = 1000 mA g−1) with a voltage range of 1.70–2.55 V on the 
Neware battery cycler. The energy density was calculated based on the total mass of the pouch 
cell including current collectors, package, and tabs.
