Since graph theory is a well-established field of study, there are methods of analysis that have been developed, tested and refined. One such type of analysis is focused on centrality which is a measure of what is most central to the network in question. Essentially, this variable determines which node (or atom) is most connected to the most connected nodes. One such measure of this is known as eigenvalue centrality. This was used, as shown in Figure S-8, to explore what the most central atoms are. For this system, the most central atom is the 3rd atom which is a sulfur and then closely followed by the two lithium ions. This makes sense from analyzing the structure since that sulfur atom serves as a link between the two parts of the structure. we used the new coordination representation and analysis to investigate all of the lithium nanocluster systems (SI-2) in order to explore Li2S phase formation.
An investigation of the eigenvalue centralities shows an interesting observation that the most central atom to the structure is almost always a lithium atom (Figure S-9). This is regardless of the size of the lithium cluster which is intriguing due to the structural variations between the clusters. In most cases, all of the atoms that are the most central are lithium atoms even if it doesn’t appear that way in the coordination representation or from a normal molecular visualization perspective.
