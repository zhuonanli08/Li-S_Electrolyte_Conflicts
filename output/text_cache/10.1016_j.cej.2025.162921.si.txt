Experimental section
Preparation of ether-based electrolytes with/without 2,4,5,6-tetrachloropyrimidine (TCPy) additive
Briefly, the ether-based electrolytes with or without TCPy additive were prepared on the basis of typical ether-based electrolyte, which is 1.0 M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI) in a mixture of dimethoxymethane/1,3-dioxolane (DME/DOL, 1:1 in volume), with 1.0 wt.% LiNO3 additive. For comparison, TCPy (98%, Macklin) additive was dissolved in the above ether-based electrolytes with different concentrations of 0, 30, 50, and 60 mM, respectively.

Preparation of polysulfide-tetra-substituted pyrimidine (PSTPy) control sample
In an argon-filled glovebox, the lithium hexasulfide (Li2S6) solution with a concentration of 0.4 M was prepared by dissolving 640 mg of sulfur (≥99.5%, Aladdin) and 184 mg of Li2S (≥99%, Aladdin) in 10 mL of DME (99.5%, Aladdin) solvent. The mixture was stirred at 60 °C until dissolved completely. Then, a 0.1 M TCPy solution was prepared by adding 218 mg of TCPy (98%, Macklin) in 10 mL of DME (99.5%, Aladdin) solvent. Subsequently, 1.5 mL of TCPy solution and 1.5 mL of Li2S6 solution were mixed to obtain the PSTPy precipitation. The precipitation was washed by DME, followed by drying in vacuum at room temperature for 24 h to obtain PSTPy polymer control sample for structural characterizations and also serving as control cathodes.
Material characterizations
The morphology features of samples were monitored by Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) (HITACH S-4800). Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDS) analyses were performed with a Bruker EDX spectrometer equipped on the SEM. Quantitative elemental analyses were conducted with an elemental analyzer (Vario MICRO cube, Elementar). Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR spectra) were collected using a Nicolet-iS50 spectrometer. Raman analysis was performed on a Horiba JY Raman spectrometer using a 473 nm laser source. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) were measured on a PHI-5000 Versa Probe instrument. Ultraviolet-Visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectra were collected with a Shimadzu UV-2456 spectrophotometer.

Fabrication and measurements of Li||S/CNTs and Li||Li symmetric batteries with or without TCPy additive in the electrolyte
The S/CNTs composite cathode material was obtained by a melt-diffusion process. Sulfur powder (≥99.5%, Aladdin) and carbon nanotubes were well-mixed in a weight ratio of 7:3, the mixture was then treated at 155°C for 12 h in sealed stainless-steel vessel. The cathodes were prepared by dispersing the mixture of S@CNTs, Ketjen-black (EC600J), and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) with a weight ratio of 8:1:1 in N-methyl-pyrrolidone (NMP) to form a uniform slurry, followed by constantly stirring for 8 h. Next, the slurry was coated on a carbon paper current collector via doctor blade, dried under vacuum at 60ºC for 12 h, and then cut into round discs for battery assembly. The areal sulfur loading of the cathodes was ~1.5 mg cm−2. 30 μL of ether-based electrolyte with TCPy additives was carefully selected, while the version without TCPy additives served as the control electrolyte. The CR-2032 coin-type batteries were assembled using lithium metal foil anode, Celgard-2400 membrane as separator, electrolytes with/without TCPy additive and S/CNTs cathode in an argon-filled glovebox. The Li||Li symmetric batteries were fabricated by using two identical metallic lithium foil as the electrodes. Ether-based electrolytes with and without TCPy were used with an addition amount of 30 μL. The Li-ion storage performances were demonstrated using a LAND-CT2001A battery analyzer. The whole specific capacities were calculated according to the loading mass of S element in the cathodes unless specified otherwise. The rate performances were demonstrated by cycling under different current densities of 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 4.0 C. The Cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves were attained under various scan rates of 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 mV s-1. EIS spectra were collected under an amplitude of 5 mV within the frequency range of 10-2 to 105 Hz using a CHI-760 (Chenhua) electrochemical workstation.

Fabrication and measurements of Li||PSTPy batteries
As control samples, the PSTPy cathodes were prepared by dispersing the mixture of PSTPy, Ketjen-black, and PVDF with a weight ratio of 7:2:1 in NMP solvent to form a uniform slurry, followed by constantly stirring for 8 h. Next, the slurry was coated on a carbon paper current collector via doctor blade, dried under vacuum at 60ºC for 12 h, and then cut into round discs for battery assembly. For each battery, 30 µL of 1.0 M LiTFSI dissolved in a 1:1 (v/v) DME/DOL mixture with 1.0 wt% LiNO3 additive was used as the electrolyte, and a piece of Celgard-2400 membrane was used as the separator. The Li||PSTPy battery was assembled using a PSTPy cathode and a lithium metal foil anode in an argon-filled glovebox. The CV curves were collected under a scan rate of 0.5 mV s-1 from 1.5 to 3.0 V. The rate performances were measured by cycling under different current densities of 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0 A g-1.

Fabrication and measurements of Li2S6-based symmetric batteries
Typically, specific material (TCPy or CB) and PVDF were uniformly mixed (in a mass ratio of 8:2) in NMP solvent to form a uniform slurry, followed by constantly stirring for 8 h. Next, the slurry was coated on a carbon paper current collector via doctor blade, dried under vacuum at 60 ºC for 12 h, and then cut into round discs for battery assembly. The mass loading of TCPy or CB was ~1 mg cm-2. The as-prepared identical electrodes were used as symmetric working and counter electrodes, and 30 µL of Li2S6 solution was added as the electrolyte, and a piece of Celgard-2400 membrane was used as the separator. The Li2S6-based symmetric batteries were assembled using TCPy or CB electrodes in an argon-filled glovebox. The CV tests were carried out using an electrochemical workstation (CHI-760) at a scan rate of 1.0 mV s-1 with a voltage range from -1.0 V to 1.0 V.

Computational details
The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed with Gaussian 16 software package. The physiochemical parameters, such as highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO), lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), and electrostatic potential (ESP) of TCPy, DOL, and DME were calculated at the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) level, including geometry optimizations and vibrational frequencies.
