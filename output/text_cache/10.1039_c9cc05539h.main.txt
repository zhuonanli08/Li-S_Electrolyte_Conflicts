Highly stable performance of lithium-sulfurized
polyacrylonitrile batteries using a lean
ether-based electrolyte†
Yi Shuai,
a Dudan Wang,a Kanghua Chen,
*ab Zeping Zhang,b Yu Wangb and
Jin Loua
Lithium dendrites are considered the worst problem for lithium
metal batteries. Herein, an ether-based electrolyte is proposed for
highly stable performance of lithium-sulfurized polyacrylonitrile
batteries with lean electrolyte and high areal capacity. This lean
ether-based electrolyte not only suppresses the growth of lithium
dendrites, but also prevents the shuttle eﬀect of lithium poly-
sulfides and maintains low volatility. By employing this ether-
based electrolyte in lithium-sulfurized polyacrylonitrile batteries,
a reversible specific capacity of 670 mA h g1 with 7 ll mg1
electrolyte and 4 mA h cm2, and a capacity fading rate of 0.08%
per cycle for 300 cycles were obtained.
Lithium–sulfur (Li–S) batteries are considered one of the most
attractive classes of lithium metal batteries (LMBs) due to their
high energy density, low cost and environmentally friendly nature.
Nonetheless, the shuttle effect of polysulfides and dendrite growth
still exist and limit their practical industrial application.1 For
lithium dendrites, it can cause poor electrochemical performance
of batteries and safety issues even if the lithium metal anode
possesses extremely high theoretical specific capacity and the
lowest negative potential.2 Besides, due to the loss of active
cathode materials, the shuttle effect of polysulfides also greatly
limited their lifespan.3 For lithium-sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (Li–
SPAN) batteries, due to the insolubility of lithium polysulfides, a
carbonate electrolyte is conventionally used to stabilize the cycling
performance of the sulfurized polyacrylonitrile cathode.4 However,
the growth of lithium dendrites in the carbonate electrolyte
becomes more severe, which may cause a short circuit in
batteries.5 Especially with high areal capacity and lean electrolyte,
the growth of lithium dendrites becomes worse and the electrolyte
is continually consumed until it causes poor performance for
Li–SPAN batteries.6
In recent years, great endeavors have been made to inhibit
the growth of lithium dendrites in an ether electrolyte and have
made great progress. It is a very eﬀective method to gain a
stable and consecutive solid electrolyte interphase (SEI). For
instance, fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) could regulate the
solvation sheath of lithium ions to realize the uniformity of
the SEI and achieve free dendrites.7–9 Besides, LiNO3 is generally
employed in the ether electrolyte for lithium sulfur batteries,
which can suppress the redox shuttle of lithium polysulfides and
the growth of lithium dendrites.10 Nevertheless, in an ether
electrolyte, due to the low sulfur content of SPAN, the dissolution
of polysulfides would induce a significant decrease of the
electrochemical performance of SPAN.11 Therefore, it is essential
to improve the electrochemical stability of sulfurized polyacrylo-
nitrile in the ether electrolyte.
Besides, previous work revealed that the amount of electro-
lyte in lithium metal batteries is generally in excess, in which,
polysulfide dissolution and electrolyte consumption can’t be
eﬀectively evaluated.12 In particular, LiNO3 is constantly being
consumed with the increasing cycle of Li–S batteries.13 In the
battery industry, to achieve high-energy density batteries, a
high areal capacity and lean electrolyte are necessary, which
signifies a greater challenge for lithium dendrites14 and limited
LiNO3 availability. As a result, to evaluate the industrial perfor-
mance of lithium sulfur batteries, high areal capacity and lean
electrolyte are indispensable.
In order to enhance the electrochemical performance of
Li–SPAN batteries with high areal capacity and a lean electro-
lyte, it is essential to suppress the growth of lithium dendrites
and the dissolution of polysulfides simultaneously. There is a
need to construct a bilateral SEI (both the SPAN cathode and
lithium anode) to enhance the performance of Li–SPAN
batteries, which has been reported rarely in an ether electrolyte.
Herein, a lean ether-based electrolyte is proposed for highly
stable lithium-sulfurized polyacrylonitrile batteries with high
areal capacity and lean electrolyte.

of Li–SPAN batteries, six kinds of electrolytes were prepared as

due to the high content of FEC or low content of LiNO3, the CE

electrolyte can provide a high quality SEI to improve the
coulombic efficiencies of lithium plating/stripping effectively.
In addition, the measurement of the interfacial properties of
the symmetric cell is systematically introduced and discussed
as well. At a current density of 1.5 mA cm2 for 2 h (Fig. 1a), it
can be seen that the polarization of the symmetric cells with the
EL3 electrolyte becomes stable with a polarization voltage (B25 mV)
and shows lifetimes in excess of 500 h. By comparison, the
symmetric cell containing the EL0 electrolyte results in a voltage
polarization (B20 mV) and shows erratic voltage response after
400 h, while for the C0 electrolyte, the symmetric cell results in a
higher voltage polarization (B50 mV) and shows erratic voltage
response after 100 h. This is attributed to the unstable interfacial
properties in the symmetric cell with the C0 electrolyte, while the
cell with the EL3 electrolyte can provide a high-quality SEI and can
eﬀectively inhibit the growth of lithium dendrites to maintain a
stable voltage profile.
The electrochemical alternating-current impedance (EIS) of
the symmetric cells is also measured to reveal the interface
features between the electrode and electrolyte (Fig. 1c). Upon a
24 hour rest, the cell with the EL3 electrolyte shows an inter-
facial resistance more than 2 times lower than the cell with the
EL0 electrolyte (170 vs. 380 O), and far less than the cell with C0
(170 vs. 900 O). Besides, after certain cycles, the cell with the
EL3 electrolyte also shows a lower interfacial resistance than
the cells with C0 and EL0 electrolytes (Fig. S1, ESI†). This shows
that EL3 can provide a high SEI to prevent electrolytes from
further decomposition and maintain a high chemical stability
of the interphase between lithium and electrolyte.
Because a lean electrolyte is employed for Li–SPAN batteries,
the volatility of the electrolyte needs to be carefully considered.
Otherwise, few electrolytes can be utilized. As shown in Fig. 1d,
the volatility of these three electrolytes can be described. It is
obvious that the EL3 electrolyte can remain more stable at
room temperature, the quality of which has hardly changed in
five minutes, and the volatilization loss is only 2.89%. However,
both EL0 and C0 electrolytes lose more quality. Statistically,
81% of the mass of the electrolyte is maintained for the C0
electrolyte, while 90% is maintained for the EL0 electrolyte in
five minutes. As a result, the EL3 hybrid electrolyte can maintain
a more stable physical property, which can mainly be ascribed to
the high boiling point (249 1C) of FEC.

As shown in Fig. 2c, for the cell with E0, a significant decrease of
the specific capacity of discharge is observed, and a low and
unstable CE of around 50% is maintained, which can be ascribed
to the dissolution of polysulfides and causes the redox shuttle of
lithium polysulfides.15 For the cell with EL0, the specific capacity
of discharge and the values of CE remain high and stable,
implying that the addition of LiNO3 can suppress the dissolution
of polysulfides. From previous literature, it is found that the
N-modified SEI could inhibit the dissolution of polysulfides.15
Besides, an ether-based electrolyte is proposed for Li–SPAN
batteries. To evaluate effectively the dissolution of polysulfides
and consumption of electrolyte or LiNO3, limited electrolyte
(7 ml mgSPAN
1) and high areal capacity (4 mA h cm2) are
indispensable in Li–SPAN batteries. As shown in Fig. 2d, for the
cell with the EL0 electrolyte, it can be seen that the CE remains at
100% in the first 20 cycles as well, and a sharp decrease begins to
occur, which results in less than 80% CE. However, it can be seen
that the specific capacity of the cell with the EL0 electrolyte shows
no obvious rapid decrease before 100 cycles and remains at
around 600 mA h g1, signifying that the electrolyte is able to
support the normal charge/discharge of the batteries and the
growth of lithium dendrites has been inhibited effectively when
compared to the C0 electrolyte. The sharp decrease of CE can be
ascribed to the high areal capacity and lean electrolyte of Li–SPAN
batteries; a huge change in volume could cause the continuous
destruction and formation of membranes of the solid electrolyte
interphase during the long time cycles, which would exhaust
LiNO3 before the electrolyte is used up. As a result, the redox
shuttle of the dissolved lithium polysulfides occurs again, and a
stable cycle performance can be obtained when approaching the
ultimate solubility of lithium polysulfides in lean EL0. Moreover,
for the cell with an FEC-modified ether-based electrolyte, a high
and stable CE is obtained, which remains at 100% at least for
100 cycles. Especially for EL3, a considerable specific capacity of
620 mA h g1 and a 52% capacity loss are obtained after 500 cycles
at 1 C with 4 mA h cm2 and 7 ml mgSPAN
1 electrolyte. Besides,
during the test at 0.2 C, 670 mA h g1 and only a 25% capacity loss
after 300 cycles can be achieved. It can be implied that the growth
of lithium dendrites and the dissolution of polysulfides can both
be suppressed effectively using the EL3 electrolyte. For EL1 or EL5,
due to the low content of FEC, or the high content of FEC and low
content of LiNO3 (Fig S2, ESI†), the growth of lithium dendrites
and the dissolution of polysulfides cannot be inhibited effectively
when compared to the EL3 electrolyte, as a result, the EL3 cell
shows a better cycle performance than the EL1 and EL5 ones.

In Fig S3a and b (ESI†), a lithium lump sticks to the PP separator for
the EL0 cell, while it cannot be observed in the EL3 cell. It can
be ascribed to the dissolution of lithium polysulfides. When the
electrolyte dries up, lithium polysulfides will cling to the PP separator

Moreover, the full cells of EL0, EL3, and C0 are disassembled and the
cathode was immersed in DME solvent; it is clear that the color of
EL0 becomes yellow, and EL3 and C0 show no marked change, and
no shuttle effect of lithium polysulfides was observed (Fig S3c, ESI†).
As a result, it can be implied that an obvious shuttle effect of
lithium polysulfides can be observed in the Li–SPAN cell with the
EL0 electrolyte, while a relatively stable lithium anode and
cathode can be obtained in the EL3 electrolyte to enhance the
performance of Li–SPAN batteries.
FEC and LiNO3 are commonly introduced into an ether electro-
lyte, which not only regulate the solvation sheath of lithium ions,
but also induce the formation of an abundance of LiF, LiNxOy and
Li2SxOy on the surface of both the lithium anode and SPAN
cathode.16,17 Such a high quality SEI not only suppresses the growth
of lithium dendrites, but also stable SPAN (Fig. 2a).
In summary, a FEC-modified ether-based electrolyte is proposed
for highly stable lithium-sulfurized polyacrylonitrile batteries with
high areal capacity and lean electrolyte. Compared to the conventional
carbonate or ether electrolyte, this FEC-modified ether-based electro-
lyte not only suppresses the growth of lithium dendrites, but also
improves the electrochemical stability of sulfurized polyacrylonitrile,
avoiding the shuttle eﬀect of lithium polysulfides. By employing this
hybrid electrolyte in a practical Li–SPAN battery, a considerable
specific capacity of 670 mA h g1 and only a 25% capacity loss
are obtained after 300 cycles at 200 mA g1 with 4 mA h cm2
and 7 ml mgSPAN
1 electrolyte.
