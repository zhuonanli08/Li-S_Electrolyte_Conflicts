We evaluated the performance of Li-S batteries with the traditional 1M DOL/DME electrolyte under practical conditions. Fig. S1a shows an evaluated result of overall energy density of Li-S batteries at different E/S ratios. Under an E/S ratio of 10 L mg−1, the mass ratio of the electrolyte is high to 77% for the Li-S battery (Fig. S2a), and the available energy density is low to 174 Wh kg−1 (detailed calculation in Table S1 and S2), inferior to that of commercial LiFePO4 (LFP)-based (> 190 Wh kg−1) and LiNixMnyCo1−x−yO2 (NCM)-based (> 260 Wh kg−1) Li-ion batteries. Even under an E/S ratio of 5 L mg−1 that is regarded as the threshold of lean electrolyte, the mass ratio of electrolyte is still high to 63% (Fig. S2b), and the Li-S battery can only output an energy density of 283 Wh kg−1. To meet the pursuit of energy density > 300 Wh kg−1, the E/S ratio must be decreased below to < 5 L mg−1. However, Li-S batteries fail to work under such lean-electrolyte conditions. The mechanism was revealed by our previous works [S1]. With high E/S ratios (Fig. S1c), excessive electrolyte can fully dissolve LPSs intermediates and drive further reduction to Li2S2/Li2S. While with low E/S ratios (Fig. S1d), lean electrolyte transforms to a gel-like catholyte after dissolving a high content LPSs [S2, S3], and it undergoes a significant decrease in Li+ conductivity, along with severe deterioration in wettability to the cathode and the separator, which subsequently lead to the failure of the Li-S cell. 
	Besides, the 1M DOL/DME electrolyte demands high-porosity cathodes and thus leads to low volumetric energy density. Fig. S1e shows that Li-S batteries with porous cathodes deliver much lower volumetric energy densities than commercial Li-ion batteries (detailed calculation in Table S3). Unfortunately, when the cathode porosity is reduced to an extent, the discharge capacity significantly decreases as shown in Fig. S1f. The failure mechanism is illustrated in Fig. S1g. A dense cathode with limited pore volume only adsorbs a small amount of electrolyte within the electrode structure, and forms a locally low E/S ratio condition, which subsequently leads to cathode deterioration and battery failure.

The calculation of gravimetric energy densities is based on a configuration of a S cathode (64 wt%, 5.0 mg cm−2), an Al current collector (12 m), a separator, a Li foil (50 m), and a Cu current collector (5 m). The density of the 1M DOL/DME electrolyte is 1.09 g mL−1 measured by Suo et al [S4]. Table S1 lists the area mass of components in Li-S batteries. It should be noted that half the weight of current collectors is considered because electrodes are coated on both sizes of current collectors in practical production.
