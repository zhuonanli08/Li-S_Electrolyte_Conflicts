Figure S8: Electron affinity for A) DMPS species B) MPS species and C) PS species with the four different computational 
methodologies 
It is also worth noting that adding an electron to a molecule will change the multiplicity of the system. Multiplicity is directly 
related to the spin of the system. This means that if a molecule has an even number of electrons then the multiplicity will be moved 
from a singlet/triplet/etc. to a doublet/quartet/etc.  Unfortunately, comparing energies between systems introduces extra errors due 
to spin contamination. Within the Hartree-Fock (HF) formalism, an unrestricted HF (UHF) wavefunction is not a eigenfunction of 
the total spin operator (<S^2>). This technically doesn’t violate Schrodinger’s equation since spin is not directly incorporated into 
the equation. Instead spin is incorporated via slater determinants. Since UHF isn’t a wavefunction of the total spin operator, extra 
contributions from higher order spins are present which is not desirable. There are relatively sophisticated techniques aimed at 
reducing spin contamination effects. However , there are two simpler ways to account for it. One, DFT methods are known to be 
less susceptible to spin contamination than just HF wavefunctions. Second, Yamaguchi developed a spin contamination 
approximation technique. Overall we tested four different methodologies for the electron affinity calculations: double zeta basis set 
with and without the Yamaguchi correction and a triple zeta basis set with and without the Yamaguchi correction (Figure S4).
The differences between the four methodologies in all of the species examined are quite minimal. The general trends of each graph 
are conserved and the magnitudes are mostly identical except for a few special cases.  The first comparison to explore is the 
difference between the double zeta and triple zeta basis set in the systems without the Yamaguchi correction (blue and green curves 
respectively). For the DMPS and PS systems the changes are minimal. However, there are noticeable magnitude differences for the 
MPS system even though the trends are the same. As for which method is more accurate, the triple zeta basis set is larger than the 
double zeta basis set and they both contain polarization and disperse correction functions. Therefore, the triple zeta basis set is 
closer to the Hartree-Fock limit and more accurate than the double zeta classification of basis sets. The second comparison to 
examine is the addition of the Yamaguchi spin contamination correction within a particular basis set. There are some slight changes 
but nothing is too significant. As for the methodology to draw conclusions from, we chose the triple zeta basis set with the 
Yamaguchi correction since it is the most theoretically accurate out of the four considered.
