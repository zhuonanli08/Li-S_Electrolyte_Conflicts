Experimental section 
Synthesis of carbon sulfur composites 
The carbon sulfur composites here include the two samples of the graphene-sulfur (G-S) and 
Ketjen Black-sulfur (KB-S). For the preparation of G-S, the proper sulfur powder 
(Aladdin, >99.5%, China) was mixed with the graphene aqueous solution (5%, homemade) 
and then with evaporating to dryness. Then the solid (S: ~45% wt.) was collected and ground 
up for later use. The KB-S particles were fabricated by a melt-diffusion method that have 
reported in previous works. 1, 2 Firstly, Ketjen Black carbon (MTI, >99 %, China) was mixed 
with an excess amount of sulfur (about ten times), and the mixture was settled down in a glass 
bottle and then heated at 155°C for 12 h in a sealed air atmosphere. Afterwards, the redundant 
sulfur was evaporated on a hot-plate at 180 °C, while the mass change of the whole sample 
was constantly monitored. Finally, the KB-S composites was obtained with an appropriate 
sulfur content of ~70% (wt.). 
 
Material characterizations 
SEM micrographs was conducted with a scanning electron microscope (SEM, TESCAN 
MIRA) at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) were 
obtained by using a Thermo Fisher-VG Scientific XPS detector (ESCALAB250Xi). A nearly 
airtight plastic bottle was selected as a transfer tool to convert the sensitive samples (cycled 
electrodes) between these test equipment and the glovebox. The ionic conductivities of 
various electrolyte samples were measured by a conductivity meter (Leici, DDSJ-319L, China) 
at room temperature (24~25 °C). 
 
Electrochemical Measurements 
For cathode preparation, those previously mentioned carbon sulfur powders were mixed with 
carbon black (MTI, acetylene black, >99%) and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder 
(DoDoChem, ARKEMA, >99%) in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, DoDoChem, anhydrous) 
to form a homogeneous slurry. The percentages of carbon sulfur composites, carbon black, 
and PVDF in the mixture were 80%, 10% and 10% by weight, respectively. Then, the slurry 
was cast on an aluminum foil through the doctor-blade technique, followed by drying at 40 °C 
overnight. The mass loading of sulfur material was controlled to be 1.0, 2.0, 4.0 mg cm-2, or 
even above these. Subsequently, the cathode foils were cut up into circular samples (12 mm 
DIA) for electrochemical tests in CR2025 coin cells. The electrolyte here were the different

amount of LiTFSI salt dissolving in the mixed ether solvents, which made by combining 
equal parts of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane (DME). Besides, LiNO3 (Aladdin, 
anhydrous, 99.99%, China) as an additive was added into the electrolyte solution with the 
mass fraction of 1 wt.% (~0.2 M). Celgard 2400 (Celgard, USA) was served as a separator in 
coin cells, while pure lithium foil (China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd) was chosen as the anode. 
The coin cells were assembled in a glovebox filled with inert argon gas (< 0.1 ppm of H2O, < 
0.1 ppm of O2, Mikrouna, China). The cells were galvanostatically cycled at different rates in 
the voltage range 1.7-2.8 V (versus Li/Li+) at 25 °C by LAND battery test system (Wuhan 
LANHE Instruments, China). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) spectrum 
was recorded in the frequency range of 1 MHz-0.01 Hz by using Gamry electrochemical 
workstation (Reference 600+, Gamry Instruments, USA), as well as the cyclic voltammetry 
studies. 
 
Statistical Analysis 
Data of the cycling test of cells under 0 and -20 °C were extracted by LANHE software 
before being plotted. Data of EIS and CV test of cells were obtained by Echem Analyst 
(Gamry). Data used for the calculation of diffusion coefficient (Figure 6 and Table 1) were 
normalized by Excel software and then plotted by Origin software with line fitting.
