Experimental section
Materials: All the raw materials including sulfur (S8), thioctic acid, lithium sulfide (Li2S), triallyl isocyanurate (TAIC), KB, Super P, LA133, 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DOL), 1,2-diamethoxyethane (DME), polypropylene (PP) separators (Celgard 2400), carbon paper, aluminum (Al) foil, lithium (Li) foil were purchased from commercial sources and applied without further purification. The commercialized ether electrolyte (1 M LiTFSI in 1:1 v/v DOL/DME with 2% LiNO3) was purchased from DoDoChem Co., China. 
Preparation of the Li2S6 solution: The Li2S6 solution was prepared by dissolving element S and Li2S with a molar ratio of 5:1 in the commercialized ether electrolyte. The solution was stirred at 60℃ overnight. 
Preparation of the electrolyte with PTA, PTA-S and PTA-Se additives: To obtain the electrolyte with different concentrations of PTA, 0.01 M, 0.02 M, and 0.03 M thioctic acid were added into the basic ether electrolyte and stirred at 70 ℃. The preparation process of the electrolyte containing PTA-S additive can be described as below: 0.01 M Li2S6 solution was added into the electrolyte with 0.02 M of PTA additive, and stirred for two hours to obtain a yellow solution containing PTA-S additive. The preparation process of the electrolyte containing PTA-Se additive was identical to that described above, except that 0.002 M selenium powder was added when adding thioctic acid, and the excess selenium powder was removed after a static period, and the upper deep yellow solution was obtained, (denoted as PTA-Se). 
Fabrication of sulfur cathodes: In this work, two kinds of sulfur materials including KB/S and STI composites were synthesized. The KB/S composite cathode material was obtained by a melt-diffusion process. Sulfur powder and KB were well-mixed in a weight ratio of 3:1, the mixture was then treated at 155 °C for 12 hours in sealed stainless-steel vessel. STI composites was prepared as was previously reported in our lab. The active material (KB/S, STI) was mixed with Super P and LA133 with a mass ratio of 70:20:10. Water and isopropanol were added to form a slurry that was then blade cast onto carbon-coated aluminum foil and dried at 70 °C overnight under vacuum. The coated aluminum foil was then punched into small disks. Sulfur loading was about 1.0–1.5 mg cm−2. High-loading sulfur cathodes were obtained by thick coating on the carbon-coated aluminum foil in the same way.
Cells assembly and measurements: CR 2032 type coin cells were assembled in an argon filled glovebox (O2 < 0.1 ppm, H2O < 0.1 ppm). The separator was a Celgard 2400 membrane. The electrolyte amount is 60 μL per cell for Li-Li symmetric and Li-Cu cells. In the activation processes, all Li-Cu cells were discharged to 0.01 V versus Li/Li+ for two cycles. For Li-S cells, the electrolyte amount was 15 μL mg-1, and cells were cycled in the voltage range between 1.7 and 2.8 V. The galvanostatic charge-discharge processes were conducted on Land battery cyclers (Wuhan, China). The Li2S6 symmetric cells were fabricated with carbon paper as current collector, and a piece of Li foil used as the anode. 10 μL Li2S6 and 10 μL different electrolytes were dropped onto both side of electrodes. CV measurements were performed on a CHI660e electrochemical workstation at scan rate of 50 mV s−1 between −0.8 and 0.8 V. All the experiments were duplicated to minimise experimental error.
Measurement of nucleation and dissolution of Li2S: The tests were carried out with carbon papers as the cathode current collector, 0.1 M Li2S8 (tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether as solvent) as catholyte, lithium metal as anode. The 25 μL Li2S8 catholyte and 15 μL different electrolytes were dropped onto the carbon paper while 5 μL blank electrolyte was added in the anode compartment to wet the anode surface. The cells were first galvanostatically discharged to 2.06 V to consume most of soluble polysulfides, and then potentiostatically hold at 2.05 V until the current decreases to 10−5 A for Li2S nucleation and growth.
The measurement of lithium-ion transference number: To characterize the Li-ion transfer number (tLi+), coin cells of Li-Li symmetric cells were assembled, and a constant voltage of 10 mV was applied to the symmetric cells for 1000 s. The tLi+ is obtained by: tLi+= Iss × (ΔV − I0×Rss) / I0 × (ΔV − Iss×R0), in which ΔV is the voltage bias, I0 and Iss are initial and steady-state currents, R0 and Rss are the electrochemical resistances of cells before and after test, respectively.
The measurement of ionic conductivity: To evaluate the ionic conductivity, SS/SS coin cells with different electrolytes were assembled, and the ionic conductivity was calculated according to the following equation: σ = L/A×R, where L is the thickness of separator, R (Ω) is obtained from EIS plot, and A is the contact area of the electrode and electrolyte.
The measurement of exchange current density: To evaluate the exchange current density, coin cells of Li-Li symmetric cells were assembled, Tafel polarization curves was obtained using a CHI660e electrochemical workstation at scan rate of 1 mV s−1 between −0.2 and 0.2 V, and the exchange current density was calculated according to the following equation: 𝜂 = 𝑎 + 𝑏𝑙𝑜𝑔𝑖, where 𝜂 is the overpotential, 𝑎 and 𝑏 are constants, 𝑎 represents the intercept between Tafel line and potential axis, 𝑏 is Tafel slope, and 𝑖 is the current density. The exchange current densities were obtained from extrapolating the Tafel plots to the overpotential of 0 V.
Material Characterizations: The morphologies of the samples were observed by the field-emission SEM (SIRION200) equipped with an X-ray energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). Raman spectrum was performed on LabRAM HR800 Laser Confocal Raman Spectrometer with the 532 nm laser. Sulfur contents were measured by using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) through Synchronous thermal analyzer (DZH8-STA-200) under argon atmosphere. The chemical composition and the valence states of the products were investigated by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, AXIS ULTRA DLD-600 W). The 7Li nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were performed on a Bruker 400MHz NMR spectrometer.
Theoretical Simulations: The DFT calculation was carried out using DMol3 package. A double-numeric polarized basis set and all electron for the core-treatment were selected. For the numerical integration, the system was set as the open-shell (spin unrestricted) structure. The general gradient approximation with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerh function (GGA-PBE) was applied for the electronic structure. Tkatchenko-Scheffler van der Waals correction was applied for the PBE exchange-correlation function. The convergence tolerances of energy, maximum force and maximum displacement for structural optimization were 1.0 × 10-5 Ha, 0.002 Ha/Å and 0.005 Å, respectively. The self-consistent field (SCF) density convergence tolerance was 1 × 10-6. The binding energy (Ebind) of the polysulfides and monomer was defined as：
Ebind = Epolysulfides/monomer – Epolysulfides – Emonomer
where Epolysulfides/monomer, Epolysulfides and Emonomer were the energy of polysulfides combined on monomer, the energy of polysulfides, and the energy of monomer, respectively.
