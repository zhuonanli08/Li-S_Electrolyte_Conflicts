Experimental Section
1.1 Materials Preparation 
All solvents, including tetrahydropyran (THP), anisole, benzyl chloride, and 
toluene (Sigma-Aldrich), were dried over molecular sieves for more than three days 
prior to use. Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI, Arkema) was employed as the 
lithium salt without further purification. The sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) 
cathode material with approximately 46% sulfur was supplied by Adeka Corporation.
1.2 Density functional theory (DFT) calculations
All density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed with the 
Gaussian16 package. Ground-state geometries were optimized at the B3LYP/6-
311+G(d,p) level. The interaction between Li⁺ and solvent molecules was quantified 
by the binding energy, defined as 𝐸𝑏= 𝐸Li⁺–A ― 𝐸Li⁺ ― 𝐸A, where each term 
corresponds to the total energy of the complex, isolated ion, and solvent, respectively. 
Electrostatic potential (ESP) surface maps were obtained at the same theoretical level.

1.3 Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations
Classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted with GROMACS 
2024.1, 2 The OPLS-AA force field was adopted with 1.2×CM5 charges assigned to 
solvent molecules, while FSI⁻ parameters were derived from the 0.8 OPLS 2009 IL 
force field.3, 4 Initial configurations were generated via Packmol in a 7 × 7 × 7 nm³ box 
and subsequently energy-minimized with the conjugate gradient algorithm. The 
systems were equilibrated in the NPT ensemble at 1 bar through an annealing sequence: 
heating from 0 to 330 K over 1 ns, holding for 4 ns, cooling to 298 K, and equilibrating 
for another 4 ns. A 1 fs timestep was used, with pressure and temperature regulated by 
C-rescale and V-rescale algorithms. A 5 ns NPT trajectory was then collected for 
structural analysis of the electrolytes.
1.4 Electrolyte preparation
All electrolytes were prepared inside an Ar-filled glovebox. The baseline 
electrolyte (4M-THP) consisted of 4 M LiFSI in THP. For the compressed formulations, 
the THP-to-aromatic solvent ratio was maintained at 2 : 3. LiFSI was first dissolved in 
DME, followed by the addition of the designated aromatic solvent. The resulting 
electrolytes contained 2 M LiFSI in toluene, anisole, or chlorobenzene (Tol, Anisole, 
and ClBen, respectively).
1.5 Preparation of SPAN cathode
The SPAN cathode consisted of 80 wt% SPAN, 10 wt% Super P, and 10 wt% 
carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) binder. A uniform slurry was obtained by dispersing 
these components in deionized water and isopropanol with a Thinky mixer and 
subsequently casting the mixture onto carbon-coated aluminum foil. The coated 
electrodes were dried under vacuum at 50 °C overnight. Circular discs (12 mm in 
diameter) were then punched out, yielding SPAN areal loadings of approximately 4.5-
8.5 mg cm⁻².

1.6 Electrochemical measurements
Electrochemical measurements were carried out with CR2032 coin cells 
assembled inside an Ar-filled glovebox (H₂O and O₂ < 0.1 ppm). For Li || SPAN half 
cells, a 12 mm SPAN cathode and a 14.3 mm lithium anode were separated by a 19 mm 
ceramic-coated Celgard 2500 membrane. The electrolyte-to-cathode (E/SPAN) ratio 
was maintained at 3-6 μL mg⁻¹ for electrodes with SPAN loadings of 4.5-8.5 mg cm⁻². 
Specific capacities were determined on the basis of SAPN content (1C = 600 mAh g⁻¹). 
Galvanostatic cycling was performed at room temperature at 1.0 – 3.0 V with a 
Land2001 tester, following two initial activation cycles at C/20 and C/10 rates.
Temperature-dependent EIS measurements of Li‖Li cells were collected after 
100 h of cycling at 0.5 mA cm⁻² and 0.5 mAh cm⁻² at 253 – 313 K. The solid-electrolyte 
interphase (Rₓ) and charge-transfer (Rct) resistances were extracted via distribution of 
relaxation time (DRT) analysis with the Python-based pyDRTtools package developed 
by Ciucci’s group.
The activation energy (𝐸) of the charge-transfer process was derived from the 
Arrhenius equation:
𝑅𝑐𝑡= 𝐾𝑒(―𝐸/𝑅𝑇)
where 𝑅𝑐𝑡is the charge-transfer resistance, 𝐾the pre-exponential factor, 𝑅the gas 
constant (8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹), and 𝑇the absolute temperature used in the EIS 
measurements.
1.7 Characterization

Reflex system. 7Li NMR spectra were acquired on a Bruker Avance III 500 MHz 
spectrometer, with DMSO-d₆ and LiPF₆ as reference standards. NMR measurements 
were performed with coaxial tubes. The standard solution (DMSO-d₆ + LiPF₆) was 
loaded into the inner tube, while the test electrolyte was placed in the outer tube. This
