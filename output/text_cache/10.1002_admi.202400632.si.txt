Additional results

Figure S1: a) Measured discharge curve of lithium-sulfur battery at 0.05C for 1M LiTFSI/DME and 1M LiBr/DME electrolyte up to cut-off voltage of 2.0 V. Room temperature, 0.2M Li2S8 dissolved in electrolyte. Right side: corresponding simulated Li2S precipitates on a carbon surface at the middle of the second voltage plateau for b) LiTFSI electrolyte (340 mAh/g) and LiBr electrolyte (780 mAh/g), as well as the simulated Li2S structure at the end of discharge for d) LiTFSI electrolyte and e) LiBr electrolyte.
Figure S1 shows the galvanostatic discharge of lithium-sulfur batteries starting at 2.3 V for LiTFSI and 2.4 V for LiBr electrolyte for a short time before dropping to 2.1 V to a second voltage plateau and ending at 2.0 V cut-off voltage. It is assumed that Li2S precipitation on the carbon substrate surface begins at the start of the second voltage plateau. The discharge was interrupted in the middle of the second voltage plateau, as shown in Figure 1. The surface structure of LiTFSI (Figure 1b) and LiBr (Figure 1c) was recorded using SEM and the kMC model was used to reproduce the results (Figure 1d and e). Figure S1a shows the corresponding simulated structures at 340 mAh/g for LiTFSI (b) and at 780 mAh/g for LiBr (c). Continuing the Li2S precipitation simulation until the end of discharge results in the simulated Li2S structures in Figure S1d and e. The cell with the LiTFSI electrolyte exhibits a flat Li2S layer at 340 mAh/g, whose thickness increases above 5 nm by the end of the discharge at 510 mAh/g, when it still shows a flat structure. In LiBr electrolyte, the maximum Li2S thickness doubles to over 60 nm until the end of discharge, keeping the characteristic particle-like Li2S structure, thus fully covering the carbon surface. The particulate morphology for LiBr leads to a significantly higher discharge capacity of 1220 mAh/g. It must be noted that the simulation was run with a constant S2- concentration until the cut-off condition of at least 5 nm Li2S layer thickness at any position was reached; this was the end of discharge, as the restricted tunneling would cause the voltage to drop rapidly thereafter.
