Lithium Nitrate: A Double-edged Sword in the Rechargeable Lithium-Sulfur Cell
Experimental details:
The surface electrode were fabricated by mixing CTAB-modified S-GO nanocomposite, carbon black, with an elastomeric styrene butadiene rubber /carboxy methyl cellulose (SBR/CMC) binder at a weight ratio of 70:20:10 in water/ethanol solution to form a slurry using an ultrasonicator. The resulting flurry was uniformly spread via a blade (Elcometer 3450 Bird Film Applicator) on an aluminum foil. The film on the Al foil was dried in the vacuum oven at 50oC for 48 hours after keeping at room temperature for 24 hours. The electrode was punched into circular pieces with a dimeter of 12.7 mm as the cathode. The average loading of Sulfur in the electrodes was 0.8 mg/cm2.
In the electrolyte, lithium bis(trifluoromethtsulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI, signa-Aldrich) was added to the (n-methyl-(n-butyl) pyrrolidinium bis(trifluoromethtsulfonyl)imide (PYR14TFSI, sigma-Aldrich), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL), and dimethoxyethane (2:1:1 volume ratio) to form 1.0 M concentration. 0.1, 0.5, or 1.0 M LiNO3 was used as an additive in the electrolyte. The amount of electrolyte used in the work is 60 μl, the weight ratio of electrolyte to sulfur is 75.
CR2032-type coin cells were assembled by sandwiching two separators (Celgard 2400) between a lithium metal foil (99.98%, Cyprus Foote Mineral) and a CTAB-GO-S based sulfur electrode in a glove box filled with high-purity argon gas. Galvanostatic discharge and charge testing of the coin cells was performed using a battery cycler (Maccor Series 4000) at different rates between 1.5 and 2.8V. The cell capacity was normalized both by the weight of sulfur and total electrode weight.

Surface- and bulk- characterization of the cathode materials

Figure S2: (a) C K-edge and (b) S K-edge TEY and TFY signals of the cathode C-0.1, C-0.5, and C-1.0. The TEY and TFY signals were recorded in black and red, respectively. The spectra were lifted up for clear comparison. 

The surface and bulk of the cathode materials were monitored by recording the TEY and TFY spectra. The C K-edge and S K-edge XAS signals of the cathodes were recorded in Figure S3 (a) and (b), respectively. The surface-bulk differences were not obvious on the C K-edge spectra, however major differences were observed on the S K-edge spectra. Essentially, more active sulfur were observed on the bulk of the cathode than on the surface. Moreover, the surface-sensitive spectra of the cathode showed stronger S-O basing species intensities compared to those in the bulk-sensitive spectra. With the increasing of the LiNO3 concentration applied in the cells, the surface-bulk differences became more obvious, showing as less active sulfur and more S-O species on the surface.
Peak fitting of the TEY- and TFY- S K-edge XAS spectra of cathode materials

Figure S3: The S K-edge TEY- and TFY- XAS signals of cathode materials obtaining from cells containing different LiNO3 amounts. After subtracting an arctangent background, several Gaussian functions were employed to fit the XAS spectrum. The peaks representing S-S, C-S, SO32-, COSO2-, and SO42- species were labeled by cyan, magenta, purple, violet, and pink, respectively.
