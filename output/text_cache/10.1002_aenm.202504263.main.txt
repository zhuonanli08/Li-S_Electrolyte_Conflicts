Nonﬂammable Cocktail Electrolyte for Stable Li||SPAN
Batteries
Yan Li, Han Zhou, Kai Huang, Xiaoyu Jin, Chaofan Liang, Ji Wu, Zhou Fang, Xiaoqun Qi,
Ruining Jiang, and Long Qie*
Lithium-sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (Li||SPAN) batteries call for electrolytes
that are simultaneously compatible with both the SPAN cathode and Li metal
anode, and the nonﬂammable and low-cost nature is also a plus. Whereas, the
typical used ether (incompatible with SPAN and ﬂammable), carbonate
(incompatible with Li and ﬂammable), or even the recently meticulously f
ormulated ones fail to meet all the requirements. Here, a cocktail strategy is
proposed to design the electrolyte for Li||SPAN battery. Just like a cocktail
consists of multiple components, sulfolane is used as the nonﬂammable main
solvent, ﬂuorine-contained lithium bis(triﬂuoromethane sulfonyl)imide salt
and ﬂuoroethylene carbonate additive to construct a stable electrode
interface, lithium nitrate for anion-coordinated solvation structure, triethyl
phosphate to strengthen the ﬂame resistance, and
1,1,2,2-tetraﬂuoroethyl-2,2,3,3-tetraﬂuoropropyl ether as a non-solvating
diluent to modulate viscosity. Being used in Li||SPAN cells, the as-formulated
electrolyte enables the formation of robust electrolyte interface on both SPAN
cathode and Li anode, even at a low salt concertation of 0.45 m, and enables
Li||SPAN cell an exceptional cycling stability with 91.3% capacity retention
over 500 cycles and high ﬂame resistance.
1. Introduction
Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN), in which sulfur is bonded
covalently to the conductive heterocyclic polymer backbone, sup-
ports a polysulﬁde-free redox mechanism that mitigates the shut-
tle eﬀect typically observed in lithium-sulfur batteries.[1,2] When
coupled with a lithium (Li) metal anode, Li||SPAN cells emerge as
promising candidates for next-generation energy storage.[3] Nev-
ertheless, the diﬀerent electrolyte taste of SPAN and Li makes the
exploration of the electrolytes compatible with both SPAN cath-
ode and Li anode challenging.
Over the years, various electrolytes, primarily carbonate and
ether-based ones, have been employed in Li||SPAN batteries.

To address these challenges, recent studies have focused on (lo-
calized) high-concentration electrolytes as a viable compromise
solution. For instance, Liao et al. reported that a 4 m LiFSI in
tetrahydropyran (THP) enables Li||SPAN cells to maintain stable
cycling over 1000 cycles.[12] This stability enhancement originates
from the reduced number of free solvents in the high-salt con-
centration THP electrolyte, which stabilizes the Li-metal anode
by forming an inorganic-rich SEI and stabilizes the SPAN cath-
ode by mitigating polysulﬁde dissolution. Nevertheless, the el-
evated salt concentration increases electrolyte viscosity and cost,
leading to sluggish ion transport kinetics and hindering the prac-
tical scalability of Li||SPAN batteries. In this context, there is an
urgent need to develop new electrolytes that simultaneously en-
sure compatibility with both SPAN cathodes and Li metal anodes,
while oﬀering low ﬂammability and cost-eﬀectiveness.
Herein, a cocktail strategy is proposed to formulate the desired
electrolyte for Li||SPAN batteries. In details, nonﬂammable sul-
folane (SL) is selected as the main solvent, ﬂuorine-contained
lithium bis(triﬂuoromethane sulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) salt and
ﬂuoroethylene carbonate (FEC) additive are chosen to construct
stable electrode/electrolyte interface, LiNO3 to induce anion-
coordinated solvation and dense Li deposition, triethyl phosphate
(TEP) to strengthen the ﬂammable resistance, non-solvating

1.1. Cocktail Design of the D-STFE Electrolyte
To fulﬁll the complex electrolyte requirements of Li||SPAN batter-
ies, the multi-component D-STFE electrolyte was designed fol-
lowing a cocktail-inspired strategy (See the detailed design pro-
cess in Figures S1–S5 and Table S1, Supporting Information).
The formulation integrates SL, TEP, and FEC as solvents, LiTFSI
and LiNO3 as salts, and TTE as a non-solvating diluent (Figure
1a). Speciﬁcally, the solvents and diluent were ﬁrst blended (de-
noted as STFE), into which 0.25 m LiTFSI and 0.2 m LiNO3 were
subsequently dissolved to obtain the ﬁnal D-STFE electrolyte.
Each component was selected based on the following consider-
ations (Figure 1b):
1) Stable lithium interface: To stabilize the Li interface, LiTFSI
and FEC are selected to promote the formation of robust SEI
layers. The incorporation of LiNO3 introduces nitrate anions
with high deshielding eﬀects, favoring anion-involved solva-
tion structures that accelerate Li+ de-solvation and mitigate
interfacial polarization.[13,14] The resulting SEI is enriched in
inorganic species, which support uniform Li deposition.[15,16]
2) Cathode compatibility: LiNO3 and ﬁlm-forming additive FEC
contribute to the generation of a uniform cathode-electrolyte
interface (CEI), which can isolate the SPAN cathode and elec-
trolyte to inhibit side reactions and preserve the structural in-
tegrity of SPAN during cycling.
3) Salt solubility: SL and TEP were chosen to guarantee the sol-
ubility of LiNO3 within the electrolyte, ensuring suﬃcient ni-
trate availability to stabilize both anode and cathode inter-
faces.
4) Nonﬂammability:
SL
and
TEP
are
intrinsically
non-
ﬂammable, while the ﬂuorinated diluent TTE further
enhances ﬂame retardancy, collectively providing strong
thermal and safety advantages.
5) Viscosity regulation: Due to the inherently high viscosity of
SL, low-viscosity solvents such as TEP (viscosity: 1.7 cP) and
FEC (viscosity: 2.4 cP), along with the non-solvating dilu-
ent TTE, are added to reduce the overall viscosity of the
electrolyte.[17]
Through this rational formulation, the obtained D-STFE elec-
trolyte with a water content of 49 ppm (Table S2, Supporting In-
formation) simultaneously satisﬁes the electrochemical, thermal,
and kinetic demands of Li||SPAN cells, setting the stage for fur-
ther performance evaluation. In addition, to decouple the eﬀect of
two salts, 0.45 m LiNO3 in STFE (denoted as N-STFE) and 0.45 m
LiTFSI in STFE (denoted as T-STFE) were prepared as the control
electrolytes. Table S3 (Supporting Information) summarizes the
acronyms and the corresponding formulations of the electrolytes
used in this manuscript.

The composition of the SEI formed on Li metal was charac-
terized via X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). N 1s spec-

the transportation of Li+, while LiNxOy promotes the homoge-
neous Li deposition.[19,20] Scanning electron microscopy (SEM,
Figure 2f; Figure S10a, Supporting Information) and optical im-
ages show deposited Li with larger grain size (>10 μm) and metal-
lic luster in D-STFE electrolyte, in contrast to the loosen morphol-
ogy with gray color observed in T-STFE electrolyte (Figure 2g;
Figure S10b, Supporting Information). The cross-sectional imag-
ing and operando observation of Li growth further support the
uniform Li deposition in the D-STFE electrolyte (see the detailed
discussion in Figures S11–S13, Supporting Information). Corre-
spondingly, as shown in Figure 2h, Li plating in D-STFE exhibits
reduced nucleation (96 mV) and growth overpotentials (79 mV),
revealing the promoted desolvation and interphase transporta-
tion of Li+.
Coulombic eﬃciency (CE) measurements further demonstrate
the improved reversibility of the Li plating/stripping processes
enabled by D-STFE (Figure 2i). In Li||Cu cells using D-STFE elec-
trolyte, CE remains stable for over 110 cycles with an average CE
of 95.2% after the initial stabilization cycle, compared with the
rapid CE degradation in the cells with T-STFE electrolyte. Rec-
ognizing the positive eﬀects of LiNO3 on Li metal anode in our
electrolyte system, we further evaluated the cycling performance
of Li||Li symmetric cells in the N-STFE electrolyte. Notably, while
the Li||Li symmetric cell in the N-STFE electrolyte exhibits a pro-
longed cycle life compared to that in the T-STFE electrolyte, the
cell employing the dual-salt D-STFE electrolyte demonstrates the
longest cycle life among those using N-STFE, T-STFE, and D-
STFE electrolytes (Figure S14, Supporting Information).

Further investigation by TEM shows elemental sulfur parti-
cle formation in SPAN electrodes cycled with T-STFE and N-
STFE electrolytes whereas minimal sulfur evolution of the cy-
cled SPAN electrodes in D-STFE electrolyte (Figure 4a–d; Figure
S16, Supporting Information). The UV–vis spectroscopy shows
the SPAN electrode after 30 cycles in N-STFE electrolyte con-
tains 48 wt.% elemental sulfur (Figure S17, Supporting Infor-
mation). The presence of elemental sulfur particles is attributed
to irreversible cleavage of C─S bonds. In D-STFE electrolyte,
SPAN retains structural integrity, consistent with Raman spec-
tra showing strong C─S signals and reversible bond behavior
(Figure 4e; Figure S18, Supporting Information). The cycling
stability of SPAN is governed by two critical factors: 1) stable C─S
bond and 2) steady CEI. In the N-STFE electrolyte, LiNO3 decom-
position promotes the formation of a uniform CEI. However, this
electrolyte simultaneously induces severe structural degradation
of SPAN, triggering elemental sulfur precipitation with conver-
sion mechanism that disrupts CEI integrity. While SPAN in the
T-STFE electrolyte exhibits reduced sulfur evolution compared
to N-STFE, the formed CEI remains inhomogeneous and unsta-
ble. In contrast, the dual-salt D-STFE electrolyte synergistically
addresses both challenges: it suppresses sulfur generation while
facilitating a homogeneous and robust CEI.

stability of SPAN in D-STFE, achieving 91.3% capacity reten-
tion after 500 cycles with a high initial capacity of 615 mAh
g−1 versus 76.7% in T-STFE and 28.7% in N-STFE electrolytes
(Figure 4f,g). When increasing the SPAN mass loading of 6.7 mg
cm−2, Li||SPAN cell delivers a speciﬁc capacity of 465.4 mAh g−1
after 75 cycles at 0.2 A g−1, corresponding to a capacity reten-
tion of 94% (Figure S19, Supporting Information). The capacity
of the cell with higher SPAN loading of 6.7 mg cm−2 is lower
than the cells using cathodes with SPAN loading of 1.0–1.5 mg
cm−2 due to the worse wettability of the electrolyte and cathode
in high-loading cathodes. SPAN in D-STFE also shows better rate
performance (Figure 4h). Even at a current density of 3.2 A g−1,
the capacity remains ≈350 mAh g−1 (Figure S20, Supporting In-
formation). Figure 4i shows the cycling performance of pouch

To benchmark the performance of the D-STFE electrolyte, we
compared it against a conventional carbonate-based electrolyte
(1 m LiPF6 in EC/DEC, 1:1 v/v), hereafter referred to as EC/DEC.
The D-STFE electrolyte demonstrates superior properties across
wettability, ﬂammability, lithium-metal compatibility, and high-
temperature electrochemical performance. Wettability was as-
sessed via contact angle measurements and electrolyte uptake.
The D-STFE electrolyte exhibited a lower contact angle of 40.9°
and higher separator uptake of 81.3%, in contrast to EC/DEC,
indicating improved wetting and separator inﬁltration (Figure
5a). This enhanced interfacial contact is expected to reduce in-
ternal resistance (Figures S22 and S23, Supporting Informa-
tion) and improve ion transport kinetics. Table S10 (Support-
ing Information) displays that the conductivity of electrolyte-
soaked separator containing D-STFE electrolyte (0.291 mS cm−1
at 25 °C and 0.463 mS cm−1 at 60 °C) is higher than that with
EC/DEC electrolyte (0.093 mS cm−1 at 25 °C and 0.102 mS cm−1
at 60 °C).
In terms of safety, D-STFE shows dramatically improved ﬂame
resistance. The self-extinguishing time (SET) was nearly zero for
D-STFE, compared to 110 s g−1 for EC/DEC, conﬁrming its non-
ﬂammable nature (Figure 5b). Ignition tests on pouch cells fur-
ther conﬁrmed this diﬀerence: the cell with D-STFE electrolyte
remained non-ignitable under direct ﬂame exposure, while the
EC/DEC-based cell ignited instantly and continued burning for
over 2 min (Figure 5h).

3. Conclusion
In summary, we develop a low-concentration, nonﬂammable
sulfolane-based electrolyte (D-STFE) using a rational cocktail
design strategy to simultaneously stabilize both lithium metal
and SPAN electrodes. The incorporation of LiNO3 induces
anion-rich solvation structures that enhance Li+ desolvation
and enable uniform lithium deposition. In parallel, LiTFSI
and FEC contribute to the formation of robust, inorganic-
rich SEI and CEI layers, suppressing interfacial degradation
and structural transformation. This dual-interfacial stabiliza-
tion allows the Li||SPAN cell to achieve 91.3% capacity re-
tention over 500 cycles, along with strong thermal safety and
high-rate performance, even at elevated temperatures (60 °C).
The high wettability, low ﬂammability, and eﬃcient ion trans-
port of the D-STFE electrolyte make it a compelling candi-
date for practical Li||SPAN battery applications. This work pro-
vides a broadly applicable strategy for designing multifunctional
electrolytes that simultaneously address safety, stability, and
scalability.
