a b s t r a c t
Silicon-sulfur (Si-S) full cells show signiﬁcant potential in energy storage due to their high theoretical
energy density compared to commercial lithium-ion batteries. Nevertheless, a majority of electrolytes
reported for Si-S full cells still cannot meet the requirements of stable long-duration cycling. Herein, we
report a high-areal-capacity Si-S full cell consisted with prelithiated SiO, thick Li2S-Se0.05S0.95@pPAN
electrode and a triﬂuorobenzene (F3B) modiﬁed and diluted high-concentration electrolytes. This novel
electrolyte can effectively mitigate the volume expansion of the prelithiated SiO anode and enhance the
utilization of active S in the cathode, showing excellent compatibility with electrodes. The assembled Si-S
full cell using modiﬁed electrolyte displays remarkable cyclability over 100 cycles at 0.1C, exhibiting
higher capacity retention (54.0% vs. 17.5%) than the pristine electrolyte without F3B. More importantly, a
full cell with a thick sulfur cathode (16 mg/cm2) exhibits a superior initial areal capacity of 8.34 mAh/cm2
and high-capacity retention of 52% after 80 cycles, even at a lean electrolyte condition (2 mL/mg). This
work provides guidance for the achievement of high-performance Si-S full cells under lean electrolytes.

1. Introduction
The increasing demand for high-energy-density electrochemical
storage devices has promoted the worldwide development of
rechargeable batteries with high energy density [1e4]. Lithium-
sulfur batteries (Li-S) are considered to be an attractive electro-
chemical system with a high theoretical energy density (2600 Wh/
kg) [5e8]. However, the lithium metal anode exhibits a limited
cycle life and potential safety hazards due to the issue of dendrite
growth, especially in high-areal-capacity Li-S batteries [9e13]. To
avoid such issues without sacriﬁcing the theoretical capacity of the
anode, the silicon (Si) anode is a considerable candidate that has a
theoretical capacity equivalent to that of lithium metal and a low
delithiation potential (0.4 V vs. Li/Liþ) [14e16]. However, the elec-
trochemical performance of silicon-sulfur batteries is far from
realizing the expected targets due to the huge volume expansion of
the Si electrode (300%), severe polysulﬁde (PS) dissolution, and the
side reaction between high-speciﬁc-surface Si and polysulﬁde
[17e19].
For addressing the PS dissolution of the sulfur cathode, various
electrodes have been reported by employing appropriate catalysts,
designing efﬁcient structures, and regulating reaction mechanisms
[20e22]. Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (S@pPAN) has a short-chain
sulfur structure, which almost avoids the shuttle of PS during
cycling, thus exhibiting excellent cyclability and high utilization
[23e27]. Furthermore, the electrochemical performance of the
S@pPAN cathode can be further improved by doping a small
amount of eutectic accelerator (Se or Te) [28e31]. As for Si anode,
constructing a stabilized micro/nanostructure [32,33] and synthe-
sizing a composite material [34,35] are both effective ways to
alleviate the volume expansion of the electrode. Compared with
constructing complex nanostructures, the SiOx electrode acted as a
potential anode material and has received substantial attention in
recent years due to improved cyclability and relatively low volume
expansion
[36,37].

efﬁciency (CE＜80%) is still the key issue limiting the performance

Based on the features of reaction homogeneity and simplicity, SiOx
anode prelithiated by the chemical strategy is employed to realize a
high initial CE (close to 100%) and reduce the loss of active Li before
the full-cell assembly [41,42]. Hence, the full cell was designed
through prelithiated electrodes to accomplish excellent electro-
chemical performance. Nevertheless, the performance of high-
areal-capacity Si-S full cells based on prelithiated SiOx anode is
still unsatisfactory.
To overcome the poor performance of prelithiated SiOx anode and
meanwhile maintain the merits of prelithiated Se0.05S0.95@pPAN
thick cathode, a facile strategy is to regulate the SEI by tuning the
electrolyte composition, which mitigates volume expansion of anode
and improves the electrochemical performance of full cells. Hence, it
is of great signiﬁcance to develop an ideal electrolyte that shows both
remarkable compatibilities with anode and cathode. Currently, the
special ether-based electrolyte, diluted high-concentration electro-
lytes (DHCE), displays good compatibility with high-loading S elec-
trodes, which are widely used in Li-S batteries [43e45]. Our group
recently reported a ﬂuorobenzene (FB) diluent in DHCE, which in-
duces a cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) with the feature of fast
Liþ transport, thereby promoting the cyclability of the thick S elec-
trode [45]. However, the relatively inferior solid electrolyte interface
(SEI) formed in such ether electrolyte is not sufﬁcient to protect the
SiOx anode from cracking. Simultaneously, it is generally considered
that the SiOx anode displays excellent cyclability in the DHCE with
ﬂuoroethylene carbonate (FEC) as the SEI-forming additive [46].
Nevertheless, FEC usually has a high density (1.56 g/cm3) and high
cost, which would undoubtfully lower the energy density of full cells
and discourage their large-scale practical applications. Recently, Our
group proposed a triﬂuorobenzene additive (F3B), which was used to
modify the electrolyte. The additive not only has the advantages of
low density and low cost but also signiﬁcantly improves the ionic
conductivity of the electrolyte, thus improving the reaction kinetics
of the battery. In addition, the additive has excellent compatibility
with anode and cathode, which achieves a liF-rich interface protec-
tive layer [47].
In this work, a high-areal-capacity full cell is prepared with
prelithiated electrodes. Besides, by introducing F3B additives, the
modiﬁed diluted high-concentration electrolyte exhibits excellent
compatibility with high-loading S cathodes and SiO anodes, which
not only effectively accommodates the volume expansion of the
prelithiated SiO electrode but also improves active material utili-
zation of S cathode. As a result, compared with DHCE without F3B,
the full cells using such electrolyte (cathode loading:6 mg/cm2)
display remarkable cyclability and relatively high capacity reten-
tion over 100 cycles at 0.1C (54.0% vs. 17.5%). More impressively, for
the assembled Si-S full cell with a high cathode-loading of 16 mg/
cm2, it exhibits an excellent initial areal capacity (8.34 mAh/cm2)
and good cycling performance (capacity retention of 52.8% after 80
cycles) at lean electrolyte (E/S ¼ 2). This work not only provides a
promising strategy for preparing high-areal-capacity Si-S full
cells but also proposes an effective SEI formation agent that endows
ether electrolytes with excellent compatibility with electrodes.

The Se0.05S0.95@pPAN thick cathode was synthesized according
to the previously reported method [31]. The Li-naphthalene (Li-NP,
0.5 M) solution was prepared by dissolving 5 mmol of naphthalene
and 5 mmol of lithium metal with a molar ratio of 1:1 in 5 mL DME,
followed by stirring for 1 h at 30 C. Similarly, the Li-4,40-dime-
thylbiphenyl (Li-4,40-DMBP, 0.5 M) solution was prepared by dis-
solving 5 mmol of 4,40-DMBP and 20 mmol of lithium metal with a
molar ratio of 1:4 in 5 mL DME.
A Si-S full cell is prepared by the prelithiated electrodes due to
the advantages of simple synthesis and the strong lithiation ability
of chemical prelithiation. Li-NP and Li-4,40-DMBP were used as
prelithiation reagents for the cathode and anode, respectively. The
Se0.05S0.95@pPAN and SiO electrode with different loadings were
immersed in Li-NP and Li-4,40-DMBP for 15, 30, and 45 min at 30 C,
respectively.
It was worth noting that the high-loading cathode (16 mg/cm2)
adopted electrochemical prelithiation. The prelithiated electrodes
were washed several times in DME and dried in a glove box for
assembling a Si-S full cell.
2.2. Characterization
The images of treated electrodes employing various electrolytes
were carried out with scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The Raman spectra of
different electrolytes were tested by LabRAM HR800. The density
was tested at 25 C. The ascend 600 MHz was used to test hydrogen
nuclear magnetic spectra (1H NMR) of electrolytes. The impedance
measurements of the Si-S full cells were measured with a Solartron
1470E. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted on
an AXIS-ULTRA DLD-600W.
2.3. Composition of diluted high-concentration electrolyte
The triﬂuorobenzene modiﬁed and diluted high-concentration
electrolyte (FB-DHCE-F3B) was composed of LiFSI and LiTFSI
(0.66:0.33, molar ratio) in a mixed solvent of DOL, DME, FB, and F3B
(0.2:1:2.8:0.2, molar ratio). The ﬂuorobenzene diluted high-
concentration electrolyte (FB-DHCE) was made up of LiFSI and
LiTFSI (0.66:0.33, molar ratio) in a mixed solvent of DOL, DME, and
FB (0.2:1:3, molar ratio).
2.4. Electrochemical performance characterization
Half cells were assembled using Li metal and prelithiated elec-
trodes in the Ar-ﬁlled glove. For SiO electrode, it was prepared by
mixing SiO, Super P, and Sodium alginate in deionized water to
realize a uniform slurry at mass ratios of 80,10, and 10, respectively.
For the half cells, the cathode (6 mg/cm2) was prepared with an
8 mm diameter, and the dosage of electrolyte was ﬁxed at 30 mL. For
the full cells, the size of the cathode (16 mg/cm2) was 412, and the
electrochemical performance was tested under lean electrolyte (E/
S ¼ 2). Besides, in full cells, the area of the anodes is slightly larger
than that of the cathode. The Neware battery test system was used
to study electrochemical performance of all cells.

3.4. Electrochemical performance of Si-S full cells
For evaluating the superiority of the F3B additive, the electro-

further investigated. All current densities of full cells are based on

full cells using FB-DHCE-F3B deliver a higher discharge capacity of
593 mAh/g at 0.15C than that of FB-DHCE (554 mAh/g). Moreover,
with the increase of current densities, the differences in discharge
capacities of full cells in various electrolytes become more signiﬁ-
cant. And the corresponding voltage-capacity proﬁles of full cells at
varying electrolytes are shown in Fig. 5b and Fig. S16. Compared
with the full cells in FB-DHCE, the discharge capacities of 593, 518,
433, 315, and 205 mAh/g in FB-DHCE-F3B are realized at 0.15, 0.2,
0.5, 1, 1.0, and 1.5C, respectively. When the current density returns

performance of full cells (6 mg/cm2) at 0.1C is shown in Fig. 5c. The
full cells using different electrolytes both deliver a high initial
discharge capacity of 626 and 600 mAh/g, respectively. However,
the discharge capacity of full cells delivers 402 mAh/g after only 15
cycles in FB-DHCE and shows a low capacity retention of 20.5% over
100 cycles, suggesting poor compatibility between the prelithiated
electrodes and ether electrolyte. In contrast, the full cells in FB-
DHCE-F3B show an improved cycling performance and retain
338 mAh/g after 100 cycles with a capacity retention of 54%, which
proves that the F3B additive is beneﬁcial to enhancing the elec-
trochemical performance of Si-S full cells.
To verify the superiority of FB-DHCE-F3B in practical full
batteries, the electrochemical performance of the prelithiated thick
cathode (16 mg/cm2) in different electrolytes was further studied
with lean electrolyte. Fig. 5d displays the cycling performance at
0.5 mA/cm2 under limited N/P (1.2) and lean electrolyte (E/S ¼ 2).
The full cell in FB-DHCE-F3B displays a superior initial areal capacity
(8.34 mAh/cm2) and a high capacity retention (70%) after 50 cycles,
demonstrating excellent cycling stability under practical conditions
(Fig. 5e). We compared reported full cells assembled by prelithiated
S@pPAN electrode with our work in terms of areal capacity, E/S
ratio, cycle performance, capacity retention, energy density, and
decay rate per cycle [49,50]. It is worth emphasizing that this is the
highest areal capacity of Si-S full battery reported under lean
electrolyte conditions (Table S5). Most importantly, the per-cycle
decay rate and initial energy density of the full battery described in
this work are 0.82% and 834 Wh/kg (based on the cathode),
implying the great potential of the FB-DHCE-F3B to develop a high-
performance full battery in practical applications.
Fig. 6 illustrates the underlying mechanisms that contribute to
the superior electrochemical performance of a prelithiated Si-S full
cell using the proposed FB-DHCE-F3B electrolyte. In the cathode,
the LiF-rich CEI formed on the Se0.05S0.95@pPAN acts as a protective
layer
that
accelerates
ion
transport
dynamics,
resulting
in
improving the utilization of active materials, and thus realizing the
excellent electrochemical performance of Li2S-Se0.05S0.95@pPAN. In
the meantime, the robust SEI formed on the SiO surface effectively
buffers
the
volume
variation
and
maintains
electrode
stability while protecting the SiO from side reactions with diffused
polysulﬁde. In addition, the F3B additive is beneﬁcial to improving
the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte, thus boosting the reaction
kinetics of the full battery.
4. Conclusion
In summary, a high-areal-capacity Si-S full cell is prepared by
employing a prelithiated SiO anode and thick Li2S-Se0.05S0.95@pPAN
cathode. Besides, an effective triﬂuorobenzene modiﬁed diluted
high concentration electrolyte is developed for high-performance
Si-S full cells. Such electrolyte endows anode and cathode with
LiF-rich SEI and CEI, respectively, which not only effectively reduces
the volume expansion of the SiO electrode but also improves active
material utilization of the S cathode. Hence, the assembled Si-S full
cell exhibits a high areal capacity of 8.34 mAh/cm2 and remarkable
cyclability over 80 cycles under practical conditions (E/S ¼ 2). Such
design of full cell and electrolyte provides a simple and effective
way to fabricate high-performance prelithiated Si-S full cells.
