Experimental Section
Preparation of electrolytes: The Blank electrolyte consisted of 1 M lithium bis-(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI) in a DME and DOL (v/v = 1:1) with 2 wt% LiNO3. The different concentrations of TBAI3-containing electrolyte is added to the blank electrolyte with mass ratios of 0.1 %, 1 % and 2 % TBAI3, respectively. Then, the mixed liquid was stirred for 30 minutes at 25°C in Ar-filled glove box to form the uniform solution.
Electrode preparations: For the S/CNTs preparation, the S was infiltrated into CNTs using a melt-diffusion strategy at 155°C for 24h, forming well-contacted and homogeneous S/CNTs composites. The cathode materials were prepared by mixing S/CNTs, Super P, and polyvinylende fluoride (PVDF) binder in a weight ratio of 8: 1: 1 in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). The slurry was then coated onto the aluminum foil and dried at 60°C for 12h. Afterward, the dried coated aluminum foil was punched into disks with a diameter of 14 mm for Li-S coin cell use, and the loading of the active material was 1.1 to 1.5 mg cm-2. 
Materials Characterization: The morphology and structure were characterized by a JEOL JSM-7500F FESEM coupled with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer. The XPS characterization was carried out on an ESCA LAB 250Xi instrument with Al Kα (1486.7 eV) radiation. The visualization experiment of lithium polysulfide was measured using an UV–Vis spectrophotometer. Raman spectra of the cells were recorded with the LabRAM HR Evolution instrument (HORIBA Jobin Yvon) with excitation line of 532 nm. 
Cell Assembly and Electrochemical Measurements: 
Li-S cells: CR-2032 coin cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box using the S/CNTs-coated aluminum foil as the cathode, lithium foil with a diameter of 12 mm as anode, and Polypropylene film (16 mm) as the separator. 
In Situ Raman Spectroscopy: Li-S cells were sealed in a CIS-Raman-LB device (Hefei In-situ Technology Co., Ltd., China) for the in situ Raman spectroscopy analysis at 532 nm Laser. The cells (sulfur loading: 1.1 mg cm−2) were tested at a charge/discharge rate of 0.05 C.
Assembly of Li2S4 symmetric cells: A 0.2 M Li2S4 solution was prepared by dissolving S and Li2S at a molar ratio of 3:1 in solution of 1 M LiTFSI/(DME/DOL) (v/v = 1:1) with 2 wt% LiNO3. The solution was stirred at 60°C overnight. The Li2S4 solution containing TBAI3 was prepared by adding 1% TBAI3 into the above solution, then the mixed liquid was stirred for 30 minutes at 25°C. 
Li2S deposition experiment: The Li2S deposition experiment was measured with assembled cells using carbon paper as the cathode and lithium foil as the anode. In the cell assembly process, 30 𝜇L electrolyte containing 0.2 M Li2S8 was added on the carbon paper side and another 30 𝜇L electrolyte with/without TBAI3 was dropped on the Li foil side. The cell was first discharged at constant current to 2.06 V and then discharged with the constant voltage at 2.05 V until the current was below 0.01 mA for the nucleation and deposition of Li2S.
The cells for Linear Sweep Voltammetry (LSV) are assembled using stainless steel as the electrodes. All the cells were assembled in an Ar−filled glove box and tested on NEWARE-CT4008 cell test instruments at 25°C. Modeling and Simulations: Materials Studio (Version 2020) was used to simulate the electrolyte system using. When the molecules and ions were constructed, the clean module was applied to make the molecular configuration more rational. Then COMPAS III (Condensed-Phase Optimized Molecular potential for Atomistic Simulation Studies III) was used to calculate force field and charge distribution in the force field module. According to the experimental data, the electrolyte density is 1.20 g cm-3 (or 1.22 g cm-3), and the molecules and ions were initially mixed in a cubic box. Then the forcite module of molecular mechanics was applied to optimize the structure of the system. Finally, using NVT at 298K, the time step is 1 fs and the total time is 50 ps. The radial distribution function and ion diffusion coefficient were obtained by analyzing forcite data. Based on density functional theory (DFT), the linear orbital energy and binding energy are calculated using Dmol3 module.
The modeling of Li electrodeposition was simulated by using COMSOL Multiphysics 5.6 based on the finite element method. The behavior of charged ions was described by Nernst-Plank-Poisson equation. The concentration-dependent Butler–Volmer equation was employed to introduce the reaction of electrode surface. The simulation of Li+ distribution was conducted in 800×800 nm2 half-cell electrodeposition system. Initial surface protuberances were introduced in the bare Li mode as Li dendrites to simplify the computation process. Deformed geometry interfaces were applied to the electrodes interface and the moving speed of meshes was same as deposited rate. The plating voltage difference between the two ends of the model was set to 0.5 V. The Li+ concentration was 1 mol L-1, and the electrolyte conductivity of the Blank and 1% TBAI3 electrolyte was 0.761 and 0.93 S m-1, respectively.

Figure S8. Electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of the Li||Li cells of different electrolytes (a) before and (b) after 200 cycles. The enlarged figure shows the 1% TBAI3 electrolyte cell after 200 cycles.
To determine the optimal concentration of the additives, the electro-chemical impedance spectra were analyzed in 0.1%, 1%, and 2% TBAI3 electrolytes. It shows that the addition of TBAI3 additives without changed their characteristic features, indicating that there are no TBAI3-derived layers on the Li surface. The addition of 1% TBAI3 additive reduces the semicircle of the Nyquist, this is because the 1% TBAI3 effectively suppresses the “tip effect” and accelerates the uniform deposition of lithium ions. Minimal or excessive additives can not provide the optimum electrostatic shielding effect.

Figure S10. SEM images of plated Li in (a) the Blank electrolyte , (b) electrolyte with 0.1% TBAI3 (c) 1% TBAI3, and (d) 2% TBAI3 after 200 cycles at 1 mA cm-2 with a capacity of 1 mAh cm-2.
The lithium deposition behavior on the surface of corresponding electrode was observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) after 200 cycles. As demonstrated in Figure S10a, for the Blank electrolyte, the Li metal on the electrode surface exhibits unfavorable mossy dendrites. It is obvious that the dendrites are exhibited in the Minimal (0.1%) and excessive (2%) additives cells. In contract, in the SEM image of the electrode surface in 1% TBAI3 electrolyte (Figure S10c), a smooth and dendrite-free surface can be obtained. The dendrite-free Li deposition behavior in 1% TBAI3 can be mainly attributed to the “cation-shielding” effect formed by the cations in the TBA+ group on the electrode surface.
