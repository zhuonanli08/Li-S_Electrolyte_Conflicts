Experimental Section

1.1 Materials:
1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME), 2-Methyltetrahydrofuran (MeTHF), 1,4-dioxane (DOX), lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI), and lithium nitrate (LiNO3) were purchased from Duoduo Chem Co., Ltd. Li metal foil was purchased from Xinghua Benote Battery Materials Co., Ltd. The thickness of Li is 300 μm and 50 μm. Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) (Mw=150,000) and sulfur were purchased from Aladdin.
1.2 Electrolyte Preparation:
All electrolytes were prepared and stored inside an argon-filled glovebox.The DME MeTHF MTD73, MTD64, and MTD55 electrolytes were prepared by dissolving 1 M LiFSI in DME, MeTHF, MeTHF/DOX (in 7:3, 3:2, and 5:5 v/v), respectively. The 1MTD and 2MTD electrolytes were dissolved 1 M and 2 M LiFSI into MeTHF/DOX (3:2 v/v), respectively. The 2MTD1 electrolyte was prepared by dissolving 1wt% LiNO3 into the 2MTD electrolyte. The 0.25 M Li2S6 solution was prepared by mixing Li2S and S in a 1:5 molar ratio in different solvents. 
1.3 SPAN Cathode Preparation:
To synthesize sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN), PAN and S were mixed in an agate mortar at a mass ratio of 1:3. The mixture was heated in an argon-filled furnace at 350 °C for 6 h with a ramping rate of 3 °C min-1. .The SPAN cathodes (~0.85 mg cm-2 ) were prepared by uniformly mixing SPAN, carbon black (Super P) and polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) with a weight ratio of 7:2:1 and it was casted onto Carbon coated Al collector, then drying with 80 ℃ at vacuum environment for 12 h. The high-loading SPAN cathodes (~3.8 and 7.71 mg cm-2) were prepared by uniformly mixing SPAN, carbon black (Super P) and Poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) with a weight ratio of 8:1:1 and it was casted onto Carbon coated Al collector, then drying with 80 ℃ at vacuum environment for 12 h.. 
2. Characterization
2.1 Structure Characterizations: The morphologies of Li metal anodes were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-7610F, Japan). The electrolyte solution structures were analyzed by Raman spectrometer (Invia Reβex, Renishaw) with an exciting laser of 532 nm, and 1H nuclear magnetic spectroscopy (1H-NMR, Bruker 400M). The solid electrolyte interface (SEI) and cathode electrolyte interface (CEI) films on different electrodes were analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha). 
2.2 Electrochemical Measurements: The cells of Li||Cu, Li||Li, and Li||SS were assembled in standard 2400 coin-type cells with 60 μL electrolyte in an Ar-filled glove box with both oxygen and water contents below 0.1 ppm. The amount of electrolyte in Li||SPAN cells is 80 μl under the same other conditions. The charge and discharge processes were manipulated in the CT-4008 Neware system. The voltage window of SPAN cells (1C =800 mAh g-1) is 1-3 V. For the Li||Cu half cell, the diameter of Cu foil is 12 mm and Li foil is 10 mm, and need five cycles from 0 V-1 V under the current density of 0.5 mA cm-2 to form the SEI and remove the contaminants on Cu foil. The Li||Li symmetric cells were assembled with 10 mm Li foils, the testing method for Li symmetric cells is as follows: (1) charge the Li symmetric cell with a constant current density of 1 mA cm-2 for 1 h; (2) then, discharge the Li symmetric cell with a constant current density of 1 mA cm-2 for 1 h. Tafel curves of electrolytes were measured at a scan rate of 1 mV s-1 in symmetrical Li batteries with a voltage range of -0.25 to 0.25 V (vs. Li/Li+). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were tested on a CHI660E electrochemical workstation. In the CV tests, the potential was swept between 1 and 3 V with scan rates between 0.05 and 0.5 mV s−1. The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) was operated using the CT-4008 Neware system. The Li+ transference number () of the electrolyte was measured using the method from Abraham et al.[1]. The procedure was as follows: a Li symmetric cell was assembled and a constant voltage of 10 mV was applied. The polarization current including the initial current (I0) and steady-state current (Is) was recorded. Simultaneously, the initial and steady-state values of the bulk resistance (Rb0 and Rbs) and electrode-electrolyte interface resistance (Ri0 and Ris) were measured by the EIS test before and after constant potential polarization. 2.3 Calculation Details
In this system, the x, y and Z dimensions were periodic boundary conditions. The OPLS force field, which is suitable for electrolyte solution, was used to optimize sample structures for preliminary structural optimization. Atomic charges of ions were multiplied by scale factor 0.73 to correct the polarization effect of anion and cation. First, the conjugate gradient algorithm and energy minimization were performed to obtain a stable structure before using dynamic simulations. Each sample was then equilibrated under the NPT ensemble at a constant temperature of 400 K to achieve an equilibrium state with zero pressure for 5 ns. Subsequently, the system temperature was reduced from 400 K to 298 K for annealing 10 ns under the NPT ensemble (under 1 atmosphere). The Andersen feedback thermostat and Berendsen barostat algorithm were applied in the system with temperature and pressure conversion. Next, MD simulations were further carried out for 10 ns with a time step of 1 fs per integration step under the ensemble conditions of NVT (298 K). System energy can be obtained through structural optimization using the energy minimization.
All of spin-polarized calculations based on density functional theory (DFT) were performed by utilizing first-principles[2]. The generalized gradient approximation (GGA) in the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof form and Semicore Pseudopotential method (DSPP) with the double numerical basis sets plus the polarization functional (DNP) were adopted[3,4]. The double numerical basis sets plus the polarization functional (DNP) were adopted. A DFT-D correction with Grimme scheme was used to account for the dispersion interaction[5]. The SCF convergence for each electronic energy was set as 1.0 × 10–7 Ha, and the geometry optimization convergence criteria were set up as follows: 1.0 × 10–7 Ha for energy, 0.001 Ha Å–1 for force, and 0.001 Å for displacement, respectively. Binding energies (Eb) were determined using the formula Eb= Ead/sub - Ead - Esub, where Ead/sub represents the total energy of the adsorbate/substrate complex, Ead is the energy of the isolated adsorbate, and Esub is the energy of the pristine substrate.

Note 1:
As shown in Figures S1-S3, neat methyltetrahydrofuran (MeTHF) is unsuitable as an electrolyte for SPAN due to its high solubility for lithium polysulfides. Although DOX can significantly suppress the dissolution of polysulfides, it suffers from low deposition/stripping efficiency on the lithium anode. Therefore, combining these two solvents with lithium nitrate is expected to yield an electrolyte suitable for SPAN-based batteries. Given that the 2 wt% LiNO3 formulation is not soluble and the 1MTD1 electrolyte exhibits low Coulombic efficiency, 2MTD1 was selected as the optimal electrolyte.
