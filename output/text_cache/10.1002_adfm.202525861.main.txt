Dynamic Reconstruction of Polysulﬁdes to Elemental Sulfur
Enabling High-Performance Ah-Level Lithium-Sulfur Pouch
Cells
Zulong Liu, Sheng Liu, Jun Chen, Xuemei Ren, Chi Zhang, Yu Jiao,* Shuchen Liu,
Xiaodong Zhu,* Yichao Yan, Tianyu Lei, Dongjiang Chen, Yin Hu, and Wei Chen*
Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries represent a promising high-energy-density
secondary battery technology, with a theoretical energy density of 2600 Wh
kg−1. However, its performance is still hindered by the polysulﬁde (LiPS) shuttle
eﬀect, leading to the active material loss and anode electrode interface failure.
Herein, a dynamic polysulﬁde reconstruction strategy is reported employing
5-ﬂuoroisatonic anhydride (FAIn) in the electrolyte as a reversible regulator
that chemically reacts with the dissolved polysulﬁdes, reconstituting them into
elemental sulfur and thereby suppressing the polysulﬁde shuttle eﬀect. During
charging, the reaction products (Li-FAIn) are electrochemically oxidized
to regenerate the FAIn regulator, preserving the FAIn additive’s dynamic
equilibrium in the electrolyte. As a result, our design enables Li-S pouch cells
to retain 726 mAh g−1 after 199 cycles at 0.5 C (80% retention), with Ah-level
cells achieving 100 cycles at this rate. This study presents an eﬃcient dynamic
polysulﬁde reconstruction strategy through FAIn incorporation, providing a
novel approach to suppress shuttle eﬀects and enhance battery performance.

2. Result and Discussion
Soluble polysulﬁdes formation at the electrode interface gener-
ates concentration gradients, inevitably causing the shuttle ef-
fect (Figure 1a). Here, we demonstrate that this issue can be
addressed by chemically mediating the conversion of dissolved
polysulﬁdes, which are electrochemically inactive, back into ele-
mental sulfur (Figure 1b). This strategy directly reforms the dis-
solved polysulﬁdes into active elemental sulfur, enabling their re-
turn to the electrode surfaces for sustained participation in redox
reactions and improving material utilization eﬃciency. The re-
action pathway is depicted in Figure 1b in the FAIn chemically
reduces polysulﬁdes to form Li-FAIn and elemental sulfur (S8).
The insoluble S8 precipitates onto the conductive substrate for re-
utilization. Crucially, Li-FAIn regenerates FAIn during charging
(Figure S1, Supporting Information). This closed-loop reversibil-
ity enables FAIn to persist stably in the electrolyte long-term, out-
performing most reported sacriﬁcial additives.

(0.4 m, the maximum plausible polysulﬁde concentration within
the electrode) underwent a rapid color change from dark brown
to light yellow, accompanied by the formation of a precipitate
(Figure 1c; Figure S2, Supporting Information), indicating a di-
rect chemical reaction. Moreover, FAIn can retain its functional
integrity throughout the polysulﬁde reduction process, as its

Analysis of the Li+ coordination environment in the supernatant
revealed a downﬁeld shift in the 7Li NMR resonance from 0.36
to 0.49 ppm upon FAIn addition (Figure 1d), indicating a mod-

bond formation between Li+ and oxygen atoms of the FAIn an-
hydride group, a conclusion further supported by liquid-phase
Raman spectroscopy (Figure S9, Supporting Information). The
NMR signal initially exhibited a single peak in the Li2S8 solution,
and subsequently transformed into two sharp signals. The for-
mation of these two peaks may be attributed to the redissolution
of elemental sulfur, reconstituted following the reaction between
FAIn and Li2S8, causing the Li resonance peak to shift toward a
higher chemical shift.
The resulting precipitate was isolated by vacuum-drying, yield-
ing a light-yellow powder (Figure 1e, lower inset) morphology
consistent with reference monomeric sulfur (Figure 1e, upper
inset). To corroborated these ﬁndings, the precipitate was ana-
lyzed by X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) and Raman spectroscopy. The
results showed diﬀraction patterns and vibrational modes iden-
tical to those of crystalline S8 (Figure 1e; Figure S10, Supporting
Information). Furthermore, XPS analysis revealed the presence
of signiﬁcant bridging sulfur (SB) (Figure S11, Supporting Infor-
mation), consistent with the formation of elemental sulfur on the
surface (Figure S12, Supporting Information). Collectively, these
results demonstrate that FAIn rapidly reacts with LiPSs to gen-
erate insoluble S8 precipitates. In addition, the produced sulfur
could be reloaded perfectly onto the cathode surface, thereby pre-
serving the electron-conductive path and keeping it electrochem-
ically active. As shown in Figure S13a (Supporting Information),
carbon paper electrodes, pre-saturated with 0.4 m Li2S8 solution,
were immersed in a 0.9 wt% FAIn solution to initiate chemical
conversion. The reaction completed without visible sulfur precip-
itation at the container bottom (Figure S13b, Supporting Infor-
mation), suggesting that the reconstructed elemental sulfur was
retained within the electrode matrix. Subsequent scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) revealed abundant sulfur clusters densely
deposited on the carbon ﬁber surfaces (Figure S13c, Supporting
Information), conﬁrming that the chemically regenerated sul-
fur was eﬀectively re-deposited onto the conductive framework,
thereby enabling its participation in subsequent electrochemical
cycles. Elemental sulfur remains readily utilizable when in di-
rect contact with a conductive substrate, even via simple physical
deposition. To demonstrate this, we performed a control experi-
ment (Figure S14, Supporting Information) in which sulfur was
ﬁrst dispersed in ethanol solution by ultrasonication and then
drop-cast onto a carbon electrode to simulate limited electrical
contact. The resulting conﬁguration still delivered a speciﬁc ca-
pacity of up to 1000 mAh g−1, conﬁrming that sulfur retains high
electrochemical activity when inadequately interfaced with the
conductive matrix.
Following the reaction between FAIn and lithium polysulﬁdes
(LiPSs),[26] the resulting Li-FAIn product displayed the Li 1s bind-
ing energies at 55.3 and 56.5 eV, corresponding to Li─F and Li─O
bonds, respectively (Figure S15, Supporting Information). Simul-
taneously, a pronounced attenuation of the C═O peak intensity
was observed in both the C 1s and O 1s spectra (Figures S16
and S17, Supporting Information), indicating that oxygen atoms
from the anhydride group coordinate with Li in long-chain LiPSs
to the formation of a new coordination structure.[27]
Beyond enabling the regeneration of elemental sulfur from in-
soluble polysulﬁde species in the cathode, FAIn signiﬁcantly low-
ered the reaction free energy of this rate-limiting step, conﬁrming
its catalytic role in promoting the conversion of soluble polysul-
ﬁdes to solid sulfur (Figure 1f). Furthermore, analysis of the low-
est unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels showed
that FAIn has a substantially lower LUMO energy (−2.39 eV) than
DME (1.28 eV), DOL (0.984 eV), LiTFSI (−1.46 eV), and LiNO3
(−1.62 eV) (Figure 1g). This lower energy indicates higher elec-
tron aﬃnity, implying that FAIn is reduced more readily than
other electrolyte components at the lithium metal interface.
Previous experiments conﬁrmed the rapid reaction between
FAIn and LiPSs. To visually monitor LiPS dissolution and diﬀu-
sion during discharge, we assembled a visualizable electrolytic
cell and monitored real-time colorimetric changes (Figure S18,
Supporting Information). In the control cell (without additive),
yellow-brown deposits accumulated at the cathode as discharge
proceeded, consistent with the reduction of sulfur to long-chain
lithium polysulﬁdes (Li2Sn, n ≥4). This behavior indicates an
inability to suppress the polysulﬁde shuttle, allowing dissolved
intermediates to diﬀuse along concentration gradients from the
electrode into non-electrode regions. Upon further discharge, the
electrolyte color changed from yellow-brown to green, suggesting
the reduction of long-chain polysulﬁdes to short-chain species
(Li2Sn, n < 4). In contrast, cells with FAIn exhibited no apprecia-
ble color change during cycling, maintaining a transparent pale-
yellow electrolyte.

To shed light on the Li2S deposition process, we system-
atically elucidated the nucleation mechanism of Li2S in dif-
ferent electrolyte environments by constructing dimensionless
current-time transients and comparing them with four classi-
cal electrodeposition models. The results show that the FAIn-
containing sample shows an earlier nucleation response cur-
rent peak, stronger response current, and higher Li2S deposi-
tion capacity, suggesting faster and suﬃcient deposition. Analy-
sis of the nucleation mechanism indicates that in the absence of
FAIn, the Li2S deposition closely matches the 2D instantaneous
(2DI) nucleation model.[31] In contrast, the current transients in
FAIn-containing cells aligned well with the 3D progressive (3DP)
model (Figure 2h,i). Based on morphological observations of
the carbon paper electrode, the transition in nucleation behavior
mentioned above was further corroborated (Figures S26 and S27,
Supporting Information). These results indicated that the pres-
ence of FAIn alters the Li2S deposition mechanism from 2DI-
dominated to 3DP-dominated growth. This shift to a 3D growth
mode suppresses electrode passivation and enhances polysulﬁde
conversion kinetics, thereby improving active material utilization
and cycling stability.[32]
Through further investigations, at the fully charged cathode
state, we observed a distinct peak with high intensity in the
F spectra at 686.88 eV, corresponding to the FAIn monomer.
(Figure S28, Supporting Information). However, upon discharge,
the intensity of this peak decreased substantially, consistent with
the reaction between FAIn and LiPSs to form Li-FAIn com-
plexes. The same peak reappeared in subsequent cycles under
both fully charged and discharged states (Figures S29 and S30,
Supporting Information), indicating reversible involvement of
FAIn. During charging, the Li─O bonds in Li-FAIn dissociated,
releasing Li+ for migration to the anode, while the remaining Li-
FAIn complexes are electrochemical oxidized to regenerate FAIn
monomers. The cyclic voltammetry (CV) proﬁles exhibit a well-
deﬁned oxidation peak at ≈1.9 V with a current of ≈110 μA, cor-
responding to the conversion of Li-FAIn to FAIn. The high de-
gree of peak superimposition over four consecutive cycles con-
ﬁrms the electrochemical reversibility of this oxidation process
(Figure S1, Supporting Information). Moreover, the consistent
peak shape and position imply a stable concentration of regener-
ated Li-FAIn in each cycle, supporting the establishment of a dy-
namic equilibrium between FAIn and Li-FAIn during operation.
This equilibrium eﬀectively sustains a constant concentration of
active FAIn species throughout cycling. To further corroborate
the reversible conversion mechanism, the reaction process was
monitored by in situ UV–vis spectroscopy using a homemade
apparatus (Figures S31 and S32, Supporting Information). The
spectra reveal a gradual increase in the characteristic FAIn peak
intensity as the voltage rises, consistent with the oxidation of Li-
FAIn to FAIn. Conversely, during discharge, the intensity dimin-
ishes, reﬂecting the consumption of FAIn. These electrochem-
ical and spectroscopic ﬁndings are mutually consistent, provid-
ing critical evidence for the continuous regeneration of FAIn
during cycling and conﬁrming its highly reversible conversion
process. This reversible process establishes a self-replenishing
mechanism that maintains a stable FAIn concentration through-
out cycling. This reversibility ensured the dynamic stability of
the additive throughout cycling. The reversible reaction between
FAIn and LiPSs thus mitigates irreversible additive consump-
tion, which contributes to the improved long-term cycling per-
formance of Li-S batteries.
To evaluate lithium deposition behavior under practical con-
ditions, where lithium metal batteries are typically assembled
in pouch cells with negligible external pressure applied to the
anode, we conducted experiments in a pouch cell conﬁgura-
tion. This approach minimizes the inﬂuence of external pres-
sure and allows a realistic assessment of 5-ﬂuoroethylene car-
bonate (FAIn) in stabilizing the lithium interface. As shown in
Figure 3a, we ﬁrst examined the ability of FAIn to suppress
lithium polysulﬁde (LiPS) corrosion by immersing lithium foils
in 0.4 m Li2S8 solution with or without FAIn for 48 h. It is note-
worthy that UV–vis spectroscopy was employed to monitor the
concentration of FAIn before and after exposure to metallic Li for
48 h, conﬁrming only a marginal decrease (Figure S33, Support-
ing Information). Therefore, even considering the anode side re-
action to form the SEI, the eﬀective concentration of FAIn in the
electrolyte remains essentially unaﬀected. As shown in Figure
S34 (Supporting Information), the pristine lithium foil exhibited
a smooth and uniform surface. In the absence of FAIn, severe
corrosion occurred, leading to a roughened morphology with het-
erogeneous granular deposits (Figure 3b). By contrast, the ad-
dition of FAIn eﬀectively suppressed corrosion, maintaining a
smooth and largely intact surface (Figure 3c).
Accordingly, lithium symmetric cell tests revealed that elec-
trolytes incorporating the FAIn additive signiﬁcantly reduced
overpotential and delivered superior rate capability and cycling
stability compared to FAIn-free cells (Figure 3d,f). The lithium
anode without FAIn exhibited a rough, porous surface with ex-
tensive loose and dendritic deposits, reﬂecting severe parasitic re-
actions (Figure 3e). In contrast, the FAIn-treated anode retained
a dense and uniform deposition morphology (Figure 3g). These
results indicate that FAIn suppresses LiPS corrosion and miti-
gates dendritic growth by facilitating the formation of a stable SEI
layer, which facilitates homogeneous Li plating/stripping and en-
hances cycling stability.

strategy demonstrated excellent cycle stability at a 0.1C rate.
3. Conclusion
In summary, this work proposes a regulatory strategy for poly-
sulﬁde reconstruction by introducing 5-ﬂuoroisatoic anhydride
(FAIn) as an electrolyte mediator. FAIn promotes the reconver-
sion of dissolved and inactive polysulﬁdes back to S8, thereby en-
hancing active material utilization and reducing corrosion of the
lithium metal anode. We found that O atoms in FAIn coordinate
with Li ions associated with terminal S atoms in dissolved LiPSs,
forming Li─O bonds and triggering delithiation that enables
LiPS capture and in situ conversion. The resulting Li-FAIn com-
plexes were electrochemically oxidized to regenerate FAIn dur-
ing charging, establishing a dynamic additive cycle. Notably, fully
charged FAIn-containing batteries exhibited superior suppres-
sion of self-discharge, leading to extended calendar life. Accord-
ingly, pouch cells with 0.9 wt% FAIn delivered a speciﬁc capacity
of 726 mAh g−1 after 199 cycles at 0.5 C. Under identical con-
ditions, FAIn-based cells with secondary packaging retained 568
mAh g−1 after 384 cycles, demonstrating signiﬁcantly prolonged
cycle life. Notably, the FAIn-mediated system sustained stable op-
eration for 100 cycles in an Ah-class Li-S pouch cell conﬁguration
at the high rate of 0.5 C, underscoring the eﬃcacy of the polysul-
ﬁde reconstruction strategy in facilitating practical Li-S battery
commercialization.
