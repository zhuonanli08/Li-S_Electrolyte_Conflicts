1. Experimental Section
1.1 Assembly and testing of Li-S batteries
The electrodes as described above are used as cathodes, Celgard 2400 is used as a diaphragm, and 
lithium is used as the anode. Add the electrolyte without additives or with PZ, 2-FP or 2,5-DFP as 
described above. Button cells are assembled using stainless steel battery cases (Type 2025) in an 
argon filled glove box. During electrochemical performance testing, current rate and specific 
capacity were calculated based on the mass of sulfur in the cathode (1 C = 1675 mA g-1). Constant 
current discharge/charge tests were performed on a Neware battery test apparatus with a voltage 
range of 1.7-2.8 V. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements and cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) tests were performed on an electrochemical workstation (VSP-3e Biologic),

1.2 Assembly of Li2S6 symmetric cells
A 12.0 mm diameter carbon paper (CP) was used as the positive and negative electrodes, and a 
Celgard 2400 membrane was used as the diaphragm. 20 µL of 0.2 M Li2S6 -DME/DOL (v/v=1:1) 
solution was dropped onto the carbon paper, and 20 μL of the above formulated electrolyte was 
added. The CV curves of the symmetric cells were tested at -0.8 and 0.8 V with a scan rate of 1 
mV s-1.
1.3 Experiments of Li2S nucleation
A CR2032 coin cell was assembled using carbon paper (diameter: 12 mm) as the cathode and 
lithium foil as the anode. 20 μL of 0.2 M Li2S8-tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether solution was 
added to the center of the carbon paper, and 20 μL of electrolyte containing blank/PZ/2-FP/2,5-
DFP was added to the Celgard-2400 diaphragm. The assembled cells were discharged to 2.06 V 
at a constant current of 0.112 mA, and then discharged at a constant potential of 2.05 V until the 
current fell below 0.01 mA to ensure complete nucleation of Li2S.S1,S2
1.4 Physical characterization
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was performed using a FEI Scios 2 HiVac, USA. X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed using a Thermo Scientific K-Alpha, USA, with 
an excitation source of Al Kα-rays (hv=1486.6 eV). The Ultraviolet-Visible absorption spectra 
(UV-Vis) were obtained using a Shimadzu UV-2700i spectrophotometer.

Figure S1. Li-S cells were assembled using three concentrations of 2,5-DFP at 40 mM, 50 mM and 60 mM 
cycled in different multiplicities.
The capacity of the cells all increased with increasing concentration. However, the capacity 
decreased with increasing multiplicity as the concentration increased up to 60 mM. Therefore, the 
Li-S battery assembled with 2,5-DFP additive possessed the best capacity and cycling performance 
at 50 mM.
