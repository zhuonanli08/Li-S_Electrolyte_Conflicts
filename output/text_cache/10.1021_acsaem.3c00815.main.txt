ABSTRACT: Lithium−sulfur (Li−S) batteries are one of the most prospective high-
energy-density secondary batteries with ultrahigh energy density and low cost.
Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) with a unique chemical structure and satisfactory
electronic conductivity proves to be a promising sulfur cathode material. Nevertheless,
the application of Li−SPAN batteries is severely limited by sluggish sulfur kinetics and
uncontrollable Li deposition, resulting in poor cycling in ether-based electrolytes.
Herein, a dual-functional electrolyte additive, lithium selenide (Li2Se), is proposed. For
a sulfur cathode, Li2Se will continuously attack the S−S bonds of polysulfides, forming
more reactive S−Se bonds with lowered reaction energy barriers and accelerating the
kinetics. For a lithium anode, Li2Se is conducive for Li+ transportation and forms a
stable selenide-containing organic−inorganic hybrid solid electrolyte interphase (SEI),
which lowers the nucleation overpotential and enhances exchange current density for Li
deposition. Resultantly, the Li2Se-assisted Li−SPAN battery shows ultrahigh discharge
capacities, enhanced rate performance, and outstanding cycling stability (an initial capacity of 1516 mAh g−1 at 2C and 600 mAh g−1
after 500 cycles). Noticeably, it delivers an areal capacity of 10.3 mAh cm−2 under a high cathode loading of 12.0 mg cm−2. This
work demonstrates a promising dual-functional additive to advance the performance of Li−SPAN batteries.
KEYWORDS: lithium selenide, redox mediator, kinetics, lithium metal, lithium−sulfur battery
1. INTRODUCTION
To meet the ever-expanding demands of 3C electronics,
electric vehicles, and smart-grid power storage, it is imperative
to develop new-generation secondary batteries with high
energy density.1−3 Li−S batteries are deemed as one of the
most potential high-energy-density secondary batteries due to
their ultrahigh theoretical energy density of 2600 Wh kg−1 and
abundant sulfur resources.4,5 Nevertheless, the severe shuttling
effect of lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) and the sluggish redox
reaction kinetics of sulfur cathodes impede the development of
Li−S batteries.6 The limitations are closely associated with
insulating sulfur. In addition, the conversion between sulfur
and lithium sulfide unavoidably results in a massive volume
change, further crumbling the cathode structure.7 To solve the
abovementioned problems, a great variety of methods have
been proposed, such as designing core−shell-structured
cathode materials,8−11 functional binders,12−15 advanced
electrolytes,16−22 and so on.23−25 Sulfurized polyacrylonitrile
(SPAN) is a prospective sulfur cathode material because of its
unique chemical structure and excellent electrochemical
performance.26,27 The low-order sulfur species (S4−S2) are
covalently bonded to the organic skeleton of pyrolyzed
polyacrylonitrile chains, which helps mitigate the volume
change during conversion.28,29 More notably, it appears that no
high-order polysulfides are observed in carbonate-based
electrolytes, alleviating the shuttling effect.30 Unfortunately,
the SPAN demonstrates sluggish reaction kinetics and poor
cycling performance in ether-based electrolytes, which severely
limits its application in practical Li−S batteries.31,32
In order to address the above challenges, many strategies
have been proposed including combining highly conductive
carbon materials,33,34 doping selenium (Se) or tellurium
(Te),35−40 and introducing redox mediators.41−45 Se, one of
the chalcogen family elements, when used as a cathode
material, shows a high capacity of 675 mAh g−1 and an
electronic conductivity of 1 × 10−3 S m−1 (sulfur of 5 × 10−28
S m−1).46 Introducing a small amount of Se in S cathodes can
enhance the conductivity and boost the kinetics of sulfur
cathodes, demonstrating excellent electrochemical perform-
ance. In particular, our group previously reported to dope trace
Se/Te as eutectic accelerators in the SPAN cathode by forming
S−Se or S−Te bonds to accelerate sulfur kinetics, achieving

Although the strategy can accelerate sulfur kinetics to some
degree, some drawbacks are inevitable, for instance, increased
synthesis complexity, decreased sulfur content, and so on.
In recent years, redox mediators have been developed to
enhance the sulfur reaction kinetics by controlling the
conversion process of sulfur species. It is worth mentioning
that introducing redox mediators is easy to operate and can
effectively improve the electrochemical performance of Li−S
batteries. For example, Huang and co-workers have reported
several redox mediators (di-t-butyl disulfide (DtbDS),41
cobaltocene (CoCp2),42 diphenyl diselenide (PDSe),43 etc.)
in conventional ether-based electrolytes to accelerate the
conversion of Li2S in traditional sulfur/carbon cathodes,
resulting in significant improvement of the cycling perform-
ance and rate capability. Furthermore, our group proposed
PDSe as an electrolyte additive to enhance electron transfer
and Li+ diffusion kinetics for Li−SPAN batteries and to
promote the redox kinetics of SPAN cathodes.47 Zhang et al.
demonstrated that a quinone-based additive in a Li−SPAN
battery can effectively heighten the electron transportation
between conductive substrates and SPAN, contributing to
enhanced electrochemical performance.45 The abovemen-
tioned strategies are efficient to boost the redox kinetics of
sulfur cathodes. However, the regulation of Li deposition
kinetics for Li anodes has not been thoroughly explored.
In this work, we reported a dual-functional redox mediator
(Li2Se) for both accelerating the sulfur kinetics and achieving
homogeneous lithium deposition in Li−S batteries. For the
SPAN cathode, Li2Se will react with the S−S bonds of
polysulfides to form higher electrochemically active S−Se
bonds. It will result in polysulfides with decreased LOMO−
LUMO energy gaps and bond dissociation energy to accelerate
the SPAN redox kinetics. For the Li metal anode, Li2Se can
promote Li+ transportation across the interface, achieving
homogeneous Li+ flux, suppressing the growth of Li dendrites,
and therefore contributing to uniform depositing Li during
cycling.48 Consequently, the Li2Se-assisted SPAN cathode
delivers an ultrahigh capacity of 1187 mAh g−1 after 100 cycles
at 0.5C, with a capacity retention of up to 80%, and displays an
excellent rate capability of 1282 mAh g−1 at 3C. This work
provides a simple and handy strategy to promote sulfur
conversion and Li deposition kinetics utilizing Li2Se as a dual-
functional electrolyte additive, offering new insights to address
the issues for high-energy-density Li−S batteries.
2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Synthesis of the SPAN Composite. The sulfurized
polyacrylonitrile (SPAN) composite was synthesized by heating
treatment. Briefly, sulfur powder and polyacrylonitrile powder were
first mixed well through ball-milling with a weight ratio of 3:1. The
obtained mixtures were annealed at 80 °C for 1 h and at 300 °C for
2.5 h in an argon (Ar) atmosphere to obtain the SPAN composite.
2.2. Electrochemical Measurements. Cells (CR2032) were
assembled to assess the capability of Li−SPAN batteries with or
without Li2Se. The cathode electrode was prepared by blending
SPAN, Ketjen black (KB), and a binder of carboxymethyl cellulose
(CMC) and styrene butadiene rubber (SBR) with a weight ratio of
80:10:5:5. The cathode loading was 1 mg cm−2 for common
measurements and 12.0 mg cm−2 under practical conditions. The
blank electrolyte was 1 M lithium bis(trifluromethanesulfonyl)imide
(LiTFSI) in a mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxyethane
(DME, 1:1 (v/v)), with 0.2 M LiNO3. For the electrolyte with Li2Se,
the concentrations of Li2Se were 0.03, 0.04, and 0.05 M. 40 μL of
these electrolytes were added to each cell, and these coin cells were
tested with a voltage range of 1.0−3.0 V on the Land and Neware

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
According to XRD patterns, Raman spectrum, and FT-IR
spectrum characterization, the structure of the synthesized
SPAN sample was identified as similar to the previously
reported one (Figures S1−S3).36,52 The sulfur content of
SPAN was 42.02 wt % based on the element analysis (Table
S1). To evaluate the effect of Li2Se on the battery performance
of Li−SPAN batteries, Li2Se was introduced as an additive in
the electrolyte for electrochemical tests. As shown in Figures
S4 and 1a, the cyclic voltammetry (CV) analysis proves that
both Li−SPAN batteries without and with Li2Se exhibit similar
redox behavior, featuring one pair of characteristic peaks.
However, the CV curves of the Li−SPAN battery with Li2Se
exhibit not only better redox reversibility but also higher peak
current density, indicating that Li2Se helps enhance the redox
reversibility of SPAN. Cycling performance and rate capability
for the Li−SPAN batteries with different concentrations of
Li2Se are demonstrated in Figures S5 and S6. The Li−SPAN
battery with 0.05 M Li2Se delivers the best cycling stability and
high-rate capability; thus, it is chosen as the optimized
concentration in our subsequent experiments. The rate
performance of Li−SPAN batteries without and with Li2Se
at various rates are shown in Figures 1b,c and S7. The Li−
SPAN battery with Li2Se shows higher discharge capacities of
1615, 1493, 1450, 1409, 1377, 1347, 1316, and 1282 mAh g−1
at 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, and 3C rates, respectively. When
the rate is set back to 0.1C, the capacity recovers to 1491 mAh
g−1, showing excellent rate capability. In contrast, the control
sample’s discharge capacity quickly decreases to 1015 and 908
mAh g−1 at 2.0 and 3.0C, respectively.
Notably, Li−SPAN with Li2Se exhibits a higher discharge
platform, decreased capacity fading, and lower voltage
polarization at high rates of 2 and 3C. Besides, the Li−
SPAN battery with Li2Se shows excellent cycling stability over
100 cycles with a high Coulombic efficiency of up to 99.6% at
0.5C, corresponding to a higher capacity retention of 80% than
the control battery (47%, Figure 1d−f). Li−SPAN with Li2Se
also displays better long-cycling stability over 500 cycles at 2C
than the control battery (Figure 1g).

To illustrate the working mechanism of Li2Se for sulfur
cathodes in Li−SPAN batteries, the electrochemical analysis
and density functional theory (DFT) calculation were
performed. As illustrated in Figures S11 and 2a, the Li+
diffusion coefficients of the Li−SPAN battery with Li2Se are
around 95 and 3.6 times higher than those of the control
battery during oxidation and reduction processes, respectively.
Additionally, the Li2Se-assisted electrolyte exhibits a superior
ionic conductivity of 12.45 mS cm−1 than that of the control
electrolyte (11.96 mS cm−1), as listed in Table S3.
Furthermore, the specific capacities and polarization potential
during the discharge−charge process were investigated by the
GITT electrochemical method. The Li−SPAN battery with
Li2Se shows a lower polarization potential and a higher specific
capacity than those of the control (Figure 2b). Meanwhile, in
situ EIS was carried out to unravel the impedance change of
batteries during the initial three cycles (Figure 2c−e). Figure
2c shows that the Li−SPAN battery with Li2Se displays
lowered resistance than the control battery before cycling. The
equivalent circuit is illustrated in Figure S12.
At the initial discharge process, the EIS plots of both Li−
SPAN batteries with and without Li2Se display a single
semicircle arc and an oblique line, corresponding to charge
transfer resistance (Rct) and Warburg resistance (Wo),
respectively. After forming SEI in subsequent cycles, the EIS
plots of both batteries exhibit double semicircle arcs and an
oblique line, ascribed to the first semicircle of interface
impedance (Rsei) and the second semicircle of Rct. As
demonstrated in Figure 2d,e, the Li−SPAN battery with
Li2Se displays lower Rct than the control battery at different
discharge states and cycles. More importantly, when the SEI is
formed, the Li−SPAN battery with Li2Se exhibits smaller Rsei
than the control battery at varied discharge conditions. From
the above experimental phenomena and data, it can be
demonstrated that introducing a Li2Se redox mediator can
significantly boost the transport kinetics of Li+ and electrons,
thus effectively improving the electrochemical performance of
the SPAN cathodes.

accelerate the sulfur conversion and reduce the formation of
soluble LiPSs (Figure S19).

To further assess the electrochemical capability of Li2Se on
the Li anode, the Li/Li symmetrical batteries with/without
Li2Se were assembled to measure the voltage hysteresis,
reversibility, and stability in the Li plating/stripping process.
Compared with the control battery, the Li/Li symmetrical
battery with Li2Se demonstrates superior cycling stability over
500 h under 2 mA cm−2 with 2 mAh cm−2, with a larger
voltage hysteresis in the first 250 h (about 70 mV). But the
control battery demonstrates that voltage polarization increases
drastically after 250 h, as shown in Figure S23. In addition, the
Li/Li symmetrical battery with Li2Se displays a higher voltage
hysteresis than the control battery during the first 180 h at 3
mA cm−2 with 3 mAh cm−2; however, the Li2Se-assisted
batteries exhibit better cycling reversibility (Figure 5a). The
Li/Li symmetrical batteries demonstrate a similar electro-
chemical phenomenon at low and higher current densities.
More importantly, as demonstrated in Figure S24, the Li2Se-
assisted Li−Li symmetric battery shows a smaller voltage
hysteresis (around 114.1 mV at 6.5 mA cm−2) than that of the
control battery at various current densities with a plating of 1
mAh cm−2. At the same time, the voltage hysteresis can still
maintain about 16.7 mV at 1 mA cm−2 after the current
fluctuations.
Figures 5b,c and S25 show the SEM images of Li anodes for
the Li/Li symmetrical batteries with/without Li2Se after
cycling. For the control battery, the Li anode has a scraggy
and inhomogeneous surface with obvious Li dendrites and
cracks. Conversely, the Li anode with Li2Se demonstrates a
dense and smooth surface. In addition, after 200 cycles at 1C,
the Li anode of the Li−SPAN battery with Li2Se also exhibits a
metallic luster with a smooth and compact surface (Figure
S26). while for the control, black materials are observed on the
Li surface, which is corroded and rough, suggesting an unstable
SEI (Figure S27). However, the Li2Se-assisted SPAN cathode
demonstrates similar structure and morphology after cycling
compared with the control battery, indicating that the main
function of Li2Se is to protect the Li metal and accelerate the
sulfur kinetics (Figure S28).
The chemical components of the SEI in the Li−SPAN
battery after 50 cycles were explored using XPS. Figure 5e,f
demonstrates the Li 1s + Se 3d and S 2p + Se 3p spectra for
the battery with Li2Se. As shown in S 2p spectra, the
characteristic peaks are predominantly composed of oxidized
sulfur species (SOx
2−) decomposed by LiTFSI. The character-
istic peaks at about 165.1 and 59.0 eV for Se 3p and Se 3d
spectra are mostly ascribed to Se4+ (SeO3
2−) species generated
by the reaction between Li2Se and LiNO3, and the Se−O
bonds and Li2SeO3 species can increase the ionic conductivity
of SEI and facilitate Li+ transport.53−55 Therefore, the above
XPS spectra suggest a stable selenide-containing SEI, which is
beneficial to Li deposition. Moreover, the different electro-
chemical behaviors of Li/Li symmetrical batteries with/
without Li2Se were measured from Tafel curves, and the
exchange current density was calculated (Figure 5g). When
compared with the control battery, the battery with Li2Se
demonstrates a prominently improved exchange current
density (0.287 vs 0.141 mA cm−2, around 2.03 times).
According to the above results, the formed selenide-containing
SEI can promote Li+ transportation kinetically and achieve
homogeneous Li+ flux to restrain Li dendrites and achieve
uniform Li deposition, substantially enhancing the cycling life
and high-rate ability of batteries.
4. CONCLUSIONS
In conclusion, Li2Se is designed as a dual-functional additive to
facilitate sulfur conversion and lithium deposition kinetics for
Li−SPAN batteries. Specifically, introduced Li2Se can react
with the S−S bonds of polysulfides to form more electro-
chemically active S−Se bonds, effectively accelerating the
sulfur kinetics. For the Li anode, Li2Se is conducive to
improving the reversibility and reducing the nucleation
overpotential of the Li anode, resulting in promoted Li+
diffusion kinetics and uniform Li deposition. As a result, the
Li−SPAN batteries with Li2Se exhibit outstanding rate
performance (1331 mAh g−1 at 3C) and excellent long-cycling
stability (over 500 cycles at 2C). This work displays a dual-
functional additive to solve multiple problems of Li−S
batteries, providing a great possibility for constructing high-
energy-density metal−S batteries.
