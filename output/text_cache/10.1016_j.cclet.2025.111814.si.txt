Experimental Methods
Synthesis of hollow carbon spheres (HCSs)
The detailed preparation of hollow carbon spheres is described in the previous work [1]. Hollow carbon spheres were synthesized by the self-assembly of resin and silicate. Ethanol (375 mL), deionized water (125 mL), and NH3⋅H2O (15 mL) were mixed and stirred at 30 °C for 30 min. Tetraethylorthosilicate (15 mL) was then added dropwise, followed by the injection of the resorcinol‒formaldehyde (RF, 2.0 g‒2.8 mL) mixture. The precursor solution was successively agitated at 30 °C for 24 h and was then heated at 100 °C for an additional 24 h in a Teflon-lined autoclave. After centrifugation and drying, the resulting product, SiO2@RF, was collected. Subsequently, the SiO2@RF was heated at 800 °C for 2 h in an N2 gas atmosphere to obtain the core-shell nanostructure of SiO2/C. HCSs were obtained by soaking the SiO2/C in 2.8 mol/L NaOH for 4 d to remove the SiO2 spheres.
Synthesis of HEA/HCS 
The HEA catalysts were synthesized using an impregnation method. H2PtCl6·6H2O (41.94 mg), FeCl3·6H2O (19.53 mg), NiCl2·6H2O (23.38 mg), CoCl2·6H2O (23.38 mg), and CuCl2·2H2O (16.8 mg) were mixed and sonicated to form a uniform solution. Subsequently, HCSs (50 mg) were added to the resulting mixture and stirred overnight. The mixture was dried with a rotary evaporator. The resulting powder was heated at 1000 °C for 2 h under H2/Ar (v/v = 5%) in a tube furnace to obtain HEA/HCS. The elemental atomic content of 0.7%, 0.7%, 1.5%, 1.1%, 0.7%, and 95.3% for Pt, Cu, Fe, Co, Ni, and C, respectively, corresponds to a total HEA loading content of 4.7% in the HEA/HCS composite. The same process prepared the Pt/HCS and Cu3Pt/HCS.
Preparation of HEA/HCS cathodes
To fabricate HEA/HCS/S, HEA catalysts and S were mixed in a mass ratio of 7:3, sealed in a container, and heated at 155 °C for 12 h. The HEA mass fraction in the electrode is 11.9%. The HEA/HCS/S, PVDF (5 wt% in NMP), and Super P (in a mass ratio of 8:1:1) were mixed and blended in NMP to form a uniform cathode slurry. This slurry was then dripped onto the hyphae carbon nanobelts (HCNBs) to prepare the HEA/HCS cathode. Further details on the preparation process of HCNBs can be found in the reference [2].
Material characterization
The samples were characterized using X-ray diffraction (XRD, D/max2600, Hitachi, Japan, Cu Ka radiation, k = 1.5418 Å), scanning electron microscope (SEM, SU70, Hitachi, Japan), and transmission electron microscope (TEM, FEI, Tecnai TF20). Ultraviolet-visible was carried out using a spectrometer (UV-vis, Cary 4000). Raman analysis (LabRAM, HR800, HORIBA Jobin Yvon, France) was equipped with an Olympus BX microscope and an argon ion laser (488 nm). 
Electrochemical measurements
Typical CR2032 coin-type cells were assembled, comprising a HEA/HCS cathode, a lithium foil anode, and a porous polypropylene (Celgard 2400) separator. The electrolyte contained LiTFSI (1.0 mol/L) and LiNO3 (0.2 mol/L) in a solvent mixture of DOL/DME (v/v, 1:1). The electrolyte/sulfur ratios (E/S) were controlled at approximately 30, 10, and 4.5 μL/mg. The sulfur loading is 1.5 mg/cm2. Galvanostatic charge−discharge (GCD) tests were conducted on a LANCT battery test system at various rates within a voltage range of 1.7-2.8 V at room temperature. The kinetic voltage is obtained through the discharge curves at each respective rate. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were performed between 1.7 V and 2.8 V using a VMP3 multichannel electrochemical workstation (BioLogic, France). Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests were carried out between 2.8 V and 1.7 V with a current pulse of 0.1 C for 20 min, followed by a rest period of 2 h.
UV–vis measurements
3 mL of ethanol and different volumes of DOL/DME solvent are uniformly mixed as the standard solution. 20 mg HEA was added into DOL/DME (2 mL) solution and rested for 3 h. Then 1 mL of supernatant was added to 3 mL of ethanol for UV–vis measurements.
Nucleation of Li2S test
A 0.5 mol/L Li2S8 electrolyte was prepared by mixing S and Li2S with a molar ratio of 7:1 in tetraglyme as the solvent. In separate experiments, a HEA/HCS and a Li metal foil were used as cathodes and anodes, respectively. On the cathode side, 15 μL of Li2S8 electrolyte was added, and on the anode side, 15 μL of a routine electrolyte was used. The mass load of HEA/HCS, Cu3Pt/HCS, and Pt/HCS is about 1.0 mg/cm2. Finally, the assembled cells were discharged galvanostatically at 0.112 mA until the voltage reached 2.06 V and then discharged potentiostatically to 2.05 V using the VMP3 electrochemical workstation (BioLogic, France) to nucleate the Li2S.
Polarization decoupling of Li‒S batteries  
An EIS combined GITT (EIS‒GITT) method was used to decouple the polarization of Li–S batteries at the E/S ratio = 10 μL/mg. Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) tests were carried out between 2.8 and 1.7 V with a current pulse of 0.1 C for 20 min, followed by a rest period of 2 h. An additional EIS measurement was conducted before the galvanostatic discharge process to obtain the ohmic resistance (Rohm). EIS was conducted at the open circuit voltage with a sinusoidal voltage amplitude of 10 mV and a frequency spectrum ranging from 0.1 Hz to 10 kHz. The ohmic resistance was obtained by the intercept at the Z’ axis. 
ηtotal was determined as the voltage difference between the thermodynamic voltage and the kinetic voltage of the Li–S batteries and can be divided into concentration polarization (ηcon), ohmic polarization (ηohm), and activation polarization (ηac). The voltage recovery during the rest step in GITT resulted from the concentration gradient built in the discharge step and was adopted as the ηcon. ηohm was determined by multiplying the ohmic resistance obtained through the EIS measurements with the applied current density. ηac was obtained though ηac = ηtotal ‒ ηohm ‒ ηcon.
Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) analysis.
For 1H and 19F NMR tests, the electrolyte from the cycled cells is extracted with 1 mL of deuterated dimethyl sulfoxide (d6-DMSO). Before battery cycling, known amounts of uncycled electrolyte are injected into the coin cells, extracted following the same process, and used as a reference. Fluorobenzene is selected as the internal reference for both 1H and 19F tests because its chemical shift is far from those of DOL/DME solvent and TFSI− anions in both 1H and 19F spectra. 5 μL of fluorobenzene is added to the extracted solution for NMR quantifications. The 1H and 19F peaks of fluorobenzene are normalized to 100%. Due to the known content of internal reference, the retention of both DOL/DME solvent or TFSI− anions in the electrolyte can be quantified by comparing the peak areas. 
Computational Methods
All calculations were conducted using spin-polarized DFT within the framework of the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) code [3]. The projected augmented wave method was employed, along with exchange-correlation functional described by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) within the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof scheme (PBE) framework [4-6]. energy and force convergence thresholds for comprehensive structural relaxation were set at 10-4 eV and 0.02 eV/Å per atom, respectively. For surface structure optimization, Gamma-centered Monkhorst-Pack k-point grids of 3 × 3 × 1 were utilized, with a plane-wave cutoff energy of 520 eV. To prevent interactions between periodic images, a vacuum layer of 15 Å was introduced along the Z-direction.
