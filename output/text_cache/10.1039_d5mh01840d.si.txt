1. Experimental section
1.1 Preparation of the electrolyte
Potassium selenocyanate (KSeCN, purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical 
Technology Co., Ltd.) was added to the blank electrolyte as the additive at a 
concentration of 100 mM. The blank electrolyte was purchased from DoDo Chem and 
contains 1M lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl)imide (LiTFSI) dissolved in 1:1 
volume ration of 1, 2-dimethoxyethane (DME) and 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL), with the 
addition of 2 wt% lithium nitrate (LiNO3).
1.2 Preparation of the S/C cathode
The S/C was prepared by mixing 70 wt% sulfur powder and 30 wt% Ketjen black (KB), 
followed by heating at 155 ℃ for 12 h. The cathode slurry was prepared by mixing 80 
wt% S/C, 10 wt% Super P, and 10 wt% polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in N-methyl-
2-pyrrolidone (NMP) solvent to from a uniformly dispersed mixture. The slurry was 
then coated onto carbon-coating Al foil and dried overnight at 60 ℃ in a vacuum oven. 
The coated foil was then cut into circular electrodes with a diameter of 10 mm and a 
sulfur loading of ~1 mg cm-2 for typical use. In the case of pouch cell fabrication, KB 
was replaced with multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs).
1.3 Material characterizations
Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were tested to observe the chemical shift 
of 7Li of the electrolyte both with and without KSeCN by Bruker Ascend NEO 
600MHz. The cycled cathodes analyzed by Raman spectroscopy were obtained from 
Li–S cells (sulfur loading ~1 mg cm-2) with KSeCN added to the electrolyte. After 
several charge/discharge cycles, the cells were disassembled in an argon atmosphere, 
and the cathode powder was removed from the current collector for spectroscopic 
characterization. Raman spectra were acquired using a Witec alpha300R equipped with 
633 nm laser, a spot size of ∼1 µm2, and a 50× objective lens. The morphology of 
cycled electrode was examined using the scanning electron microscope (SEM, Verios 

<<PAGE 3>>
G4 UC) operating at 10 kV and transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM 
ARM200F). The chemical compositions of the cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI) and 
solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) were analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy 
(XPS) (ESCALAB 250 instrument with Al Kα radiation 15 kV, 150 W) and time of 
flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) (ION TOF-SIMS5). X-ray 
diffraction (XRD Brucker D2 Advance diffractometer) was used to track the evolution 
of crystals in the cathode in conjunction with our well-designed in-situ cells.
1.4 Electrochemical characterization
1.4.1 Assembly and testing of Li-S coin cells 
CR2032-type coin cells were assembled using S/C cathodes, Celgard 2400 
polypropylene separator, lithium metal anode, and either the blank electrolyte or 
KSeCN-modified electrolyte. The electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio (E/S) ratio was mixed to 
15 µL mg-1 unless otherwise specified. All operations were performed in an Ar-filled 
glove box with oxygen and moisture levels below 0.1 ppm. Galvanostatic 
charge/discharge tests were performed on a LAND equipment within a voltage window 
of 1.7-2.7 V. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were carried out on a VSP-300 
electrochemical workstation between 1.7 and 2.8 V with various scan rates.
1.4.2 The preparation of Li2S8 solutions and Li2S deposition tests
The Li2S8 solutions were prepared by mixing the sulfur powder and Li2S powder at a 
molar ratio of 7:1 in 1:1 (v/v) DME/DOL solvent mixture at 60 ℃ for 12 h. For Li2S 
deposition experiments, 30μL of 10 mM Li2S8 solutions was added into a carbon cloth 
(CC) substrate serving as the cathode, with lithium foil as the anode. The cells were 
first galvanostatic discharged to 2.1 V, followed by a potentiostatic discharged step at 
2.05 V for 10 h.
1.4.3 Fabrication of symmetric cells and corresponding tests
The stainless steel||stainless steel symmetric cells were assembled with a Celgard 2400 
PP separator and electrolyte with or without KSeCN to determine ionic conductivity.

1.4.4 Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests.
EIS measurements were carried out on a VSP-300 workstation within the frequency 
range of 100 mHz to 100 kHz using a voltage amplitude of 10 mV. In-situ EIS was 
conducted on assembled Li-S coin cells with a sulfur loading of ~1 mg cm-2. Each 
measurement was taken after holding the cell at a designated voltage for 10 minutes 
during cycling.
1.4.5 Assembly of Li-S pouch cells
Li-S pouch cells were assembled using the 4×4 cm2 S/C cathode, a Li-coated Cu foil 
anode, and vacuum-sealed Al-plastic film packaging. The E/S ratio was maintained at 
10 µLmg-1 and the total sulfur loading was 208 mg.
1.5 Theoretical calculations
The structure relaxations and electronic structure analyses of DME, DOL, LiNO3, 
LiTFSI, and KSeCN were calculated using the Gaussian 16 program package with 
density functional theory (DFT). The B3LYP functional and a 6-311+G (d, p) were 
selected for structural optimization and frontier orbital calculations of all molecules. 
For the geometry optimizations, convergence was considered achieved when the 
following thresholds were met within the atomic unit system: the maximum force on 
any atom was less than 0.00045 Hartree/Bohr, the root-mean-square (RMS) force was 

<<PAGE 5>>
below 0.00030 Hartree/Bohr, the maximum displacement was under 0.00180 Bohr, and 
the RMS displacement was below 0.00120 Bohr. Molecular dynamics (MD) 
simulations were performed using the GROMACS package.1-4 The initial atomic 
coordinates were constructed with the Packmol program.5 Atomic partial charges were 
assigned using the restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) method, which were 
derived from electrostatic potential (ESP) charges calculated with the Multiwfn 
program.6 The GAFF force field topology was generated using the Sobtop.7 The 
simulation process was detailed as follows: the 5000-step steepest descent method and 
5000-step conjugate gradient method were used to eliminate unreasonable contacts in 
the system. Subsequently, simulations were carried out in the isothermal-isobaric 
(NPT) ensemble with the Parrinello-Rahman barostat maintaining a pressure of 1 atm 
and the V-rescale thermostat controlling the temperature at 298.15 K. The integration 
time step was set to 1 fs. Short-range van der Waals and electrostatic interactions were 
truncated at 1.2 nm, while long-range electrostatic interactions were treated using the 
particle-mesh Ewald (PME) method. All bonds involving hydrogen atoms were 
constrained with the LINCS algorithm.
