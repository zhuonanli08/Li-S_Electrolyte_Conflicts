Figure S16. (a) Galvanostatic charge/discharge profiles of the cells without or with different 
mediators at 0.1 C. (b) The enlarged galvanostatic discharge profiles around the second 
discharge plateaus of the CC@CoSNC and CC@CoSNC/CoCp2 cells at 0.1 C. The total energy 
barrier of the conversion reaction from Li2S4 to Li2S is 1.80 eV for the optimized simulation 
model of CoS1.097 system without CoCp2. By contrast, the corresponding total energy barrier 
decreases to 1.46 eV for the model of CoS1.097@CoCp2 system (Figure 5e). Therefore, the 
relative Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) of these two systems is 0.34 eV. In general, the 
conversion process from Li2S4 to Li2S offers three quarters of the theoretical discharging 
capacity (1254 mAh g-1), which is equivalent to the transfer of 12 electrons in the sulfur 
electrochemical reaction. According to formula of ΔG=neΔU, the theoretical voltage difference 
(ΔU) between the CoS1.097 system and CoS1.097@CoCp2 system in the conversion from Li2S4 to 
Li2S is calculated to be ~28 mV. 
The conversion reaction from Li2S4 to Li2S corresponds to the second discharge plateau of the 
Li-S battery, and the formation of Li2S occurs in the late stages of the second discharge plateau. 
It is revealed that the discharge plateau of the CC@CoSNC/CoCp2 cell is about 21 mV higher 
than that of the CC@CoSNC cell at the discharging capacity of 900 mAh g−1, which is close to 
the theoretical value (~28 mV).
