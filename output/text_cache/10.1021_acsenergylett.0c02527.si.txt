S1.2 UV-Vis spectroscopy of Li2S6 solution in DME.
Li2S6 solution was prepared by mixing Li2S and S with a stoichiometric ratio of 1:5 into DME solvent, 
i.e., Li2S + 5S = Li2S6. After that, the solution was heated at 60°C for 48 hours in a sealed vial within an Ar-
filled glovebox. The prepared solution was diluted to 2 mM in concentration. Finally, UV-Vis spectroscopy 
of the diluted Li2S6 solution was measured on a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (CARY 100 Cone) with a light 
source of deuterium (200-350 nm) and tungsten (350-800 nm) lamp. It is noted that the continuous 
spectrum was acquired by switching two lamps at 350 nm. The experimental UV-Vis spectrum was then 
interpreted using TD-DFT11,12 at M06-2X/6-311+G(2d,p) level of theory with the SMD solvation model, 
according to the benchmark study by Truhlar et al.13

Figure S1. Structures and natural transition orbital pairs of S18 for Li2S6(I), which is labeled as 1(I) in 
Figure 2b of the main text. The corresponding excitation, oscillation strength (f), NBO eigenvalue (Λ), 
energy gap (∆E), and the absorption wavelength (λ) are also listed.
As shown in Figure S1, four NTO pairs are shown to describe the S18 of Li2S6(I), which is labeled as 
1(I) in the computational UV-Vis spectra of Figure 2b of the main text. The oscillation strength (f), NTO 
eigenvalue (Λ), transition energy gap (∆E), and the corresponding absorption wavelength (λ) are also listed for 
clearness. All the hole orbitals can be attributed to the non-bonding p orbitals on the sulfur atoms, while 
the electron distributions can be attributed to the anti-bonding σ* orbitals. Thus, these two hole-electron 
NTO pairs corresponding to the adsorption band 236.0 nm are n → σ* excitations. The electron 
distributions for Λ = 0.30 and 0.29 are difficult to characterize the type of orbitals, which need to be studied 
by other efficient means.
