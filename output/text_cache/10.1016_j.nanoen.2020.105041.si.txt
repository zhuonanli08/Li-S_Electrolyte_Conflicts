Supplementary Figure 2 ǀ Activated carbon fiber cloth (ACFC) as a model material to study sulfur reduction pathways under lean electrolyte conditions. Cyclic voltammograms of Li-S cells using ACFC substrate in the (a) E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4, (b) E_DOL/DME (1 M LiTFSI_0.30 M LiNO3 in DOL/DME), and (c) E_G2 (1 M LiTFSI_0.3 M LiNO3 in G2) ; (d) Cyclic voltammograms of Li-S cells using ECF (electrospun carbon fiber) as the carbon matrix and  E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 electrolyte.  All CV curves were obtained in the potential range of 1.8–2.8 V at 0.2 mV s−1 and 30 °C. Despite different sulfur sources (i.e., S8 and Li2S4) for cells using ACFC, no Li2Sn diffusion/migration from ACFC is evident from the higher current peak for the oxidation of high-order Li2Sn into S8 than the previous peak, indicating high-order Li polysulfide is confined in ACFC. However, the cell using macroporous ECF shows the reverse process, indicating the failure to confine high LiPS. Sulfur reduction is thus supposed to take place within the limited pore volume of ACFC.

Supplementary Notes
Effect of Li2S4 content on lithium metal anode
In addition to the challenge at the sulfur cathode (including polysulfide shuttle effect etc.), dendrite growth on the Li metal anode is a parallel problem in Li-S chemistry. Effects of optimal Li2S4 retention on Li metal have been investigated. Supplementary Fig. 9a shows electrolyte dependence of the nucleation overpotential for lithium electrodeposition onto Cu substrate. The nucleation overpotential is the potential difference between the potential dip and the potential plateau at the onset of lithium bulk electrodeposition [4]. A LiǀǀCu cell using E_G2_0.50 M Li2S4 shows an ultralow nucleation overpotential (≲2 mV) compared to those for E_DOL/DME and E_G2, which are in the range of 20–30 mV; this indicates that the surface film generated by synergy between LiNO3 and 0.50 M Li2S4 is highly lithiophilic. In Supplementary Fig. 10, the dV/dQ curve shows that the reduction of Z_0.5 M Li2S4 onto the Cu substrate to generate this lithiophilic film occur at 1.60 V, 1.32 V, 1.15 V, and 0.55 V vs. Li/Li+ [5]. Morphologies of electrodeposited Li on Cu were studied using E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 at 1.0 mA cm−2, 1.0 mAh cm−2, and 30 °C, without effects of crimping pressure or separators, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 11a-b. The size of each “island” is in the range of 20–30 µm, and some of these are coalesced into larger islands.[6, 7] Lithium electrodeposits (medium-gray areas in Supplementary Fig. 11a) are selectively surrounded by reduced surface film (darker edges), but not homogeneously dispersed on the Cu substrate (lightest areas), which further demonstrates the highly lithiophilic property of surface film generated by E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4. Supplementary Fig. 11c shows a typical layer-by-layer growth morphology of lithium electrodeposits after the nucleation stage for a cell using E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4, but smaller dendrites for the cell using E_G2 alone (Supplementary Fig. 11d). 
The average coulombic efficiency of the LiǀCu cell using E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 is as high as 99.78% for 3.0 mAh cm−2, and 99.0% for 6.0 mAh cm−2 at 2.0 mA cm−2 and 30 °C, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 9b and Supplementary Fig. 12g-h, respectively. For comparison, the average coulombic efficiencies are 86.6% for E_DOL/DME, 47.1% for E_G2, and 97.5% for E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4, by the same method, at 2.0 mA cm−2, 6.0 mAh cm−2, and 30 °C, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 12a-f.[8] It has been well documented that the presence of LiPSs originating from the initial discharges of sulfur cathodes can greatly enhance the CE of LiǀǀCu cell  using E_DOL/DME[9-11]. We chose to compare CE using a LiǀǀCu cell with E_DOL/DME_saturated Li2S4 and that with E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 [12]. The average CE of LiǀǀCu cell with E_DOL/DME_saturated Li2S4 is calculated to be 98.29 %, lower than 99.78 % for cell using E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4, for 3.0 mAh cm-2 at 2.0 mA cm-2 and 30 oC in the Supplementary Fig. 9b . 
Cyclability of symmetric LiǀǀLi cells was examined at 2.0 mA cm−2, 6.0 mAh cm−2, and 30 °C; results are shown in Supplementary Fig. 9c. The cell using E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 shows a steady overpotential of around 35 mV during cycling, while the cells using E_G2 and E_G2_DOL/DME each show a surge in the overpotential. 
To explain the performance difference, ex-situ X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were performed. The major inorganic compounds in the Li 1s XPS spectra in E_DOL/DME and E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 are Li2O at 53.6 eV and Li2Sx at 54.5 eV (i.e., Li2S and Li2S2), respectively, both of which increase in concentration from the surface to the bulk of electrodeposited Li; these spectra are shown in Supplementary Fig. 9d. An evolution trend was evidenced by its O 1s spectra at 528.2 eV, where a second dominant contributor was confirmed to be LiOH/Li2CO3 at 531.4 eV. However, the Li2SO3, with a peak at 530.8 eV is the dominant contributor in the O 1s spectra of Li in E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 (green traces in Supplementary Fig. 9e), where the Li2O peak height increases with depth. The sulfate Li2SO3 was further evidenced by the S 2p spectra at 166.8 eV, which is supposed to originate the oxidation of NO3− onto low-order lithium sulfides (i.e., the key role of LiNO3 in the shuttle effect) [13]. The binding energy of 161.4 eV is a characteristic S 2p binding energy for Li2S2 [14]. The contribution of Li2S2 is almost equal to that of Li2S at 160 eV in the S 2p spectra of Li in E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4. However, the Li S 2p spectrum shows Li2S as the predominant compound in E_DOL/DME. Both insoluble LixNOy at around 397.5 eV and sulfates of LixSOy were reported to passivate Li in the Li-S cell, which has been clearly demonstrated on the Li generated by E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 13a. It is reasonable to assume that Li2S2 is the major inorganic Li2Sx in the Li 1s spectra of Z_0.5 M Li2S4 by its relatively higher sulfate and lower lithium atomic percentages shown in Supplementary Fig. 13b-c. Accordingly, the abundant inorganic solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) compound couples can be determined to be Li2S2/Li2SO3 for Z_0.5 M Li2S4 and Li2O/LiOH_Li2CO3 for E_DOL/DME. The former couple is supposed to account for highly reversible Li plating/stripping using E_G2_0.5 M Li2S4 by a potential synergistic effect on the improved lithium ion transport[15].
