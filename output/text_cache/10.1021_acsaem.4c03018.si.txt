EXPERIMENT and METHOD
Chemicals.
Sulfur (99.98%), lithium sulfide (Li2S, 99.98%), lithium nitrate (LiNO3,99.99%), 
HPLC grade methanol, HPLC grade water, methyl triflate, pyridine (Py), 
pentadeuteropyridine (d5Py), 2-methylpyridine (2MePy), 3-methylpyridine (3MePy), 
4-methylpyridine (4MePy), 2-fluoropyridine (2FPy), 3-fluoropyridine (3FPy) (from 
Sigma), 4-fluoropyridine (4FPy) hydrochloride (from TCI), dimethoxyethane (DME), 
1,3-dioxolane (DOL), and lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (LiTFSi) 
(battery grade from 3M) were purchased and used without further treatment.
Sample preparation and methods.  
The polysulfide solutions used in this work were 15 mM and 1 M stoichiometric 
Li2S6 in various solvents including DME/DOL, Py, 2FPy, and 3FPy. These solutions 
were prepared by mixing S8 and Li2S (molar ratio of 5:8, to form stoichiometric Li2S6) 
in the different electrolytes, followed by stirring for 96 hours at room temperature in 
Ar-filled glove-box (Innovative Technology).
The derivatization of the 15 mM Li2S6 polysulfide solution was carried out as 
following: 480 uL of DME and 25 uL of methyl triflate were added into a 2 mL air-
tight HPLC sample vial.  The mixture was vortexed for 1 minute. Then 120 uL of the 
polysulfide solution was added and the mixture was vortexed for an additional 2 
minutes. This process converted the polysulfides (Sn2-) into CH3SnCH3 which were stable 
under HPLC mobile phase conditions and can be separated based on the chain length 
of sulfur. For 1 M Li2S6 polysulfide solution, the derivatization procedure is same only 
the DME volume and the sample volume were changed to 990 uL and 10 uL

The solubilities of S8 in different solvents were obtained by the reported HPLC 
method. In short, the excessive amount of S8 was mixed with different solvents (without 
lithium salts) for 96 hours, then the clear supernatant was used for HPLC analysis after 
diluted by a factor of 50. According to the chromatographic peak area at 17.44 min (for 
S8), the solubilities of S8 in different solvents were calculated from a calibration curve 
of S8 standard solutions with concentration from 0.0151 mM to 1.889 mM.1 
A Shimadzu LC-20AD quaternary pump. equipped with a Shimadzu SIL-20A 
autosampler was used to deliver a methanol/water mobile phase through a Shimadzu 
C18 column (4.6*50 mm) at a flow rate of 0.70 mL min-1. The injection volume for all 
analyses was 5 uL. A binary gradient of mobile phases was used to elute the injected 
sample under the following condition: 25% methanol (75% water) at 0 min, 100% 
methanol at 10 min, maintained until 23 min and back to 25% methanol at 26 min. The 
entire flow from the HPLC was introduced into the Shimadzu SPD-M20A detector, and 
the full wavelength range from 190 to 800 nm was recorded by Shimadzu LabSolutions 
Lite software. The chromatograms discussed in this work were replotted from collected 
data at 230 nm.2
The true solubilities of the prepared 1 M Li2S6 polysulfide solutions (without 
adding any other lithium salt) in various solvents were obtained by measuring the Li+ 
concentration in those polysulfide solutions with an Optima 2100 DV ICP-OES (from 
Perkin-Elmer). The Li+ standard calibration curve was made by a series of Li+ standard 
solutions with concentration from 0.125 to 2.000 mg L-1 (ppm). Samples of Py and

The CID spectra from ESI/MS are obtained by a Quattro LC mass spectrometer 
(triple quadrupole, controlled by Masslynx 4.0 from Micromass-Waters) with ESI 
source was operated under either positive mode ((+) ESI, for the study of preferential 
solvation of lithium cation with different solvents) or negative mode ((-)ESI, for the 
study of polysulfide distribution in different solvents). The typical source parameters 
were set as follows: capillary, 2.60 kV; cone, 20 V; extractor, 0 V; RF lens, 0.5 V; 
source temperature, 100 °C; desolvation temperature, 250 °C; N2 gas flow, 50 L h-1 for 
nebulizer and 600 L h-1 for desolvation. For ESI-MS scan, the m/z ratio from 20 to 400 
was recorded; for CID scan (or daughter ion scan), the m/z ratio from 20 to 400 was 
recorded, collision voltage was set at 20, and argon was used as the collision gas. The 
different binary solvent electrolytes with 50 mM LiTFSi (by mixing pyridine with 
others in 1:1 molar ratio) and the 15 mM Li2S6 polysulfide solutions were introduced 
into the mass spectrometer through a syringe pump at a flow rate of 40 μL min-1. 
Sulfur cathodes were prepared by first mixing S (60%wt) with CMK3 mesoporous 
carbon (20%wt) through grinding. The mixture was then sealed in an autoclave filled 
with argon and heated at 170 ℃ for 12 hours. After cooling down to room temperature, 
carbon black Super C-65(10%wt) and PVDF binder (10%wt) were added into the 
mixture, with N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) as solvent to form homogenous slurry. 
This slurry was coated onto an alumina foil by a doctor-blade coater, and vacuum-dried

transferred into an argon-filled glovebox (both oxygen and moisture are less than 5 ppm) 
for 24 hours before being tested. The sulfur cathodes have a theoretical areal sulfur 
loading around 1.6 mg cm-2.
All battery tests were conducted in two-electrode CR2032 coin cells at room 
temperature, at 0 ℃, and at 60 ℃. Each battery used 8 μL (E/S ratio around 5) of 
different electrolytes, with a Celgard 3401 membrane as the separator and lithium chip 
as the anodes. After assembled in the Ar-filled glovebox, all batteries were rested for 
12 hours before electrochemical testing. For cycling tests, a galvanostatic 
discharge/charge procedure was conducted between 1.7 to 3.0 V either at a constant 
rate of 0.05 C or at different C rate (0.02 C, 0.04 C, 0.1 C, 0.2 C, 0.5 C, 1 C) by a battery 
testing system (Arbin BT2000). For the cycling tests at 0 ℃, and at 60 ℃, the batteries 
were cycled in two temperature and humidity chambers separately (CSZ Z8 Plus).
The cyclic voltammetric investigation was carried out using an AutoLab PGSTAT30 
potentiostat either in a two-electrode battery or in a typical three-electrode cell.  For 
CV test of battery, the scan range is 1.5 to 3.2V, scan direction is reduction first, and 
scant rate is 0.1 mV S-1. For CV test of three-electrode cell, a glassy carbon electrode 
(3mm ID, 0.07 cm2) was used as the working electrode, and two pieces of lithium 
ribbons were used as the counter electrode and reference electrode, respectively. The 
scan range is 1.5 to 3.2V, scan direction is reduction first, and scant rate is 10 mV S-1.
