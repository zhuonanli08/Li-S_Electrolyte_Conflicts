Methods: 
Chemicals. Li metal foil (purity 99.9%, thickness 0.6 mm) was obtained from MTI 
Corporation. Lithium sulfide (Li2S, 99%, Alfa-Aesar) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO, 
anhydrous, 99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich) were used as received. 
Ion soft landing (ISL) of polysulfide anions on Li surfaces. The LiS4- anions were 
produced using electrospray ionization (ESI) of a 500 µM Li2S solution in anhydrous 
DMSO. Initially, the Li2S solution was electrosprayed and mass spectra were collected 
using an LTQ Orbitrap XL in the ion trap mode (M/ΔM = 40,000 resolution, negative ion 
mode, Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA). LiS4- anions were then soft landed onto 
oxide rich lithium surfaces using a custom-built ISL instrument.1 A typical ISL deposition 
was conducted as follows: The Li2S solution in DMSO containing LiS4- was electrosprayed 
and transferred into vacuum chamber using a heated stainless steel inlet which ensured 
complete desolvation of the anions. The heated ESI inlet is also enclosed with Ar flow to 
prevent ambient moisture and contaminants from entering into vacuum chamber. The anions 
were subsequently transferred using a high transmission dual ion funnel system, mass-
selected using a quadrupole mass filter,1-2 and deposited onto a Li surface at a controlled 
kinetic energy of ~5 eV. The pressure in the deposition chamber of the ISL instrument was 
maintained at ≤ 5 ×10-7 Torr. The total number of ions deposited was calculated by 
integrating the ion current during the deposition and was set at 2 x 1013 ions for all of the 
samples prepared in this work. As received Li surfaces were cleaned by Ar+ ion sputtering 
and analyzed to verify the presence of surface lithium oxides (O-rich surface is denoted as 
LiO-Li). After soft landing of the polysulfides, the sample was transferred to the XPS 
analysis chamber in vacuum without exposing it to ambient conditions using our custom-
built in-vacuo sample transfer system. Through in vacuo transfer, the original condition of 
the soft-landed sample was maintained for XPS analysis. 
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) 
measurements were obtained using a Kratos Axis Ultra DLD spectrometer, which consists 
of a high-performance Al Kα monochromatic X-ray source (1486.6 eV, 150 W) and a high-
resolution hemispherical mirror analyzer positioned normal to the sample surface. Prior to 
ISL, the as-received Li surfaces were sputter cleaned using Ar+ over a 3 mm2 area using an 
in-build ion gun in XPS chamber to prepare a high-purity Li2O/Li metal surface free of 
adventitious hydrocarbons. The Li sample was cleaned for two hours with an Ar ion energy 
of 5 keV. The sputter cleaned and XPS analyzed Li-Substrate was transferred to the ISL 
instrument using our custom-built in-vacuo sample transfer system where the selected 
polysulfide anions were deposited. After ISL, the samples were returned to the XPS for 
post-analysis through in-vacuo sample transfer system. Despite our efforts to maintain a 
clean Li surface, small amounts of adventitious carbon species (hydrocarbon or carbonates) 
always form immediately after sputtering and before soft landing. The amount of carbon 
species also increases after soft landing. We attribute the origin of the adventitious carbon 
to contaminants in the vacuum instruments (XPS and soft landing) and in the sample stage 
during in-vacuo transfer between the soft landing and XPS instruments. The high reactivity

of Li metal may also accelerate the formation of surface contaminants compared to less 
reactive surfaces. We propose that the heterogeneity of the surface chemistry on our sputter 
cleaned Li metal may be more representative of the heterogeneous Li metal used in real Li 
batteries. Therefore, we consider the reactivity of these contaminants during XPS 
characterization and theoretical modeling.
All the XPS data were collected with a hybrid lens mode over an analysis area of 
700 × 300 μm2. Survey spectra were obtained at a pass energy of 160 eV with 0.5 eV step 
size, and high-resolution spectra were obtained at a pass energy of 40 eV with 0.1 eV step 
size. The imaging XPS data were collected in the analysis area of 800 μm2 with a spatial 
resolution of ∼5 μm and a pass energy of 160 eV at each peak position and background 
energy. A low-energy electron charge neutralizer was used to mitigate charging effects on 
the samples during analysis. All sample acquisition was performed at a pressure of ≤5 ×10-9 
Torr.

Computational Methods. We used density functional theory (DFT) and ab-initio 
molecular dynamics simulations (AIMD) to examine the molecular-level details of the 
interfacial reactions. The model systems used in this study were developed with the Biovia 
Materials Studio software suite. Two different molecular structure of lithium polysulfide 
species, namely Li2S4 and Li6S12were studied. Li2S4 is a monomer and is representative of 
the soft landed LiS4- anion, whereas the Li6S12 is a trimer species which may likely be 
formed by clustering of the soft landed monomer LiS4- at experimental conditions. The 
structures of Li2S4 and Li6S12 were obtained from a previous study7 and reoptimized in the 
gas phase using the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP).8 These calculations are 
based on the projector augmented wave (PAW)9 DFT formalism, with exchange-correlation 
terms computed with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized 
gradient approximation (GGA).10 A plane-wave basis set with a cutoff energy of 400 eV 
was used to expand the electron wave functions.11 A tritium mass was used for hydrogen in 
order to allow for a 1 femtosecond timestep in the simulations. Unit cell crystal structures 
for each surface species were cleaved in the desired plane and extended to the necessary 
length across a periodic boundary in each spatial direction. The surfaces were then annealed 
from 0 to 300K using AIMD with a canonical NVT (constant number of molecules, 
temperature, and volume) ensemble in VASP. MD simulations were performed for each 
system at 300K for 5 - 15 ps with 1 femtosecond timesteps. Additional MD simulations 
were performed in order to explore the influence of the impact energy of the soft-landing 
process. These calculations were performed in VASP using the same simulation parameters 
as the previous calculations, but with an initial velocity towards the surface applied to the 
polysulfide species. System charges were calculated using the Bader method.12 Images and 
videos of the stimulated systems were created using the Open Visualization Tool.13
