Supplementary Text
1. Materials
Phenylboronic acid (PBA) and 3,5-bis(trifluoromethyl)phenylboronic acid (BPBA) powders were purchased from Bide Pharmatech Ltd. with a purity of over 98%. LiNO3 (99%) was ordered from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Li2S (99.98%) and sublimed sulfur (99%) were purchased from Aladdin. Chromatographic-grade DME and DOL were obtained from J&K Scientific (China). Base electrolyte [1 M LiTFSI in DOL: DME (1:1, in volume) + 2 wt% LiNO3] was purchased from Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology Co., Ltd. The PBA or BPBA electrolyte was prepared by correspondingly dissolving certain amount of PBA or BPBA powders into the Base electrolyte in an argon-filled glove box (O2 < 0.01 ppm, H2O < 0.01 ppm) under magnetically stirring at 55℃. The optimized concentration of arylboronic acid in the electrolyte was determined to be 0.2 M (with a molar ratio of 5:8 for arylboronic acid to LiNO3). Li2S8, Li2S6, and Li2S4 solutions were prepared by dissolving stoichiometric amounts of Li2S and sulfur (1:7 for Li2S8, 1:5 for Li2S6, and 1:3 for Li2S4) in DOL/DME (1:1, in volume) or pure DME and stirring the mixture magnetically at 70 °C in an argn-filled glove box for 48 hours.
2. Material characterizations
XRD was carried out on a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer with Cu K radiation to see the crystalline structure of the reaction products between arylboronic acid and LiPSs. 7Li and 11B NMR were conducted on Bruker Avance NEO 400MHz. UV-vis was performed on PE lambda 750. MS was performed on Thermo Scientific Q Exactive. Ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectroscopy (UPLC-QTof-MS) was conducted on Waters Acquity UPLC coupled with a Xevo G2-XS Qtof mass spectrometer to verify that lithium arylborate (LiABO) could still exist in the electrolyte and thus maintain its mediation effect after long-term cycling. XPS was performed on PHI 5000 VersaProbe II to see the SEI components in different Ar+ sputtering depth. The SEI component distribution was visualized by TOF-SIMS (PHI Nano ToF2). The Li deposition morphology on the Base or BPBA anode was observed by SEM (HITACHI SU8010). AFM was carried out on Bruker Dimension Icon in the argon-filled glove box to characterize the DMT modulus and surface topography of Base and BPBA anodes. All the above Li anodes used for XPS, TOF-SIMS, SEM, and AFM characterizations were obtained from Li-S batteries after 100 cycles and washed by DOL:DME (1:1, in volume) solvent to remove surface residues on the anode before the tests.
3. Li2S deposition and dissolution tests
A piece of carbon paper served as the cathode, while Li foil and Celgard 2500 were used as the anode and separator, respectively. 20 μL of 0.5 M Li2S8 solution was dropped onto the cathode side, and another 20 μL of electrolyte (Base, PBA, or BPBA electrolyte) was dropped onto the anode side. The cell was then galvanostatically discharged with a current of 112 μA to 2.10 V and potentiostatically discharged at 2.05 V until the current fell below 0.01 mA. For the dissolution test, the post deposition cell was further galvanostatic discharged to 1.7 V and remained at this potential until the current was less than 0.01 mA. Afterwards, the cell was potentiostatically charged at 2.35 V until the current was lower than 0.01 mA.
4. Electrode preparation and electrochemical measurements
Sublimed sulfur and BP2000 conductive carbon black were thoroughly mixed at a mass ratio of 8:2 using a planetary ball-milling machine and then sealed in an autoclave. The mixture was heated to 155°C for 12 hours to prepare the S/C composite materials. The obtained S/C composites, acetylene black, and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) were mixed at a mass ratio of 8:1:1 in N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) to form a slurry. A specific amount of slurry was dropped onto a carbon felt substrate and heated on a hotplate at 60°C to evaporate the residual solvent, thereby fabricating a cathode with a designed sulfur loading (typically 2 mg cm-2, unless otherwise specified). For CR2032-type coin cell assembly, a Li disk (450 μm thick) was used as the anode coupled with the prepared sulfur cathode, and Celgard 2500 served as the separator. The cells were assembled in an argon-filled glove box with the E/S ratio controlled to 20 μL mgS-1, unless otherwise specified in the manuscript. For pouch cell assembly, S/C composites, acetylene black, and PVDF binder were mixed at a mass ratio of 90:5:5 in NMP under magnetically stirring to form a uniform slurry. The slurry was evenly dropped onto a piece of ultrathin carbon felt (1 mm) and dried in an air-circulating oven at 60°C to obtain the sulfur cathode. In a drying room with a dew point below -40°C, the sulfur cathode, separator, and Li foil (150 μm thick) were stacked, which was then packaged by aluminum-plastic films. The GDC tests were conducted using LAND battery test systems (CT2001A, Wuhan, China) and NEWARE battery test systems (CT-4000, Shenzhen, China). CV and EIS measurements were performed on a Bio-Logic multichannel electrochemical workstation (VSP-3e), with EIS frequencies ranging from 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz. For the Li||Cu and Li||Li cells (CR2032), the reference electrodes are Li metal, and the working electrodes are Cu and Li disk, respectively. 40 μL electrolyte was used for each coin cell. The CE of the Li||Cu cell was calculated by (nQc + QR) / (nQc + QT), where QT and Qc represent the initial total Li plating capacity of 10 mAh cm-2 and the fixed cycling capacity of 1 mAh cm-2, respectively, n is the cycling number, and QR is the capacity retention measured by a final Li stripping process.
5. Validation of the universality for the proposed mechanism
Arylboronic acid is an important type of organoboron material and has diverse molecule structures (Figure S48). To show that coordination and mediation chemistry can be extended to other arylboronic acid systems, we added a new type arylboronic acid, 3,5-dimethoxyphenylboronic acid (denoted as OCH3-PBA), into the Base electrolyte. The OCH3-PBA powders were purchased from Bide Pharmatech Ltd. with a purity of over 98%. A series of experiments including NMR, MS, XRD, and cycling test were conducted and the corresponding results are shown in Figure S49.
6. Computational methods
Density Functional Theory (DFT) and Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics (AIMD) calculations were performed using the CP2K/Quickstep software package. The electronic structure calculations employed the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional. Van der Waals interactions were accounted for by incorporating the Grimme D3 dispersion correction. The plane wave energy cutoff was set at 800Ry to ensure the accuracy of the wavefunction expansion, which utilized Triple-Zeta Valence with Polarization (TZVP) basis sets. The self-consistent field (SCF) convergence criterion was strictly maintained at 1×10-6 Hartree to achieve high precision in the electronic calculations. Solvation effects were modeled using a hybrid approach. The first solvation shell was explicitly represented by DOL molecules, selected over DME due to their superior stability in coordinating with Li ions, making them more suitable for accurately depicting the primary solvation layer in simulations. The broader solvent environment was simulated using the Polarizable Continuum Model (PCM). Within this model, the solvent, a mixture of DME and DOL, was treated as a dielectric continuum with a dielectric constant of 7.0. Geometry optimizations for static calculations were conducted using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) algorithm. All AIMD simulations were performed under the NVT ensemble at a controlled temperature of 300 K, using the Nose-Hoover thermostat to maintain thermal stability. The simulations utilized a time step of 1.0 fs and were conducted for a duration exceeding 10 ps to ensure adequate system equilibration. Initial configurations were derived from the optimized geometries obtained from static calculations. Figure S20 | Spatial distribution of (arylboronic acid-NO3)- and LiABO in electrolytes of Li-S batteries. The purple dashed line indicates the relative abundance of (arylboronic acid-NO3)- across the electrolyte from the cathode side to the anode side, while the orange dashed line suggests the relative abundance of LiABO throughout the electrolyte. 
According to the previous illustrated mechanism of coordination/mediation chemistry, the relative abundance of coordinated NO3- and LiABO is determined by the concentration gradient of LiPSs across the cell. Because LiABO is generated from the reaction between (arylboronic acid-NO3)- and LiPSs, the relative abundance of LiABO is higher in the region with a high LiPS concentration (near the cathode side). Correspondingly, more (arylboronic acid-NO3)- complexes in this region are consumed, resulting in a low abundance. While in the region with a low LiPS concentration (near the anode side), less (arylboronic acid-NO3)- complexes are consumed by the reaction with LiPSs, resulting in a low abundance of LiABO and a high abundance of coordinated NO3-.

Here, to show that coordination and mediation chemistry is applicable in some other arylboronic acid systems, we added a new type arylboronic acid, 3,5-dimethoxyphenylboronic acid (denoted as OCH3-PBA), into the Base electrolyte. As shown in Figure S49a, the OCH3-PBA electrolyte with LiNO3 is transparent and bright orange, while OCH3-PBA cannot be dissolved in the electrolyte without LiNO3. The 11B nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy (Figure S49b) clearly shows a peak in the high-field, confirming the occurrence of tetra-coordination between electron-donating NO3- and electron-deficient B atoms. After the reaction between OCH3-PBA and Li2S6, there are also some precipitates generated in the vial (Figure S49c), which is verified as elemental sulfur by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) test (Figure S49d). Besides, the mass spectrometry (MS) analysis proves that lithiated OCH3-PBA is one of the products of the reaction (Figure S49e), which is the base of mediation chemistry. The -OCH3 group grafting on the 3 and 5 sites show weak electron withdrawing ability, offering a coordination and mediation ability stronger than PBA but weaker than BPBA. As a result, the OCH3-PBA cell show a capacity retention of 76% upon 500 cycles and 57% upon 1000 cycles, which is inferior than BPBA but better than PBA (Figure S49f, 49g). The aforementioned results regarding OCH3-PBA confirm that the fundamental mechanisms of coordination and mediation chemistry holds potential to be extended to other arylboronic acids. By grafting different functional groups onto appropriate sites, the performance of arylboronic acids can be effectively modulated, thereby offering valuable insights for further studies on arylboronic acid systems.
