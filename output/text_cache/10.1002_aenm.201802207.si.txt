in both electrolytes in Fig. S7a.

2. Experimental  
RRDE voltammetry measurement The RRDE experiment was conducted by a three-electrode 
cell configuration, which composed of a platinum wire as the counter electrode (CE, diameter 
0.5 mm, length 23 cm, ALS CO., Ltd, Japan), a RE-7 nonaqueous reference electrode 
(Ag/Ag+) (ALS CO., Ltd, Japan) which was calibrated against Li metel (0 
VLi≈ -3.27V±0.03 V vs. Ag/Ag+), a rotating ring disk electrode as the working electrode 
(WE, the disk electrode is 4.0 mm in diameter, the inner diameter of the ring electrode is 5.0 
mm and external diameter is of 7.0 mm). The electrolyte of 4 mM S8, 1.0 M LiTFSI, 0.4 M

no solid was observed. The RRDE working electrode, the Pt wire counter electrode, and the 
reference electrode, and a glass bubbler allowing for direct flow of Argon gas into the 
solution or blanketing atop the electrolyte were all assembled inside the glove box. The 
RRDE was connected to the 700D Series Electrochemical Analyzer/Workstation (CH

potential stepping of disk and ring separately was reported previously.[8a] 
 
Measurement of solvent viscosity The dynamic viscosity (η) of electrolytes (DMA and DMF) 
contained 4 mM S8, 1.0 M LiTFSI and 0.4 M LiNO3 are measured by a rotational rheometer

glovebox and was filled in the measurement gap between the two parallel plates (PP50) with a 
diameter of 5 cm and a gap dimension of 0.25 mm (outside of glovebox). The sample 
electrolytes were measured three times, using CSR test mode with a shear rate (γ) range from 
0.1 s-1 to 1,000 s-1 under controlled 25 °C. The viscosities were taken at shear rate γ=100 s-1.

XANES  
1. Experimental 
S L-edge XANES spectra from discharged electrodes were obtained at the Canadian Light 
Source (CLS) on the spherical grating monochromator (SGM), where all the samples were 
stored in argon and transferred through an argon-filled glove box into the vacuum chamber. 
XANES spectra were recorded in both the surface sensitive total electron yield (TEY, with 
penetration depth ~10 nm) using specimen current and bulk fluorescence yield (FY, with 
penetration depth ~70 nm) mode using a photodiode. 
2. Results and Discussion 
 
Figure S11. XANES of S L-edge of Li2S deposition. XANES spectra of electrode samples 
after potentiostatic discharge of Li2S8 catholyte at 2.00 VLi. 
Discussion of Figure S11: 
Synchrotron-based XANES technique was exploited to probe the electronic structure of Li2S 
deposition at 2.00 VLi. Compared to the widely utilized S K-edge XANES (white line: 2,470 
eV)[9], S L-edge (white line: 162 eV)[10] spectra provides more sensitive information of the 
local bonding environment of S due to the well-resolved fine features induced by spin-orbit 

<<PAGE 20>>
 
 
19 
 
coupling of S 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 electrons[11]. Figure S11 shows the spectra obtained under 
florescence yield (FLY, with a penetration depth of ~70 nm) with reference spectra for 
comparison and peak assignments: Li2S, S8 and FeS2. Among five Li2S electrode spectra of S 
L-edge, the spectra patterns of those produced from glyme-based electrolytes (red curves, G2, 
G3 and G4) exhibited much sharper features than those from high donicity solvents (blue 
curves, DMA and DMF). Such obscurity of absorption peak could be caused by the lack of 
long range order and chemically inhomogeneous local environment of S site which wash out 
the modulation of the absorption coefficient[12]. The XANES results support XRD and TEM 
that the crystallinity of the Li2S formed in glymes is higher than that formed in high donicity 
solvents.

Discussion of Figure S12:

solvents largely depends on the polarity, which can be roughly estimated from both dielectric 
constant and dipole moment.[13] Dielectric constant describes the electrostatic interactions 
between electric charges, which has a decisive influence on the electrostatic solute-solvent 
interactions.[14]
