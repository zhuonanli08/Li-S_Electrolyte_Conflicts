Experimental Section
Preparation of electrolytes: Diglyme (G2, 99.9%, Adamas), acetonitrile (AN, 99.9%, SafeDry, Adamas), 1,3-dioxolane (DOL, 99%, SafeDry, Adamas), trimethylene glycol monomethyl ether (G3, 99.9%, Adamas), tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether (G4, 99.9%, Adamas), 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (TTE, 99.2%, DoDoChem), lithium bis (trifluoromethane sulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI, Aladdin) were used as received. Electrolytes were prepared by dissolving certain amounts (close to solvents’ solubility limit respectively) of LiTFSI into the mixtures of TTE and solvents (e.g., G2, G3, and AN) with a volume ratio of 2 : 1 by magnetically stirring for 12 h at 60 °C. The detailed formula is listed in Table S1. We used the G2-based SSEs that have good compatibility with Li-metal anode for all of the electrochemical tests and the assembly of pouches. SSEs with solvents of AN, G3, and G4 are used to verify the universality of the dipole-dipole strategy.

Table S1. Formulas of the sparingly-solvating electrolytes

Fabrication of Cathodes: Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF, ARKEMA) with a mass fraction of 5% dissolved into 1-Methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP, 99.5%, Aladdin) to gain PVDF binder. Sulfur powder (99.95%, Aladdin) was firstly mixed with Ketjen black (KB, ECP-600JD, Lion Specialty Chemicals) with a mass ratio of 7 : 3 by ball-milling at 600 rpm for 12 h. The mixture was then heated at 155 ºC for 12 hours under an argon atmosphere to obtain the S/KB composite. For the S/KB@CuS composite, which was prepared with the same procedure as that of the S/KB composite, the mass ratio of sulfur powder, KB, and cupric sulfide powder (99.0%, Macklin) is 7 : 2.5: 0.5. The cathode slurry was prepared by mixing the as-obtained composites, Super P (Timcal), and Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF, ARKEMA) binder with a mass ratio of 8: 1: 1 with a mixing machine (AR−100, THINKY) for 15 minutes. The slurry was then uniformly cast onto a carbon-coated aluminum foil (16 m in thickness, Shenzhen Kejing Corporation) to obtain the sulfur cathodes. Cathodes with lithium cobalt oxide (LiCoO2, 99.8%, Macklin), tricobalt tetraoxide (Co3O4, 99.9%, Macklin), and molybdenum disulfide (MoS2, 99.9%, Macklin) are prepared like the S/KB@CuS cathode.

Electrochemical tests: Coin cells and pouch cells were assembled in an Argon-filled glove box with the water and oxygen content below 0.01ppm. The coin cell was composed of a sulfur cathode, a Li metal plate (China Energy Lithium Co., Ltd.), and a Celgard 2325 separator. About 15 μL mg−1 electrolyte was used for coin cells. Instead, the practical electrolyte-to-sulfur ratio is 3.8 μL mg−1 for pouch cells. The galvanostatic charge/discharge tests were performed on a Neware Battery test station at 25 ºC. Cyclic voltammetry (CV, 0.1 mV s−1), electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS, 100 KHZ-100 mHZ), and Tafel plots (Tp, 0.1 mV s−1) tests were performed on an electrochemical station (VMP3, BioLogic).

Fabrication of Li||CuS half cells: The Li||CuS half cells are assembled to evaluate the capacity of CuS. CuS, KB, and Super P were mixed with a mass ratio of 5 : 3 : 1 to prepare the CuS composites. The cathode slurry was prepared by mixing the as-obtained composites and PVDF binder with a mass ratio of 9 : 1 in a mixing machine (AR-100, THINKY) for 15 minutes. The slurry was then uniformly cast onto a carbon-coated aluminum foil (16 m in thickness, Shenzhen Kejing Corporation) to obtain the CuS cathode. The Li||CuS half cells are assembled with the as-obtained CuS cathodes, Li metal anodes, and G2-based electrolytes.

Fabrication of Li||Li2S cells: To confirm the efficiency of the catalytic effect of CuS in accelerating the conversion of Li2S to S, we assembled Li||Li2S cells to ensure that the particle size of cathodes with or without CuS is consistent in the initial charging process. Firstly, we grinded Li2S and KB with a mass ratio of 6 : 3 to obtain composites. In the control group, the as-obtained composites and Super P with a mass ratio of 8 : 2 were dispersed in DME. In the experimental group, the as-obtained composites, Super P, and CuS with a mass ratio of 8 : 1 : 1 were dispersed in DME too. Both dispersions were stirred overnight and then dropped about 10 μL onto the surfaces of carbon papers (the diameter is 8 μm) to prepare Li2S electrodes.

The potentiostatic discharge/charge experiments: The KB@CuS powder is prepared by ball milling the KB and CuS powders at a mass ratio of 9:1 for 12h. The KB@CuS (KB) cathode slurry was prepared by mixing the as-obtained composites (or KB) and PVDF binder with a mass ratio of 9 : 1 in a mixing machine for 15 minutes. The slurry was then uniformly cast onto a carbon-coated aluminum foil (16 μm in thickness, Shenzhen Kejing Corporation) to obtain the KB@CuS cathodes (KB cathodes). Then we prepared the Li2S8 solution by adding Li2S and S with a molar ratio of 1 : 7 into DME solvent, stirring overnight at 60 °C. 10 μL of Li2S8 solution was dropped in the KB@CuS (KB) cathode, with 25 μL of G2-based SSE in the side of lithium anode to assemble cells for subsequent testment. For the potentiostatic discharge experiment, we first discharged cells to 2.07 V at a current of 0.112 mA, then discharged at a constant voltage of 2.03V until the current reached to < 0.01 mA. For the potentiostatic charge experiment, the cell was first discharged to 1.6 V, then charged at a constant voltage of 2.35 V until the current reached to < 0.01 mA.

Fabrication of pouch cells: The well-mixed slurry was single-side coated on Al foil and dried at 60 ºC for 12 h. Then, electrodes (40 mm × 45 mm) with a sulfur loading of 3.5 to 4.0 mgsulfur cm–2. The Li anode (thickness is 100 μm ) sheets were cut with the size of 42 mm × 47 mm with copper current collectors. A Celgard 2500 membrane was used as the separator. G2-based SSEs were used as the electrolyte, and the electrolyte/sulfur ratio (E/S) is around 3.8 µL mgsulfur−1. The cells were galvanostatically cycled between 1–2.8 V on the Neware Battery test station at 25 ºC.

Calculation of activation energy: We conducted EIS tests on cells at 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 °C respectively, and obtained the exact value of the impedance by fitting the Nyquist curves. The temperature at the time of measurement and the corresponding impedance value are substituted into the Arrhenius formula, and the slope of the curve obtained is the reaction activation energy of the cell in its current state. We measured the above steps under different charging voltages to obtain the changes in the activation energy of the cell during the charging process

Characterization: The intermolecular interaction energy is calculated by molecular dynamics simulation. The scanning electron microscope (SEM) images were obtained from discharged cathodes after 25 cycles on a field emission SEM (Quanta650, FEI). The surface elemental composition of the cathodes is obtained by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, AXIS-ULTRA DLD-600W) from charged cathodes after 25 cycles.
