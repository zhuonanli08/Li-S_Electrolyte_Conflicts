Charging lithium polysulfides by cationic lithium nitrate species for low-temperature lithium–sulfur batteries
 

Experimental Procedures
DFT calculations.
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed in Gaussian 09W to conduct analysis of the HOMO–LUMO. B3LYP method and 6-31+G(d, p) basis set were chosen. The solvation effect was considered with the universal solvation model of SMD [1]. The SCF convergence on energy is 10−6 Hartree. Frequency analysis was performed to ensure the ground state of optimized molecules.

Molecular Dynamic simulations.
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using the Materials Studio 2020. The time step was fixed to be 1 fs for all simulations. The systems were first equilibrated in an isobaric-isothermal (NPT) ensemble for 4 ns to maintain a temperature of 253 K/298 K and a pressure of 1 atm with time constants of 1 ps, respectively. A production run of 6 ns at 253 K/298 K in a canonical (NVT) ensemble under Nose–Hoover thermostat was finally conducted. The simulations were output every 1000 steps and used for the analysis of the radial distribution function [3]. 
To verify the coordinating structure of charged LiPSs and Liδ[LiNO3]δ+, the force field charge is changed to “Use current” mode, and the charge number is obtained from the optimization. Molecular dynamics simulations of the two electrolyte systems (E-[Li2S4-T-N] and E-[Li2S4-T]) were performed at room temperature (25°C) and low temperature (−20°C), and the equilibrium trajectories were analyzed.
1.3 Materials preparation.
Lithium foils (200 μm in thickness and 16 mm in diameter, Canrd). PP (polypropylene, DodoChem) membrane with a diameter of 19 mm were used as separator. LiTFSI (lithium bis((trifluoromethyl)sulfonyl)azanide, DodoChem), LiNO3 (lithium nitrate, DodoChem), Li2S (lithium sulfide, Canrd), S (sulfur, Merck), DME (ethylene glycol dimethyl ether, Adamas), DOL (1,3-dioxolane, Adamas), PVDF (polyvinylidene difluoride, DodoChem), CNTS (carbon nanotubes, DodoChem), and NMP (N-methyl-2pyrrolidone, DodoChem) are used as received without further treatment. 
Typically, 6.0 g CNTs and 14.0 g S were mixed and kept in a glass bottle at 160°C for 4 h. The S/CNT mixture was further ball-milled with PVDF binder at a weight ratio of 9:1 in NMP solvent to form a homogeneous slurry. After that, the obtained slurry was directly coated on an Al current collector by a doctor blade, which was further dried at 60°C for 12 h. The S/CNT electrode was then available after punching into disks. The areal sulfur loading of cathode was ~1.1 mg cm−2.
The nominal stoichiometry of Li2S4 solution was firstly prepared by mixing S and Li2S powders at a molar ratio of 3:1 in DOL/DME (1/1=v/v), followed by vigorous magnetic stirring for 24 h. The electrolyte called E-[Li2S4] was composed of 0.1 mol L−1 Li2S4 in a mixed solvents of DOL/DME (1/1=v/v). The electrolyte called E-[Li2S4-T] was composed of 0.1 mol L−1 Li2S4 and 0.1 mol L−1 LiTFSI in a mixed solvents of DOL/DME (1/1=v/v). The electrolyte called E-[Li2S4-T-N] was composed of 0.1 mol L−1 Li2S4 and 0.1 mol L−1 LiTFSI in a mixed solvents of DOL/DME (1/1=v/v) with 0.05 mol L−1 LiNO3.

1.4. Materials characterizations
7Li NMR was performed on 0.1 M Li2S4 in d-methanol/G4 (G4 repreents for tetraethylene glycol dimethyl ether) at 25οC or −20οC.

The cyclic voltammetry (CV) test used scanning rate of 0.1 mV/s at −20οC.
Before the shuttle current test, the cells were charged at 2.7 V, then kept the voltage at 2.6-2.2V to obtain the shuttle current.
The ultraviolet and visible spectrum (UV-vis) and high-eesolution mass spectrometry (HR-MS) test used the low-concentration electrolyte of E-[Li2S4-T] and E-[Li2S4-T-N].
All cells were assembled in 2025-type coin cells inside a glovebox. Cells were assembled in an Ar-filled glove box (with H2O < 0.1 ppm and O2 < 0.1 ppm). The Li–S cells were cycled in the voltage range of 1.7–2.8 V at 0.05 C for 3 cycles, followed by cycling at 0.1 C (1.0 C = 1675 mA g−1) in –20οC.

Figure S8. The CV (Cyclic voltammetry) test of E-[T-N] and E-[T] at −20°C.

The cell with E-[T-N] electrolyte delivers higher redox peaks and narrower voltage gap than that of E-[T] based cell. Especially, a distinct reduction peak (at 2.05 V) points out the rapid kinetic response of the charged LiPSs. In contrast, the peaks of E-[T] based cell are broader, corresponding to the sluggish kinetics due to the prevalence of clustering LiPSs.




Figure S9. The activation energy of E-[Li2S4-T] and E-[Li2S4-T-N] Li, EIS test from 60°C to 0°C, the Rct fitting in Table S6.

The activation energy barrier (Ect) of cells with E-[Li2S4-T-N] and E-[Li2S4-T] electrolytes are obtained by fitting the charge transfer impedance (Rct) based on the Arrhenius equation (4) in SI. The Rct in the cell with E-[Li2S4-T-N] exhibits the lower activation energies (40.53 kJ mol-1) comparing with the E-[Li2S4-T] based cell (46.99 kJ mol-1), which implicates the kinetically favorable process in the charge transfer process after charging LiPSs (Fig. S9).




Figure S10. The shuttle current of E-[Li2S4-T] and E-[Li2S4-T-N] at −20°C, constant voltage at 2.6-2.2 V.

The shuttle current of Li–S cells at low temperature was tested after charging to 2.7 V. A current value was obtained by discharging at constant voltage at different potentials in the range of 2.6~2.2 V. The shuttle current of the cell with E-[T-N] is much smaller than that of the cell with E-[T] at around 2.3 V that are dominated by soluble LiPSs. It is presumed that the negatively charged LiPSs tend to move to the positive electrode, which is also an important factor to affect shuttle effect beside the Li anode protection by LiNO3 (Fig. S10).

Figure S12. The Electrochemical performance of Li−S batteries at −20°C. (a) The initial charge and discharge profiles and (b) cyclability of cells with different LiTFSI and LiNO3 concentrations.

The high Li concentration elicits a large number of associated ion species (CIP and AGG) due to the reduced ion–ion interaction distance, which triggers the stronger electrostatic interaction between the negatively charged LiPSs anions and positively charged Li+, generating more uncharged LiPS clusters. The composition of electrolytes is abbreviated to the format of E-[xT-yN], in which E, T and N represent electrolyte, LiTFSI and LiNO3, and x and y indicate the concentration values. For example, E-[5T-5N] indicates the electrolyte composed of 0.5 M LiTFSI and 0.5 M LiNO3. As a result, the cells based on E-[8T-2N] shows poor capacity because excessive LiTFSI boosts the clustering tendency of LiPSs. The cells based on E-[5T-5N] and E-[2T-8N] shows the advanced performance compared with E-[8T-2N] (Fig. S12a). Besides, the cell with charged LiPSs electrolyte exhibits a stable cyclability compared with the clustering electrolyte (Fig. S12b).




Figure S13. The Electrochemical performance of Li−S batteries at −20°C. (a) The initial charge and discharge profiles and (b) cyclability of cells with different LiTFSI and LiNO3 concentrations.

To quantify the LiPS charging effect, different LiNO3 concentrations are added into the electrolytes. The results and discussions can be referred to SI (Fig. S13). 
Li–S cells with E-[2T-2N] and E-[2T-8N] show similar capacity, showing that excessive LiNO3 does not bring further capacity improvement in case the LiTFSI concentration is low (0.2 M), probably due to the low Li conductivity of entire electrolyte and insufficient Li sources to participate LiPS redox reactions (Fig. S13a). It is noticed that simultaneously increasing LiTFSI and LiNO3 concentrations yield better capacity and cyclability (Fig. S13a, b). Increasing LiTFSI to a certain amount provides sufficient Li conductivity and Li sources for redox reactions, while increasing LiNO3 exerts sufficient Li arresting effect to charge LiPSs.




Figure S14. The Electrochemical performance of Li−S batteries at 55°C.

The charged LiPSs also exhibit better cyclability at relatively high temperature (55°C). E-[T-N] based cells deliver a high capacity of 1182 mAh g–1, superior to the E-[T] based cell (917 mAh g–1). It is noticed that the LiPS clustering at high temperature is significantly alleviated, leading to the high 2nd-plateau capacity even without LiNO3 for the E-[T] based cell.
