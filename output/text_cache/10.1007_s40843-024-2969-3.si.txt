An organometallic salt as the electrolyte additive to regulate lithium
polysulfide redox and stabilize lithium anodes for robust lithium-sulfur
batteries
Yixuan Meng1, Meifang Zhang2, Youliang Wang1, Chen Liu1, Ze Zhang1*, Ji Yu1, Jianxin Cai1 and
Zhenyu Yang1,3*
ABSTRACT
Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries with high theo-
retical specific energy are considered to be one of the highly
promising next-generation energy storage systems. However,
the shuttle effect of lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) and the in-
terfacial instability of Li anodes have seriously hindered the
practical application of Li-S batteries. Optimizing the elec-
trolyte composition with additives can significantly improve
the battery performance and has attracted great attention.
Herein, we propose an organometallic salt, i.e., nickel bromide
dimethoxyethane (NiBr2DME), as an electrolyte additive,
which serves as the dual function of regulating LiPSs redox
and synchronously stabilizing Li anodes. We reveal that
NiBr2DME can interact with LiPSs via Ni–S and Li–Br bonds,
and accelerate the mutual transformation of LiPSs, thus re-
ducing the accumulation of LiPSs in the electrolyte. In addi-
tion, NiBr2DME can form a stable LiBr-containing interfacial
layer on the Li metal surface, and promote the uniform elec-
trodeposition of Li+ ions, and inhibit the formation of Li
dendrites. Thus, Li-S batteries with a concentration of 0.5
mmol L−1 NiBr2DME show an initial capacity of 919.8 mAh g−1
at 0.2 C, and a high capacity retention of 89.3% after 100
cycles. Even at the 4 C rate, a high discharge capacity of
602.9 mAh g−1 is achieved. Surprisingly, the good cycling
performance is maintained under poor electrolyte conditions
with sulfur loading of 4.8 mg cm−2 and electrlyte/sulfur ratio
of 5 μL mg−1. This work provides a positive solution to achieve
the suppression of shuttle effect, the regulation of LiPSs redox
and the stabilization of Li anodes.
Keywords: Li-S batteries, electrolyte additive, nickel bromide
dimethoxyethane, regulating LiPSs redox, stabilizing Li anode
INTRODUCTION
In the face of the rapid development of intelligent electronic
devices and electric vehicles, the demand for green and efficient
energy storage systems is increasing day by day [1,2]. Due to the
low cost, environmental friendliness and abundant resources of
sulfur, lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries are considered to be one of
the next generation of advanced energy storage systems with
high specific capacity (1672 mAh g−1) and energy density
(2600 Wh kg−1) [3–7]. However, Li-S batteries have a complex
multiphase conversion process, and the soluble lithium poly-
sulfides (LiPSs) migrate back and forth between the positive and
negative electrodes, which greatly reduces the utilization of the
active sulfur, and causes rapid capacity decay, low Coulombic
efficiency (CE) and Li anode corrosion. The main cause of the
shuttle effect is the slow conversion of soluble LiPSs to insoluble
Li2S2/Li2S, resulting in their accumulation in the electrolyte,
especially at long cycles and high sulfur loading [8–11]. Mean-
while, the unstable solid electrolyte interface (SEI) film on the Li
metal surface is unable to withstand stress changes during the
repeated Li plating/stripping processes, resulting in uneven Li
deposition and final dendrite formation [12,13].
Many host materials have been reported to address the shuttle
effect at the sulfur cathode from all aspects of physical limitation
and chemisorption, such as choosing porous and conductive
carbons as carrier materials for sulfur to alleviate the huge
volume expansion (80%) during cycling [14–16], or exploring
polar materials such as functional carbon [17,18], transition
metal compounds [19–21] and metal-organic frameworks [22–
24] to anchor LiPSs on the cathode side. However, the shuttling
effect in Li-S batteries cannot be well solved only by physical
domain limiting or chemisorption. Therefore, catalysis is
introduced into Li-S batteries to realize strong adsorption and
rapid catalytic conversion of LiPSs [25,26].
Besides exploring enhancements to cathode materials to
mitigate the shuttle effect, optimizing the electrolyte composi-
tion stands out as an effective strategy to impede the diffusion of
LiPSs [27]. Furthermore, the introduction of electrolyte addi-
tives aids in forming a stable SEI film on the Li anode surface,
facilitating uniform Li deposition [28,29]. Some electrolyte
additives also serve as redox mediators within the electrolyte,
catalyzing the conversion of LiPSs and suppressing the shuttle
effect [30]. Notably, catalytic electrolyte additives like cobalt
phthalocyanine derivatives (CoPcCl) play a crucial role in
enhancing the conversion of LiPSs, leading to a substantial
improvement in sulfur utilization [31]. Additionally, certain
electrolyte additives promote lithium deposition by generating
metal ions that create a positive electrostatic shielding effect

Herein, an organometalilc salt, nickel bromide dimethox-
yethane (NiBr2DME), was introduced as an electrolyte additive
for Li-S batteries. Dimethoxyethane (DME) as the ligand makes
inorganic salt NiBr2 more soluble in the organic electrolyte
because it is a common solvent for the ether-based electrolytes in
Li-S batteries [34]. The dual function of NiBr2DME additive in
regulating LiPSs redox and synchronously stabilizing Li anode is
illustrated in Fig. 1. On the one hand, the metal cation Ni2+ has a
strong interaction with polysulfide anions, thereby inhibiting the
shuttle effect and accelerating the conversion of LiPSs. On the
other hand, due to the strong interaction between Br−and Li+, a
stable SEI layer containing LiBr is formed on the surface of Li
anodes, which realizes the uniform Li plating/stripping without
dendrite generation. Therefore, combining the advantages of
Ni2+ and Br−of NiBr2DME, the presence of NiBr2DME additive
helps effectively inhibit the shuttle of polysulfide, facilitate the
LiPSs conversion, and protect the Li anode for robust Li-S
batteries.

cell with NiBr2DME additive shows higher redox current, and
lower charge transfer resistance (Fig. 3b). These results indicate
that NiBr2DME can promote the transformation kinetics of

Fig. 3c, d shows the static discharge curve of Li2S deposition
with/without NiBr2DME. The battery with the NiBr2DME
additive has higher response peak current of 0.31 mA and larger
deposition capacity of 567 mAh g−1. Fig. S1 shows the SEM
image of carbon paper after Li2S deposition. In the presence of
NiBr2DME additive, the deposited Li2S are uniformly covered on
the surface of carbon fibers. In the absence of NiBr2DME
additive, only a small amount of Li2S was deposited on the
surface of carbon fibers. The energy dispersive X-ray spectro-

elements exist on carbon paper, which indicates that NiBr2
interacts with LiPSs and promotes the uniform deposition of
Li2S. In the dissolution process of Li2S, the NiBr2DME additive
enables higher dissolution current response, earlier dissolution
time and higher dissolution capacity. These results vividly prove
that NiBr2DME additive significantly accelerates the conversion
of LiPSs to Li2S in the discharge process, and accelerates the
conversion of Li2S to LiPSs in the charge process [43–46]. Such a
bidirectional catalytic effect on the polysulfide redox of NiBr2
DME additive is favorable for suppressing the polysulfide shuttle
and promoting the reaction reversibility of sulfur cathodes.
On the anode side, the electrodeposition behaviors of Li
greatly affect the morphology and cyclability of Li anodes, which
finally would be reflected in the performance of Li-S batteries.

additive on the Li deposition during plating/stripping. Fig. 4a
shows the rate performance under different electrolyte condi-

better rate cycle performance, and the polarization voltage
increases regularly with the increase of current density. By
contrast, in the blank cell without NiBr2DME, the polarization
voltage does not change significantly at a low current density,
but fluctuates greatly as the current density increases. At a

The morphology changes of the Li metal after 100 cycles in
different electrolytes were compared. As shown in Fig. 4c, the
surface of the Li metal electrode with NiBr2DME is smooth
without obvious dendrites. The cross-section image (Fig. 4d)
shows dense, uniform and thin SEI layer with a thickness of
~19.4 μm. The surface of Li metal without NiBr2DME (Fig. 4e) is
rough and uneven with many large cracks, and the cross-section
image (Fig. 4f) shows a loose, porous and thick SEI layer
(~56.3 μm), which is caused by the unstable SEI layer during the
Li plating/stripping process. XPS measurements reveal the sur-
face composition of the SEI film produced by the electrolyte with
NiBr2DME additive after cycling. The full XPS spectrum (Fig.
S3) of the cycled Li metal reveals the existence of Li, F, O, N, and
Br elements on the surface of Li metal, and the Li 1s XPS
spectrum shows the Li–Br, Li–F, Li–O, and Li–N interactions.
Li–F, Li–O and Li–N bonds are produced by the decomposition
of LiTFSI/LiNO3 in the electrolyte [47–49], while the Li–Br
interaction demonstrates that NiBr2DME is involved in the
formation of the SEI layer. The strong interaction between Br−
and Li+ can weaken the interaction between Li+ and solvent
molecules, and reduce the nuclear barrier of Li. The formation of
a stable LiBr interfacial layer on the surface of the Li anode is
conducive to accelerating the deposition of lithium and inhi-
biting the growth of lithium dendrites [50–52].
Li/Cu cells under Li plating/stripping were used to explore the
effect of NiBr2DME on the long-term stability and electrode
polarization of Li metal anode [53–55]. As shown in Fig. 5a,
when the fixed deposition capacity is 1 mAh cm−2 and the
current density is 1 mA cm−2, the Li/Cu cell with NiBr2DME
maintains high and stable CEs (>99%) after 500 cycles. However,
the CE of the cell without NiBr2DME decreases sharply after 100
cycles. Even at the current density of 2 mA cm−2, the cell with
NiBr2DME remains high and stable CEs (Fig. 5b). In addition, as
shown in Fig. 5c, with the Li plating/stripping voltage curve at
1 mA cm−2, the cell with NiBr2DME at the initial Li deposition
on the surface of Cu foil exhibits a lower nucleation over-
potential of 175 mV than the bare cell (454 mV). As shown in
Fig. 5d and Fig. S4, the cell with NiBr2DME additive has lower
polarization potentials and cycle stability at different time per-
iods. The above results indicate that NiBr2DME can reduce the
nucleation overpotential of Li+ ions and has a high reversibility
of Li plating/stripping. Fig. 5e, f show the SEM images of Cu foil
surface of Li/Cu cells with or without NiBr2DME after cycling
for 100 h at a current density of 1 mA cm−2. Compared with the
cell without NiBr2DME, the one with NiBr2DME shows a uni-
form distribution of dense structure, presenting more flat
morphology. Fig. 5g, h show the morphology of Li foil surface.
Li foil of the bare cell presents uneven, rough cracks and sharp
prominent surfaces. However, the one of the cell with NiBr2
DME shows a uniform and smooth morphology, which indicates
that NiBr2DME can achieve the uniform Li deposition and
effectively inhibit the dendrite growth.

Consistent with the analysis of discharge/charge behaviors,
CV measurements of Li-S batteries were shown in Fig. 6d. In the
cathodic scan, the CV curve has two reduction peaks at 2.04 and
2.28 V, which correspond to the two-step reduction of S8 to
soluble LiPSs and insoluble Li2S2/Li2S, while two oxidation peaks
appear at 2.34 and 2.38 V during the anodic scan, representing
the reversible oxidation process of Li2S2/Li2S to S8. Compared
with the battery without NiBr2DME, the reduction peaks shift
positively and the oxidation peaks shift negatively for the battery
with NiBr2DME, indicating the reduced polarization potential
and the improved redox reversibility. It means that the addition
of NiBr2DME is favorable for enhancing the conversion kinetics
of sulfur cathodes. Fig. 6e, f and Fig. S7 respectively show the
Tafel slopes based on the reduction peaks and oxidation peak in
CV curves, and it is revealed that the battery with NiBr2DME has
lower Tafel slopes, indicating the faster kinetics. CV curves at
different scanning rates (Fig. S8) are also used to characterize the
Li+ ion diffusion characteristics and redox reaction kinetics.
With the addition of NiBr2DME, the peak shape of CV curves is
basically consistent with the increase of sweep speed (Fig. S8a).
Without the addition of NiBr2DME, the peak shape of CV
curves changes gradually with the increase of sweep speed, and
especially the reduction peaks become widened (Fig. S8b),
indicating the decreased conversion kinetics from LiPSs to Li2S.
The linearity between the current and the square root of scan-
ning rates is profiled, and the slope reveals the diffusion coef-
ficients of Li+ ions according to Randles-Sevcik equation. It can
be seen from Fig. S8c, d that the slopes of the battery with
NiBr2DME are higher than those without NiBr2DME, indicating
that NiBr2DME enhances the diffusion velocity of Li+ and the
redox kinetics of LiPSs [60–63].
Cyclic performance of the Li-S batteries with different con-
centrations of NiBr2DME at 0.2 C is consistent with the rate
performance. The electrolyte with 0.5 mmol L−1 NiBr2DME
achieves the highest initial capacity of 919.8 mAh g−1 at 0.2 C,
and the capacity remains at 821.6 mAh g−1 after 100 cycles
(Fig. 7a), demonstrating the highest capacity retention ratio of
89.3%. EIS profiles of the Li-S batteries with or without NiBr2
DME were measured to further explore the improvement of the
sulfur redox reaction by NiBr2DME. As seen from Fig. S9, EIS
curve consists of a semicircle in the high frequency region and a
slant line in the low frequency region, representing the charge
transfer resistance and diffusion impedance at the cathode and
electrolyte interface, respectively. The Li-S battery with NiBr2
DME has lower charge transfer resistance than the battery
without NiBr2DME. After 100 cycles, the charge transfer resis-
tances of Li-S batteries are significantly reduced, especially the
Li-S battery with NiBr2DME. It shows that the addition of
NiBr2DME improves the kinetics of sulfur redox reaction and
enables the rapid diffusion rate of Li+ ions. This is attributed to
the catalytic action of NiBr2DME on the deposition and dis-
solution of Li2S. Thus, with the optimal concentration of NiBr2
DME, the battery has an initial capacity of 769.6 mAh g−1 at
0.5 C and a capacity retention rate of 79% after 200 cycles
(Fig. 7b). Even at 1 C, NiBr2DME additive endows the Li-S
battery with a superior cycle stability and a capacity decay rate of
0.043% (Fig. 7c). In addition, the electrochemical performance
of Li-S batteries under lean electrolyte condition was investi-
gated. As shown in Fig. 7d, in the presence of NiBr2DME
additive, the battery with a 2.8 mg cm−2 sulfur cathode delivers
an initial capacity of 901.6 mAh g−1, and exhibits a good cycling
performance with a remaining capacity of 567.2 mAh g−1 after
100 cycles at the electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio of 5 μL mg−1.
When increasing the sulfur loading to 4.8 mg cm−2, the initial
capacity is 609.8 mAh g−1, and capacity of 313.3 mAh g−1
remains after 100 cycles. These results indicated that NiBr2DME
could accelerate the catalytic conversion of LiPSs, inhibit the
shuttle effect, and improve the utilization efficiency of active
sulfur [64–69].

with no obvious cracks (Fig. S10a, b), and the formed SEI layer is
tight and thin (~23.8 μm in thickness, Fig. S10c). However,
without NiBr2DME (Fig. S10d–f), the cycled Li anode presents
severe crushing, rough and porous surfaces, and thick SEI layers

CONCLUSIONS
In conclusion, NiBr2DME is added as an electrolyte additive of
Li-S batteries to regulate the redox kinetics of sulfur cathode,
inhibit the shuttle effect, and stabilize the Li anode. In the pre-
sence of NiBr2DME, there is strong adsorption between Ni2+ and
polysulfides, which can capture polysulfides in the electrolyte,
and regulate the nucleation and deposition of Li2S. At the same
time, the NiBr2DME additive is favorable for inducing uniform
Li electrodeposition and forming a uniform and stable SEI layer,
which realizes the inhibition of dendrite growth and the pro-
tection of the Li anode interface. With the aid of NiBr2DME
additive, the cycle life of Li/Li cells is extended to 4000 h at
1 mA cm−2, and Li/Cu cells show highly reversible Li plating/
stripping with high CE. The assmebled Li-S batteries exhibit
superior rate capability, and remarkable cycle stability with a
high capacity retention of 89.3%. Even at 4.8 mg cm−2 sulfur
loading and 5 μL mg−1 E/S ratio, the battery shows an initial
capacity of 609.8 mAh g−1 and a good cycle stability within 100
cycles at 0.2 C. This work proposes a strategy of developing
functional electrolyte additives to catalyze sulfur redox reactions
and stabilize Li anodes for advanced Li-S batteries.

一种调节锂多硫化物氧化还原性和锂负极稳定性的
金属有机盐电解质添加剂
孟怡轩1, 张梅芳2, 王有亮1, 刘琛1, 张泽1*, 吁霁1, 蔡建信1,
杨震宇1,3*
摘要
锂硫电池具有较高的理论比能, 被认为是极具发展前景的下一
代储能系统之一. 然而, 多硫化锂的穿梭效应和锂阳极的界面不稳定性
严重阻碍了锂硫电池的实际应用. 利用添加剂优化电解液的组成可以
显著提高电池的性能. 本工作中, 我们提出了一种有机金属盐, 即溴化
镍-二甲氧基乙烷(NiBr2DME)作为电解质添加剂, 它具有调节LiPSs氧
化还原和稳定锂阳极的双重功能. 我们发现NiBr2DME可以通过Ni–S和
Li–Br键与LiPSs相互作用, 加速了LiPSs的相互转化, 从而减少了LiPSs在
电解质中的积累. 此外, NiBr2DME能在锂金属表面形成稳定的含LiBr
界面层, 促进锂离子的均匀电沉积, 抑制锂枝晶的形成. 使用浓度为
0 . 5 m m o l L −1
N i B r 2D M E 的L i - S 电池在0 . 2 C 下的初始容量为
919.8 mAh g−1, 循环100次后容量保持率高达89.3%. 即使在4 C的速率
下, 也可以实现602.9 mAh g−1的高放电容量. 在含硫量为4.8 mg cm−2、
液硫比为5 μL mg−1的较差电解质条件下, 电池仍能保持良好的循环性
能. 本研究为实现抑制穿梭效应、调控LiPSs氧化还原和稳定锂阳极提
供了积极的解决方案.
