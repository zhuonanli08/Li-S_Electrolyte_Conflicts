Materials and Methods  
Materials 
Dimethyl ether (99%) was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. The salts Lithium 
bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (99.9%) were purchased from Gotion. 1,2-dimethoxyethane (99.5%) was 
purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and stored over molecular sieves. 1 M LiPF6 in EC:EMC (3:7 wt%) 
was sourced from Gotion Ltd.  
The preparation of SPAN electrodes involves a step-by-step process, including active 
material (SPAN) (80 wt%), sodium carboxymethyl cellulose binder (NaCMC) (10 wt%), and a 
SuperP carbon conductive agent (10 wt%). The SPAN active materials were synthesized followed 
by previous work[36]. Firstly, a binder solution is made by mixing 0.1 g of NaCMC with 2 g of 
deionized water (DI) until complete dissolution. Next, 0.1 g of SuperP conductive agent is added 
to the binder solution, along with 1 g of DI water, and this mixture is vigorously mixed for 1 hour 
using a Thinky machine at 1800 rpm. Subsequently, 0.8 g of SPAN active material is added to the 
mixture and mixed for an additional hour under the same conditions. The resulting slurry is then 
cast onto a carbon-coated Al substrate using a doctor blade with a gap of 200 μm. The freshly cast 
electrode can naturally dry at room temperature overnight to prevent cracking from rapid 
evaporation. After a night of drying, no visible moisture should be present. Finally, the electrode 
is transferred to a 70 °C air blowing thermostatic oven for further drying, typically lasting 4 hours 
to complete the preparation process. The loading of fabricated SPAN electrode is around 3 – 3.5 
mAh cm-2. 
Electrochemical measurements 

<<PAGE 4>>
 
3
Electrolytic conductivity measurements were performed in custom fabricated stainless-
steel coin cells, using polished stainless-steel (SS 316L) as both electrodes. The cell constant was 
calibrated frequently from 0.447 to 80 mS cm-1 by using OAKTON standard conductivity 
solutions. The obtained ionic conductivity values (mS cm-1) were converted to S cm-1 with the 
natural logarithm takenfor the plot in Fig. 2b. 
For Li||Cu cells, CR-2032-type coin cells were assembled using 7/16-inch Li foils (99.9% 
purity, sourced from China Energy Lithium Co.), 18 mm diameter Cu foil discs, a three-layer 
25µm porous PP/PE/PP membrane (Celgard 2325), and 50 μL of electrolytes. To assess CE at 
room temperature, Li||Cu cells were subjected to cycling: plating 1 mAh cm-2 of Li at 0.5 mA cm-
2 and stripping to 1.0 V using the electrolytes of interest.  For Li/Cu cycling tests in different 
temperatures, the cells were soaked at the testing temperature in a temperature chamber (Espec) 
for at least 2 hours before cycling. 
For Li||SPAN cells, CR-2032-type coin cells were constructed with SPAN cathodes (12 
mm diameter), Li foils, a three-layer 25µm porous PP/PE/PP membrane (Celgard 2325), and the 
desired electrolyte (75 μL). The specific capacity is considered as 600 mAh/g and the cycling 
charge cutoff voltage is 3 V and the discharge cutoff voltage is 1 V. Each cells have two activation 
cycles at C/10 rate (0.3 mA/cm2) followed by C/3 (1 mA/cm2) discharge rate and C/5 (0.6 mA/cm2) 
charging rate at both room temperature and +50 oC. For Li/SPAN discharge tests in different 
temperatures, the cells were soaked at the testing temperature in a temperature chamber (Espec) 
for at least 2 hours before cycling. Cells are firstly activated for two cells at C/10 rate at room 
temperature and then used C/5 discharge rate and C/10 charge rate at -20 oC. 
Material characterization 

<<PAGE 5>>
 
4
The density and molar ratio of LiFSI to Me2O of Sat. LiFSI-Me2O are determined by the 
mass difference before and after heating under vacuum of a fixed volume of solution. For instance, 
a 200 µL of Sat. LiFSI-Me2O solution is added into a glass vial and its initial mass is recorded. 
After 4.5 hours heating under vacuum at 60 oC, the remaining mass is considered as LiFSI salt 
mass only. The mass loss comes from the evaporation of Me2O. The density is roughly calculated 
by the mass loss divided by 200 µL. The molar ratio of LiFSI to Me2O can also be calculated 
accordingly. The average density of Sat. LiFSI-Me2O is 1.09 g/cm and the average LiFSI to Me2O 
molar ratio is 1:2.4 after a three-time measurement. 
Differential scanning calorimetry and thermogravimetric analysis is performed on Netzsch 
STA 449 Jupiter. The electrolyte/solvent (Approx. 25-30 uL) is injected and sealed in Cu-backed 
Al pans. All sample preparations are done in an Argon-filled glovebox (<0.5 ppm O2, <0.5 ppm 
H2O). The Aluminum pan is pierced by a needle after loading into the furnace chamber. The 
temperature is ramped up to 250 °C from room temperature at the rate of 10 C/min. All 
measurements are performed under a constant Ar flow rate of 40 ml/min. 
Raman spectra of liquefied gas electrolytes are carried on Renishaw inVia confocal Raman 
microscope with an excitation wavelength of 532 nm. All spectra are calibrated with Si (520 nm) 
and analyzed by Wire 3.4 software developed by Renishaw Ltd. The Raman of Me2O-based 
electrolytes are performed in a customized-built pressurized cell[37]. 
Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra of studied electrolytes including Sat. LiFSI in 
Me2O are collected on a Varian Inova 500 MHz NMR spectrometer using an SW Probe. 
Electrolyte samples are prepared and transferred to sealed NMR tubes in the glovebox before data 
collection. 7Li data are acquired at 194.22 MHz with a spectral width of 3884 Hz, with an

acquisition time of 4 seconds for 31076 complex points using a 1 second relaxation delay for 16 
scans. The data is processed and analyzed on MestReNova 14.0.0 software. 
X-Ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed using a Kratos AXIS Supra DLD 
XPS with monochromatized Al Kα radiation (λ= 0.83 nm and hυ=1486.7 eV) under a base pressure 
<5*10-8 Pa. To avoid moisture and air exposure, samples are transferred to the XPS chamber 
directly from a glovebox via air-tight transfer. All spectra are calibrated with hydrocarbon C-H C 
1s (284.6 eV) and analyzed by CasaXPS software. To remove residual salt on the surface, all 
samples are rinsed with DME and dried in glovebox antechamber before analysis. The etching 
condition are set as an Ar1000+ cluster at 5 keV. The etching times were 60 s and 180 s. 
The polysulfide dissolution tests were performed by immersing stoichiometric ratios of 
Li2S and S corresponding to 0.25 M Li2S6 in 4 M LiFSI-DME and Sat. LiFSI-Me2O electrolytes 
followed by 24 hours of resting.  
Molecular Dynamics Simulations  
Classical, fixed-charge Molecular Dynamics simulations were performed in LAMMPS, 
with the solvents and Li+ described using the General Amber forcefield. FSI- was described with 
the potential of Gouveia et al[38]. The initial simulations cells were constructed using in-house 
scripts, with stoichiometric compositions corresponding to the solutions of interest. For the Me2O 
solutions, the solvent was initially configured in a gaseous state with densities matching room-
temperature conditions. This initial configuration allowed for the observation of composition-
dictated Coulombic condensation over the timescales described above. Van der Waals interactions 
not explicitly specified in the forcefields were generated using Lorentz-Berthelot mixing rules. In 

<<PAGE 7>>
 
6
all cases the charges of the Li+ and FSI- molecules were scaled to the high-frequency dielectric 
properties of the solvents present in the system, which was 0.73 for DME and 0.76 for Me2O. 
 
For each system, an initial energy minimization at 0 K (energy and force tolerances of 10-
4) was performed to obtain the ground-state structure. After this, the system was slowly heated 
from 0 K to room temperature at constant volume over 0.01 ns using a Langevin thermostat, with 
a damping parameter of 100 ps. In the DME systems, the electrolyte was then subjected to 5 cycles 
of quench-annealing dynamics in an attempt to eliminate any meta-stable solvation states, where 
the temperature was slowly cycled between 298 K and 894 K with a ramp period 0.025 ns followed 
by 0.1 ns of dynamics at either temperature extreme, for 1.25 ns of total time. The Me2O 
electrolytes were not exposed to this protocol due to their volatility. Afterwards, the electrolytes 
were subjected to a constant temperature, constant pressure (1 bar) NPT ensemble for 10 ns using 
the Andersen barostat (pressure relaxation constant of 1 ps). Finally, we performed 10 ns of 
constant volume, constant temperature (NVT) production dynamics from which the solvation 
information was extracted. Radial distribution functions and density profiles were obtained using 
the Visual Molecular Dynamics (VMD) software. Snapshots of the various solvation shells, sampled 
from the simulation trajectory, were also obtained using VMD.

Supplementary Figure 2. Determination of saturation point. 
The determination of the saturation point (or solubility) of LiFSI in Me2O was conducted 
using the following procedure: 
1. A 3 M LiFSI solution in Me2O was prepared in a pressurized window cell, and its 
homogeneity was observed to ensure that all the LiFSI salt was fully dissolved. 
2. Me2O was then slowly purged from the pressurized window cell via Swagelok valves. 
The resultant solution was left undisturbed in a glovebox environment for two days to allow for 
equilibration. While the solution typically reaches equilibrium within a few hours, the two-day 
resting period was employed as a conservative approach to ensure complete stabilization. 
3. To determine whether the solution had reached its saturation point, an additional 0.1 M 
LiFSI was incrementally added to the equilibrated solution. The inability of the additional LiFSI 
to dissolve confirmed that the solution was saturated.

Supplementary Figure 14. Li/Cu cycling in Sat. LiFSI-Me2O and 4 M LiFSI-DME electrolytes. 
 
The applied plating and stripping current is 0.6 mA cm-2 and 1 mA cm-2 respectively, 
corresponding to C/5 charge and C/3 discharge rates in Li/SPAN testing protocol (Supplementary 
Fig. 14). As shown in Supplementary Fig. 14, there is no significant difference in average CE 
within the first 60 cycles. However, it is observed that the CE for cells with 4 M LiFSI-DME starts 
to fluctuate after 50 cycles, and the cycling overpotential is higher than that for cells with Sat. 
LiFSI-Me2O (Supplementary Fig. 14b,c). This outcome can also explain the early capacity 
degradation in the Li/SPAN test due to the ineffective passivation of Li metal.
