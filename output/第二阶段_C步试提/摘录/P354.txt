- Interfaces 2023, 15, 37467-37476 Read Online ACCESS Metrics & More Article Recommendations * sı Supporting Information ABSTRACT: High-concentration lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)- imide/1,2-dimethoxyethane (LiFSI/DME) electrolytes are promis- ing candidates for highly reversible lithium-metal anodes.
- The results showed that the combination of carbon with mesopores of 2-3 nm diameter and 3 M LiFSI in DME/ 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropylether (HFE) (1:1 by vol.) provided a high-rate capability (943 mA h g-1 at a rate of 2 C).
- Moreover, the ratio of the 50th discharge capacity to the 2nd discharge capacity (capacity retention) improved from 50.0 to 61.6% with HFE dilution of high-concentration LiFSI/DME.
- At the cathode, sulfur reacts with lithium ions to form lithium polysulfide (Li2Sx) during the charge-discharge process.6,7 In particular, Li2Sx (x = 6-8) exhibits high solubility in most organic electrolytes.8,9 The dissolution of Li2Sx causes loss of the active material, redox shuttling, and pollution of the lithium anode, which hinders the stable cycling of Li-S batteries.
- To address this problem, Nazar et al. reported a method for confining sulfur in the pores of highly ordered mesoporous carbon.10 The dissolution of Li2Sx into the electrolyte is kinetically suppressed owing to the interaction between the carbon materials and Li2Sx.
- However, mesoporous carbons cannot completely inhibit polar Li2Sx dissolution because the carbon surface is generally nonpolar.11 Hence, surface modification of meso- porous carbons with polymers, functional groups, or metal compounds should be applied to impart polarity to the carbon surface.12-15 Lithium-metal anodes also have fundamental problems.
- This results in a low coulombic efficiency and high cell resistance.16,17 More- over, the lithium-metal anode undergoes repeated deposition and dissolution during the charge-discharge process, which induces lithium dendrite formation.18 The lithium dendrites Received: May 9, 2023 Accepted: July 18, 2023 Published: July 26, 2023 Research Article www.acsami.org © 2023 American Chemical Society 37467 https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.3c06624 ACS Appl.

- Furthermore, the heterogeneous dissolution of lithium dendrites during the discharge process forms dead lithium, which loses its electronic contact with the anode.19 Dead lithium is electrochemically inactive and causes capacity fading.
- Various electrolytes and additives have been investigated to suppress the excess decomposition of electrolytes and formation of lithium dendrites.20,21 A high-concentration lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide/1,2-dimethoxyethane (LiFSI/DME) electrolyte has been reported to achieve a stable SEI and dendrite-free lithium-metal anode.22 In particular, a Li/Cu cell with 3-5 M LiFSI/DME showed a high coulombic efficiency.
- Furthermore, we prepared a suitable high-concentration LiFSI/DME electrolyte for use with the micro-mesoporous carbon-sulfur composite cathode and analyzed the effect of the electrolyte on the cathode and anode. 1,1,2,2-Tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3- tetrafluoropropylether (HFE) was used for the electrolyte optimization.
- Because HFE is one of the hydrofluoroethers with the lowest lithium-solvating ability,28 dilution with HFE significantly reduces the viscosity of high-concentration LiFSI/ DME while maintaining the locally high concentration of the LiFSI/DME electrolyte.29 Moreover, HFE suppresses the side reaction between Li2Sx and lithium-metal anodes, and improves the deposition morphology of lithium-metal anodes;30 thus, HFE is a promising solvent for electrolyte optimization.

- Electrochemical Performances of Porous Carbon-Sulfur Composite Cathodes with 3 M LiFSI/DME.
- Galvanostatic charge-discharge measurements were performed to investigate the effect of the pore structure of porous carbons on the performance of Li-S batteries with 3 M LiFSI/DME.
- Figure 3a shows the charge-discharge curves of the Li-S batteries at the 1st and 50th cycles.
- Cmicro-S showed only one plateau at low potential (∼1.6-1.9 V).
- This plateau was attributed to the formation of Li2Sx (x = 1, 2).14 In contrast, Cmicro-meso-S and Cmeso-S exhibited a plateau at a high potential (∼2.0 V) in addition to a low-potential plateau.
- The high-potential plateau was attributed to the reduction of sulfur to Li2Sx (x = 4-8).14 Figure 3b shows the cycle plots of the Li-S batteries.
- Cmicro-S had high capacity retention (83.4%), which was calculated by dividing the discharge capacity at the 50th cycle (934.5 mA h g-1) by the discharge capacity at the 2nd cycle (1121 mA h g-1).
- Furthermore, Cmicro-S showed high coulombic efficiency (average 99.4%).
- This stable cycle performance could be due to the suppression of Li2Sx dissolution, which causes loss of the cathode active material and redox shuttling for two reasons: (i) micropores suppress the formation of Li2Sx (x = 4-8) and42 (ii) the decomposition products of the electrolyte formed by charging and discharging completely cover the pores, which inhibits contact between the electrolyte solvent and the cathode active material.27 The coulombic efficiency of Cmeso-S significantly exceeded 100% at the 11th cycle, and the Li-S battery with the Cmeso-S cathode did not reach the charge cut-off voltage at the 14th cycle.
- Qian and Fu reported that the molar ratio of Li+ to DME in 3 M LiFSI/DME is 1:2.2, and Li+ is coordinated with five oxygen atoms of FSI-and DME molecules.22,43 From this electrolyte composition, they suggested that it contained uncoordinated DME molecules.
- In the case of Cmeso-S, Li2Sx (x = 6-8) would be able to dissolve in the uncoordinated DME of the 3 M LiFSI/DME electrolyte because Cmeso has mainly mesopores, from which Li2Sx dissolves into uncoordi- nated DME easily.
- The dissolved Li2Sx in the electrolyte removed from the cycled cell were confirmed by UV-vis spectroscopy (Figure S6).
- Cmeso-S and Cmicro-meso-S had a peak ascribed to Sx 2-at 350-500 nm; the peak intensity of Cmeso-S was approximately four times higher than that of Cmicro-meso-S.
- In addition, the Sx 2-peak was not observed in the Cmicro-S spectrum.24,44 Thus, Cmeso-S could not reach the cut-off voltage owing to the redox shuttle, pollution of the lithium anode, and the irreversible reaction between dissolved Li2Sx and LiFSI.23 The cell with Cmicro-meso-S was discharged and charged with a capacity decay.

- The capacity decay of the Cmicro-meso-S/ Li battery with 3 M LiFSI/DME was due to Li2Sx dissolution caused by a few mesopores and uncoordinated DME molecules.
- Some reports have shown that high-concentration electrolytes contain less uncoordinated DME; thus, the Li2Sx dissolution is suppressed.24,45 High-concentration LiFSI/DME above 3.6 M without uncoordinated DME would realize Cmicro-meso-S/Li batteries with high energy density and high cycle stability.
- Figure 4a shows the charge-discharge curves of Li-S batteries with Cmicro-meso-S cathodes and various concen- trations of LiFSI/DME electrolytes at the 1st and 50th cycles.
- For 3 M LiFSI/DME, two plateaus were observed.
- The high- potential plateau, which is related to readily soluble Li2Sx (x = 6-8), disappeared as the concentration of LiFSI increased (Figure S7).
- In the case of 3.6 and 4 M LiFSI/DME, nearly all of the DME were coordinated to Li+ because the molar ratios of LiFSI and DME were 1:2 and 1:1.4, respectively.22 Thus, the dissolution of Li2Sx was significantly inhibited and the high- potential plateau disappeared.
- To investigate the suppression effect of the electrolytes on Li2Sx dissolution, solutions prepared by mixing each electrolyte and 0.2 M Li2S8/DME at a volume ratio of 3:1 were observed (Figure S8).
- This color change suggests that soluble Li2Sx (x = 6-8) was transformed into insoluble short-chain Li2Sx.44,46,47 Therefore, a higher electrolyte concentration suppresses the dissolution of Li2Sx.
- The morphology of the Li deposition in 3, 3.6, and 4 M LiFSI/DME was observed using SEM (Figure S9).
- The deposited lithium had a nodule-like structure with round edges, which is consistent with previously reported results.22 As the electrolyte concentration increased, Li was densely and uniformly deposited.
- Nevertheless, a higher concentration of the electrolyte led to significant capacity decay (Figure 4b).
- The capacity retentions of 3.6 and 4 M LiFSI/DME, calculated by dividing the discharge capacity at the 50th cycle (82.3 and 161.6 mA h g-1) by the discharge capacity at the 2nd cycle (1578.8 and 1475.4 mA h g-1), were 5.21 and 11.0%, respectively.
- Furthermore, the average coulombic efficiencies of 3.6 and 4 M LiFSI/DME were 90.8 and 92.6%, respectively.

- As a result, 3.6 and 4 M LiFSI/ DME showed higher discharge capacities than 3 M LiFSI/ DME.
- Thus, the high-concentration LiFSI/DME electrolyte effectively improved the Li deposition morphology and suppressed Li2Sx dissolution; however, its high viscosity reduced the Li-S battery performance at high current densities.
- Diluted electrolytes with HFE have reduced viscosity and localized high concentrations of LiFSI because LiFSI does not dissolve in HFE.24 Herein, 3 M LiFSI/ DME was diluted with HFE to improve the cycling performance at high current densities and suppress Li2Sx dissolution.
- Figure 5a shows the charge-discharge curves of the Li-S batteries with Cmicro-meso-S cathodes and 3 M LiFSI in DME/ HFE electrolytes at the 1st and 50th cycles.
- A high-potential plateau, which is attributed to the Li2Sx (x = 4-8) formation reaction, was not observed during the discharge of the Li-S batteries with 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE.
- Therefore, the dissolution of Li2Sx was suppressed in 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE.
- To investigate the suppression effect of 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE on Li2Sx dissolution, a solution prepared by mixing 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE and 0.2 M Li2S8/DME at a volume ratio of 3:1 was observed (Figure S8), and the dark color of Li2S8 changed to yellow because of the presence of Li2Sx (x = 1, 2).47 Thus, dilution with HFE suppresses the dissolution of Li2Sx.
- Figure 5b shows the cycle plots of Li-S batteries with Cmicro-meso-S cathodes and 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE electrolytes.
- The HFE dilution suppressed the continuous capacity decay of 3 M LiFSI/DME and improved the capacity retention rate, calculated by dividing the discharge capacity at the 50th cycle (732.3 and 830.4 mA h g-1) by the discharge capacity at the 2nd cycle (1462.9 and 1347.7 mA h g-1), from 50.0 to 61.6%.
- Moreover, the average coulombic efficiency improved from 97.1 to 98.5%.
- This improved cycling performance with HFE dilution was due to the mitigation of the non-uniform current distribution by decreasing the viscosity of the electrolyte (Table 2).
- Furthermore, the decreased viscosity provided 3 M LiFSI in DME/HFE with superior performance at high current density (Figure 5c).
