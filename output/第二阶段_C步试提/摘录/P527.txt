- A commonly used electrolyte in Li-S batteries is a solution of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI, usually 1 M) with a LiNO3 additive (usually ≤0.5 M) in a mixture of 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) (1:1 v/ v).
- Unfortunately, Li-S batteries that contain this electrolyte experience premature corrosion of the Li metal anode and severely suffer from the shuttle effect in part because the ethereal solvents facilitate the disso lution of polysulfides [25-28].
- As such, new electrolyte systems have been proposed, including those that feature high concentrations of Li+ or are based on ionic liquids [29,30].
- Highly solvating elec trolytes (HSEs), which utilize high donor-number (DN) solvents, are a promising alternative as they have been shown to accelerate cathode reaction kinetics, enhance sulfur utilization, and alleviate deleterious surface passivation effects [31-33].
- HMPA has a high DN value (37.8 kcal mol-1) and forms robust Li+ solvation structures.
- These beneficial ef fects manifested in high performance devices as Li//Li symmetric cells containing a HMPA-based electrolyte generated a significantly lower polarization voltage in stripping/plating cycling tests (for more than 600 h at 5 mA cm-2) when compared to analogous cells that contained a conventional electrolyte (c.f., 0.06 vs. 0.25 V, respectively).
- Further more, Li-S cells containing a HMPA-based electrolyte also exhibited improved performance metrics when compared to cells that contained a conventional electrolyte, including a markedly improved rate capability (e.g., 606.4 vs. 181.5 mAh g-1 at 2 C) and a greatly increased cycling stability (e.g., 619.6 vs. 295.2 mAh g-1 after 300 cycles at 0.2 C).
- Our work establishes a new method for overcoming the longstanding issues that have limited the practicality of Li-S batteries and, because the method is straightforward and cost-effective, it is poised for adaption to other energy storage devices. 2.

- Electrolyte design The DN number of HMPA was measured to be 37.8 kcal mol-1 via nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy (see Fig.
- S1 for de tails), which is significantly higher than the values of commonly used ether-based solvents (e.g., 20.0 and 18.0 kcal mol-1 for DME and DOL [34], respectively) and amide-based solvents (e.g., 26.6, 27.3, 28.0, and 29.0 kcal mol-1 for N,N-dimethylformamide, N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone, N,N-dimethylacetamide, and 1,3-dimethyl-2-imidazolidinone [35-37], respectively).
- The solubility of the electrolyte salt, LiTFSI, in HMPA was measured to be approximately one tenth of the value in DME (ca. 0.6 M and 5.8 M, respectively).
- As such, the solubility of polysulfides in a HMPA-based electrolyte should be reduced due to the common ion effect [38,39].
- The effects of HMPA on other aspects of sulfur cathode chemistry has been recently reported [40].
- A molecular electrostatic potential analysis indicated that the negatively charged regions of HMPA and DME are located on their respective oxygen atoms (Fig. 1a).
- When used as a co-solvent with DOL to dissolve LiTFSI, which is poorly soluble in HMPA, the formation of contact ion pairs (CIPs) and ion aggregates (AGGs) between Li cations and TFSI anions becomes plausible (Fig. 1b, left).
- With these considerations in mind, an electrolyte that consisted of LiTFSI, DOL, and DME was selected as a comparative control (Fig. 1b, right).
- Such solvent-separated ion pairs (SSIPs) typically result in the formation of SEI layers with a rela tively poor content of LiF as well as uneven Li deposition [41].
- Indeed, Li-S batteries that are prepared with DOL/DME electrolytes often exhibit a pronounced polysulfide shuttle effect as well as signifi cant Li dendrite formation. 2.2.

- Impact of the DOL/HMPA-6 electrolyte on sulfur cathodes To study how the DOL/HMPA-6 electrolyte affects sulfur cathodes over a charge/discharge cycle, the surface morphology of the sulfur cathode was investigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM).
- Coin-type Li-S cells that contained either the DOL/HMPA-6 or the DOL/ DME electrolyte were used.
- After electrochemical cycling, the sulfur cathode in the cell that contained the DOL/HMPA-6 electrolyte was found to exhibit a porous surface (Fig. 4a) which matched that of its original (pre-cycled) morphology (Fig.
- The morphological feature was ascribed to the presence of HMPA, which, due to its high DN, was expected to facilitate Li2S dissolution [50-52] and maintain the initial porous morphology of the sulfur cathode by facilitating a uniform deposition of Li2S [33].
- In contrast, after electrochemical cycling, the surface of the sulfur cathode exposed to the DOL/DME electrolyte was found to be smooth and compact along with cracks (Fig. 4b).
- Such morphology was attributed to two-dimensional Li2S deposition, which prevented further accessibility to the conductive host and the conduc tive reagents.
- Collectively, the SEM data showed that the DOL/HMPA-6 electrolyte is effective at alleviating surface passivation and enhancing structural integrity of the sulfur cathode during charge/discharge cycling.

- Preparation of the electrolytes: The DOL/DME electrolyte was prepared by dissolving LiTFSI (2.8709 g, 10 mmol) and LiNO3 (0.2622 g, 2 wt%) in a mixture of DOL (5 mL) and DME (5 mL).
- The DOL/HMPA electrolyte was prepared by dissolving LiTFSI (2.8709 g, 10 mmol) and LiNO3 (0.2622 g, 2 wt%) in a mixture of DOL and HMPA (10 mL in total).
- The HMPA loading was 4 vol%, 6 vol%, 8 vol%, or 10 vol% and the resultant electrolytes were designated as DOL/HMPA-4, DOL/HMPA-6, DOL/HMPA-8, and DOL/HMPA-10, respectively.
- To prepare the samples that were characterized using SEM or XPS, the Li anode and the sulfur cathode were isolated after disassembling a tested Li//Li symmetric cell (after 5 charge/discharge cycles) and a tested Li-S cell (after 10 charge/discharge cycles), respectively.
- The Li anode and sulfur cathode were washed with DOL to remove any impurities, followed by drying for 5 h in a glove box.
- NaTFSI was first dissolved in a series of solvents, including acetonitrile (DN=14 kcal mol1), DME (DN=20 kcal mol1), pyridine (DN=33 kcal mol1), dimethyl sulfoxide (DN=30 kcal mol1), triethylamine (DN=55 kcal mol1), and HMPA.
