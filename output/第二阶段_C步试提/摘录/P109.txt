## 实验部分
2.1. Objects studied and justification of their choice Electrochemical studies were performed in airtight stainless steel two-electrode disc cells by own design and production (Swagelok® cells). The sulfur electrodes (d ¼ 2.85 cm, A ¼ 6.38 cm2) used in this study had the following composition: S (99.5%, Acros) - 70% wt., Ketjenblack EC-600JD (Akzo Nobel) - 10% wt., polyethylene oxide (ММ ¼ 4  106, Aldrich) - 20% wt. The surface capacity of sulfur electrodes was 2 mAh/cm2, which is equivalent to the sulfur load about 1.2 mg/cm2. The sulfur content in the positive electrodes (in mg of sulfur per cm2) was determined by thermogravimetry. The theoretical capacity of the positive electrodes (in mAh per cm2) was calculated from the sulfur content in the electrodes. The negative electrodes (d ¼ 2.55 cm, A ¼ 5.10 cm2) were made of lithium foil (99.9%, Russia) with a thickness of 120 mm. The microporous polypropylene Celgard®3501 with a thickness of 25 mm (d ¼ 2.85 cm, A ¼ 6.38 cm2) was used as the separator. Solutions of lithium perchlorate (LiClO4, 99.99% battery grade, Aldrich) in sulfolane (99.8%, GC, Sigma-Aldrich) were studied as the electrolyte solutions. Lithium perchlorate is an effective polysulfide shuttle inhibitor [40]. Sulfolane is a solvent possessing high ionizing capability and well dissolving various lithium salts [41], including lithium polysulfides. It should be noted that sulfolane has a high FlashPoint (>166 C [42]). Gaseous products are not gener- ated at the reaction of sulfolane with metallic lithium. The con- centration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane was 0.1М, 0.5М, 1.0М, 1.5М, 2.0М, 2.4М and 2.8М. The content of electrolytes into lithium- sulfur cells was 4 ml per 1 mAh, which is equivalent to 6.7 ml per 1 mg of sulfur. The electrolyte was introduced into lithium-sulfur cells using a “MICROLITER™Syringe” (Hamilton Company, USA) with a volume of 50 ml. The accuracy of electrolyte dosing into the cells was ±5%. The ionic conductivity and viscosity of highly concentrated so- lutions of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane were measured using techniques similar to those presented previously [41]. All operations for the preparation of electrolyte solutions and assembling of lithium-sulfur cells were performed in a glove box in a dry air atmosphere (the water content did not exceed 10 ppm). Galvanostatic charge and discharge cycling of lithium-sulfur cells were carried out using a BT-05PG battery tester in the voltage range of 1.5e2.8 V at þ 30 C. The current density of the charge and discharge were 0.3 mA/cm2 (0.15C, 250 mA/g(S)). The results of the electrochemical studies were processed using the Data Analyzer program, which is part of the specialized ElChemLab V1© software package. The Data Analyzer program is E.V. Karaseva et al. / Electrochimica Acta 296 (2019) 1102e1114 1103 designed to build charge-discharge voltage profiles and calculate the energy parameters of the battery (e.g., capacity, energy, coulomb efficiency, coefficient of efficiency), the internal resistance of battery, corrosion current density, and the signals from external sensors (e.g., dilatometric, thermometer). The program allows to process files with experimental data presented in a table containing information on the change in time of the current, the voltage on the cell, the potential of the working electrode vs. the reference elec- trode and other information (for example, data from external sensors). The internal resistance of lithium-sulfur cells was calculated by Equation (6) at the specified depth of discharge: R ¼ j Uch  Udch j=ðjichj þ jidchjÞ (6) where: R e internal resistance at the specified depth of discharge, Ohm$cm2; Uch e voltage on the cell at charge at the specified depth of the discharge, V; Udch e voltage on the cell at discharge at the specified depth of the discharge, V; jichj e module of current density of charging, A/cm2; jidchj emodule of current density of discharging, A/cm2. To calculate the resistance value of lithium-sulfur cells at different charge states, a special calculation algorithm was devel- oped, implemented in the ElChemLab V1© software package developed by us. When developing the calculation algorithm, we studied the change in the self-discharge current depending on the degree of charge of lithium-sulfur cells and found that the self- discharge current in the low voltage charge stage is substantially lower than at the high voltage stage. When calculating the resis- tance of lithium-sulfur cells, the mean values of self-discharge currents at the low voltage and high voltage stages were calcu- lated from the difference in the charge and discharge capacities at the low voltage and high voltage areas from the values of which the loss of the charging capacity was calculated for the low voltage and high voltage charging stages of lithium-sulfur cells. For a unit of DoD (100% DoD), the experimentally obtained discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells was taken. The correctness of this approach for calculating the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells was confirmed by independent impedance studies. - Introduction Lithium-sulfur batteries are batteries with a liquid cathode, since the electroactive materials of the positive electrode (sulfur and intermediate products of its electrochemical reduction, lithium polysulfides) are soluble in the electrolyte [1e7].
- By chemical nature, lithium polysulfides (Li2Sn) are salts of polysulfane acids (H2Sn) [8].
- Lithium polysulfides, dis- solved in aprotic dipolar solvents, can undergo heterolytic (elec- trolytic) [9e11] (Equations (1) and (2)) and homolytic dissociation [12e22] (Equations (3) and (4)).
- The heterolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides, which can occur both by the first and second stages, produces charged parti- cles (lithium cations and polysulfide anions): Li2Sn4Liþ þ LiSn (1) LiS n 4Liþ þ Sn2 (2) At electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides e Li2Sn, the bond of Li-S breaks with the formation of lithium cations and mono- or dianions of polysulfides (Equations (1) and (2)).

- In the case of lithium polysulfides, the most stable radical is S3-, whose formation is shown in many works, for example [23].
- Homolytic dissociation of Li2Sn can occur as in polar and as in low polar solvents [15,16,19].
- Low polar solvents stabilize the low- ordered lithium polysulfides, and the high polar ones stabilize the high-ordered lithium polysulfides and the anion radical S3-.
- High polar and high donor solvents promote homolytic dissociation to form the radical anion S3-.
- The radical species of lithium polysulfides, similarly to the mo- lecular ones, can undergo electrolytic dissociation (Equation (5)): (5) As a result of the liability of lithium polysulfides to undergo heterolytic (electrolytic) and homolytic dissociation, electrolyte solutions generally simultaneously contain a lot of diverse molec- ular, radical, ion radical and ionic species of sulfur with various number of atoms that exist in dynamic equilibrium.
- Solutions of lithium polysulfides in aprotic dipolar solvents have high ionic conductivity that is comparable to that of readily ioniz- able lithium salts [24,25].
- For example, the 0.3m solution of Li2S6 in sulfolane has conductivity of 0.987 mS/cm and viscosity of 30 mPa s and 0.5m solution of LiClO4 in sulfolane has conductivity of 1.897 mS/cm and viscosity of 19 mPa s [26].
- This indicates that lithium polysulfides form inter- molecular associates in solutions [24e26].
- In support of arguments on suppression of electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides, it is also possible to bring the fact that there is no synergetic effect in the specific conductivity of mixed solutions of lithium polysulfides and lithium perchlorate [26].
- Furthermore, the support lithium salts, present in electrolytes, result in cationic linking of polysulfide anions [24], which impairs the transport properties of electrolyte systems and the segmental mobility of sulfur atoms in polysulfide chains.
- The effect of lithium polysulfides on the properties of electrolyte solutions has been studied in a number of works [25e29].
- For example, the effect of the concentration of lithium salts on the performance of lithium-sulfur cells was studied in a number of publications [6,30e39].
- The purpose of this work is to clarify the mechanism by which the concentration of support salts in electrolytes affects the per- formance of lithium-sulfur cells, such as the depth of electro- chemical reduction of sulfur, the rate of capacity depletion and the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells. 3.
- Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane on the charge-discharge voltage profiles of lithium-sulfur cells Studies have shown that the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane affects the charge-discharge voltage pro- files of lithium-sulfur cells (Fig.1а).
- As an exception, the discharge voltage profiles of the cells with a high- concentration electrolyte containing 2.8М LiClO4 manifest only one high voltage plateau.
- However, as lithium-sulfur cells with the high-concentrated electrolyte (2.8М LiClO4) are cycled, a low voltage plateau appears on the charge and discharge voltage pro- files, and both the charge and discharge capacities increase (Fig. 1b and c).
- As the concentration of lithium perchlorate in the electrolyte solution increases, the discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells first increases and then starts to decrease at salt concentrations in the electrolyte close to 1M (Fig. 2).
- As the lithium perchlorate concen- tration in the electrolyte increases, the voltages of the high voltage plateaus decrease, whereas those of the low voltage plateaus pass through a maximum (Fig. 3a).
- The voltage of the high voltage stage was determined as the voltage in the middle of the first section of the high voltage plateau on the discharge curve, which usually corresponded to 100 mAh/g(S).
- The voltage of the low voltage stage of the discharge was assumed to be equal to the value of the voltage at the maximum, which in most cases corresponded to the middle of the low voltage discharge plateau (Fig. 3b). 3.2.
- Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane on capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling The general regularities of charge and discharge capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling weakly depend on the support salt concentration, except for lithium-sulfur cells con- taining high-concentrated electrolytes (2.4М and 2.8М LiClO4) (Fig. 4).
- At moderate support salt concentrations in the electrolyte (0.5e2.0М), the discharge capacity first quickly decreases during the cycling, then the capacity decrease slows down, and then the capacity starts to decrease abruptly again.
- Of special interest is the dependence of capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells with the electrolyte containing 2.4М LiClO4 on cycle number.
- The cells with the electrolyte containing 2.4М LiClO4 feature the smallest rate of depletion in both the charge and discharge capacities during cycling.
- As lithium-sulfur cells with the high-concentration electrolyte (2.8М LiClO4) are cycled, the charge and discharge capacities in- crease and the maximum capacity values are reached by about the 40th cycle (Fig. 4).

- At the initial stages of cycling (first 10e30 cycles), the main contribution to the reduction in capacity is made by the displacement of sulfur onto the face of the sulfur electrode.
- It should be mentioned that all of the factors listed above act simultaneously, but at different stages of the cycling, their contri- bution to the overall depletion in capacity is different. 3.3.

- Discussion Three aspects should be kept in mind in a consideration of the mechanisms of the effect of the concentration of support salts on the discharge capacity and cycling performance of lithium-sulfur cells:  species of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions;  the association-dissociation equilibria in electrolyte solutions containing lithium salts and lithium polysulfides;  the processes of solvation of lithium ions contained in lithium polysulfides and support salts. 4.1.

- On the surface of sulfur and lithium electrodes, various species of lithium polysulfides (di-anions, anions, radicals, and neutral species) and support salts participate in electrochemical and chemical reactions. 4.3.
- Dissolution of lithium polysulfides in the electrolyte solution results in binding of solvent molecules in solvate shells of lithium ions contained in lithium polysulfides (Equations (8), (12) and (15)).
- Karaseva et al. / Electrochimica Acta 296 (2019) 1102e1114 1108 elementary sulfur is completely reduced to lithium tetrasulfide.
- Electrochemical reduction of lithium tetrasulfide is not accompa- nied by the increase in the concentration of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions (Equations (16) and (18)).
- The increase in the concentration of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions increases their viscosity and decreases the ionic conductivity [25].
- As a result, the viscosity of elec- trolyte solutions somewhat decreases and the ionic conductivity increases [49].
- Equations (8), (12) and (15) make it possible to calculate the number of solvent molecules required for the solvation of lithium tetrasulfide, as well as the molar ratio of sulfur and solvent ensuring complete reduction of sulfur.
- Studies that we performed previously [45] have shown that the solvate number of lithium ion in lithium tetrasulfide in sulfolane solutions is 2.
- In this case, the solvate number of lithium tetrasulfide is 4, while the molar ratio of the solvent to elementary sulfur is 1:1.
- To estimate the possible depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur in the high voltage plateau, we performed calculations on the effect of average solvate number of lithium ion and support salt concentration on the number of free solvent molecules that can solvate the lithium ions in lithium tetrasulfide formed (Table 1, Equations S.1 & S.2 are given in Supplementary materials).

- In other words, in the case of sulfolane, the minimum required value of solvate number is 2.

- For example, at a salt concentration of 2.0M, the amount of free mol- ecules of sulfolane in the electrolyte is 5.7M.
- This amount of free sulfolane molecules is sufficient to fully solvate lithium ions in the system if the solvate number of the lithium ion is 2.

- The internal resistance of lithium- sulfur cells is composed of three constituents: the electrolyte resistance plus the charge transfer resistance on the positive and negative electrodes (Equation (22)): Rcell ¼ Rel þ RLi þ RS (22) where: Rcell is the DC resistance of a lithium-sulfur cell, Ohm,cm2; Rel is resistance of the electrolyte solution, Ohm,cm2; RLi is the charge transfer resistance on the lithium electrode, Ohm,cm2; and RS is the charge transfer resistance on the sulfur electrode, Ohm,cm2.
- The dependence of the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells on the depth of discharge is a curve with a complex shape where three regions corresponding to different discharge depths can be distin- guished (Fig. 7a).
- It is convenient to use the results of impedance studies [49] to analyze the contributions of various components to the total resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at various discharge depths.
- As impedance studies have shown, at small discharge depths of 5e15% (1st region) the main contributions to the resis- tance of lithium-sulfur cells are made by the charge transfer resistance on the positive electrode in the electrochemical reduc- tion of high-ordered lithium polysulfides (Li2Sn; n > 4) and by the resistance of passivating layers on the electrodes.
- Therefore, at depth of discharge of lithium-sulfur cells about 25e35%, the increase in their resistance is observed, with the main contribution coming from the resistance of the electrolyte solution.
- Therefore, the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at depths of discharge of 60e80% is determined more considerably by the charge transfer resistance in the reduction of low-ordered lithium polysulfides (Li2Sn; n < 4) and the resistance of passivating layers on the sulfur electrode than by the electrolyte resistance and the charge transfer resistance on the lithium electrode.
- Thus, the effect of the salt concentration on the reactivity of lithium polysulfides can be determined from the resistances in the minimum in the 1st and 3rd regions of the dependence of resistance on the depth of discharge of lithium-sulfur cells.

- In high-concentrated electrolyte so- lutions (with LiClO4 concentrations above 1.5M) the coulomb effi- ciency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells is higher than in less concentrated solutions and remains nearly unchanged during cycling.
- High values of coulomb efficiency (more than 100%) in the high concentrated electrolyte (2.8M LiClO4) are explained by the fact that the experimentally determined discharge capacity of lithium- sulfur cells is greater than the charge capacity of the cells.
- The increase in the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium- sulfur cells with the increase in concentration of support salt is most likely due to the slowness of interelectrode sulfur transfer due to the increase in the viscosity of electrolyte solutions [50].
