## 实验部分
Materials: 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether (TTE) (98%) and 9,10-dimethylanthracene were purchased from Energy Chemical Co., Ltd. 1,2-Dimethoxyethane (CP > 99.5%) and lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulphonyl) imide were purchased from Aladdin Co., Ltd. 1.0 m LiTFSI in DOL: DME = 1:1 vol% with 1.0% LiNO3 (LS002) was purchased from DoDoChem. Co., Ltd. Preparation Methods—Preparation of TDE Electrolyte: TDE electrolyte was obtained by simply mixing the TTE and 2 m LiTFSI/DME (with 0.1 m LiNO3) electrolyte in 4:1 by volume. Preparation of Nanoconductive Carbon-Coated Cellulose Nonwoven: the CS-coated separator was prepared by mixing Super P and PVDF binder in mass ratio of 1:1 using N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) as solvent to form a uniform slurry, then the slurry was casting on the cellulose nonwoven using doctor’s blade, then dry at 60 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 h before use. Preparation of Bare Sulfur Cathode: Bare sulfur cathode was prepared by mixing sulfur, Super P, and PVDF binder in mass ratio of 6:3:1 using NMP as solvent to form the cathode slurry, then the slurry was casting on the aluminum foil by doctor’s blade, then dry at 60 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 h before use. The sulfur mass loading of ≈1.2 and ≈4 mg cm-2 was prepared in the same way. All the batteries are tested using LAND CT2001A system in the voltage range of 1.8 V-2.8 V. Preparation of Li2S6 solution: sublimate sulfur and lithium sulfide were mixed in mole ratio of 5:1, then add in DME solvent and heated at 80 °C for 6 h. Characterization: The morphology of samples was characterized by scanning electron microscope (SEM Hitachi S-4800). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was carried out using Perkin- Elmer PHI 550 spectrometer equipped with Ar+ sputtering gum using Al Kα source. Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectroscopy (TOF- SIMS) measurement (IONTOF GmbH, Münster, Germany) with Cs+ sputtering. The in situ electrochemistry optical microscopy test was carried out using Cossim CMY-400Z optical microscopy. The cell mold was customized and as shown in Scheme 1. Liner Sweep Voltammetry: Li/electrolyte/stainless steel cells were assembled in the CR-2032 type coin cell, the Li metal as the counter electrode and reference electrode, the stainless steel as the working electrode, the scan rate was 0.1 mV s-1. Ionic Conductivity Measurement: the ionic conductivity was calculated by the equation of σ = l/SR, the σ is the ionic conductivity, the l is the thickness of separator, S is the electrode area, R is the impedance of electrolyte. the impedance was measured by the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), stainless steel/electrolyte/stainless steel cells were assembled in the CR-2032 type coin cell, the frequency range was from 7 MHz to 100 mHz with the amplitude of 10 mV. Lithium Transfer Number Measurement: Li/electrolyte/Li cells were assembled in the form of CR-2032 coin cells, the Li+ transfer number (tLi+) of electrolyte was calculated by the Bruce-Vincent-Evans equation: t = Is/I0 × (V - I0R0)/(V - IsRs). The I0 and Is are obtained via chronoamperometry (CA) under the voltage of 10 mV, R0 and Rs is the initial interface resistance and stable interfacial resistance after the CA process.

- Introduction Sulfur is a promising cathode material for the next-generation energy storage batteries due to its high theoretical capacity of 1675 mAh g-1.[1] When coupled with lithium metal anode (theoretical capacity of 3860 mAh g-1), theoretical capacity Conventional lithium-sulfur batteries often suffer from fatal problems such as high flammability, polysulfide shuttling, and lithium dendrites growth.
- Here, highly-safe lithium-sulfur batteries based on flame-retardant electrolyte (dimethoxyether/1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether) coupled with functional separator (nanoconductive carbon-coated cellulose nonwoven) to resolve aforementioned bottle-neck issues are demonstrated.
- In addition, Li/Li symmetrical cells using such flame-retardant electrolyte deliver extraordinary long- term cycling stability (less than 10 mV overpotential) for over 2500 h at 1.0 mA cm-2 and 1.0 mAh cm-2.
- Moreover, bare sulfur cathode-based lithium-sulfur batteries using this flame retardant electrolyte coupled with nanoconductive carbon-coated cellulose separator can retain 83.6% discharge capacity after 200 cycles at 0.5 C.
- Under high charge/discharge rate (4 C), lithium-sulfur cells still show high charge/discharge capacity of ≈350 mAh g-1.
- Even at an elevated temperature of 60 °C, discharge capacity of 870 mAh g-1 can be retained.
- More importantly, high-loading bare sulfur cathode (4 mg cm-2)-based lithium-sulfur batteries can also deliver high charge/discharge capacity over 806 mAh g-1 after 56 cycles.
- Undoubtedly, the strategy of flame retardant electrolyte coupled with carbon-coated separator enlightens highly safe lithium-sulfur batteries at a wide range of temperature. of lithium-batteries can reach up to 2600 Wh kg-1, which is much higher than that of conventional lithium ion batteries.[2] Unfortunately, there are still several problems need to be solved.
- For the cathode side, polysulfide can dissolve into ether-based electrolyte and then shut- tling to anode side, reacting with lithium metal anode and eventually leading to irreversible capacity fading and low Cou- lombic efficiency (CE).[3] For the lithium metal anode side, uneven Li+ platting/ stripping easily leads to the rapid growth of lithium dendrites, which can easily pen- etrate separator and causing internal short circuit.
- Lithium dendrites and fragile elec- trolyte/electrode interface could detach from lithium metal anode to form “dead Li,” more electrolyte would be consumed and the lithium utilization is reduced.[4] For electrolyte, conventional ether-based electrolytes are easily volatile and highly flammable,[5] which is detrimental for safety of lithium-sulfur batteries.
- To address the problem of lithium polysulfide shuttling, various strategies have been explored, including using modified structure sulfur host[6] and using ionic liquid electrolyte,[7] etc.
- To find out the solution to a dendrite free lithium metal anode, researchers have done many attempts such as design special architecture lithium metal anode,[8] pre-formed solid electrolyte interface,[9] modified separators[10] and etc.
- To restrain the dendrite growth and prevent polysulfide shuttling simultaneously, most of the researches focused on the solid or quasi-solid state electrolyte.[6f] Armand et al.[11] studied solid state polymer electrolytes (SPEs) using poly (ethylene oxide) (PEO) as polymer matrix, lithium-sulfur batteries using PEO-based SPE exhibits high specific discharge capacity of 1395 mAh g-1.
- He et al.[12] developed solid state lithium-sulfur batteries using Li1.5Al0.5Ge1.5(PO4)3 (LAGP) as the electrolyte, under the elevated temperature of 55 °C and the current density of 20 mA g-1, a capacity of 1524 mAh g-1 could be obtained.

- Flame retardant electrolyte has attracted wide interest as a new research hotspot, Wang et al.[13] added tris(2,2,2-trif- luoroethyl) phosphite as the flame retardant additive into the conventional carbonate electrolyte to obtain the flame inhib- iting electrolyte, which enables the sulfur@PAN composite cathode lithium-sulfur battery superior long cycle stability and rate performance (800 mAh g-1 at 10 C).
- Continues study was carried on and they proposed an intrinsic flame retardant electrolyte using flame-retardant tri- ethyl phosphate and high flashpoint 1H,1H,5H-octafluoro- pentyl-1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl ether (TTE) as cosolvent,[14] the lithium-sulfur batteries delivers high discharge capacity and stable cycle performance.
- Hexafluorocyclotriphosphazene as a kind of flame retardant additive was introduced into conven- tional 1,3-dioxolane (DOL)/dimethoxyether (DME) electro- lyte for lithium-sulfur batteries,[15] but the cycle performance is not stable enough.
- Recently, Wang et al.[16] reported a kind of localized high concentration electrolyte composed by the inert fluoroalkyl ether of TTE and highly concentrated lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl) imide/DME, the high flash point and inert nature of the fluoroalkyl ether makes the electrolyte hard to be ignited.
- The discharge capacity of 775 mAh g-1 was obtained, but the rate performance and cathode sulfur loading needs to be further improved.
- What is more, the batteries using TDE electrolyte could maintain a stable cycle performance at high temperature (60 °C).
- To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work of flame retardant electrolyte reported for the high areal capacity bare sulfur cathode-based lithium-sulfur battery so far. 2.
- Results and Discussion 2.1.
- Physical and Chemical Properties TDE electrolyte was prepared by simply mixing 2 m LiTFSI/ DME (with 0.1 m LiNO3, all of the following mentioned con- tain the same amount) with TTE in different volume ratio.
- Due to the good compatibility of TTE and DME, the as prepared TDE electrolyte shows a uniform and transparent character- istic (Figure 1a).
- According to the flame retardant test shows in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), when the volume ratio of TTE in the mixture solvent is higher than 80% (TTE:2 m LiTFSI/DME = 4:1), spray formed by the TDE electrolyte can effectively extinguish the burning paper roll, however, with the volume ratio of inert TTE increasing, the ionic conduc- tivity is decreasing, thus the volume ratio of TTE: 2 m LiTFSI/ DME = 4:1 is chosen for the flame retardant TDE electrolyte (the concentration of LiNO3 in the TDE electrolyte is around 0.025 m).
- As shown in Figure 1b, TDE electrolyte exhibits the viscosity of 1.091 mPa s, lower than pristine 2 m LiTFSI/DME and conventional electrolyte (1 m LiTFSI DOL/DME with 1 wt% LiNO3, denoted as LS002 in the following section).
- Raman spectroscopy (Figure 1c) was used to investigate the solvate structure and the coordination between Li+ and solvent.
- With the increasing of LiTFSI concentration in DME, the peak of COC located around 820-830 cm-1 is weakened, peak intensity of interaction between Li+ and O atom at around 870-880 cm-1 is growing, indicating the interaction between Li+ and O atom is getting stronger.
- However, no matter what concentration of DME is mixed with TTE in volume ration of 1:4, the COC peak of TTE around 830-840 cm-1 is hardly changed in intensity and location, demonstrated that the TTE molecular barely binds with Li+.
- As can be seen from Figure S2 (Supporting Information), the band at 1246.7 cm-1 is attributed to aggregated LiTFSI, the band at 1243.1 cm-1 is attributed to free TFSI-.
- Both 1 m LiTFSI/DME and 2 m LiTFSI/DME shows the same TFSI- Raman shift at 1243.1 cm-1, which means the solvate structure of 1 m and 2 m LiTFSI/DME is the same.
- After blended 2 m LiTFSI/DME with TTE in the volume ratio of 1:4 to get TDE electrolyte, the band of TFSI- shifts towards higher wave number at 1244.8 cm-1, this is very close to the TFSI- peak position of 5 m LiTFSI/DME, which means the contact ion pair is formed.
- The above experimental results show that after blend 2 m LiTFSI/DME with TTE, the interaction between DME, Li+, and TFSI- is enhanced (Figure 1d).
- The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) plots of Stainless steel/Stainless steel cells using TDE, LS002 electrolyte, and 2 m LiTFSI/DME electrolyte at room temperature is pre- sented in Figure 2a, ionic conductivity of TDE (0.990 mS cm-1) is slightly lower than that of the other two comparisons, and this can be attributed to the lower salt concentration (0.4 m LiTFSI with 0.025 m LiNO3) in the TDE electrolyte.
- Figure 2b shows the electrochemical stability window of the 2 m LiTFSI DME, LS002 electrolyte, and TDE electrolyte, the decompo- sition voltage of TDE electrolyte starts at 4.7 V versus Li/Li+, and it is stable enough for the lithium-sulfur battery.

- Li-S Battery Performance Li/S cells with bare sulfur cathode using TDE and LS002 electro- lyte were assembled respectively (sulfur loading of ≈1.2 mg cm-2, equals to areal capacity at around 2 mAh cm-2), as shown in Figure 6a, all the batteries were charged/discharged at 0.5 C (equals to current density at around 1 mA cm-2).
- The lower solvation ability to the polysulfide could lower the utilization of sulfur,[21] thus the bare sulfur cathode-based Li/S batteries using TDE electrolyte shows relatively lower dis- charge capacity (≈200 mAh g-1 at 0.5 C) than LS002 electrolyte.
- But it also brought some positive effects, the cycling stability of TDE electrolyte-based Li/S batteries is improved, the discharge capacity keeps almost unchanged over 900 cycles.
- Nanoconductive carbon-layer-coated separator (denoted as CS) could further suppress the polysulfide shuttling, furthermore, nanoconductive carbon layer could act as the polysulfide scavenger by provide more redox reaction sites,[22] thus, the utilization of sulfur can be improved.
- To fully improve the battery’s cycle stability and performance, CS separator with the coating layer thickness of 10 um were utilized in Li/S batteries (Figure S6, Supporting Information).
- As shown in Figure 6a, batteries using TDE electrolyte coupled with CS separator showed much higher discharge capacity (first dis- charge capacity 903 mAh g-1 at 0.5 C) than cells using TDE electrolyte alone.
- When coupled with CS separator, batteries using LS002 electrolyte showed slightly higher discharge capacity than batteries using TDE electrolyte during the first 200 cycles.
- This is because higher amount of polysulfide dissolved into LS002 electrolyte and captured by nanoconductive carbon coating, thus the sulfur utilization is significantly improved in a short term.
- However, after around 200 cycling, the leading edge of the LS002 electrolyte disap- peared.
- After 1000 cycles, batteries using TDE electrolyte coupled with CS separator shows 49.4% capacity retention, on the contrary, batteries using LS002 electrolyte coupled with CS separator is only 23.7% of the initial capacity remaining.
- Large amount of polysulfide dissolved out from cathode in cells using LS002 electrolyte, excess polysulfide can still pass through the nanoconductive carbon coating layer to reach the lithium metal anode, leading to the fast capacity fading.
- What’s more, TDE electrolyte helps the lithium metal anode forming a dendrite- free interface, thus the cells using TDE electrolyte is more stable than cells using LS002 electrolyte (Figure 7).

- After several cycles of charge-discharge, the poly- sulfides dissolved into the electrolyte to form the “catholytes,” during the resting period of lithium-sulfur batteries, the as formed catholytes react with lithium metal anode spontane- ously, causing self-discharge and resulting in voltage drop and irreversible capacity loss.[23] Due to the high solubility of LS002 electrolyte to the polysulfide, the severe shuttling effect can cause serious self-discharge, OCV of cells using conventional LS002 electrolyte shows an obvious decay from 2.35 to 2.15 V, which indicates severe capacity loss during resting (Figure S7, Supporting Information).
- It is noteworthy that the utilization of TDE electrolyte improves the resistance of self-discharge, after charged to the cutoff voltage of 2.8 V, the cells were resting for 72 hours, during the resting, cells using TDE electrolyte shows stable open cutoff voltage (OCV) at around 2.3 V without obvious decay.
- TDE electrolyte/CS separator-based batteries presents superior rate performance (Figure 6d), the capacity maintains 350 mAh g-1 even at the discharge rate of 4 C (equals to cur- rent density of 6.7 mA cm-2 at the sulfur loading of 1 mg cm-2).
- After 4C charge/discharge, the discharge rate turns back to 0.5 C, and the discharge capacity can still maintains about 900 mAh g-1.
- In order to verify the stability of the electrolyte under extreme operating conditions, batteries using TDE and LS002 electrolytes were cycled at 60 °C (Figure 6e).
- TDE-CS separator- based batteries electrolyte shows initial discharge capacity of 1557 mAh g-1, close to theoretical capacity, and after 100 cycles the capacity of batteries still remains 870 mAh g-1.
- Bat- teries using LS002 electrolyte coupled with CS separator shows initial discharge capacity of 1671.5 mAh g-1, then the capacity is sharply declined.
- The SEM image of Li metal anode in the Li/S cells after 10 cycles at 60 °C is shown in Figure 6f,g, the Li metal anode cycled in LS002 electrolyte exhibits a porous, cracked surface.
- TDE-CS separator-based batteries exhibit excellent stability and discharge capacity at 60 °C, which is due to opti- mized electrode-electrolyte interface.
- To meet the requirements for practical application, higher areal sulfur mass loading of over 4 mg cm-2 cathode was employed, the long term cycling performance is shown in Figure 6h.
- The first discharge capacity of Li/S batteries using TDE electrolyte coupled with CS separator could reach up to 1187 mAh g-1, after 56 cycles, discharge capacity of 806 mAh g-1 remained, CE is higher than 97%.
