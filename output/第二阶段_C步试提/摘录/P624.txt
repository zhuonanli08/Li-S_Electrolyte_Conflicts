- Therefore, designing electrolytes capable of adaptively adjusting solvating forces in response to changes in the electrostatic interactions of Li2Sn is imperative for practical Li-S batteries.
- Herein, we propose an electrostatic interaction force matching concept that utilize high polarizability solvents with dynamical solvating power to accommodate the fluctuation in electrostatic interactions of Li2Sn.
- Molecules with conjugated structure exhibit significantly delocalized electron cloud, which is capable of responding in real time to the stimulus of local-electric-field variations from Sn 2- to enable charge-density-dependent solvation regulation of Li2Sn.
- Weakly or strongly polarized solvents induced by Sn 2- with different charge-density reduce the solubility of long-chain Li2Sn (n > 4) by over 50% and enable a magnitude-level increase in the solubility of short-chain Li2Sn (n ≤4), promoting the reaction kinetic and pre venting the accumulation of Li2Sn.
- Consequently, optimized Li-S batteries achieve 406.2 Wh/kg in 3 Ah-level pouch cells with E/S ratio of 3 mL/gsulfur.
- The findings deepen the understanding of polarizable systems and provide an innovative electrolyte design principle. 1.
- Among emerging solutions, Li-S batteries stand out due to their remarkable theoretical energy density of 2600 Wh/kg and abundant raw material reserves, positioning them as a promising next-generation energy storage system [1-4].
- However, their practical energy density and cycling stability are severely restricted by sluggish reactions kinetics and shuttle effect, especially at lean electro lyte conditions [5-7].
- It has been demonstrated that regulations of electrolyte solvating forces have the capacity of optimizing the solvation and reactivity of Li2Sn and addressing above issues [8-10].
- Conventional electrolytes with strong solvating forces can enhance the dispersion of short-chain Li2Sn with strong electrostatic interactions and improve re action kinetics, while those with weak solvating forces can confine the long-chain Li2Sn, which are prone to diffusion, on the cathode side to prevent shuttling [11-14].
- Although these conventional electrolytes can address part issues in Li-S batteries, their solvating forces fail to adapt to the variations in electrostatic interactions of Li2Sn and achieve force-matching with all Li2Sn, which may exacerbate other issues: highly solvating electrolytes exacerbate the Li2Sn shuttle effect, while sparingly solvating electrolytes aggravate the sluggish reaction kinetics.
- It’s difficult for conventional electrolytes to simultaneously ensure the cycling stability and the improvement of energy density of Li-S batteries [19-21].
- Detailed analysis of the properties of Li2Sn is essential to elucidate the fundamental reasons behind the bidirectional regulation re quirements and design electrolytes that address the inherent contra dictions in Li-S batteries.
