## 正文（含数字的句子与图表注）
- Energy Mater. 2025, 8, 1796-1802 Read Online ACCESS Metrics & More Article Recommendations * sı Supporting Information ABSTRACT: A novel high-donor 3-fluoropyridine (3FPy) electro- lyte has been introduced for use in Li-S batteries, demonstrating an inhibition effect on the polysulfide shuttle, even without the addition of LiNO3.
- In this study, fluoropyridine electrolytes, including 2-fluoropyridine (2FPy) and 3FPy electrolytes, are studied using electrochemical analysis, mass spectrometry (MS), and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) methods.
- Collision-induced dissociation spectra revealed that Li+ preferen- tially solvates with different fluoropyridines, with 2FPy exhibiting a stronger interaction due to ortho-fluorine’s influence, compared to 4FPy and 3FPy.
- However, MS and HPLC analyses showed that 2FPy is reactive with polysulfides, while 3FPy offers high solubility for polysulfides and sulfur without reacting with them at room temperature.
- Despite 3FPy performing well at room temperature, further electrochemistry studies at elevated (60 °C) and reduced (0 °C) temperatures reveal the challenges.
- At high temperatures, LiNO3 is essential to suppress the polysulfide shuttle; and at low temperatures, the performance with the 3FPy electrolyte significantly lags behind that of the ether-based electrolyte.
- KEYWORDS: Li-S battery, HPLC, ESI/MS, CID spectra, fluorinated pyridine electrolyte 1.
- However, it is rarely used in the studies of lithium-based rechargeable batteries because of its chemical reactivity with lithium metal.1 Interestingly, despite this chemical incompatibility, fluorinated pyridines have been introduced into the lithium-based rechargeable batteries by some researchers, either as electrolyte solvents or as additives.1-10 For example, Abudula et al. reported the use of 2-fluoropyridine (2FPy) and 3-fluoropyridine (3FPy) as electrolyte solvents and additives to develop dendrite-free lithium metal batteries with a commercial NMC cathode.
- Recently, Choi et al.25 introduced a dual-functional high-donor electrolyte formulated with 3FPy as a solvent for Li-S batteries.
- Through solvation tests and XPS analysis, they found that the high solubility of polysulfides and the high LiF content in the SEI layer were key factors contributing to the excellent cyclability of the Li-S battery with very high sulfur loading, even under LiNO3-free and lean electrolyte conditions.
- Moreover, based on the calculated DNs of 2-, 3-, and 4-fluoropyridine (2FPy, 3FPy, and 4FPy), they attributed the high solubility of polysulfide in 3FPy with its high DN.25 2.
- RESULTS AND DISCUSSION The DN of a solvent indicates its basicity, primarily influencing the solvation of cations in the electrolyte.26,27 However, the DN alone has limitations in evaluating the solvation preference.

- For example, a pyridine molecule and a deuterated pyridine (d5Py) molecule combine to form the lithiated adduct of [Py + Li + d5Py]+.
- This lithiated adduct is then subjected to collision-induced dissociation (CID) with argon, producing fragments [Py + Li]+ and [d5Py + Li]+.
- In the case of [Py + Li + d5Py]+, since both Py and d5Py have equal interactions with Li+, the fragment ions [Py + Li]+ and [d5Py + Li]+ show the same relative abundance in the CID spectra (Figure 1A and Table 1).
- For the lithiated adducts of Py and different methylpyridines (including 2-methylpyridine (2MePy), 3-methylpyridine (3MePy), and 4-methylpyridine (4MePy)), the CID spectra for [Py + Li + 2MePy]+, [Py + Li + 3MePy]+, and [Py + Li + 4MePy]+ (Figure 1B-D) reveal that the electron-donating effect of the methyl group on the pyridine ring increases the Lewis basicity of three methylpyr- idines compared to pure pyridine.
- As a result, the relative abundances of [2MePy + Li]+, [3MePy + Li]+, and [4MePy + Li]+ are all higher than that of [Py + Li]+, indicating a stronger interaction between Li+ and methylpyridine in the solvation shell.
- This is reflected in the CID spectra, where the relative abundances of [2FPy + Li]+, [3FPy + Li]+, and [4FPy + Li]+ (Figure 1E-G and Table 1) are lower than that of [Py + Li]+, indicating a weaker interaction between Li+ and fluoropyridine in the solvation shell.
- A key observation from the CID spectra of different lithiated adducts (Figure 1 and Table 1) is that 2FPy has a stronger interaction with Li+ than 3FPy when both are referenced to a pyridine solvent.
- According to calculations by Choi et al.,25 the DNs for 2FPy and 3FPy are 15.8 and 29.7 kcal mol-1, respectively, suggesting that 3FPy should exhibit a stronger interaction with Li+ than 2FPy.
- However, this prediction is inconsistent with the CID data (Table 1), which shows that a relative abundance of [2FPy + Li]+ is 55.1% (compared to the abundance of [Py + Li]+) and the relative abundance of [3FPy + Li]+ is only 9.6%.
- In their study of the Li2S affinities with polymer binders containing different functional groups, Cui et al.29 showed that Li+ interacts favorably with fluorine in the fluoropolymer based on ab initio simulations.
- This insight may explain the stronger interaction of Li+ with 2FPy.
- In 2FPy, the fluorine atom is positioned close to the nitrogen atom in the pyridine ring, possibly enhancing the Li+ interaction with nitrogen through a synergistic effect, even though fluorine’s electron-withdrawing nature slightly reduces the electron density on nitrogen (Scheme S-1).
- In contrast, the fluorine atom in 3FPy is positioned farther from the nitrogen atom in pyridine, lacking this synergistic effect.
- The electrochemical performance of Li-S batteries with two distinct electrolytes (1 M LiTFSi/2FPy and 1 M LiTFSi/3FPy, both with the LiNO3 additive) was further studied (Figures 2, S-1, and S-2).
- Cycling stability tests indicate that both electrolytes exhibit a comparable capacity retention, with only 50-60% of the original capacity retained after 50 cycles at 0.05C.
- However, rate performance tests (Figure S-2) demonstrated that the Li-S battery with the 2FPy electrolyte outperforms that with the 3FPy electrolyte at a higher rate.
- Detailed analysis of the cyclic voltammograms (CVs) and charge/discharge profiles for the Li-S batteries with the two electrolytes (Figure 2A,B) reveals a distinct sulfur redox behavior.
- Specifically, the discharge profile of the Li-S battery with the 3FPy electrolyte showed an extended high discharge plateau (corresponding to the reduction of elemental sulfur to long-chain polysulfides) and a reduced low discharge plateau (representing the reduction of long-chain to short-chain polysulfides and sulfides) relative to the 2FPy electrolyte.

- In both Py and 3FPy solvents (Figure 3), [LiSn -] species such as S5 2-, S6 2-, S7 2-, S8 2-, and S9 2-are present in very high relative abundances and exhibit similar distributions, except for a notably higher relative abundance of S3 -in the Py solvent.

- However, a detailed quantitative analysis of chromatographic peak intensities (Table S-1) shows small but significant differences in the polysulfide distribution between Py and 3FPy solvents.
- Using S6 2-as the center point, the Py solvent displayed higher intensities of the medium-chain-length polysulfide species (S3 2-, S4 2-, S5 2-) compared to 3FPy, while the 3FPy solvent showed greater intensities of the long- chain-length polysulfide species (S7 2-, S8 2-, S9 2-) than in the Py solvent.
- These differences likely reflect the distinct solvation capabilities of Py and 3FPy; in the Py solvent, the stabilization of S3 -radicals appears to shift the polysulfide equilibrium toward shorter chains, whereas in 3FPy, the stabilization of longer polysulfide chains favors a shift toward extended polysulfide species.
- The ESI/MS spectrum of a 15 mM Li2S6 solution in 2FPy (Figure 3B) reveals a distinct profile compared with the spectra for the same solution in 3FPy and Py.

- This observation suggests a possible reaction or interaction between 2FPy and polysulfides.
- HPLC analysis further supports this hypothesis; at least four new chromatographic peaks appear that cannot be assigned to methylated polysulfides (CH3SnCH3) (Figure 4B).
- Additionally, HPLC- (+)ESI/MS analysis of the methylated polysulfide mixture in 2FPy (Figure S-15) confirms that two of these new chromatographic peaks (Figure 4B) correspond to Py-S-CH3 and Py-S2-CH3.
- It is well established that in pyridine rings, nucleophilic aromatic substitution primarily occurs at the ortho (o) or para (p) position, while electrophilic substitution typically targets the meta (m) position.31 Although fluorine substitution in the pyridine ring may reduce the reactivity of 2FPy, the nucleophilic substitution of fluorine cannot be entirely prevented when strong nucleophiles, such as polysulfides, are present.
- The presence of Py-S-CH3 and Py-S2-CH3 in the HPLC chromatogram provides evidence for the formation of Py-S-and Py-S2 -, which are likely products of the nucleophilic substitution reaction between 2FPy and polysulfides.
- Further reexamining the ESI/MS spectrum of the polysulfide mixture in 2FPy (Figure 3B) reveals low-abundant ions at m/z = 110 and m/z = 142, corresponding to Py-S-(110) and Py-S2 - (142), respectively.
- Additionally, in the charge/discharge profile of the Li-S battery with the 2FPy electrolyte (Figure 2C), a small but clear discharge plateau appears between 2.4 and 2.6 V after extended cycling, although it is absent at the beginning and only emerges gradually with cycling.
- According to the prior research on organodisulfides (e.g., dipyridyl disulfides) by Fu et al.,32 this small discharge plateau above 2.4 V likely results from organosulfide species formed through the ongoing reactions between 2FPy and polysulfides during the cycling.
- In contrast, due to the different reactivity of 3FPy and the electron-withdrawing effect of meta-fluorine, 3FPy remains stable without reacting with polysulfides, as demonstrated in Figures 2E, 3A, and 4A.
- In a previous study by Choi et al., the high polysulfide solubility in 3FPy (up to 1.5 M Li2S6) was demonstrated through visual observation.25 In this study, we systematically examine polysulfide solubilities in Py, 2FPy, 3FPy, and DME/ DOL using HPLC and inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES).
- The HPLC analysis (Figure S-16 and Table S-2) confirms that 3FPy exhibits a higher polysulfide solubility than Py, whereas both 2FPy and DME/DOL show significant lower solubilities compared to 3FPy and Py.
- The ICP-OES analysis of the Li+ concentration in supernatants from four 1 M Li2S6 mixtures (Table S-3) aligns with the HPLC findings, indicating a descending order of solubility: 3FPy (2134 mM) > Py (1834 mM) > 2FPy (459.7 mM) > DME/DOL (451.5 mM).
- Furthermore, the addition of 1 M LiTFSi to the 1 M Li2S6/3FPy mixture results in only a slight reduction in polysulfide solubility (Figure S-17 and Table S-4), whereas the addition of 1 M LiTFSi to 1 M Li2S6/2FPy significantly decreases the polysulfide solubility in 2FPy.
- Beyond high polysulfide solubility, 3FPy also demon- strates greater sulfur (S8) solubility than 2FPy (Table S-5).
- Interestingly, a comparison of HPLC results, as listed in Tables S-2 and S-4, reveals that the addition of the LiTFSi salt reduces the polysulfide solubility in 2FPy and 3FPy, whereas it unexpectedly increases the polysulfide solubility in Py and DME/DOL.
- In Choi and Coskun’s study, the Li-S battery using the 3FPy electrolyte at room temperature showed no polysulfide shuttle effect in the absence of the LiNO3 additive.25 However, at elevated temperatures, both 2FPy and 3FPy electrolytes (even with 2.5 M LiTFSi) exhibited a severe polysulfide shuttle effect in Li-S batteries without LiNO3 (Figure S-18A,C).
- Remarkably, the addition of LiNO3 to the 3FPy electrolyte significantly reduced the shuttle effect, although the capacity retention remained suboptimal (Figure S-18D).
- For the 2FPy electrolyte, the low solubility of LiNO3 limited its stabilizing effect, and even with saturation of LiNO3, the Li-S battery with 2FPy overcharged severely at 60 °C (Figure S-18B).
- These findings underscore the crucial role of LiNO3 in stabilizing the SEI layer on the Li anode, even in fluoropyridine-based electrolytes.
- Comparative low-temper- ature performance results (Figure S-19) show that Li-S batteries with fluoropyridine electrolytes perform poorly at 0 °C, achieving specific discharge capacities of only 300 mAh g-1 (3FPy electrolyte) and 550 mAh g-1 (2FPy electrolyte) after 25 cycles.
- In contrast, the Li-S battery with the DME/DOL electrolyte maintained a significantly higher discharge capacity of over 750 mAh g-1 under the same conditions, highlighting its superior low-temperature performance relative to fluoropyr- idine electrolytes. 3.
- CID spectra revealed that Li+ exhibits preferential solvation in different fluoropyridines, with 2FPy showing a stronger interaction with Li+ than 4FPy and 3FPy, likely due to the ortho-fluorine interaction unique to 2FPy.
- However, (-)ESI/MS and HPLC analyses indicated that 2FPy is reactive with polysulfides, while 3FPy not only provided the stability of polysulfides but also supported high solubility.
- This stability and solubility make 3FPy a promising electrolyte for a Li-S battery at room temperature.
- Further studies of the Li-S battery performance at varied temperatures highlighted certain challenges with fluoropyridine electrolytes, particularly with 3FPy.
