## 实验部分
2.1. Fabrication of LNO-SRF Li-Nafion powder was prepared according to previous reports [36]. LiNO3 (10 wt%) and Li-Nafion (1 wt%) powder were fully dis- solved in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) under magnetic stirring. The mixed solution was blade-coated onto a polypropylene melt- blown cloth and dried in a vacuum oven at 80 C. The obtained LNO-SRF was punched into 15-mm round disks and transferred to a glovebox. The mass of LNO-SRF is about 3 mg cm2, and the LiNO3 loading is about 1.2 mg cm2. The LNO-SRF was placed between separator and Li electrode or Cu electrode. 2.2. Electrochemical measurements All electrochemical tests were performed in CR2032-type coin cells with Celgard 2400 separators. Three types of cells (symmetric Li||Li, Li||Cu, and Li||S) were assembled in a glovebox. Electrochem- ical tests were conducted using an electrochemical workstation (BioLogic, France) and a LAND battery test system. Details of the electrochemical tests are described in the Supporting Information. 2.3. Calculations and multiphysics simulation Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) to obtain the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and electrostatic potential (ESP) charge distributions of various solvents and anions. Finite element modelling (FEM) was conducted using COMSOL multiphysics software. Details of the computation and simulation are provided in Supporting Information.

## 正文（含数字的句子与图表注）
- LiNO3 is widely used as an additive in electrolytes to build a high-quality SEI, but its self-sacrificial nature limits the abil- ity to mitigate the shuttle effect and stabilize Li anode during long-term cycling.
- To counteract LiNO3 con- sumption during long-term cycling without using a high initial concentration, inspired by sustained- release drugs, we encapsulated LiNO3 in lithiated Nafion polymer and added an electrolyte co-solvent (1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethylene 2,2,2-trifluoromethyl ether) with poor LiNO3 solubility to construct high- quality and durable F- and N-rich SEI.
- Hence, the F- and N-rich SEI improves the Li anode cycling CE to 99.63% and alleviates the shuttle effect during long-term cycling.
- The lithium anode with sustainable F- and N-rich SEI shows a stable Li plating/stripping over 2000 h at 1 mA cm2.
- As expected, Li||S full cells with this SEI achieved a long lifespan of 250 cycles, far exceeding cells with a rou- tine SEI.
- The Li||S pouch cell based on F- and N-rich SEI also can achieve a high energy density of about 300 Wh kg1 at initial cycles.
- This strategy provides a novel design for high-quality and durable SEIs in LSBs and may also be extendable to other alkali metal batteries.  2023 Science Press and Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

- The lithium-sulfur battery (LSB) is a promising rechargeable battery owing to its high theoretical specific energy of 2570 Wh kg1 [1,2].
- Nonetheless, the low Li cycling efficiency and shuttle effect of lithium polysulfides (LiPSs) lead to quick failure of LSBs and pre- clude their commercialization [3,4].
- These problems are closely linked to an unstable routine solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) [5,6].
- Firstly, the shuttle effect originates from long-chain LiPSs dis- solved in the electrolyte, which will pass through the routine SEI and react with the metallic Li anode [7,8].
- Secondly, the routine SEI with low Li+ conductivity and poor electrical insulation leads to formation of Li dendrite, and thus lowers Li cycling coulombic efficiency (CE) [9,10].
- The properties of an SEI are determined by its components and structure [11,12].
- Recently, extensive efforts have been devoted to constructing high-quality SEIs, mainly by tailoring the electrolyte composition or preparing artificial SEIs [13,14].
- Among these, addi- tives in the electrolyte such as benzoselenol [15], cerium nitrate [16], 3,5-bis(trifluoromethyl)thiophenol [17], and LiNO3 [18] have been proven to be a facile and effective strategy for optimizing the components and structure of SEI.
- Today, LiNO3 is regarded as a superstar additive in the field of LSBs, because a small amount of NO3  can generate a dense SEI enriched with high Li+ conductiv- ity LiNxOy and Li3N components [19-21].
- Such a compact SEI strongly mitigates the shuttle effect by preventing the LiPSs from contacting with the Li anode, while a high Li+ conductivity N-rich https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jechem.2023.05.020 2095-4956/ 2023 Science Press and Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

- A certain amount of LiNO3 can be dissolved in the standard ether-based electrolytes used in LSBs [24], but this additive is grad- ually depleted during the constant rupture and reconstruction of the SEI.
- Once NO3  is exhausted, the electrochemical performance of LSBs deteriorates rapidly in terms of CE and discharge capacity.
- The rate of LiNO3 depletion is related to its original amount in the electrolyte and the battery cycling capacity.
- Under mild conditions (e.g., at a low S loading and a high electrolyte/sulfur (E/S) ratio), LiNO3 can significantly enhance the lifespan and stability of LSBs [25].
- However, a practical LSBs requires the use of a high S loading cathode (>6 mg cm2), lean electrolyte (<3 µL mgS 1), and a thin Li anode (<50 µm) [26].
- Under such harsh conditions, even a high concentration of electrolyte failed to achieve satisfac- tory LSB cycling performance due to premature failure of Li anode [7].
- Although a higher initial LiNO3 concentration in the electrolyte directly ensures the sustainability and compactness of SEI, a LiNO3 concentration above 0.5 M not only converts the ‘‘active S” in cath- ode into inactive sulphate but also increases the cost and viscosity of the electrolyte [27].
- Researchers recently identified various slow-release strategies for LiNO3 to achieve sus- tainable SEI in ester-based electrolytes (low LiNO3 solubility), including pre-impregnation of the separator, encapsulation in a polymer gel [28], intercalation between bilayers of the separator [29], and encapsulation in nano capsules [30].
- However, these strategies are not applied to LSB electrolytes because of the high solubility of LiNO3 in 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) and dimethoxy ethane (DME) [31].
- In the drug field, reducing the drug release rate is a common means of improving the effectiveness of the drug [32,33].
- Compared to conventional methods, drugs in sustained release systems can maintain effective concentrations for longer periods of time, prolong the duration of action, increase the effec- tiveness, and improve the utilisation of the drug [34,35].
- Herein, inspired by sustained-release drugs strategies, we encapsulated LiNO3 in polymers and added ether-based co- solvent for solubility-mediated sustained release during LSB cycling.
- Specifically, the LiNO3 nanoparticles were dispersed in lithiated Nafion (Li-Nafion) polymer and coated on a polypropylene melt-blown cloth.
- This LiNO3 sustained-release film (LNO-SRF) swells in the electrolyte to achieve sustained release of LiNO3.
- At the same time, a fluorinated ether with ultralow Li salts solubility (1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethylene 2,2,2-trifluoromethyl ether, TFTFE) was added to the electrolyte as a co-solvent to further slow down the release of LiNO3 and generate insulating LiF component in the SEI, inhibiting electron leakage.
- During battery operation, a high-quality and durable SEI enriched with F and N was formed, and it increased the Li plating/stripping CE to 99.63% at a current of 1.0 mA cm2, a capacity of 1.0 mA h cm2.
- In a Li||S full cell with a high S loading cathode, a low E/S ratio, and a limited excess Li anode, the shuttle effect of LiPSs was effectively suppressed during cycling and an outstanding cycling lifespan of 250 cycles was achieved. 3.
