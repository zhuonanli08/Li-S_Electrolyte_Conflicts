## 实验部分
2.1 Preparation and activation of azulmic carbon (AZC) AZC was prepared according to a previous publication.17,18 AZA was carbonized at 800 °C for 1 h under a nitrogen atmosphere; subsequently, the AZA carbides were mixed with an alkaline activator (KOH) in a ratio of 1 : 2 by weight in deionized water, stirred, heated at 100 °C for 5 h, and dried. The sample was activated at 800 °C for 1 h in a quartz tube (3 cm in diameter) under an argon atmosphere; subsequently, the activated sample was dispersed in deionized water and neutralized with dilute hydrochlo- ric acid. The samples were then filtered, washed with excess deionized water, and vacuum-dried at 80 °C for more than 12 h to obtain AZC. 2.2 Preparation of the sulfur-composite AZC (AZC-S) and AZC-S cathode The amount of sulfur composite in the AZC was calculated from the pore volume measured by nitrogen adsorption/desorption experiments. AZC (0.4 g) and sulfur (0.6 g) were mixed in an agate mortar and placed in a simple reaction vessel made of stainless steel. The mixture was heated to 155 °C in an air atmosphere and maintained for 5 h to melt the sulfur and enable its diﬀusion into the AZC pores. The temperature was then increased from 155 to 300 °C and maintained for 2 h to remove the excess sulfur outside the AZC pores. Temperature increments were carried out at a rate of 10 °C min¹1, while air cooling was used to cool the samples to room temperature. The AZC composite formed with sulfur was labeled AZC-S. To fabricate the sulfur cathode, AZC-S, carbon nanotubes (CNT), carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC), and styrene- butadiene rubber (SBR) were first mixed in a weight ratio of 89 : 5 : 3 : 3 in deionized water to form a slurry. Subsequently, the slurry was coated on an Al foil using the doctor-blade method and dried in an oven at 40 °C for more than 3 h; the residual water was removed by drying in a vacuum oven at 50 °C for more than 12 h. The amount of sulfur in the electrode was 3.0 mg cm¹2. 2.3 Electrolyte preparation and assembly of Li-S cells Electrolytes were prepared by mixing diﬀerent concentrations of lithium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl) imide (LiTFSI, Solvey) (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 M) in a solvent containing sulfolane (SL, Kishida Chemical) and 1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoro- propyl (HFE, Daikin Industries) in a 1 : 1 ratio by volume (2.00 : 1.26 by mol). For comparison, the sulfolane electrolyte reported by Watanabe et al. was prepared using LiTFSI : SL : HFE = 1 : 2 : 2 (mol) and labeled the standard SL.37 A two-electrode cell was constructed using an AZC-S electrode as the positive electrode, Li foil as the anode, and a polyolefin microporous membrane as the separator; a glove box filled with Ar, maintained at a dew point of less than ¹80 °C, was used for cell construction and electrolyte preparation, to prevent changes due to moisture. 2.4 Physical properties and electrochemical tests An Autosorb iQ instrument (Anton-Paar) was used for the nitrogen adsorption/desorption measurements at ¹196 °C, while thermogravimetric analysis (TGA, DTG-60H, Shimazu Co. Ltd.) was used to estimate the sulfur loading. Charge-discharge tests of the Li-S batteries were carried out at a current density of 167.2 mA g¹1 (0.1 C) per sulfur weight in the voltage range of 1.0- 3.0 V (vs. Li/Li+). A scanning rate of 0.1 mV s¹1 was used for cyclic voltammetry and linear sweep voltammetry, and all the electrochemical measurements were performed at 25 °C. A Nano- finder30 (TOKYO INSTRUMENTS) green laser (at 532 nm) was used for Raman spectroscopy. The viscosity of the electrolyte was measured with a microVISC (Viscotech) micro diﬀerential pressure viscometer, and the ionic conductivity was estimated with a Seven2Go Cond meter S3 in combination with a Cond probe InLab 751-4mm (METTLER TOLEDO). These physical properties of the electrolyte were evaluated at 25 °C in dry air with a dew point below ¹40 °C.

## 正文（含数字的句子与图表注）
- A previously reported sulfolane electrolyte in the solvated state exhibits a short cycle life of eight cycles, whereas the sulfur-containing microporous-carbon cathode with a sulfolane-based electrolyte prepared in the ratio of lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) : SL : HFE = (0.572-0.953) : 2.00 : 1.26 (mol) exhibits a discharge capacity of more than 550 mAh g-1, even after 50 charge-discharge cycles.
- This study could guide the design and development of electrolytes for sulfur- containing microporous-carbon cathodes. © The Author(s) 2022.

- The inter- calation materials used in lithium-ion batteries at present are expected to reach their energy-density limit in the near future;1 therefore, all-solid-state, lithium-sulfur (Li-S), and lithium-air batteries are attracting immense attention as next-generation rechargeable batteries.
- Li-S batteries have been widely studied since the 1970s.2,3 Sulfur (S), the cathode material in Li-S batteries, exhibits several advantageous properties, such as, light weight, low cost, and abundance; moreover, sulfur has a high theoretical capacity (1672 mAh g¹1) as its redox reaction involves a two- electron transfer reaction per S atom (S8 + 16Li+ + 16e¹ ¼ 8Li2S).4 Although Li-S batteries exhibit high potential as next-generation rechargeable batteries, numerous limitations that hinder their commercial applications.
- A major limitation is the rapid dissolution of lithium polysulfides (reaction intermediates formed during charging and discharging) in organic electrolytes, causing irrever- sible side reactions with specific electrolytes.5 Highly soluble long- chain lithium polysulfides (Li2S8-4), formed as intermediates during the sulfur-cathode discharge process, diﬀuse into the lithium anode and are reduced to insoluble short-chain lithium polysulfides (Li2S1-2).
- This shuttle behavior (labeled redox shuttle reactions) lowers the actual capacity and cycle life of Li-S batteries because the leached lithium polysulfide exhibits nonconductor behavior at the positive and negative electrode surfaces;6,7 electrolyte-based coun- termeasures are used to overcome this limitation.

- Highly concentrated electrolytes (HCEs), such as solvated electrolytes, are used as electrolytes for Li-S batteries without lithium nitrate.16-18 The HCE, with no free spaces for lithium polysulfide dissolution, physically traps the lithium polysulfide in the electrolyte.

- The 0.5-SL electrolyte was less eﬀective in inhibiting lithium polysulfide elution; the separator stained dark yellow, confirming the presence of a large amount of sulfide.
- Although the separator appeared slightly yellow while using the 1.5- SL electrolyte, the system exhibited a greater inhibition of lithium polysulfide elution compared to 0.5-SL system.
- Figure 6 shows the CV measurements for the first three cycles.
- The 0.5-SL electrolyte showed a wide peak that keeps the response current flowing up to 1 V during discharge, indicating lithium polysulfide dissolution from the first cycle.
- The 1.0-SL electrolyte showed a sharp response current peak with a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of 0.29 V in the first cycle, with a slightly wider peak from the second cycle, indicating lithium polysulfide dissolution; this was in agreement with the coulombic-eﬃciency results of the constant-current charge-discharge tests.
- For 1.5-SL (FWHM: 0.27 V) and 2.0-SL (FWHM: 0.33 V), the peak width of the response current changed negligibly during discharge, indicating a suppression of lithium polysulfide dissolution.
- Although 2.5-SL exhibited a wide response current peak with FWHM of 0.70 V from the first-cycle discharge, the response current continued to flow at 1.0 V, unlike the peak shape caused by lithium polysulfide dissolution in 0.5-SL.
- The standard-SL (FWHM: 0.78 V) and 2.5-SL electrolytes showed similar CV behavior, indicating that an extremely high lithium-salt concen- tration could reduce the response current in microporous carbon- activated carbon-sulfur composite cathodes, possibly due to the inhibition of lithium-ion transport by sulfur-filled micropores and the low wettability of the highly concentrated and solvated electrolyte.
- The low cycle life of the AZC-S electrode in the standard-SL electrolyte (Fig. 4) would correlate with its low response current and high overvoltage in the CV (Fig. 6), which suggests a problem with low reaction activity.
- Therefore, under these low HFE conditions, the number of moles of LiTFSI to SL was reduced, thus increasing the free SL “to the extent that it does not dissolve the polysulfide” (examples are 2.0-, 1.5- and 1.0-SL), thereby increasing the activity of the cathode reaction.
- These measures are regarded as electrolyte optimization in this study. 4.
