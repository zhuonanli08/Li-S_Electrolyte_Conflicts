## 实验部分
 The procedure to prepare S-coated multi-walled carbon nanotubes (S-CNT) was previously reported. [ 37 ] Elemental sulfur (S 8 , Alfa Aesar) and the MWCNTs (Cheap Tubes Inc.) (80:20 w/w) were mixed together by mechanical ball milling for 5 h. The mixture was then sealed in a polytetrafl uoroethylene (PTFE) container and heated to 155 °C in an oven for 24 h under the protection of inert Ar gas. Morphology observations were performed with a dual-focus ion beam (FIB) SEM (FEI Helios). The 7 Li and 17 O NMR spectra for these electrolytes were obtained from Fourier transformed free induction decays after a single pulse excitation at Larmor frequencies of 233.2 and 81.4 MHz, respectively, at ambient temperature (≈25 °C) using a 600 MHz NMR spectrometer (Agilent, USA) equipped with a 5 mm liquid NMR probe (Doty Scientifi c, USA). The 7 Li and 17 O chemical shifts for a 1 M LiCl in H 2 O solution and dilute water were used as external references (i.e., 0 ppm), respectively. The ionic conductivity of the electrolytes was measured by a conductivity meter (Orion 3 Star, Thermo Scientifi c) at 30 °C. The shear viscosity ( η ) of the electrolytes was measured using a viscometer (Brookfi eld, DV2T) at 30 °C. All of the measurements were performed more than twice to ensure their reliability. Calculation of the Variation of Hydrodynamic Radius, r s , Li /r Li : The temperature-dependent diffusion coeffi cients measured for LiTFSI and LITDI-based electrolytes plotted in Figure S1 (Supporting Information) were in well agreement with the Stokes-Einstein theory of diffusion, D = k B T /6π ηr s , where k B , T , η , and r s are the Boltzmann constant, absolute temperature, viscosity, and hydrodynamic radius of diffusing molecules, respectively. Based on the variations of diffusion coeffi cient and δ ( 17 O) of solvent molecules, the interaction of DOL molecules to other electrolyte components was assumed negligible than the hydrodynamic radius of DOL molecules; ( r s,DOL ) will be the same to its actual size for all samples, r s,DOL = r DOL . And if both Li + and DOL were completely isolated from other components of the electrolyte, the diffusion ratio D 0,Li / D 0,DOL = r DOL / r Li , where D 0,Li and D 0,DOL were the diffusion coeffi cients of Li + and DOL without any interaction to other electrolyte components and r DOL and r Li were the sizes of DOL and Li + cation. Therefore, the variations of hydrodynamic radius of Li + cation were as follow: ( D 0,Li / D 0,DOL )/( D Li / D DOL ) = ( r DOL / r Li )/( r s , DOL / r s , Li ) = r s , Li / r Li . This value was proportional to the degree of ion association between Li + cations and anions. Using the diffusion coeffi cients the ratio r s,Li / r Li for LiTFSI and LiTDI-based electrolytes was calculated with and without 0.05 M Li 2 S 8 . The larger value of r s,Li / r Li refl ected the larger size of Li complexes. Electrochemical Measurements : The thin cathode with <1 mg S cm -2 loading was prepared by mixing 80 wt% S-CNT composite powder, 10 wt% Super P (Timcal Graphite & Carbon), and 10 wt% polyvinylidene fl uoride (PVDF) binder together and adding N -methyl-2-pyrrolidinone to form a slurry. The slurry was then coated onto an Al foil current collector and dried at 60 °C under vacuum overnight. A thick cathode with ≈3 mg S cm -2 loading was prepared similarly by mixing 80 wt% S-CNT composite powder, 10 wt% Super P, 5 wt% LA133 binder (Chengdu indigo Co.), and 5 wt% styrene-butadiene-rubber(SBR) binder (MTI Corp.) and forming a slurry with water. This was coated onto a C-coated Al foil current collector (MTI Corp.). Electrodes were punched to a 0.9 cm 2 diameter size. The electrolytes used were either 1 M LiTFSI or 1 M LiTDI dissolved in a mixture of DOL and DME (1/1 v/v), respectively. Nominal Li 2 S x solutions were obtained by mixing stoichiometric Li 2 S and S 8 and stirring at 60 °C in oil bath for 1-10 h inside the glove box. The solubility was obtained by fi rst adding extra amount of stoichiometric Li 2 S 8 followed by diluting the saturated solution gradually for every 10 h until all species were dissolved. Half cells with Li metal foil (400 µm thick from MTI Corp.) as the anode and Celgard 3501 as the separator were assembled using CR2032 coin cells in an argon-fi lled glove box. The active S/E was carefully controlled, [ 29 ] ranging from 15 g L -1 (15 g sulfur per liter of electrolyte) to 100 g L -1 . The discharge/charge cycles were tested galvanostatically using a Lanhe battery tester at room temperature in a voltage cutoff protocol between 2.8 and 1.5 V. The specifi c capacity (SC) was based on the sulfur mass in the C/S composites, and the AC was calculated by the equation AC = SC × sulfur loading ratio. Computational Details : For AIM-D simulation, the calculations were carried out with plane wave basis sets as implemented in the VASP code. [ 38,39 ] All the calculations were spin-polarized and carried out using the gradient corrected exchange-correlation functional of PBE (Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof) under the projector augmented wave method, with plane wave basis set up to a kinetic energy cutoff of 300 eV. [ 40,41 ] The van der Waals method of Grimme (i.e., DFT-D2) was used throughout AI-MD calculations with the convergence criterion of the total energy set to be within 1 × 10 -4 eV. [ 42 ] For the simulation of the electrolyte, the simulation box (18 × 18 × 18 Å 3 ) was consisted of randomly populated 20 solvent molecules (DOL:DME = 1:1) and 5 salt molecules (i.e., LiTDI or LiTFSI) with a solvated Li 2 S 8 molecule with presumed liquid density of ≈0.96 g cm -3 . To investigate the thermodynamic stability of the system at room temperature, all the system were thermally equilibrated at T = 300 K based on an Nose-Hoover paticles, volume, temperature (NVT) system ensemble with a time step of 1 fs, and the production run was obtained after thermal equilibration of ≈2.5 ps.

- In most studies, a low sulfur loading (less than 1 mg cm -2 ) is used, but a high S loading is desired for high-capacity full cells. [ 25,26 ] In this paper, lithium trifl uoromethyl-4,5-dicyanoimidazole (LiTDI) is studied for Li-S cells.
- The performance of this electro- lyte was investigated with a high sulfur (i.e., practically usable) loading at 3 mg cm -2 under a high current density.
- A comparison of the physical properties of electrolytes with either 1 M LiTFSI or LiTDI in DOL/DME (1/1, v/v) is shown in Table 1 .
- Both electrolytes have a comparable conductivity and viscosity, suitable for the electrochemical needs, but an 83% decrease in the Li 2 S 8 solubility is observed in the LiTDI electrolyte.
- Restricting the polysulfi de solubility to a low level is expected to improve the Li-S cell cycle life because of the sup- pression of the polysulfi de shuttle mechanism, reduction of anode contamination, and the minimization of the loss of active material from the cathode. [ 22 ] To understand the relationship between the polysulfi de solu- bility and electrolyte supporting salt, 7 Li and 17 O nuclear mag- netic resonance (NMR) spectroscopic measurements were used to aid in understanding the solvation interactions.
- The δ ( 7 Li) for the LiTDI and LiTFSI-based electrolytes without polysulfi des was -0.8 and -1.2 ppm (relative to Li + cations fully solvated by H 2 O molecules) ( Figure 1 a-c), respectively.
- The δ ( 17 O) for the DME molecules shifts to ≈ -27.7 and -26.9 ppm in the LiTDI and LiTFSI-based electrolytes, respectively, from -24.3 ppm in a neat DOL/DME solvent mixture.
- The δ ( 17 O) for the DOL molecules near 35 ppm shifts by 1-2 ppm.
- The smaller shift of δ ( 17 O) for the DOL molecules relative to the DME molecules and signifi cant broadening of the peaks for the latter sug- gest that the Li + cations may be preferentially solvated by the DME. - This is principally caused by the dissolution of the lithium polysulfi des generated during the discharge/charge reactions. [ 1,2 ] The soluble polysulfi des easily migrate out of the cathode and diffuse into the electrolyte.

- To explain the Li + ion solvation with polysulfi des, ab initio molecular dynamics (AI-MD) simulation was performed on Li 2 S 8 in the electrolyte (Figure 1 d,e).
- AI-MD simulation reveals that the solvated Li 2 S 8 species in different Li-salt electrolytes at same concentration yields different subsequent lithium poly- sulfi de species under equilibration at T = 300 K.
- For the LiTDI electrolyte, two Li 2 S 4 were formed, whereas for the LiTFSI electrolyte, the Li 2 S 3 and Li 2 S 5 were gener- ated.
- The two Li 2 S 4 species in LiTDI solu- tion tend to agglomerate into a dimer with Li 4 S 8 -like cluster, in contrast to the separa- tion of Li 2 S 3 and Li 2 S 5 in LiTFSI solutions as observed in previous studies. [ 28 ] The radius of the Li 4 S 8 dimer is much larger than that of the separated Li 2 S 4 , which is well aligned with the hydrodynamic radius observation in the NMR studies.
- TDI anion contains more reactive nitrogen-containing functional group like nitrile and imide, which has been proven to affect the polysulfi de stability in the carbon host. [ 10 ] It should be noted that the chemical synthesized Li 2 S 8 solution in the experiment is a complex mixture of dif- ferent state polysulfi des, including Li 2 S 6 and S 3 - radicals. [ 20,23 ] However, with the same simulation conditions and the Density func- tional theory (DFT) calculations of Gibbs energy, the favorable species in different elec- trolytes were determined with reaction a and reaction b shown in Figure S3 (Supporting Information), respectively.
- This case indicates that supporting salts would affect the chem- ical reduction reaction of long-chain poly- sulfi de (i.e., Li 2 S 8 ) and result in a manageable optimization of the polysulfi de solubility.

- In order to obtain a reproduc- ible result from the batch-to-batch study, the sulfur to electrolyte ratio (S/E) needs to be optimized fi rst for each formulation, as shown in Figure S2 (Supporting Infor- mation). [ 32 ] One of the most critical issues for the practical utilization of Li-S batteries is to increase the cell performance with a high sulfur loading.
- Another challenge associated with such cathodes is that rapid Li anode degradation occurs for cycled cells with a high areal capacity (AC). [ 33,34 ] Figure 3 compares the charge-discharge curves and cycling performance of a cell with 3 mg S cm -2 loading.
- The cell is fi rst discharged/ charged at a C/10 (i.e., 10 h) rate (0.5 mA cm -2 ) for 5 cycles, followed by a C/2 (i.e., 2 h) rate (2.4 mA cm -2 ) up to 300 cycles.
- Both of the cells with the 1 M LiTFSI or LiTDI in DOL/DME electrolytes have only a 300 mAh g -1 discharge capacity due to the large polarization for the thick sulfur cathode.
- The poly- sulfi de additives will promote the polysulfi de dissolution to an equilibrium state and the EIS spectrum of the cell would be greatly reduced so that it would not only promote the elec- trode initial capacity delivery but also help in the capacity reten- tion. [ 35,36 ] However, after adding polysulfi des, LiNO 3 is needed to prevent the shuttle problem toward the anode side.
- In this study, only 40 × 10 -3 M Li 2 S 8 (which is only 1% comparing to the sulfur in the cathode) and 0.2 M LiNO 3 were added to the electrolytes and the polysulfi de additive itself does not con- tribute to the calculation of the total capacity.
- For the cells with the electrolytes containing the additives, the sulfur cathodes were well wetted in both the LiTFSI and LiTDI-based electro- lytes and both the cells achieved a high initial capacity of 1140 and 1110 mAh g -1 , respectively.
