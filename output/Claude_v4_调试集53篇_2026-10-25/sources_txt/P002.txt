[M0008 p1] [ 21 ] Switching to other solvents with high dielectric constant such as dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) or dimethylacetamide (DMA) facilitates the production of sulfur radicals and the full utilization of the sulfur, but the cycling stability of the cell is also sacrifi ced and the unstable interface between the reactive DMSO/DMA solvent and Li metal raises other concerns.
[M0009 p1] [ 22,23 ] Alternative cell designs incorporating solid-state electrolytes completely prevent the dissolution of polysulfi des, but the utilization of the sulfur (i.e., the reversible capacity) is also largely reduced.
[M0010 p1] [ 24 ] Therefore, the solubility of the polysulfi des in cells with liquid electrolytes needs to be carefully tuned and well understood.
[M0011 p1] In most studies, a low sulfur loading (less than 1 mg cm –2 ) is used, but a high S loading is desired for high-capacity full cells.
[M0012 p1] [ 25,26 ] In this paper, lithium trifl uoromethyl-4,5-dicyanoimidazole (LiTDI) is studied for Li-S cells.
[M0013 p1] We study the effect of LiTDI on the solubility of polysulfi des.
[M0014 p1] The performance of this electrolyte was investigated with a high sulfur (i.e., practically usable) loading at 3 mg cm –2 under a high current density.
[M0015 p1] A comparison of the physical properties of electrolytes with either 1 M LiTFSI or LiTDI in DOL/DME (1/1, v/v) is shown in Table 1 .
[M0016 p1] Both electrolytes have a comparable conductivity and viscosity, suitable for the electrochemical needs, but an 83% decrease in the Li 2 S 8 solubility is observed in the LiTDI electrolyte.
[M0017 p1] Restricting the polysulfi de solubility to a low level is expected to improve the Li-S cell cycle life because of the suppression of the polysulfi de shuttle mechanism, reduction of anode contamination, and the minimization of the loss of active material from the cathode.
[M0018 p1] [ 22 ] To understand the relationship between the polysulfi de solubility and electrolyte supporting salt, 7 Li and 17 O nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopic measurements were used to aid in understanding the solvation interactions.
[M0019 p1] The δ ( 7 Li) for the LiTDI and LiTFSI-based electrolytes without polysulfi des was –0.8 and –1.2 ppm (relative to Li + cations fully solvated by H 2 O molecules) ( Figure 1 a–c), respectively.
[M0020 p1] The δ ( 17 O) for the DME molecules shifts to ≈ –27.7 and –26.9 ppm in the LiTDI and LiTFSI-based electrolytes, respectively, from –24.3 ppm in a neat DOL/DME solvent mixture.
[M0021 p1] The δ ( 17 O) for the DOL molecules near 35 ppm shifts by 1–2 ppm.
[M0022 p1] The smaller shift of δ ( 17 O) for the DOL molecules relative to the DME molecules and signifi cant broadening of the peaks for the latter suggest that the Li + cations may be preferentially solvated by the DME.
[M0023 p1] [ 27 ] It has been reported that the solvation of Li + cations in the electrolyte affects the short-chain polysulfi de solubility by solvating the Li + ion in the polysulfi des, but it did not affect the Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) batteries have attracted wide interest due to their potential high capacity (1672 mAh g −1 ), low environmental impact, and low cost.
[M0024 p1] The commercialization of Li-S batteries is hindered, however, by capacity fading upon extensive cycling.
[M0025 p1] This is principally caused by the dissolution of the lithium polysulfi des generated during the discharge/charge reactions.
[M0026 p1] [ 1,2 ] The soluble polysulfi des easily migrate out of the cathode and diffuse into the electrolyte.
[M0027 p1] The “corrosive” electrolyte containing polysulfi des continuously reacts with the Li metal anode (the so-called shuttle mechanism), thereby increasing the cell impedance by forming an insulating layer on the anode.
[M0028 p1] All these factors contribute to the poor cycling stability and low round-trip effi ciency of Li-S batteries.
[M0029 p1] [ 3 ] A general approach to address the capacity fading issue is to confi ne the soluble polysulfi de species in carbon scaffolds (the sulfur active material is an insulator so the carbon also serves as to the electron conductor for the sulfur) with various architectures including porous carbons, carbon nanotubes, and graphene.
[M0030 p1] [ 4–9 ] The functionalization of carbon substrate or the direct incorporation of oxides such as metal-organic frameworks further immobilizes the polysulfi de anions at the atomic level.
[M0031 p1] [ 10–13 ] Different polymers have also been studied to stabilize the polysulfi des through the formation of chemical bonds with the sulfur.
[M0032 p1] [ 14–16 ] In contrast, reducing the polysulfi de solubility by modifying the electrolyte formulation has been less explored, possibly due to the complicated interactions between the ions, solvent molecules, and polysulfi des during the electrochemical processes.
[M0033 p1] [ 17,18 ] The state-of-the-art electrolyte formulation used for Li-ion batteries (e.g., LiPF 6 in an ethylene carbonate/dimethyl carbonate (or other acyclic carbonate) solvent mixture) is not suitable for Li-S cells due to the incompatibility between the carbonate solvents and the polysulfi des and S 3 · radicals.

[M0045 p2] To explain the Li + ion solvation with polysulfi des, ab initio molecular dynamics (AI-MD) simulation was performed on Li 2 S 8 in the electrolyte (Figure 1 d,e).
[M0046 p2] AI-MD simulation reveals that the solvated Li 2 S 8 species in different Li-salt electrolytes at same concentration yields different subsequent lithium polysulfi de species under equilibration at T = 300 K.
[M0047 p2] This could be due to the different Li + solvation level in the electrolyte and the different anion interaction.
[M0048 p2] For the LiTDI electrolyte, two Li 2 S 4 were formed, whereas for the LiTFSI electrolyte, the Li 2 S 3 and Li 2 S 5 were generated.
[M0049 p2] The two Li 2 S 4 species in LiTDI solution tend to agglomerate into a dimer with Li 4 S 8 -like cluster, in contrast to the separation of Li 2 S 3 and Li 2 S 5 in LiTFSI solutions as observed in previous studies.
[M0050 p2] [ 28 ] The radius of the Li 4 S 8 dimer is much larger than that of the separated Li 2 S 4 , which is well aligned with the hydrodynamic radius observation in the NMR studies.
[M0051 p2] TDI anion contains more reactive nitrogen-containing functional group like nitrile and imide, which has been proven to affect the polysulfi de stability in the carbon host.
[M0052 p2] [ 10 ] It should be noted that the chemical synthesized Li 2 S 8 solution in the experiment is a complex mixture of different state polysulfi des, including Li 2 S 6 and S 3 − radicals.

[M0054 p2] This case indicates that supporting salts would affect the chemical reduction reaction of long-chain polysulfi de (i.e., Li 2 S 8 ) and result in a manageable optimization of the polysulfi de solubility.

[M0109 p5] Experimental Section The procedure to prepare S-coated multi-walled carbon nanotubes (S-CNT) was previously reported.
[M0110 p5] [ 37 ] Elemental sulfur (S 8 , Alfa Aesar) and the MWCNTs (Cheap Tubes Inc.) (80:20 w/w) were mixed together by mechanical ball milling for 5 h.
[M0111 p5] The mixture was then sealed in a polytetrafl uoroethylene (PTFE) container and heated to 155 °C in an oven for 24 h under the protection of inert Ar gas.
[M0112 p5] Morphology observations were performed with a dual-focus ion beam (FIB) SEM (FEI Helios).
[M0113 p5] The 7 Li and 17 O NMR spectra for these electrolytes were obtained from Fourier transformed free induction decays after a single pulse excitation at Larmor frequencies of 233.2 and 81.4 MHz, respectively, at ambient temperature (≈25 °C) using a 600 MHz NMR spectrometer (Agilent, USA) equipped with a 5 mm liquid NMR probe (Doty Scientifi c, USA).
[M0114 p5] The 7 Li and 17 O chemical shifts for a 1 M LiCl in H 2 O solution and dilute water were used as external references (i.e., 0 ppm), respectively.
[M0115 p5] The ionic conductivity of the electrolytes was measured by a conductivity meter (Orion 3 Star, Thermo Scientifi c) at 30 °C.
[M0116 p5] The shear viscosity ( η ) of the electrolytes was measured using a viscometer (Brookfi eld, DV2T) at 30 °C.
[M0117 p5] All of the measurements were performed more than twice to ensure their reliability.
[M0118 p5] Calculation of the Variation of Hydrodynamic Radius, r s , Li /r Li : The temperature-dependent diffusion coeffi cients measured for LiTFSI and LITDI-based electrolytes plotted in Figure S1 (Supporting Information) were in well agreement with the Stokes–Einstein theory of diffusion, D = k B T /6π ηr s , where k B , T , η , and r s are the Boltzmann constant, absolute temperature, viscosity, and hydrodynamic radius of diffusing molecules, respectively.
[M0119 p5] Based on the variations of diffusion coeffi cient and δ ( 17 O) of solvent molecules, the interaction of DOL molecules to other electrolyte components was assumed negligible than the hydrodynamic radius of DOL molecules; ( r s,DOL ) will be the same to its actual size for all samples, r s,DOL = r DOL .
[M0120 p5] And if both Li + and DOL were completely isolated from other components of the electrolyte, the diffusion ratio D 0,Li / D 0,DOL = r DOL / r Li , where D 0,Li and D 0,DOL were the diffusion coeffi cients of Li + and DOL without any interaction to other electrolyte components and r DOL and r Li were the sizes of DOL and Li + cation.
[M0121 p5] Therefore, the variations of hydrodynamic radius of Li + cation were as follow: ( D 0,Li / D 0,DOL )/( D Li / D DOL ) = ( r DOL / r Li )/( r s , DOL / r s , Li ) = r s , Li / r Li .
[M0122 p5] This value was proportional to the degree of ion association between Li + cations and anions.
[M0123 p5] Using the diffusion coeffi cients the ratio r s,Li / r Li for LiTFSI and LiTDI-based electrolytes was calculated with and without 0.05 M Li 2 S 8 .
[M0124 p5] The larger value of r s,Li / r Li refl ected the larger size of Li complexes.
[M0125 p5] Electrochemical Measurements : The thin cathode with <1 mg S cm −2 loading was prepared by mixing 80 wt% S-CNT composite powder, 10 wt% Super P (Timcal Graphite & Carbon), and 10 wt% polyvinylidene fl uoride (PVDF) binder together and adding N -methyl-2-pyrrolidinone to form a slurry.
[M0126 p5] The slurry was then coated onto an Al foil current collector and dried at 60 °C under vacuum overnight.
[M0127 p5] A thick cathode with ≈3 mg S cm −2 loading was prepared similarly by mixing 80 wt% S-CNT composite powder, 10 wt% Super P, 5 wt% LA133 binder (Chengdu indigo Co.), and 5 wt% styrene-butadiene-rubber(SBR) binder (MTI Corp.) and forming a slurry with water.
[M0128 p5] This was coated onto a C-coated Al foil current collector (MTI Corp.).
[M0129 p5] Electrodes were punched to a 0.9 cm 2 diameter size.
[M0130 p5] The electrolytes used were either 1 M LiTFSI or 1 M LiTDI dissolved in a mixture of DOL and DME (1/1 v/v), respectively.
[M0131 p5] Nominal Li 2 S x solutions were obtained by mixing stoichiometric Li 2 S and S 8 and stirring at 60 °C in oil bath for 1–10 h inside the glove box.
[M0132 p5] The solubility was obtained by fi rst adding extra amount of stoichiometric Li 2 S 8 followed by diluting the saturated solution gradually for every 10 h until all species were dissolved.
[M0133 p5] Half cells with Li metal foil (400 μm thick from MTI Corp.) as the anode and Celgard 3501 as the separator were assembled using CR2032 coin cells in an argon-fi lled glove box.
[M0134 p5] The active S/E was carefully controlled, [ 29 ] ranging from 15 g L −1 (15 g sulfur per liter of electrolyte) to 100 g L −1 .
[M0135 p5] The discharge/charge cycles were tested galvanostatically using a Lanhe battery tester at room temperature in a voltage cutoff protocol between 2.8 and 1.5 V.
[M0136 p5] The specifi c capacity (SC) was based on the sulfur mass in the C/S composites, and the AC was calculated by the equation AC = SC × sulfur loading ratio.
[M0137 p5] Computational Details : For AIM-D simulation, the calculations were carried out with plane wave basis sets as implemented in the VASP code.
[M0138 p5] [ 38,39 ] All the calculations were spin-polarized and carried out using the gradient corrected exchange-correlation functional of PBE (Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof) under the projector augmented wave method, with plane wave basis set up to a kinetic energy cutoff of 300 eV.
[M0139 p5] [ 40,41 ] The van der Waals method of Grimme (i.e., DFT-D2) was used throughout AI-MD calculations with the convergence criterion of the total energy set to be within 1 × 10 −4 eV.
[M0140 p5] [ 42 ] For the simulation of the electrolyte, the simulation box (18 × 18 × 18 Å 3 ) was consisted of randomly populated 20 solvent molecules (DOL:DME = 1:1) and 5 salt molecules (i.e., LiTDI or LiTFSI) with a solvated Li 2 S 8 molecule with presumed liquid density of ≈0.96 g cm –3 .
[M0141 p5] To investigate the thermodynamic stability of the system at room temperature, all the system were thermally equilibrated at T = 300 K based on an Nose–Hoover paticles, volume, temperature (NVT) system ensemble with a time step of 1 fs, and the production run was obtained after thermal equilibration of ≈2.5 ps.
