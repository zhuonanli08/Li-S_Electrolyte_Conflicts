[M0007 p1] Introduction Sulfur is a promising cathode material for the next-generation energy storage batteries due to its high theoretical capacity of 1675 mAh g−1.[1] When coupled with lithium metal anode (theoretical capacity of 3860 mAh g−1), theoretical capacity Conventional lithium–sulfur batteries often suffer from fatal problems such as high flammability, polysulfide shuttling, and lithium dendrites growth.
[M0008 p1] Here, highly-safe lithium–sulfur batteries based on flame-retardant electrolyte (dimethoxyether/1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl 2,2,3,3-tetrafluoropropyl ether) coupled with functional separator (nanoconductive carbon-coated cellulose nonwoven) to resolve aforementioned bottle-neck issues are demonstrated.
[M0009 p1] It is found that this flame-retardant electrolyte exhibits excellent flame retardancy and low solubility of polysulfide.
[M0010 p1] In addition, Li/Li symmetrical cells using such flame-retardant electrolyte deliver extraordinary longterm cycling stability (less than 10 mV overpotential) for over 2500 h at 1.0 mA cm−2 and 1.0 mAh cm−2.
[M0011 p1] Moreover, bare sulfur cathode–based lithium–sulfur batteries using this flame retardant electrolyte coupled with nanoconductive carbon-coated cellulose separator can retain 83.6% discharge capacity after 200 cycles at 0.5 C.
[M0012 p1] Under high charge/discharge rate (4 C), lithium–sulfur cells still show high charge/discharge capacity of ≈350 mAh g−1.
[M0013 p1] Even at an elevated temperature of 60 °C, discharge capacity of 870 mAh g−1 can be retained.
[M0014 p1] More importantly, high-loading bare sulfur cathode (4 mg cm−2)–based lithium–sulfur batteries can also deliver high charge/discharge capacity over 806 mAh g−1 after 56 cycles.
[M0015 p1] Undoubtedly, the strategy of flame retardant electrolyte coupled with carbon-coated separator enlightens highly safe lithium–sulfur batteries at a wide range of temperature. of lithium-batteries can reach up to 2600 Wh kg−1, which is much higher than that of conventional lithium ion batteries.[2] Unfortunately, there are still several problems need to be solved.
[M0016 p1] For the cathode side, polysulfide can dissolve into ether-based electrolyte and then shuttling to anode side, reacting with lithium metal anode and eventually leading to irreversible capacity fading and low Coulombic efficiency (CE).[3] For the lithium metal anode side, uneven Li+ platting/ stripping easily leads to the rapid growth of lithium dendrites, which can easily penetrate separator and causing internal short circuit.
[M0017 p1] Lithium dendrites and fragile electrolyte/electrode interface could detach from lithium metal anode to form “dead Li,” more electrolyte would be consumed and the lithium utilization is reduced.[4] For electrolyte, conventional ether-based electrolytes are easily volatile and highly flammable,[5] which is detrimental for safety of lithium–sulfur batteries.
[M0018 p1] Great efforts have been carried out to solve the issues associated with lithium– sulfur batteries noted above.
[M0019 p1] To address the problem of lithium polysulfide shuttling, various strategies have been explored, including using modified structure sulfur host[6] and using ionic liquid electrolyte,[7] etc.
[M0020 p1] To find out the solution to a dendrite free lithium metal anode, researchers have done many attempts such as design special architecture lithium metal anode,[8] pre-formed solid electrolyte interface,[9] modified separators[10] and etc.
[M0021 p1] Generally speaking, these methods usually only solve the problems of the cathode or anode unilaterally.
[M0022 p1] To restrain the dendrite growth and prevent polysulfide shuttling simultaneously, most of the researches focused on the solid or quasi-solid state electrolyte.[6f] Armand et al.[11] studied solid state polymer electrolytes (SPEs) using poly (ethylene oxide) (PEO) as polymer matrix, lithium–sulfur batteries using PEO-based SPE exhibits high specific discharge capacity of 1395 mAh g−1.
[M0023 p1] He et al.[12] developed solid state lithium–sulfur batteries using Li1.5Al0.5Ge1.5(PO4)3 (LAGP) as the electrolyte, under the elevated temperature of 55 °C and the current density of 20 mA g−1, a capacity of 1524 mAh g−1 could be obtained.

[M0027 p2] What’s more, the active mass loading usually cannot meet the demand of practical application.
[M0028 p2] Therefore, there is still a long way to go before all-solid state lithium– sulfur batteries application.
[M0029 p2] Flame retardant electrolyte has attracted wide interest as a new research hotspot, Wang et al.[13] added tris(2,2,2-trifluoroethyl) phosphite as the flame retardant additive into the conventional carbonate electrolyte to obtain the flame inhibiting electrolyte, which enables the sulfur@PAN composite cathode lithium–sulfur battery superior long cycle stability and rate performance (800 mAh g−1 at 10 C).
[M0030 p2] However, this flame inhibiting electrolyte could still caught fire even though the electrolyte could be self-extinguished after several seconds.
[M0031 p2] Continues study was carried on and they proposed an intrinsic flame retardant electrolyte using flame-retardant triethyl phosphate and high flashpoint 1H,1H,5H-octafluoropentyl-1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethyl ether (TTE) as cosolvent,[14] the lithium–sulfur batteries delivers high discharge capacity and stable cycle performance.
[M0032 p2] Hexafluorocyclotriphosphazene as a kind of flame retardant additive was introduced into conventional 1,3-dioxolane (DOL)/dimethoxyether (DME) electrolyte for lithium–sulfur batteries,[15] but the cycle performance is not stable enough.
[M0033 p2] Recently, Wang et al.[16] reported a kind of localized high concentration electrolyte composed by the inert fluoroalkyl ether of TTE and highly concentrated lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl) imide/DME, the high flash point and inert nature of the fluoroalkyl ether makes the electrolyte hard to be ignited.
[M0034 p2] The discharge capacity of 775 mAh g−1 was obtained, but the rate performance and cathode sulfur loading needs to be further improved.
[M0035 p2] These excellent works on the flame retardant electrolyte of lithium–sulfur batteries still have some unsatisfactory aspects, current researches are mainly focused on S@PAN and S@C composite cathode, their moderate sulfur content and energy-consuming preparation process in the inert atmosphere at high temperature hinders the commercialization of lithium–sulfur batteries.
[M0036 p2] Herein, we proposed a strategy of inflammable electrolyte combined with nanoconductive carbon-coated separators to improve the overall performance of bare sulfur cathode–based lithium–sulfur batteries.
[M0037 p2] The as-prepared inflammable electrolyte was composed by simply mixing 2 m LiTFSI/DME with TTE in the volume ratio of 1:4 (denoted as TDE, the average LiTFSI concentration is about 0.4 m), it possess both properties of reducing polysulfide shuttling and stabilizing the lithium metal, the inflammable nature of TTE can remarkably improve the safety of lithium–sulfur batteries. the TDE electrolyte coupled with nanoconductive carbon-coated separator (denote as CS separator) enables the bare sulfur cathode–based batteries excellent rate performance of ≈350 mAh g−1 at 4 C, the initial discharge capacity could reach up to 1187 mAh g−1 at the cathode sulfur loading of 4 mg cm−2 (equals to the areal capacity of 6.7 mAh g−1).
[M0038 p2] What is more, the batteries using TDE electrolyte could maintain a stable cycle performance at high temperature (60 °C).
[M0039 p2] To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work of flame retardant electrolyte reported for the high areal capacity bare sulfur cathode–based lithium–sulfur battery so far. 2.
[M0040 p2] Results and Discussion 2.1.
[M0041 p2] Physical and Chemical Properties TDE electrolyte was prepared by simply mixing 2 m LiTFSI/ DME (with 0.1 m LiNO3, all of the following mentioned contain the same amount) with TTE in different volume ratio.
[M0042 p2] Due to the good compatibility of TTE and DME, the as prepared TDE electrolyte shows a uniform and transparent characteristic (Figure 1a).
[M0043 p2] According to the flame retardant test shows in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), when the volume ratio of TTE in the mixture solvent is higher than 80% (TTE:2 m LiTFSI/DME = 4:1), spray formed by the TDE electrolyte can effectively extinguish the burning paper roll, however, with the volume ratio of inert TTE increasing, the ionic conductivity is decreasing, thus the volume ratio of TTE: 2 m LiTFSI/ DME = 4:1 is chosen for the flame retardant TDE electrolyte (the concentration of LiNO3 in the TDE electrolyte is around 0.025 m).
[M0044 p2] As shown in Figure 1b, TDE electrolyte exhibits the viscosity of 1.091 mPa s, lower than pristine 2 m LiTFSI/DME and conventional electrolyte (1 m LiTFSI DOL/DME with 1 wt% LiNO3, denoted as LS002 in the following section).
[M0045 p2] Raman spectroscopy (Figure 1c) was used to investigate the solvate structure and the coordination between Li+ and solvent.
[M0046 p2] With the increasing of LiTFSI concentration in DME, the peak of COC located around 820–830 cm−1 is weakened, peak intensity of interaction between Li+ and O atom at around 870–880 cm−1 is growing, indicating the interaction between Li+ and O atom is getting stronger.
[M0047 p2] However, no matter what concentration of DME is mixed with TTE in volume ration of 1:4, the COC peak of TTE around 830–840 cm−1 is hardly changed in intensity and location, demonstrated that the TTE molecular barely binds with Li+.
[M0048 p2] As can be seen from Figure S2 (Supporting Information), the band at 1246.7 cm−1 is attributed to aggregated LiTFSI, the band at 1243.1 cm−1 is attributed to free TFSI−.
[M0049 p2] Both 1 m LiTFSI/DME and 2 m LiTFSI/DME shows the same TFSI− Raman shift at 1243.1 cm−1, which means the solvate structure of 1 m and 2 m LiTFSI/DME is the same.
[M0050 p2] After blended 2 m LiTFSI/DME with TTE in the volume ratio of 1:4 to get TDE electrolyte, the band of TFSI- shifts towards higher wave number at 1244.8 cm−1, this is very close to the TFSI− peak position of 5 m LiTFSI/DME, which means the contact ion pair is formed.
[M0051 p2] The above experimental results show that after blend 2 m LiTFSI/DME with TTE, the interaction between DME, Li+, and TFSI− is enhanced (Figure 1d).
[M0052 p2] The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) plots of Stainless steel/Stainless steel cells using TDE, LS002 electrolyte, and 2 m LiTFSI/DME electrolyte at room temperature is presented in Figure 2a, ionic conductivity of TDE (0.990 mS cm−1) is slightly lower than that of the other two comparisons, and this can be attributed to the lower salt concentration (0.4 m LiTFSI with 0.025 m LiNO3) in the TDE electrolyte.
[M0053 p2] Figure 2b shows the electrochemical stability window of the 2 m LiTFSI DME, LS002 electrolyte, and TDE electrolyte, the decomposition voltage of TDE electrolyte starts at 4.7 V versus Li/Li+, and it is stable enough for the lithium–sulfur battery.
[M0054 p2] Lithium transfer number (tLi+) of TDE electrolyte is around 0.4842.

[M0069 p4] Both TDE and LS002 electrolyte steadily cycled over 2500 h at the current density of 1.0 mA cm−2 to a 1.0 mAh cm−2 capacity (Figure 3d), the overpotential of TDE electrolyte–based Li/Li symmetric cell is always less than the LS002 electrolyte–based Li/Li symmetric cell (insert plot in Figure 3d), which indicated the lower interface impedance of TDE electrolyte/electrode interface.

[M0078 p5] Interface Characterization Lithium metal was peeled off from Li/Li symmetric cells after 100 h cycles at the current density of 1.0 mA cm−2 and 1.0 mAh cm−2, the morphology of lithium metal surface and cross section (Figure 3e–h) is tested by scanning electron microscopy (SEM).
[M0079 p5] Li metal electrode from Li/Li symmetric cells using the LS002 electrolyte shows a porous and fragmented surface (Figure 3e), the specific area of Li metal surface was enlarged thus more interfacial reactions would be introduced and causing unstable SEI interface and continuous electrolyte consumption.
[M0080 p5] The rough and easily peeled surface is easy to loss electrical connection with Li metal electrode and then bring about dead Li, which eventually leading to battery failure.
[M0081 p5] On the contrary, the deposited Li film from Li/Li symmetric cells using TDE electrolyte shows dendrites free surface (Figure 3g), besides, it can be seen from the cross section (Figure 3f,h) that the lithium electrode from Li/Li cells using TDE electrolyte has a denser deposit Li layer.
[M0082 p5] The deposited Li layer is flexible enough even if the lithium electrode is distorted during cutting with a scalpel, the deposited Li layer is still firmly attached to the Li electrode.
[M0083 p5] To further reveal the unique surface properties in TDE electrolyte, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and time-offlight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) analysis was employed to reveal the component and the component’s distribution of the SEI layer from Li/Li symmetric cells using different electrolytes.
[M0084 p5] As can be seen from Figure 4a–f, the CC/CH, CF3, LiF, Li2CO3, Li2O, and organic OCO peaks appear on the surface of Li electrode from the symmetric cells using TDE electrolyte, those chemical components is possibly generated from two ways: 1) reaction with air before sample transfer into XPS vacuum chamber, the Li2CO3 and Li2O could be generated; 2) residual of LiTFSI (CF3) and decomposition of TDE electrolyte (LiF, Li2O, and Li2CO3).

[M0109 p7] Li–S Battery Performance Li/S cells with bare sulfur cathode using TDE and LS002 electrolyte were assembled respectively (sulfur loading of ≈1.2 mg cm−2, equals to areal capacity at around 2 mAh cm−2), as shown in Figure 6a, all the batteries were charged/discharged at 0.5 C (equals to current density at around 1 mA cm−2).
[M0110 p7] TDE electrolyte can effectively reduce polysulfide shuttling, but there are still small amount of polysulfide could be dissolved in the electrolyte according to the UV–vis results of transparent cells mentioned above.
[M0111 p7] The lower solvation ability to the polysulfide could lower the utilization of sulfur,[21] thus the bare sulfur cathode–based Li/S batteries using TDE electrolyte shows relatively lower discharge capacity (≈200 mAh g−1 at 0.5 C) than LS002 electrolyte.
[M0112 p7] But it also brought some positive effects, the cycling stability of TDE electrolyte–based Li/S batteries is improved, the discharge capacity keeps almost unchanged over 900 cycles.
[M0113 p7] Nanoconductive carbon-layer-coated separator (denoted as CS) could further suppress the polysulfide shuttling, furthermore, nanoconductive carbon layer could act as the polysulfide scavenger by provide more redox reaction sites,[22] thus, the utilization of sulfur can be improved.
[M0114 p7] To fully improve the battery’s cycle stability and performance, CS separator with the coating layer thickness of 10 um were utilized in Li/S batteries (Figure S6, Supporting Information).
[M0115 p7] As shown in Figure 6a, batteries using TDE electrolyte coupled with CS separator showed much higher discharge capacity (first discharge capacity 903 mAh g−1 at 0.5 C) than cells using TDE electrolyte alone.
[M0116 p7] This could be attributed to the faster reaction kinetics of polysulfide with the help of CS separator.
[M0117 p7] Accelerated conversion of soluble high order polysulfide to insoluble low order polysulfide avoids the TDE electrolyte gets saturated, the continuous solvation ability of TDE electrolyte towards polysulfide improves the utilization of sulfur.
[M0118 p7] When coupled with CS separator, batteries using LS002 electrolyte showed slightly higher discharge capacity than batteries using TDE electrolyte during the first 200 cycles.
[M0119 p7] This is because higher amount of polysulfide dissolved into LS002 electrolyte and captured by nanoconductive carbon coating, thus the sulfur utilization is significantly improved in a short term.
[M0120 p7] However, after around 200 cycling, the leading edge of the LS002 electrolyte disappeared.
[M0121 p7] In the subsequent cycles, cells using TDE electrolyte shows higher discharge capacity and more stable cycle performance.
[M0122 p7] After 1000 cycles, batteries using TDE electrolyte coupled with CS separator shows 49.4% capacity retention, on the contrary, batteries using LS002 electrolyte coupled with CS separator is only 23.7% of the initial capacity remaining.
[M0123 p7] Large amount of polysulfide dissolved out from cathode in cells using LS002 electrolyte, excess polysulfide can still pass through the nanoconductive carbon coating layer to reach the lithium metal anode, leading to the fast capacity fading.
[M0124 p7] The TDE electrolyte has a weaker ability to dissolve polysulfide, and the polysulfide shuttle is further inhibited with the help of the carbon coating layer on the cathode side of the separator.
[M0125 p7] What’s more, TDE electrolyte helps the lithium metal anode forming a dendritefree interface, thus the cells using TDE electrolyte is more stable than cells using LS002 electrolyte (Figure 7).

[M0139 p9] Self-discharge, which is a key roadblock for the lithium– sulfur batteries, is caused by the shuttling of the lithium polysulfide.
[M0140 p9] After several cycles of charge-discharge, the polysulfides dissolved into the electrolyte to form the “catholytes,” during the resting period of lithium–sulfur batteries, the as formed catholytes react with lithium metal anode spontaneously, causing self-discharge and resulting in voltage drop and irreversible capacity loss.[23] Due to the high solubility of LS002 electrolyte to the polysulfide, the severe shuttling effect can cause serious self-discharge, OCV of cells using conventional LS002 electrolyte shows an obvious decay from 2.35 to 2.15 V, which indicates severe capacity loss during resting (Figure S7, Supporting Information).
[M0141 p9] It is noteworthy that the utilization of TDE electrolyte improves the resistance of self-discharge, after charged to the cutoff voltage of 2.8 V, the cells were resting for 72 hours, during the resting, cells using TDE electrolyte shows stable open cutoff voltage (OCV) at around 2.3 V without obvious decay.
[M0142 p9] The released self-discharge in the Li–S cells can be ascribed to the reduced polysulfide solubility of TDE electrolyte.
[M0143 p9] TDE electrolyte/CS separator–based batteries presents superior rate performance (Figure 6d), the capacity maintains 350 mAh g−1 even at the discharge rate of 4 C (equals to current density of 6.7 mA cm−2 at the sulfur loading of 1 mg cm−2).
[M0144 p9] After 4C charge/discharge, the discharge rate turns back to 0.5 C, and the discharge capacity can still maintains about 900 mAh g−1.
[M0145 p9] In order to verify the stability of the electrolyte under extreme operating conditions, batteries using TDE and LS002 electrolytes were cycled at 60 °C (Figure 6e).
[M0146 p9] TDE-CS separator– based batteries electrolyte shows initial discharge capacity of 1557 mAh g−1, close to theoretical capacity, and after 100 cycles the capacity of batteries still remains 870 mAh g−1.
[M0147 p9] Batteries using LS002 electrolyte coupled with CS separator shows initial discharge capacity of 1671.5 mAh g−1, then the capacity is sharply declined.
[M0148 p9] The SEM image of Li metal anode in the Li/S cells after 10 cycles at 60 °C is shown in Figure 6f,g, the Li metal anode cycled in LS002 electrolyte exhibits a porous, cracked surface.
[M0149 p9] On the contrary, lithium metal anode cycled in TDE electrolyte shows a dendrite free interface with only several nodule-like Li deposit.
[M0150 p9] Under the elevated temperature, conventional electrolytes are more reactive with lithium metal anode, continued consumption of electrolyte and severe shuttle of polysulfide cause a sharp capacity drop in high temperature.
[M0151 p9] TDE-CS separator–based batteries exhibit excellent stability and discharge capacity at 60 °C, which is due to optimized electrode-electrolyte interface.
[M0152 p9] TDE electrolyte and CS separator synergistically reduce polysulfides shuttling is also an important contributor.
[M0153 p9] Excellent high temperature performance allows the battery to withstand extreme conditions, thereby broadening the scope of battery application.
[M0154 p9] To meet the requirements for practical application, higher areal sulfur mass loading of over 4 mg cm−2 cathode was employed, the long term cycling performance is shown in Figure 6h.
[M0155 p9] The first discharge capacity of Li/S batteries using TDE electrolyte coupled with CS separator could reach up to 1187 mAh g−1, after 56 cycles, discharge capacity of 806 mAh g−1 remained, CE is higher than 97%.
[M0156 p9] The initial discharge capacity of Li/S batteries using LS002 electrolyte coupled with CS separator could reach up to 1223.9 mAh g−1, however, the deteriorated anode/electrolyte interface (Figure 6i) lead to low CE value (lowest CE value is at around 80.6%, the highest CE value is at around 93.8%) and rapid capacity decline.
[M0157 p9] To the best of our knowledge, this is the best performance of flame-retardant electrolyte for high sulfur mass loading bare sulfur cathode– based Li/S cells (Table S1, Supporting Information). 3.
