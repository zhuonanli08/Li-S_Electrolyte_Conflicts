[M0002 p1] Therefore, designing electrolytes capable of adaptively adjusting solvating forces in response to changes in the electrostatic interactions of Li2Sn is imperative for practical Li-S batteries.
[M0003 p1] Herein, we propose an electrostatic interaction force matching concept that utilize high polarizability solvents with dynamical solvating power to accommodate the fluctuation in electrostatic interactions of Li2Sn.
[M0004 p1] Molecules with conjugated structure exhibit significantly delocalized electron cloud, which is capable of responding in real time to the stimulus of local-electric-field variations from Sn 2– to enable charge–density–dependent solvation regulation of Li2Sn.

[M0008 p1] Introduction The escalating energy crisis and environmental challenges have sparked unprecedented interest in renewable energy and efficient en­ ergy storage technologies.
[M0009 p1] Among emerging solutions, Li–S batteries stand out due to their remarkable theoretical energy density of 2600 Wh/kg and abundant raw material reserves, positioning them as a promising next–generation energy storage system [1–4].
[M0010 p1] However, their practical energy density and cycling stability are severely restricted by sluggish reactions kinetics and shuttle effect, especially at lean electro­ lyte conditions [5–7].
[M0011 p1] It has been demonstrated that regulations of electrolyte solvating forces have the capacity of optimizing the solvation and reactivity of Li2Sn and addressing above issues [8–10].
[M0012 p1] Conventional electrolytes with strong solvating forces can enhance the dispersion of short-chain Li2Sn with strong electrostatic interactions and improve re­ action kinetics, while those with weak solvating forces can confine the long-chain Li2Sn, which are prone to diffusion, on the cathode side to prevent shuttling [11–14].
[M0013 p1] Although these conventional electrolytes can address part issues in Li-S batteries, their solvating forces fail to adapt to the variations in electrostatic interactions of Li2Sn and achieve force-matching with all Li2Sn, which may exacerbate other issues: highly solvating electrolytes exacerbate the Li2Sn shuttle effect, while sparingly solvating electrolytes aggravate the sluggish reaction kinetics.

[M0015 p1] It’s difficult for conventional electrolytes to simultaneously ensure the cycling stability and the improvement of energy density of Li-S batteries [19–21].
[M0016 p1] Therefore, designing electrolytes that can both enhance reaction kinetics and suppress the shuttle effect is imperative for the practical application of Li–S batteries and remains substantia challenges.

[M0023 p2] The fixed solvating forces of electrolyte and bidirectional regulation requirements for solvating forces of electrolyte dictated by the dynamic changes in the charge density of Sn 2– result in distinct sol­ vation microenvironments for different Li2Sn, in which the degree of solvation and reactivities of Li2Sn differ significantly and lead to the contradiction between shuttle suppression and kinetic optimization.

[M0026 p2] In this work, we propose an electrostatic interaction force matching concept that leverages the Sn 2––charge–density–dependent ion–dipole interaction between Li2Sn and high polarizability solvent molecules to achieve selective solvation and stable dispersion of various Li2Sn.
[M0027 p2] The solvent molecules with high electronic polarizability are capable of undergoing electronic rearrangement in response to the local electro­ static field of Sn 2– across different chain lengths, and the degree of po­ larization and dipole charge density depend on the charge density of Sn 2– [27].

[M0227 p8] Although DMTMSA behaves as a "weak solvent" in interactions with Li+, its nitrogen atoms acquire a positive charge upon induc­ tion–polarization, enabling the formation of ion–dipole interactions with Sn 2–, whose strength depends on the charge densities of Sn 2–.
