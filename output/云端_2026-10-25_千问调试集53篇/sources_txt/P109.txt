[M0011 p1] Introduction Lithium-sulfur batteries are batteries with a liquid cathode, since the electroactive materials of the positive electrode (sulfur and intermediate products of its electrochemical reduction, lithium polysulfides) are soluble in the electrolyte [1e7].
[M0012 p1] The presence of active materials of the positive electrode in the liquid phase determines the considerable effect of the physicochemical properties of the electrolyte system on the regularities of chemical, physicochemical and electrochemical processes that occur in lithiumsulfur batteries during their charge, discharge and storage.
[M0013 p1] Ionic conductivity and viscosity are the most important properties of electrolyte systems as they affect the regularities of electrochemical transformations of the dissolved active materials (sulfur and lithium polysulfides).
[M0014 p1] The ionic conductivity and viscosity of the electrolyte systems are determined by many factors, the main ones being: the polarity and solvation properties of the solvents; the size, geometry and donor properties of anions in the support salts; and the concentration of the support salts in the electrolyte solutions.
[M0015 p1] The regularities of electrochemical transformations of sulfur and lithium polysulfides are determined by their species in the electrolyte solutions.
[M0016 p1] By chemical nature, lithium polysulfides (Li2Sn) are salts of polysulfane acids (H2Sn) [8].
[M0017 p1] Lithium polysulfides, dissolved in aprotic dipolar solvents, can undergo heterolytic (electrolytic) [9e11] (Equations (1) and (2)) and homolytic dissociation [12e22] (Equations (3) and (4)).

[M0030 p2] The radical species of lithium polysulfides, similarly to the molecular ones, can undergo electrolytic dissociation (Equation (5)): (5) As a result of the liability of lithium polysulfides to undergo heterolytic (electrolytic) and homolytic dissociation, electrolyte solutions generally simultaneously contain a lot of diverse molecular, radical, ion radical and ionic species of sulfur with various number of atoms that exist in dynamic equilibrium.
[M0031 p2] The species of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions determine their reactivity.
[M0032 p2] Solutions of lithium polysulfides in aprotic dipolar solvents have high ionic conductivity that is comparable to that of readily ionizable lithium salts [24,25].
[M0033 p2] A specific feature of polysulfide solutions is that they have high viscosity, which is considerably higher than that of solutions of lithium salts with the same concentration.
[M0034 p2] For example, the 0.3m solution of Li2S6 in sulfolane has conductivity of 0.987 mS/cm and viscosity of 30 mPa s and 0.5m solution of LiClO4 in sulfolane has conductivity of 1.897 mS/cm and viscosity of 19 mPa s [26].
[M0035 p2] This indicates that lithium polysulfides form intermolecular associates in solutions [24e26].
[M0036 p2] The presence of support lithium salts in the electrolytes of lithium-sulfur cells affects the species of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions and hence their reactivity.
[M0037 p2] In fact, in the presence of well ionizable salts (salts with high electrolytic dissociation constants), the electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides will be suppressed to a considerable extent.
[M0038 p2] In support of arguments on suppression of electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides, it is also possible to bring the fact that there is no synergetic effect in the specific conductivity of mixed solutions of lithium polysulfides and lithium perchlorate [26].
[M0039 p2] The degree of electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides will also decrease in the presence of salts with low electrolytic dissociation constants but to a much smaller extent.
[M0040 p2] Furthermore, the support lithium salts, present in electrolytes, result in cationic linking of polysulfide anions [24], which impairs the transport properties of electrolyte systems and the segmental mobility of sulfur atoms in polysulfide chains.
[M0041 p2] The effect of lithium polysulfides on the properties of electrolyte solutions has been studied in a number of works [25e29].
[M0042 p2] It has been shown that the presence of lithium polysulfides affects the ionic conductivity of electrolyte solutions and increases their viscosity.
[M0043 p2] Even a brief consideration of the structure and properties of lithium polysulfide solutions in the electrolyte systems allows one to assume that the properties of lithium-sulfur batteries will largely depend on the nature and concentration of lithium salts in electrolytes.
[M0044 p2] For example, the effect of the concentration of lithium salts on the performance of lithium-sulfur cells was studied in a number of publications [6,30e39].
[M0045 p2] However, the published results of studies do not allow one to judge to the full extent about the mechanisms of the effect of the concentration of support salts on the processes into lithium-sulfur cells.
[M0046 p2] The purpose of this work is to clarify the mechanism by which the concentration of support salts in electrolytes affects the performance of lithium-sulfur cells, such as the depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur, the rate of capacity depletion and the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells. 2.

[M0079 p3] Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane on the charge-discharge voltage profiles of lithium-sulfur cells Studies have shown that the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane affects the charge-discharge voltage profiles of lithium-sulfur cells (Fig.1а).
[M0080 p3] The discharge voltage profiles of lithium-sulfur cells obtained in the first cycle contain two plateaus, irrespective of the lithium perchlorate concentration.
[M0081 p3] As an exception, the discharge voltage profiles of the cells with a highconcentration electrolyte containing 2.8М LiClO4 manifest only one high voltage plateau.
[M0082 p3] However, as lithium-sulfur cells with the high-concentrated electrolyte (2.8М LiClO4) are cycled, a low voltage plateau appears on the charge and discharge voltage profiles, and both the charge and discharge capacities increase (Fig. 1b and c).
[M0083 p3] As the concentration of lithium perchlorate in the electrolyte solution increases, the discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells first increases and then starts to decrease at salt concentrations in the electrolyte close to 1M (Fig. 2).
[M0084 p3] The decrease in the discharge capacity occurs due to a predominant capacity decrease on the second (low voltage) plateau.
[M0085 p3] As the lithium perchlorate concentration in the electrolyte increases, the voltages of the high voltage plateaus decrease, whereas those of the low voltage plateaus pass through a maximum (Fig. 3a).
[M0086 p3] At the high voltage plateau, there are two sections with different rates of capacity change.
[M0087 p3] The voltage of the high voltage stage was determined as the voltage in the middle of the first section of the high voltage plateau on the discharge curve, which usually corresponded to 100 mAh/g(S).
[M0088 p3] The voltage of the low voltage stage of the discharge was assumed to be equal to the value of the voltage at the maximum, which in most cases corresponded to the middle of the low voltage discharge plateau (Fig. 3b). 3.2.
[M0089 p3] Effect of the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane on capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling The general regularities of charge and discharge capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling weakly depend on the support salt concentration, except for lithium-sulfur cells containing high-concentrated electrolytes (2.4М and 2.8М LiClO4) (Fig. 4).
[M0090 p3] At moderate support salt concentrations in the electrolyte (0.5e2.0М), the discharge capacity first quickly decreases during the cycling, then the capacity decrease slows down, and then the capacity starts to decrease abruptly again.
[M0091 p3] The shapes of the capacity depletion dependences of lithiumsulfur cells with high-concentration electrolytes on cycle number differ from similar dependences observed for lithium-sulfur cells with moderately concentrated electrolytes.
[M0092 p3] Of special interest is the dependence of capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells with the electrolyte containing 2.4М LiClO4 on cycle number.
[M0093 p3] The cells with the electrolyte containing 2.4М LiClO4 feature the smallest rate of depletion in both the charge and discharge capacities during cycling.
[M0094 p3] As lithium-sulfur cells with the high-concentration electrolyte (2.8М LiClO4) are cycled, the charge and discharge capacities increase and the maximum capacity values are reached by about the 40th cycle (Fig. 4).
[M0095 p3] After that, both the charge and discharge capacities start to decrease.
[M0096 p3] It should be noted that the dependences of capacity depletion on the number of charge-discharge cycles are functions of several parameters, such as the rate of destruction of electrolyte system components on the negative electrode, the passivation rate of the surface of carbon particles, the sulfur redistribution in the volume of the positive electrode in lithium-sulfur cells, etc.
[M0097 p3] The concentration of the support salt in the electrolyte affects the rates of all these processes but to a different degree.
[M0098 p3] Therefore, no strict and unambiguous correlations are observed between the shapes of the dependences of capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during E.V.

[M0101 p4] At different stages of cycling, the rate of capacity depletion is determined by the action of various factors.
[M0102 p4] In the first-second cycles, the redistribution of sulfur over the volume of the lithiumsulfur cells has a significant contribution to the depletion in the capacity.
[M0103 p4] At the initial stages of cycling (first 10e30 cycles), the main contribution to the reduction in capacity is made by the displacement of sulfur onto the face of the sulfur electrode.
[M0104 p4] The resulting uneven distribution of sulfur over the volume of the sulfur electrode leads to a rapid decrease in the charge and discharge capacities of lithium-sulfur cells.
[M0105 p4] With subsequent cycling, the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells depletes mainly due to the simultaneous action of two factors - the decomposition of the electrolyte on the lithium electrode and the passivation of the surface of the carbon component of the sulfur electrode with poor soluble products of electrochemical reactions.
[M0106 p4] With a limited electrolyte content in lithium-sulfur cells after a certain number of cycles, the cell capacity decreases sharply to almost zero.
[M0107 p4] It should be mentioned that all of the factors listed above act simultaneously, but at different stages of the cycling, their contribution to the overall depletion in capacity is different. 3.3.

[M0115 p5] Discussion Three aspects should be kept in mind in a consideration of the mechanisms of the effect of the concentration of support salts on the discharge capacity and cycling performance of lithium-sulfur cells:  species of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions;  the association-dissociation equilibria in electrolyte solutions containing lithium salts and lithium polysulfides;  the processes of solvation of lithium ions contained in lithium polysulfides and support salts. 4.1.
[M0116 p5] Species of sulfur and lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions The electrochemical reduction of sulfur is a complex multistage process [43,44] involving electrochemical reactions, reactions of electrolytic and homolytic dissociation, and disproportionation of intermediate products of sulfur reduction - lithium polysulfides.
[M0117 p5] Species of sulfur are determined by the state of charge of lithium-sulfur cells.
[M0118 p5] Sulfur in the totally charged lithium-sulfur cell exists mostly in molecular forms.
[M0119 p5] Since elementary sulfur is prone to form molecules with various lengths and can exist in various crystallographic modifications, available experimental data hardly provide enough grounds for judging about the particular species of sulfur in charged lithium-sulfur cells after a number of charge- Fig. 2.

[M0123 p6] It can only be stated that sulfur in charged lithium-sulfur cells exists in solid state due to its poor solubility in the electrolyte.
[M0124 p6] However, it should also be kept in mind that even totally charged lithium-sulfur cells can contain a fraction of sulfur in high ordered lithium polysulfides dissolved in the electrolyte, as well as lithium sulfide.
[M0125 p6] The fraction of molecular species of sulfur in lithium-sulfur cells decreases with discharge, whereas the fraction of ionic species increases.
[M0126 p6] Ionic species of sulfur are lithium polysulfides with various lengths.
[M0127 p6] Lithium polysulfides do not exist in individual state but do exist as solvates with various compositions in solutions.
[M0128 p6] Solvate complexes of lithium polysulfides can be isolated in individual state.
[M0129 p6] When solvate complexes of lithium polysulfides are formed, solvent molecules are bound in solvate shells of lithium ions contained therein [45].
[M0130 p6] The species of lithium polysulfides in electrolytes are determined by the support salt concentration.
[M0131 p6] Due to the nature of the anions, it should be expected that the electrolytic dissociation constants of lithium polysulfides are considerably smaller than that of lithium perchlorate.
[M0132 p6] If this assumption is correct, the fraction of ionic species of lithium polysulfides in the electrolyte solution would decrease with the increase in the concentration of lithium perchlorate whereas the fraction of neutral species would increase.

[M0141 p7] The species of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions would affect their electrochemical activity.
[M0142 p7] It can be assumed that, due to the compensation of the negative charge on polysulfide anions by lithium ions, lithium polysulfides in neutral (molecular) and radical species would have the highest reactivity, whereas lithium polysulfides in ionic species would have the lowest reactivity.
[M0143 p7] We believe that compensation of the negative charge on the polysulfide chain will facilitate the transfer of the electron from the electrode to the polysulfide anion due to the decrease in the electrostatic interaction of the electron and the polysulfide anion. 4.2.
[M0144 p7] Association-dissociation equilibria of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions A specific feature of lithium polysulfides is their liability to disproportionation reactions that result in lithium polysulfides with both smaller and larger amount of sulfur atoms in the polysulfide chain, as well as lithium sulfide.
[M0145 p7] The liability of lithium polysulfides to undergo disproportionation, electrolytic and homolytic dissociation results in the situation that the electrolyte solutions in lithium-sulfur cells simultaneously contain lithium polysulfides in various species: molecular, ionic, ion-radical and radical species.
[M0146 p7] The ability of lithium polysulfides to undergo disproportionation, electrolytic and homolytic dissociation affects the mechanisms of electrochemical processes that occur in lithium-sulfur cells during charge and discharge.

[M0158 p7] Our hypothetical scheme of electrochemical reduction of sulfur is in good agreement with the results of other studies.
[M0159 p7] The review [48] shows that the electrochemical reduction of sulfur involves complex and sequential equilibrium - reactions of electrochemical reduction of sulfur and lithium polysulfides and disproportionation and association with formation of anion and radical (mainly S3-) species of lithium polysulfides.
[M0160 p7] It is suggested that lithium sulfide (Li2S) can be formed both in electrochemical and chemical reactions.
[M0161 p7] On the surface of sulfur and lithium electrodes, various species of lithium polysulfides (di-anions, anions, radicals, and neutral species) and support salts participate in electrochemical and chemical reactions. 4.3.
[M0162 p7] Effect of solvation of lithium ions contained in lithium polysulfides and support salts on the discharge capacity of lithiumsulfur cells The electrochemical reduction of sulfur is accompanied by the formation of lithium polysulfides, compounds that are well soluble in electrolytes.
[M0163 p7] Dissolution of lithium polysulfides in the electrolyte solution results in binding of solvent molecules in solvate shells of lithium ions contained in lithium polysulfides (Equations (8), (12) and (15)).
[M0164 p7] As lithium-sulfur cells are discharged, the concentration of lithium polysulfides in electrolytes and hence the number of bound solvent molecules increase.
[M0165 p7] The increase in concentration of lithium polysulfides in the electrolyte solutions occurs until E.V.

[M0170 p8] The increase in the concentration of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions increases their viscosity and decreases the ionic conductivity [25].
[M0171 p8] During reduction, the mean molecular mass of lithium polysulfides decreases.
[M0172 p8] As a result, the viscosity of electrolyte solutions somewhat decreases and the ionic conductivity increases [49].
[M0173 p8] Furthermore, the mobility of lithium polysulfides increases due to two factors that act in the same direction, i.e., a decrease in their molecular mass and a decrease in the viscosity of electrolyte solutions.
[M0174 p8] Since solvent molecules are involved in the electrochemical reduction of sulfur and high-ordered lithium polysulfides, the depth of sulfur reduction is determined by the number of free solvent molecules, i.e., the solvent molecules not bound in solvate shells of lithium ions in the support salts.
[M0175 p8] To ensure complete sulfur reduction, enough free solvent molecules should be available for the solvation of lithium tetrasulfide.
[M0176 p8] Equations (8), (12) and (15) make it possible to calculate the number of solvent molecules required for the solvation of lithium tetrasulfide, as well as the molar ratio of sulfur and solvent ensuring complete reduction of sulfur.
[M0177 p8] Studies that we performed previously [45] have shown that the solvate number of lithium ion in lithium tetrasulfide in sulfolane solutions is 2.
[M0178 p8] In this case, the solvate number of lithium tetrasulfide is 4, while the molar ratio of the solvent to elementary sulfur is 1:1.
[M0179 p8] To estimate the possible depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur in the high voltage plateau, we performed calculations on the effect of average solvate number of lithium ion and support salt concentration on the number of free solvent molecules that can solvate the lithium ions in lithium tetrasulfide formed (Table 1, Equations S.1 & S.2 are given in Supplementary materials).
[M0180 p8] It follows from the calculations that electrochemical reduction of sulfur to lithium tetrasulfide in electrolytes with the lithium perchlorate concentrations studied and with the electrolyte content of 6.7 ml per 1 mg of S (4 ml per 1 mA h(S)) is only possible if the average solvate number of lithium ions is close to 2.
[M0181 p8] In other words, in the case of sulfolane, the minimum required value of solvate number is 2.
[M0182 p8] The calculation results are in good agreement with the experimental data.
[M0183 p8] In fact, as follows from Fig. 1, electrochemical reduction of sulfur to lithium tetrasulfide occurs even in electrolytes with high lithium perchlorate concentrations.
[M0184 p8] However, the reduction depth is limited by the number of free solvent molecules.
[M0185 p8] For example, at a salt concentration of 2.0M, the amount of free molecules of sulfolane in the electrolyte is 5.7M.
[M0186 p8] This amount of free sulfolane molecules is sufficient to fully solvate lithium ions in the system if the solvate number of the lithium ion is 2.
[M0187 p8] Sulfolane, due to the presence of two equivalent oxygen atoms, can act as a bidentate ligand, and one molecule of sulfolane can saturate two coordination sites in the solvation shell of lithium ion.

[M0190 p8] If the free solvent molecules are not sufficient to completely reduce the sulfur to lithium tetrasulfide, then on the discharge curves of lithium-sulfur cells, the capacity value obtained at the high voltage plateau, is less than the theoretical value of 418 mAh/g(S).
[M0191 p8] A comparison of the calculated and experimentally obtained discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells shows that the formation of lithium tetrasulfide is possible in all studied electrolyte solutions:  if the average solvate number of lithium ion is 2, the formation of lithium tetrasulfide is possible in all the electrolyte systems studied;  if the average solvate number of lithium ion is 3, the formation of lithium tetrasulfide is possible in electrolytes containing from 0.1M to 1.0M lithium perchlorate  if the average solvate number of lithium ion is 4, the formation of lithium tetrasulfide is possible in electrolytes containing from 0.1M to 0.5M lithium perchlorate. 4.4.

[M0197 p9] The dependence of the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells on the concentration of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane is bell-shaped with a maximum at a salt concentration of about 1M (Fig. 2).

[M0200 p9] On the one hand, the increase in the support salt concentration increases the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte system, and on the other hand, it decreases the mobility of lithium polysulfides due to the increase in viscosity (Fig. 6).
[M0201 p9] We believe that the depth of the electrochemical reduction of lithium polysulfides is determined to a greater extent by the rate of charge transfer than by the transport properties of the medium.
[M0202 p9] The rate of charge transfer is controlled by the species of lithium polysulfides in electrolyte solutions, which depend on the concentration of the support salt.
[M0203 p9] Probably, that is why there is a correlation between the shape of isotherms of the conductivity and the depth of the electrochemical reduction of sulfur.
[M0204 p9] The rate of electrochemical reduction of high ordered lithium polysulfides is greater than the for low ordered lithium polysulfides indicated on the reactivity control of the electrochemical reduction of lithium polysulfides.
[M0205 p9] At the same time, the mobility of high ordered lithium polysulfides is less than the mobility of low ordered lithium polysulfides.
[M0206 p9] The rate of electrochemical reduction of lithium polysulfides with different order can be judged from the value of the internal resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at low and high depths of discharge, which is mainly determined by the resistance of charge transfer.
[M0207 p9] A low value of the internal resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at low discharge depths and a greater resistance at high discharge depths (Fig. 7a) indicate greater rate of reduction of sulfur and high ordered lithium polysulfides compared to the reduction rate of low ordered lithium polysulfides.

[M0209 p9] The assumption made by us is only a hypothesis and needs additional experimental confirmation.
[M0210 p9] The reactivity of various species of sulfur and lithium polysulfides decreases in the series: S > LiSn > Li2Sn > LiSn > Sn2.
[M0211 p9] The reactivity of sulfur and lithium polysulfides is determined by the electron density on the polysulfide chain.
[M0212 p9] In the case of elemental sulfur, all sulfur atoms are neutral, so elemental sulfur has the greatest reactivity.
[M0213 p9] In the radical species of sulfur LiSn, the negative charge on the polysulfide chain is compensated by the positive charge of the lithium cation.
[M0214 p9] Due to the peculiarities of the electronic structure, the radical species of lithium polysulfides have a greater reactivity than neutral ones, such as Li2Sn.
[M0215 p9] Ionic species of sulfur have a lower reactivity than neutral ones due to the presence of a negative charge on the polysulfide chain.
[M0216 p9] The higher the density of the negative charge, the lower the reactivity.
[M0217 p9] The decrease in the discharge voltage at the high voltage stage with the increase in the support salt concentration (Fig. 3) is apparently due to the suppression of the homolytic dissociation of high-ordered lithium polysulfides.
[M0218 p9] This results in the decrease in the concentration of neutral and radical species of lithium polysulfides that whose reactivity is higher than that of molecular species.
[M0219 p9] The increase in discharge voltage (Fig. 3) and the increase in the depth of electrochemical reduction of sulfur at the low voltage stage (Fig. 2), which observed in the ascending region of the Qdisch. 1⁄4 f(Csalt) dependence, indicate that the increase in lithium perchlorate concentration in the electrolyte solution results in the increase in the reactivity of sulfur into low-ordered lithium polysulfides (n  4).
[M0220 p9] The increase in Li2Sn reactivity is apparently due to an equilibrium shift of electrolytic dissociation of lithium polysulfides (Equations (1) and (2)) towards the formation of molecular species of lithium polysulfides that possess higher reactivity than ionic species.
[M0221 p9] The subsequent decrease in the sulfur reduction depth in the descending region of the Qdisch. 1⁄4 f(Csalt) dependence can be explained by a considerable decline of the transport properties of the electrolyte system due to the increase in its viscosity (Fig. 6).
[M0222 p9] It is further decreased at high concentrations of lithium perchlorate (above 2М) by the shortage of free solvent molecules required for the solvation of lithium polysulfides.
[M0223 p9] The solvation energy of the lithium ion in electrolyte systems with different concentrations of lithium perchlorate practically does not change, therefore discharge voltage is determined to a greater degree by the kinetics of the electrode processes than by the thermodynamics of solvation of the lithium ion.
[M0224 p9] Similar studies were performed in electrolyte systems based on lithium trifluoromethanesulfonate (CF3SO3Li) and the nature of the anion of the support salt practically does not affect the general laws of electrode processes.

[M0233 p10] Effect of the support salt concentration on the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells The effect of the concentration of support salts on the reactivity of various species of sulfur and lithium polysulfides can be determined from the dependence of the DC resistance of lithium-sulfur cells vs. their depth of discharge.
[M0234 p10] The internal resistance of lithiumsulfur cells is composed of three constituents: the electrolyte resistance plus the charge transfer resistance on the positive and negative electrodes (Equation (22)): Rcell 1⁄4 Rel þ RLi þ RS (22) where: Rcell is the DC resistance of a lithium-sulfur cell, Ohm,cm2; Rel is resistance of the electrolyte solution, Ohm,cm2; RLi is the charge transfer resistance on the lithium electrode, Ohm,cm2; and RS is the charge transfer resistance on the sulfur electrode, Ohm,cm2.
[M0235 p10] The dependence of the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells on the depth of discharge is a curve with a complex shape where three regions corresponding to different discharge depths can be distinguished (Fig. 7a).
[M0236 p10] It is convenient to use the results of impedance studies [49] to analyze the contributions of various components to the total resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at various discharge depths.
[M0237 p10] As impedance studies have shown, at small discharge depths of 5e15% (1st region) the main contributions to the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells are made by the charge transfer resistance on the positive electrode in the electrochemical reduction of high-ordered lithium polysulfides (Li2Sn; n > 4) and by the resistance of passivating layers on the electrodes.
[M0238 p10] As high-ordered lithium polysulfides are reduced, the total concentration of lithium polysulfides in the electrolyte solutions increases, which considerably decreases the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte system.
[M0239 p10] Therefore, at depth of discharge of lithium-sulfur cells about 25e35%, the increase in their resistance is observed, with the main contribution coming from the resistance of the electrolyte solution.
[M0240 p10] This region corresponds to the complete reduction of elementary sulfur to soluble lithium polysulfides.
[M0241 p10] Therefore, the dependence of resistance on the depth of discharge passes through a maximum caused by the low electrolyte conductivity due to its saturation with lithium polysulfides.
[M0242 p10] Upon further discharge of lithium-sulfur cells, electrochemical reduction of lithium tetrasulfide and low-ordered lithium polysulfides occurs.
[M0243 p10] Since low-ordered lithium polysulfides possess higher mobility and affect the viscosity of the electrolyte system more weakly, the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte system increases, hence the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells decreases.
[M0244 p10] Therefore, the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at depths of discharge of 60e80% is determined more considerably by the charge transfer resistance in the reduction of low-ordered lithium polysulfides (Li2Sn; n < 4) and the resistance of passivating layers on the sulfur electrode than by the electrolyte resistance and the charge transfer resistance on the lithium electrode.
[M0245 p10] Thus, the effect of the salt concentration on the reactivity of lithium polysulfides can be determined from the resistances in the minimum in the 1st and 3rd regions of the dependence of resistance on the depth of discharge of lithium-sulfur cells.

[M0247 p10] As one can see from Fig. 7b, the resistance of lithium-sulfur cells at the high voltage stage (0.10 DoD) at moderate concentrations of lithium perchlorate (up to 2M) nearly does not depend on the salt concentration but decreases at the low voltage stage (0.75 DoD), Fig. 7.

[M0254 p11] Effect of the support salt concentration on the cycling performance of lithium-sulfur cells One of the most complex problems that needs to be resolved for creating commercially acceptable lithium-sulfur batteries is that their capacity fast decreases during charge-discharge cycling.
[M0255 p11] The decrease in the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling results from the simultaneous action of several factors, the main of which are listed below:  sulfur redistribution over the volume of lithium-sulfur cells;  sulfur transfer from the back-side space of the positive electrode to the front-side space;  sulfur encapsulation (as lithium sulfide) in the pore space of the carbon component;  passivation of the carbon component surface of the positive electrode;  decomposition of electrolyte system components (solvents and support salt anions).
[M0256 p11] The impact of these factors is determined by the concentration of the support salt.
[M0257 p11] As the support salt concentration increases, the rate of capacity depletion decreases, while the capacity of lithiumsulfur cells containing a high-concentrated solution increases rather than decreases in the initial stage of cycling.
[M0258 p11] One of the reasons of the decrease in the capacity of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling may lie in the passivation of the surface of carbon particles by hardly soluble products of electrochemical reactions - sulfur during the charge and lithium sulfide during the discharge.
[M0259 p11] If this hypothesis is correct, the decrease in the rate of capacity depletion of lithium-sulfur cells during cycling with the increase in the support salt concentration can be explained by hindrance of sulfur and lithium sulfide crystallization in high-concentrated electrolyte solutions. 4.7.
[M0260 p11] Effect of the support salt concentration on the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells The fact that the active material of the positive electrode of lithium-sulfur cells is located in electrolyte solutions results in a very undesirable phenomenon, i.e., direct reactions of sulfur and lithium polysulfides, dissolved in the electrolyte, with the metallic lithium electrode.
[M0261 p11] That decreases the coulomb efficiency of electrochemical processes in lithium-sulfur cells.
[M0262 p11] The rates of these reactions are determined by two factors: the transport properties of the electrolyte system and the transport properties of the surface electrolyte layer on the lithium electrode.
[M0263 p11] The composition and properties of the solid electrolyte on the lithium electrode surface and the transport properties of electrolyte systems are determined, to a considerable extent, by the concentration of the support salt.
[M0264 p11] As the support salt concentration in the electrolyte increases, the viscosity of the electrolyte system increases exponentially (Fig. 6).
[M0265 p11] Dissolution of lithium polysulfides in the electrolyte increases the viscosity of the electrolyte system even further.
[M0266 p11] Sulfur contained in molecular species of lithium polysulfides can be transferred only by diffusion, whereas sulfur within charged species (polysulfide anions) can be transferred both by diffusion and migration.
[M0267 p11] Since molecular species of lithium polysulfides are larger in size than ionic ones, the rate of sulfur transfer within lithium polysulfide molecules is lower than that within ionic particles.
[M0268 p11] Therefore, as the support salt concentration increases, the overall (diffusional and migrational) mobility of sulfur-containing particles decrease due to the increases in the viscosity of the electrolyte system.
[M0269 p11] Changes in the ionic composition of the electrolyte and in the mobility of sulfur-containing particles will also affect the composition of the surface film on the lithium electrode, and hence its transport properties.
[M0270 p11] It follows from the experimental data obtained (Fig. 5) that the coulomb efficiency quickly decreases at initial stages of lithiumsulfur cell cycling (5e10 cycles) in electrolyte solutions with low and moderate lithium perchlorate concentrations (0.5e1.5М) and then almost does not change.
[M0271 p11] In high-concentrated electrolyte solutions (with LiClO4 concentrations above 1.5M) the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells is higher than in less concentrated solutions and remains nearly unchanged during cycling.
[M0272 p11] High values of coulomb efficiency (more than 100%) in the high concentrated electrolyte (2.8M LiClO4) are explained by the fact that the experimentally determined discharge capacity of lithiumsulfur cells is greater than the charge capacity of the cells.
[M0273 p11] This is probably due to the fact that in high-concentrated electrolytes, because of their high viscosity, only a part of sulfur in the positive electrode is partly electrochemically reduced at the first cycles.
[M0274 p11] During cycling, the part of sulfur electrochemically reduced increases, which leads to the increase in the discharge capacity of lithium-sulfur cells and the decrease in coulomb efficiency.
[M0275 p11] The decrease in coulomb efficiency at initial stages of cycling in electrolytes with low and moderate concentrations of lithium perchlorate can be due either to improvement of the transport properties of the surface film on the lithium electrode or to a growth of the lithium electrode surface due to dispersion of metallic lithium.
[M0276 p11] It is possible that both factors act simultaneously.
[M0277 p11] Identification of the reasons of the decrease in the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells requires additional experimental studies.
[M0278 p11] The increase in the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithiumsulfur cells with the increase in concentration of support salt is most likely due to the slowness of interelectrode sulfur transfer due to the increase in the viscosity of electrolyte solutions [50].
[M0279 p11] The decrease in the self-discharge (shuttle) current density of lithium-sulfur cells with the increase in the viscosity of solutions of lithium perchlorate in sulfolane (Fig. 5b) may indicate that the increase in the coulomb efficiency of cycling of lithium-sulfur cells with the increase in the concentration of the support salt is mainly due to the delay in interelectrode transfer of sulfur and lithium polysulfides. 5.
